### RUG BUYING TIPS FOR NERVOUS RUG SHOPPERS:

#### 1) Find the Right COMPANY  
- **Rug Galleries**: Look for reputable stores selling hand-woven oriental rugs (new or antique). Avoid "going out of business sales," which often sell flawed merchandise. Ask local rug cleaners for recommendations.  
- **Furniture & Flooring Stores**: These may carry rugs but often lack specialized knowledge. Inspect fiber and construction quality carefully.  
- **Antique/Consignment Stores**: Potential treasures exist but verify quality, as owners may not be experts.  

#### 2) Find the Right RUG: Fibers, Dyes, and Construction  
**Fibers**:  
- **GOOD**: Wool (durable, hides soil, sustainable), silk (luxurious but delicate), and cotton.  
- **BAD**: Synthetics (nylon, acrylic, polyester)—less durable, soil-prone.  
- **UGLY**: Viscose/rayon (weak, bleeds), sisal/jute (stains easily).  

**Dyes**:  
- **GOOD**: Colorfast dyes (natural or synthetic). Test with a damp cloth.  
- **BAD**: Fugitive dyes (bleed when wet). Common in tribal rugs with poor rinsing.  
- **UGLY**: Over-dye treatments (e.g., tea wash) or ink disguising flaws.  

**Construction**:  
- **GOOD**: Woven rugs (hand or machine-made). Check by flipping a corner to see if the design mirrors the front. Hand-woven rugs have knots around warp threads.  
- **BAD**: Tufted rugs (latex-backed, less durable). Avoid for high-traffic areas or pet homes.  
- **UGLY**: Custom/synthetic shag rugs—difficult to clean and maintain.  

#### 3) Find the Right PRICE  
- Hand-woven wool rugs start at ~$10/sq. ft. (higher for quality/antiques).  
- Value depends on craftsmanship, age, and materials. Focus on rugs that resonate with you.  
- Inspect: wool quality, dye stability, weaving flaws, and hidden damage. Consult a cleaner for pre-purchase checks.  

**Final Advice**: Buy from transparent sellers, prioritize natural fibers, and choose rugs you love. Avoid "too-good-to-be-true" deals and synthetic-heavy rugs.  

Happy Rug Shopping!
