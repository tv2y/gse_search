Qingming Festival (清明节 or Qīngmíngjié), also called Tomb Sweeping Day or Pure Brightness Festival, is an important traditional Chinese holiday celebrated in China and among Chinese communities worldwide. The main activity involves cleaning ancestors' tombs.  

The holiday occurs 15 days after the Spring Equinox, usually between April 3rd and 5th. It is a public holiday in mainland China.  

### Origins  
Qingming Festival originated from the ancient Cold Food Festival (寒食节, Hánshíjié), which commemorated Jie Zitui, a loyal follower of Duke Wen of Jin. After Jie Zitui and his mother died in a fire set by the duke, people avoided fire and ate cold food for days to honor him. Over time, the traditions merged with Qingming Festival.  

### Activities  
1. **Tomb-Sweeping (扫墓, sǎomù):** Families clean ancestors' graves, especially in rural areas where tombs are on hillsides. Offerings like rice, liquor, and meat are left, followed by burning incense and hell money. Firecrackers were traditional but are now banned in cities.  
2. **Spring Outings (踏青, tàqīng):** People enjoy nature and parks after tomb visits.  
3. **Kite Flying:** Kites with lanterns are flown at night; cutting the string is believed to bring good luck.  

### Food  
Traditional foods include:  
- **青团 (qīngtuán):** Green sticky rice dumplings with sweet fillings.  
- **馓子 (sǎnzi):** Crunchy, cold, deep-fried dough twists.  

Qingming Festival remains a significant time for honoring ancestors and celebrating spring.
