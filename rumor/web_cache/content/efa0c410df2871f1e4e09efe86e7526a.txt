Hackers Deploy Stealth Backdoor in WordPress Mu-Plugins to Maintain Admin Access

July 24, 2025 • By info@thehackernews.com (The Hacker News)

Cybersecurity researchers have discovered a new stealthy backdoor in WordPress sites, concealed within the "mu-plugins" directory. This backdoor allows threat actors to gain persistent access and perform arbitrary actions. The malware is hidden in a PHP script called "wp-index.php" and fetches a remote payload from an obfuscated URL. It then executes the payload, giving the attacker full administrator access and allowing them to inject malicious code, steal data, or redirect site visitors. To mitigate this threat, site owners should update WordPress, themes, and plugins regularly, secure accounts with two-factor authentication, and audit the site regularly.

Why outsourcing cybersecurity is rising in the Adriatic region

July 24, 2025 • By Mirko Zorz

Aleksandar Stančin, Board Member at Exclusive Networks, discusses the state of cybersecurity in the Adriatic region. He notes that local markets often lag behind EU regulations, despite facing similar threats to other European countries. Organizations in the region tend to approach security issues reactively, but there is a shift towards a more proactive and systematic approach, particularly in the governmental and banking sectors. However, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) struggle with limited budgets, skills, and understanding of security risks. Stančin highlights the trend of companies outsourcing security concerns to managed security service providers (MSSPs) and managed security operations centers (MSOCs), which is improving the region's security posture. He also notes the growing interest in automation, orchestration, and AI assistance in security. The main challenges in the region include the lack of skilled talent, limited budgets, and the complexity of regulatory requirements. To address these challenges, companies are investing in education and training, and sponsoring development to retain talent. Stančin emphasizes the importance of board conversations in securing budget and understanding of security risks, and notes that regulatory push is driving progress in the region. Overall, while there are challenges, the cybersecurity landscape in the Adriatic region is improving, with a growing focus on resilience, awareness, and public-private partnerships.

Most data breaches have unknown causes as transparency continues to fall

July 24, 2025 • By Help Net Security

The Identity Theft Resource Center (ITRC) reported 1,732 publicly disclosed data breaches in the first half of 2025, a 5% increase from the same period in 2024. Cyberattacks were the primary cause, with 1,348 incidents reported, resulting in 114,582,621 victim notices. The financial services and healthcare industries were the most targeted, with 387 and 283 compromises, respectively. The number of data breach notices without information about the root cause increased to 69%. Supply chain attacks and AI-powered phishing attacks are growing threats, with 79 supply chain breaches reported, affecting 690 entities and compromising 78,320,240 individuals. The ITRC also noted a rise in physical attacks, with 34 reported in the first half of 2025, exceeding the total for 2024.
