What is the best AI model as of June 15, 2025?

### Felipe Sanchez Garzon

 Published Jun 16, 2025 

+ Follow

It seems like every day there’s a new breakthrough, a new controversy, or a new debate. Let’s break down the current state of AI in simple terms, explaining how it’s rapidly evolving, how we measure its “intelligence,” and what it means for all of us.

AI models are growing at an incredible speed. Just imagine, the “frontier barrier” of AI intelligence, which once seemed vast, is now shrinking geographically, meaning more countries are developing top-tier AI. There’s been a truly significant jump in AI capabilities just since last November. It appears that AI follows a “scaling law”: the more computing power (chips) and money invested, the better the results, and surprisingly, these results are now being achieved with less money per operation.

So, how do experts gauge how “smart” an AI model really is? They use a variety of sophisticated tests and metrics:

### GPQA Diamond Test

This is a very challenging science test, at the PhD level. AI models that perform better than typical human PhDs on this test are considered highly intelligent:

Reasoning models such as OpenAI’s “O” series models (like O3 or O4 mini high). Source: 

There’s a significant difference between a basic AI model and what we now call a “reasoning model”. Think of it like this: some AI models, especially the free or default versions, react quickly, offering an “instinctive reaction” (like “thinking fast”). However, “reasoning models” such as OpenAI’s “O” series models (like O3 or O4 mini high) are designed to “wait a minute, think, and then respond”. This “thinking slow” process allows them to tackle hyper-complex questions and automate tasks that were previously impossible for AI. For example, while an older GPT-4 model could handle tasks a human might do in a minute, a reasoning model like O3 can successfully handle problems that would take a human 20 minutes. These advanced reasoning models are also much better at avoiding “hallucinations,” which are those instances where AI models generate inaccurate or nonsensical information.

### Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index

This is a comprehensive ranking that combines results from seven different evaluations. It gives a weighted average score to AI models. For instance, the free version of GPT-4O scored 41 points, while an updated DeepSeek R1 scored 68, and the most advanced frontier models reached 70–71 points:

Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. 

What’s even more fascinating is that this rapid progress isn’t just happening in Silicon Valley. A Chinese company called High Flyers, which previously focused on high-frequency trading, developed DeepSeek R1 and achieved results comparable to the most advanced frontier models, even without the same access to cutting-edge chips. This kind of innovation, spurred partly by open-source models like Meta’s LLaMA (which was famously leaked by an anonymous engineer, prompting Meta to officially release it), is helping regions like China and Europe (with companies like Mistral) quickly close the gap with U.S. AI leaders.

### Humanity’s Last Exam

This is considered one of the most critical evaluations. It consists of thousands of incredibly difficult questions from the cutting edge of human science and technology. The goal is to measure the percentage of accurate answers. If an AI ever reached 100% on this exam, it would imply it could solve anything knowable by humans. Less than a year ago, GPT-4O only scored 2.7%, but today, Gemini 2.5 Pro has reached an impressive 21%.

Humanity’s Last Exam. 

### Context Window

This metric measures how much information an AI model can “remember” and process without forgetting. It includes everything you type and everything the AI generates in a conversation. Early models had context windows of around 16,000 tokens (a token is roughly a word or part of a word). Now, models like Gemini 2.5 Pro can handle a staggering 1 million tokens. This massive “memory” allows AI to tackle incredibly complex tasks, such as processing enormous amounts of data to reorganize 6,000 online courses, achieving results far superior to what human experts could manage alone.

### The Astonishing Pace of Advancement of AI

AI is already showing its power in creative ways. Google’s Veo3, for example, can generate stunning videos from a single text prompt. It’s also being used to uncover ancient texts, decode the language of whales, and discover new archaeological sites like Nazca lines using satellite imagery. And looking ahead, self-driving car companies like Waymo are predicted to grow 15 times their current size by 2030, potentially automating the most common job on the planet: driving. This truly indicates that AI is akin to the microchip itself, opening doors for a whole new class of products and capabilities.

Beyond the Hype: Real-World Implications and Challenges

While the advancements are thrilling, there are also significant implications and challenges:

*   Privacy: When you use many current AI models like ChatGPT or Gemini, your data is sent to a server. This raises privacy concerns because the data is often sent “plain,” meaning unencrypted, which could allow the server owner to “read your mind” or access sensitive information. This is why companies like Apple are pushing for AI models that run entirely “on-device” (locally), prioritizing user privacy. It’s also why many businesses are opting to run AI models like DeepSeek or LLaMA on their own servers for security. In fact, a U.S. court recently ordered OpenAI to store all user input and output tokens, even for enterprise users, a decision OpenAI is currently appealing.
*   Economic Impact: While AI is transforming industries, its effect on overall economic growth (GDP) isn’t immediately obvious yet, partly because creating the technology comes before widespread product implementation. However, the shift in labor is becoming noticeable. While general unemployment isn’t spiking, it is becoming harder for new graduates to find their first professional jobs.
*   Social Good: Beyond the concerns, AI is already proving to be a powerful tool for good. A pilot program in Nigeria, using an older version of GPT-4, helped students progress the equivalent of two years in just six weeks. This highlights AI’s potential to revolutionize education and improve lives globally.

The Bottom Line

Whether AI models “think” or are “conscious” isn’t the most important question right now. What truly matters is the undeniable economic and social impact they are having and will continue to have. We are living in a truly unique and special moment for technology, akin to the invention of the microchip itself. It’s an immense opportunity for those who choose to be creators rather than just consumers of this incredible technology.
