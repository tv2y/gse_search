Internal rhyme occurs within a verse or phrase, either within the same line or across multiple lines, to enhance musicality and rhythm. Below are examples from notable poems showcasing this technique:

1. **"The Raven" by Edgar Allan Poe**  
   Uses repeated internal rhymes like "dreary," "weary," "napping," and "tapping," creating rhythmic harmony.

2. **"The Rime of the Ancient Mariner" by S.T. Coleridge**  
   Words such as "cheered," "cleared," "higher," and "guest" create melodic allure in its stanzas.

3. **"Annabel Lee" by Edgar Allan Poe**  
   Features internal rhymes like "love," "soul," "dreams," and "bride," though less frequent than in "The Raven."

4. **"The Cloud" by Percy Bysshe Shelley**  
   Rhymes like "snow," "below," "towers," and "bowers" appear sporadically, enhancing rhythm and meaning.

5. **"The Chimney Sweeper" by William Blake**  
   Brief internal rhymes like "weep," "sweep," and "sleep" add rhythmic depth.

6. **"The Owl and the Pussy-Cat" by Edward Lear**  
   Pairs such as "owl"/"fowl" and "married"/"tarried" contribute to its playful musicality.

7. **"Boy with a Moon and Star on His Head" by Yusuf Islam**  
   Internal rhymes like "joy"/"boy" and "hands"/"sands" blend lyrical flow with narrative.

8. **"The Pied Piper of Hamelin" by Robert Browning**  
   Examples include "rats"/"cats" and "speaking"/"shrieking," adding whimsical rhythm.

9. **"Mine – by the Right of the White Election!" by Emily Dickinson**  
   Sparse internal rhymes (e.g., "right"/"white") intertwine with end rhymes for melodic structure.

10. **"Galoshes" by Rhoda W. Bacmeister**  
    Repetitive sounds like "splishes"/"sploshes" and "stamp"/"tramp" create a vivid, rhythmic effect.  

These examples highlight how internal rhymes enrich poetic rhythm, sound, and emotional resonance.
