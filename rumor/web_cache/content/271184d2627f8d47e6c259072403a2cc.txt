FAIR Take | October 2025

Los Angeles County officials voted 4-1 to declare a state of emergency over federal immigration enforcement operations. The declaration authorizes the county to provide rent assistance to illegal aliens who have fallen behind on payments due to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE)operations.

Under this pronouncement, illegal aliens will be able to apply through an online government portal, expected to launch within two months, for taxpayer-funded rental assistance. The program aims to prevent evictions of illegal aliens whose household members have been detained or deported by ICE.

County Supervisors Lindsey Horvath and Janice Hahn, who introduced the measure, claimed the enforcement operations have led to “decreased attendance at workplaces, disruption of local economies, and strain on critical services,” according to the county’s emergency proclamation.

The lone dissenting voice, County Supervisor Kathryn Barger, said “Stretching emergency powers for federal immigration actions undermines their purpose, invites legal challenges, and circumvents the public process.” Barger’s mention of legal challenges may be referring to fears that this declaration will spur an eviction moratorium like the Covid pandemic that spawned multiple lawsuits and further deterioration of affordable housing in the county.

But the inevitable effects on the LA housing market are at least one degree removed from the main issue facing this emergency declaration: what exactly constitutes an“emergency”?

To be clear: federal law enforcement conducting lawful immigration enforcement operations do not constitute an emergency. Natural disasters, like wildfires, are an emergency. Terrorist attacks are emergencies. ICE enforcing immigration law is not an emergency; it is the federal government doing its job.

In fact, as White House Spokeswoman Abigail Jackson stated, “The real emergency is the countless criminal illegal aliens that Joe Biden let waltz through our Southern Border and terrorize American communities.”

If LA County can declare an emergency over lawful federal law enforcement, what can’t be deemed an emergency? This establishes a troubling precedent: local governments weaponizing emergency powers to obstruct federal authority whenever they disagree with policy or even federal law. LA County officials are using their positions not to uphold the law, but to actively undermine it, protecting lawbreakers from accountability while forcing taxpayers to bear the costs.

The emergency declaration undeniably raises significant legal questions. When asked whether the county’s actions constitute “aiding and abetting in lawbreaking,” U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi responded: “It is.”

But federal preemption and violations of the Constitution’s Supremacy Clause are not the only basis for possible legal challenges. Property owners could challenge the measure for preventing them from collecting rental income or evicting non-paying tenants, like they did during the pandemic. Additionally, a local government using taxpayer funds to assist individuals in violation of federal law may face legal scrutiny, particularly if residents challenge the allocation of resources to non-citizens over citizens. Finally, California law requires that emergency declarations be based on actual emergencies posing imminent threats to public safety – whether legal federal law enforcement meets this threshold is exceedingly questionable.

This emergency declaration represents the latest action by California officials in their systematic obstruction of federal immigration enforcement. Last month, Governor Gavin Newsom signed legislation banning ICE agents from wearing masks, a transparent political move designed to hinder enforcement operations. Moreover, California officials have promised to _push back_ against immigration enforcement at every turn.

But at what cost? The emergency declaration mobilizes public resources, diverts funds from legitimate county needs, and sets up future legal battles. Meanwhile, struggling American citizens in LA County, those facing homelessness, those unable to afford rent themselves, those dealing with crime, high state unemployment, and failing schools, receive no such emergency assistance.

This dangerous and shortsighted declaration prioritizes illegal aliens over American citizens, punishes property owners, and establishes perverse incentives for future illegal immigration. Worst of all, it sends an unmistakable message to the world: _Come to Los Angeles illegally, and even if federal authorities catch up with you, local officials will use taxpayer money to subsidize your stay_. This isn’t compassion or a progressive policy; it’s a dereliction of duty with profound consequences for the rule of law.
