James Sale’s *HellWard* is the first volume of a planned trilogy entitled *The English Cantos*. It is considered the best epic poem written in English in several centuries, reviving the traditional epic form in a secular, materialistic age where such works are rare. The poem follows an infernal journey, structured in terzains (three-line stanzas), inspired by Dante’s *Divine Comedy* but reinterpreted for a modern audience, with Hell depicted as hospital wards rather than Dante’s rigid circles.  

Sale’s work combines classical and Christian themes, blending intellectual depth with poetic craft. Unlike many modern poets, Sale avoids narcissism and postmodern fragmentation, instead upholding traditional values while critiquing contemporary moral and intellectual decline. The poem is philosophical, darkly humorous, and at times satirical, with vivid depictions of historical and fictional figures in Hell. Critics praise its technical mastery, narrative propulsion, and moral vision, positioning it as a potential future classic, despite the declining cultural relevance of epic poetry today.  

While Sale acknowledges his debt to Dante and Milton, *HellWard* is original in its thematic focus on intellectual error as the root of damnation and its modernized portrayal of Hell. The poem’s linguistic complexity, enjambment, and near-rhymes reflect its layered meaning. Despite the challenges of reviving epic in a post-literate society, Sale’s work stands as a bold attempt to restore sublimity and moral instruction to poetry.
