TV’s at Best Buy are all on a special “showroom” setting where the brightness and contrast are cranked to the max with each one trying to outdo the ones adjacent to it. This setting should be changed once you bring it home, usually the “cinema” setting or similar is the most natural color.

I presume that Verizon is feeding you at least a 720p signal, but can’t say for sure.

The difference between a good tv and an average or poor tv is that even blown up to the larger size, even a low quality SD channel will still look pretty good. Cheap tv’s that look good in the showroom tend to look really awful at home, especially with lower quality content.

If you really want to save money, search Craigslist for a plasma tv, and then look up the suggested calibration settings online. Most of these will look better than anything you can buy for $1000+ new, and they are often available for under $100.

All tv's in a showroom (doesn't matter the store) are set to look good in a bright setting as default from the factory.

The difference between a $500 vs $1000 tv is how many settings you can change, usually the panel is the same between price points.

No modern tv's are not tube units, grainy isn't a thing anymore - get the biggest tv you can fit in the room based on how far away your seating area is.

The type of internet you have doesn't have anything to do with picture quality in this sense "resolution" refers to the number of pixels that compose the picture on the TV. HD is the worst picture you can buy at 720p :

720p 1,280x720 HD, High Definition TVs 

 1080p 1,920x1,080 Full HD, FHD, HD, High Definition TVs, monitors

 WUXGA 1,920x1,200 Widescreen Ultra Extended Graphics Array Monitors, projectors

 2K 2,048x[unspecified] none Projectors

 UHD 3,840x2,160 4K, Ultra HD, Ultra-High Definition TVs, monitors

 8K 7,680x4,320 8K UHD TVs

You want to look for something in the 2K or UHD resolution, 8K content is not really a mass produced thing yet.

1) Are the Best Buy tv's fed an artificial signal so the pictures looks that good?

 A: Sometimes absolutely yes. Typically, this is done with settings. I found this (at Tweeter, etc) back in 2002 when I bought my first crt HDTV with this year's model next to last year's. I took the remotes and went into settings. The last year's model was purposely set to "crap". I set the 2 the same and there was zero difference between the two. I bought last year's for $2500. This year's was $10,000. (remember this was in 2002). The feed going to the tv is likely identical and if you don't think so, get behind the demo unit and switch inputs.

2) When going from my 32" model to a 43 or 55 inch, won't the picture look grainy since everything is larger?

 A: Somewhat, but not noticeable. Go look at one.

3) When it says the tv is HD, is that what FIOS is sending or will I have to pay extra?

 A: Yes. Not only do you pay extra for the HD signal, you pay double for the cable box. (I have fios). Non HD will be at 720P and will blow away your previous picture quality.

Extra: Get a $35 HD outside antenna. They're small and not noticeable unless you point it out to someone. The picture quality over the air will be better than any cable signal you get. We have one and if I want to switch to clear local channels, I go to the antenna.

I've bought a few LED computer monitors so I'm not clueless, just not sure about tv's. My 27" monitor was $400 but there were same brand and size selling for $250 but the display was not as sharp.

TVs are much cheaper than monitors. DS#1 (engineer) just bought his own 55" 4k tv for his room from BB. It cost $450 and is a TKL. For reference, TKL is Best Buy's store brand, formerly called insignia. They use Samsung screens and build the tv at their contractor. You may be able to get far more of a discount with these as there's a huge markup on them. We've gone through several tvs over the past 20 years. You know what? All our brand name ones have died. Samsung.....LG.....all dead. We took my son's insignia when he went off to college and are still using it. It's the only tv in our house that hasn't died.

If I were buying a tv for ME, I would buy the lowest priced TV that would fit in our entertainment center. Our fios is at 720p so even the "low end" 1080p tv's now will be more than we need. We're at about a 42" tv and I wouldn't pay more than about $250 for one.

You may wish to go to your public library and read the most recent Consumer Reports on this topic.

Verizon FIOS sends 1080, no matter which TV package you have. However, some people may have a Standard Definition set-top box in one of their home locations because it may be without the monthly charge for HD boxes. That is, we pay monthly for our HD box and got a free SD box as part of our "bundle". It's uncommon to otherwise have an SD cable box today.

I don't know if you want streaming. It's nice to have a TV that offers Apps and a network plug so that you don't need an additional box to stream. However, we don't enable all our "smart TV" features on our LG flat screen because it requires a privacy waiver we don't want to sign. We do have Netflix and Amazon buttons on the same remote that came with our LG TV, which is nice. In Covid-19 times, we have some YouTube things we watch directly on the LG, which has a (poor) web browser, as well. But because YouTube, Amazon, Netflix provide the Apps, they are improved and kept up to date. LG Operating System, not so much.

I think we would have been happy with the non-OLED Samsung that I was looking at the same time I chose LG OLED. The blacks are almost too black (dark picture) on the LG. There are lots and lots of preference settings to be made on a flatscreen. And you have to make them again for every possible input (cable, live OTA, DVD, and so on, to get the best possible picture.

One thing that I would recommend is that you buy it at Costco. They automatically extend the warranty to 2 years and if you buy it with a Costco credit card they extend it another 2 years for a total of four years. Since you are not in a hurry you could even get a Costco membership and credit card if you do not already have one. If you find that you are not using the Costco membership enough to make it worthwhile then you can cancel your membership to get a refund. You may also be able to get a friend or relative to buy it for you at Costco if you do not have a membership.

For a basic TV it would be hard to go wrong by just going into Costco and buying whatever they have in a $300 price range. It is real easy to over analyze this. Coming from a SD TV anything you get will me amazing once you get it set up. You can of course get higher end stuff but to me that is not worth the price.

One note: For TV's Visio and TLC are both widely known name brands so do not be turned off if you have not heard of them. Some of their models may not be high end but they are not a some random unknown company.

There are really only two "gotcha's".

1) You need to make sure that it has the right connectors if you are going to connect things like DVD players, Game systems, sound systems, etc. Figure out what you need then check any TV to make sure that it has the connectors that you need.

2) Some "TV's" do not have the tuner to receive over the air TV signals so they are really monitors and not TV's. Since you will be using a cable box this may not be a problem but you should know that when you buy the TV.

There are a few basic things to plan for.

1) Where will you put it on? It likely will not fit on your old TV stand. You should also plan on securing it so it will not tip over, especially if you have kids. You will need to be a bit handy and you will need at least one helper if you are going to try to mount it on a wall. Do not mount it too high on a wall or over a fireplace.

2) You should plan on getting at least a basic sound bar. The tiny speakers they build into TVs are pretty much a joke an only really useful for checking to see if the sound is working.

3) How big a TV to get? It is possible and not all that expensive to get a TV that is too big for your room. I found this size guide with a quick search but there are lots of web pages that will give you more information on how to pick the right size TV for your room.

https://www.crutchfield.com/S-qkAIg1H5j ... ement.html

4) How will you get it home? If you get a large TV then you need to figure out if it will fit in your car. You may need to get someone with a van or pickup to help you get it home.

5) As other people have said you need to check with your cable company to see if you will have an HD signal and if it will cost extra.

6) You will likely need several HDMI cables. Many stores will try to sell them at as ridiculous price of $20 or more, but you can get them online for around $5 or less. Monoprice is a good place to get them.
