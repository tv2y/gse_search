**[Hangzhou to Abolish Academic Ranking in Primary and Secondary Schools From Autumn 2025, Implementing Progressive Evaluation System]**  
**[September 1, 2025]**  
**[Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province]**  

In a landmark educational reform, the Hangzhou Municipal Education Bureau announced today that all primary and secondary schools in the city will abolish traditional academic ranking systems starting from the 2025 autumn semester. Instead, a progressive evaluation system will be implemented to foster holistic student development and reduce excessive competition.  

The decision was formally unveiled at a press conference held at the Hangzhou Education Center, attended by Bureau Director Li Wei, renowned educational psychologist Professor Zhang Yaling, and representatives from over 50 schools. Director Li emphasized that the move aligns with national policies promoting quality education and mental well-being. "Ranking students by scores has long exacerbated anxiety and stifled individuality. Our new system will focus on personalized growth, encouraging students to surpass their own benchmarks rather than compete against peers," Li stated.  

Under the progressive evaluation framework, teachers will assess students based on multidimensional criteria, including academic improvement, creativity, collaboration, and social responsibility. For instance, quarterly reports will highlight strengths and areas for growth instead of numerical rankings. Pilot programs conducted in 15 schools since 2023 showed a 20% reduction in student stress levels and improved classroom engagement, according to data released by the bureau.  

Parents and educators expressed mixed reactions. While some applauded the shift as "long overdue," others raised concerns about transparency in college admissions. In response, the bureau confirmed collaboration with provincial universities to adapt enrollment criteria. Professor Zhang underscored the psychological benefits: "This reform acknowledges diverse talents and fosters intrinsic motivation, which is crucial for lifelong learning."  

The policy will be phased in across all 600+ public and private schools in Hangzhou by September 2025, with teacher training workshops scheduled throughout the upcoming academic year. Hangzhou’s initiative is expected to influence broader educational reforms nationwide.
