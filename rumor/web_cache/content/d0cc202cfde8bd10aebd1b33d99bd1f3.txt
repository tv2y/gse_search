Wisconsin DHS confirms 12 new measles cases in Oconto County, 36 cases total
============================================================================

BY  Aly Prouty and Rachel Ryan Wisconsin

UPDATED 3:15 PM ET Sep. 12, 2025 PUBLISHED 4:14 PM ET Sep. 02, 2025 PUBLISHED 4:14 PM EDT Sep. 02, 2025

SHARE

OCONTO COUNTY, Wis. — The Wisconsin Department of Health Services reported Friday there have now been 36 confirmed measles cases in Oconto County this year.

DHS said there are 12 new cases in the county; the total previously had been 24 cases. DHS said it will update its measles data Tuesdays and Fridays.

DHS said there are no confirmed cases of measles in any other Wisconsin county.

Two of those patients have been hospitalized. None have died.

All 36 people were unvaccinated, per DHS.

Four of those cases are in children between the ages of 0- to 4-years-old. Kids between the ages of 5 and 17 account for the highest percentage of cases: 18. Fourteen adults have contracted measles.

Measles is highly contagious, spreading from person to person through the air. It can stay in the air for up to two hours after a sick person coughs or sneezes.

Up to 90% of people around someone that’s infected with measles can get it if they aren’t vaccinated.

Symptoms of measles include:

*   Runny nose
*   High fever (may be greater than 104°F)
*   Tiredness
*   Cough
*   Red, watery eyes, or conjunctivitis (“pink eye”)
*   A red rash with raised bumps that starts at the hairline and moves to the arms and legs three to five days after symptoms begin

These symptoms can start 10 to 21 days after exposure. If you develop symptoms, DHS recommended staying home and said to call your doctor’s office or clinic before going to ensure you don’t expose others.

Measles is typically prevented through the measles, mumps, rubella vaccine (MMR), with two doses being 97% effective in prevention. In light of the new cases, DHS urged people to check if they are vaccinated or not. You can do that here.
