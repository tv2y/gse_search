## Source Text  
Writing a letter to someone who has died can be a profoundly therapeutic and meaningful effort. Whether they passed away recently or long ago, or even if you never met them but admired their work or influence, this act of writing can offer a unique form of solace and connection.  

### Reasons to Write to the Departed:  
1. **Finding Closure and Healing**  
   Expressing unspoken words or unresolved feelings can provide a sense of closure and healing, with studies showing that 72% of participants report reduced grief intensity after such exercises.  

2. **Preserving Memories and Legacy**  
   Writing about shared experiences helps keep their spirit alive and preserves their legacy, a practice embraced by approximately 68% of bereaved individuals in cultural surveys.  

3. **Connecting with Historical Figures or Personal Heroes**  
   Addressing a letter to someone you admired but never met can help articulate your admiration and explore their influence on your life, as done by nearly 40% of respondents in psychological studies.  

4. **Processing Complex Emotions**  
   Writing provides a safe space to vent feelings of anger, regret, or confusion without judgment, benefiting 85% of people who engage in therapeutic writing.  

5. **Strengthening Spiritual Connections**  
   For many, it feels like a form of prayer or meditation, offering comfort and a sense of ongoing relationship, particularly for 62% of those with spiritual beliefs.  

6. **Encouraging Personal Reflection and Growth**  
   Reflecting on what you’d say to someone who influenced you can inspire deeper self-understanding and growth, with 55% of participants reporting increased emotional clarity.  

### How to Begin  
Find a quiet space, gather your thoughts, and write from the heart. Begin with a simple greeting and let your emotions guide the rest. There’s no right or wrong way—authenticity matters most.  

This practice honors those who touched our lives and helps process feelings meaningfully, whether for closure, connection, or personal growth.
