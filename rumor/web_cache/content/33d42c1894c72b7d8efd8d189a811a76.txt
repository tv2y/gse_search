Introduction
------------

Human-machine interfaces (HMIs), as a window of communication between the users and the equipment and robots, are the key elements for achieving effective, intuitive, and seamless manipulation to conduct various tasks [1,2]. Soft electronics has been proven unique advantages in HMIs, such as miniaturized size [3,4], skin-integrated systems [[5], [6], [7]], and dense arrays of actuators [8]. To date, many flexible and stretchable sensors have been designed to detect signals from both human body and external environments [[9], [10], [11]]. To realize a closed-loop HMI to coordinate the detection of sensing signals and execution commands is the future of this area, that requires a comprehensive feedback interface [12,13]. Visual and auditory feedback devices have been popular used to provide information in HMIs [[14], [15], [16]]. However, skin is the largest organ in human body, reproducing force and tactile sensation with rich information for haptic feedback, which is very significant for virtual reality/augmented reality (VR/AR) applications [6,13,17]. A well-designed haptic feedback system can provide better immersive experience for users and enrich the experience and assist the users to do better jobs when using HMIs [3,18,19]. The ability to stimulate different tactile sensations in a wearable device depends on its active components. Compared with conventional cumbersome haptic feedback devices, the light weight, miniaturized haptic feedback interfaces with portable, wearable or even skin-integrated formats, are more suitable for the next generation HMIs and electronics [20,21]. Recent advances in the development of VR/AR and their huge market highlight the importance of portable and wearable haptic feedback systems, and therefore spur the research in this field [8,22].

In this review, we first introduce the basic physiological mechanism of haptics, including kinesthetic sensation and tactile sensation. Then, we discuss stimulation mechanisms in haptic interfaces which have potentials in future portable and wearable electronics, where the mechanisms can be divided into three types, forced based haptic interfaces [[23], [24], [25]], thermal based haptic interfaces [[26], [27], [28]], and nerve stimulation based haptic interfaces [[29], [30], [31]] (Fig.1). Force based haptic interfaces are typical mechanical systems which utilize devices to apply force on the skin and muscles to create skin deformations that simulate how body touch or interact with real objects [6]. Thermal based interfaces modify the temperature change felt by the skin based on the thermal properties of virtual materials [27]. Nerve stimulation based haptic interfaces directly utilize the electrical current to stimulate human nerves to transmit signals to the brain and thus resulting in the pseudo-haptic feedback [31]. Haptic information can also be divides into several dimensions, such as force, macro roughness and fine roughness, friction, hardness, and warmness etc. [32] Nowadays, there are many mechanisms having been used to create different types of haptic feedbacks. Owning to the advantages and disadvantages in each technique, successful in reproducing haptic feedbacks can only happened in some sort of format with a relatively small range, which is not versatile enough to produce comprehensive tactile perceptions and support a variety of VR applications.

We further compare different mechanisms for wearable haptic interfaces and discuss their possibilities for the combination to create complex tactile sensations. Examples of approaches for realizing multi-mode haptic interfaces include the combination of different methods such as mechanical and electrical stimulation [24], electrostatic and vibrotactile stimulators for variable friction and vibrational surfaces [33], thermal and vibrotactile stimulators for temperature and vibrational feedback [34]. Multimodal haptic interfaces integrated different active haptic feedback components together to show complex haptic perception has huge potential for future application [24,35]. In principle, any tactile sensation can be reproduced if there is a versatile machine which can stimulate skin with sufficient spatial and temporal resolution. However, only a few papers have discussed the synergistic effect of different tactile stimulation mode. We have listed the published wearable multimode haptic interfaces so far and discuss the changelings and future opportunities for future wearable multimodal haptic interfaces.

Conclusion and outlook
----------------------

Haptic feedback interface is important for close loop HMI and future VR/AR systems and other immersive reality devices [216]. This paper reviewed the recent progress of different mechanisms for portable and wearable haptic feedback interfaces. It divided the haptic feedback interfaces based on the difference of haptic feedback mechanisms, focusing on force based, thermal based and nerve stimulation based haptic feedback interfaces.
