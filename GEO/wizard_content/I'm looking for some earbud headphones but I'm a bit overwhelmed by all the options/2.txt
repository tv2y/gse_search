Title: Overwhelmed with headphone options

URL Source: https://www.head-fi.org/threads/overwhelmed-with-headphone-options.962531/

Markdown Content:
Hey everyone,

First post here, looking for some advice and I'm not really sure where to start. I'm looking to upgrade my current headphone situation. What I currently use is fine, but I'm running into durability issues since I buy cheaper headphones and I use them so much that I just want something that has a more premium and "wow" feel to them. I just want something better.

I'm currently using 1 of 2 headphones. The one set is a Philips SHP9500, and the other are Tribit XFree Tune's. I generally enjoy both but I think they could both be better.

I'm not great at describing sounds so I'll try my best here. I always found the SHP9500 to be a bit harsh sounding, almost hurt my ears listening to them, but I still liked them. Although they were comfy, my ears touched the inside of the cups which would become uncomfortable after a while. The left headband adjustment is also loose, they're a little annoying to adjust now. The pads were pretty worn down so I've replaced the pads with Shure HPAEC1840 pads, which actually helped the harshness for me but now I think they sound all around kind of muddy, or muffled. I generally just use them for gaming now and even then only when I play shooters online which is kind of rare, but I find the imaging is still rather nice for those games.

The Tribit headphones are better than I thought they would be. I hardly ever actually use them as Bluetooth headphones because of the lag and/or compression. They seem to have an emphasis on the bass and nothing is harsh sounding. They have a pleasing enough sound, but they maybe lack clarity? They were only $40 though so I don't really have many complains about them. They're just a couple of years old so only time will tell how long they'll last. I mainly got these for my wife as she needs headphones once in a while and really doesn't care, but now I've been using them mostly.

So the TLDR of this I'm looking to spend some actual money on headphones now. I do have a few things I'm looking for

*   Open or closed would be fine, but I do have a slight preference to closed just for blocking out background noise and so my wife doesn't have to always hear what I'm hearing, the SHP9500's leak like crazy.
*   Replaceable cable, no preference on cable type.
*   Replaceable pads. This is just for longevity in case the stock pads fall apart or something.
*   Durability.
*   Comfort, would like to wear them for hours while I work (at home).
*   Mainly for music. I'll listen to almost anything, but mostly any kind of rock music, basically anything with guitars and drums but it all greatly varies in tone.
*   I'd like them to be good with movies and games, but it's not as important as music. And by games I just mean casual single player gaming, doesn't have to be amazing for competitive stuff, just want them to sound good.
*   Easy to drive as I'd be using these with a number of devices. I don't have an amp unless you count an old Kenwood amp from the late 70s, but I'd only really be using this for records. I'd mostly be running this through a computer using an Apple USB C adapter or an Xbox controller. I know that might limit options and that running through a controller wouldn't bring the best out of any headphones, but it's how I play casual games that way in the living room.
*   Since I'm running them from a lot of things I'd prefer something that doesn't needs to be EQ'd to be it's best.
*   Probably no more than $400 Canadian.

I think that's about it. Sound signature wise I really don't know what I want which is why I feel so lost and overwhelmed. Between the two sets that I currently own, I prefer the Tribit over the Philips. That's before and after the pad swap on the Philips as they were harsh before, and now they're muddy.

On my own I've been interested in these:

**Meze 99 Classics**

 The look alone is probably what drew me to them, but I really really like how durable they seem to be. Mostly metal, real wood cups, replaceable cable/pads/headband etc. I really wish I could hear them in person because I can't for the life of me figure out if I would like them or not. Some people say the bass is extreme and overbearing, some people they really aren't as bassy as everyone makes them out to be and they just have a solid punchy clean bass. I can't tell if they're all-rounders or not. Some say yes, some say no. I enjoy do some bass, I just don't want it to be muddy.

**Sennheiser Momentum 3**

 I think these are usually out of my budget but they're on sale at the moment. I wasn't really interested in another set of Bluetooth headphones but these seem pretty neat to me the more I look into them. I like that you can use the internal dac via USB, and instead of using the Xbox controller for audio while gaming I could use Bluetooth via Aptx low latency. Seems well built and although I don't care for using an eq, I think it's pretty neat how you can eq them diretly in the headphones for use on any device. What I don't like is how it has a battery. Obviously that's needed for what these do, but batteries don't last forever. I couldn't find a teardown of these so I don't know if it's replaceable, but I saw one for the Momentum 2 and it looked very possible there.. so maybe not a huge issue, I don't mind doing maintenance on my gear once in a while.

**Beyerdynamic DT 700 PRO X**

 I edited this original post to add these. I was originally looking at the DT 770 but heard they were very bright and treble heavy which I wasn't big on, and they also didn't have a replaceable cable. Both of those issues have been remedied on the DT 700 Pro X so I thought they might be worth looking into. It sounds like they sound flatter that my other options, which might be a good thing as the Meze's might be sounding like they're too bass heavy for my tastes. They look well made and easily user serviceable.

Sorry that's so much, that's where I'm at after hours/days/weeks of looking around. I'm just looking for feedback either on these 2 choices or on something completely different if you think these are terrible choices, all opinions welcome. Thanks so much in advance.

I've owned the SHP9500, heard the 99 Classics, and own the Momentum 3, but not long enough to tell you about battery reliability.

Of these, I'd personally go for the Meze. The bass is prominent, but it's not extreme--more than the 9500, from what I remember.

The Momentum is great but I find the pads uncomfortable after a few hours. I also don't like that there's no dedicated power button--unless you hold down the multifunction button while unfolding, it automatically turns on.
