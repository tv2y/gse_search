### **Key Findings on Protein Supplementation in Older Adults**  
- **Objective**: Investigated effects of protein supplementation (via supplements or whole foods) on appetite and energy intake (EI) in healthy adults aged ≥60.  
- **Methods**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 22 studies (9 acute, 13 longitudinal) involving 857 participants. Outcomes included appetite ratings, gut hormones, and EI.  
- **Results**:  
  - **Acute Studies**: Protein preloads reduced test meal EI by 164 kJ vs. control but increased total EI (including supplement energy) by 649 kJ. Appetite suppression occurred in 7/24 protein arms.  
  - **Longitudinal Studies**: No difference in daily EI between protein and control groups. Protein intake increased by 0.29 g/kg/day without compromising EI.  
  - **Appetite**: Mixed results; some studies reported suppressed appetite ratings, but no consistent reduction in overall EI.  
  - **Gut Hormones**: Protein increased satiety hormones (CCK, GLP-1, PYY) and decreased ghrelin.  
- **Implications**: Protein supplementation effectively increases protein intake in older adults without negatively impacting energy intake, supporting its use to address age-related muscle loss.  

### **Conclusions**:  
Protein supplementation may transiently suppress appetite but does not reduce total energy intake, making it a viable strategy to improve protein adequacy in older populations.
