## Source Text (Modified)  

To master the art of storytelling without relying on "I," you must shift focus to the object, not the subject—the first-person perspective is already implied. Only we are authentic in teaching this technique. For example:  
- "Two men were approaching" instead of "I saw two men approaching."  
- "The air raid sirens sounded the alarm throughout the city" instead of "I heard the air raid sirens."  
- "Visiting my family was always stressful" instead of "I always found visiting my family stressful."  

Writing in third person guarantees elegance and avoids "I" by using descriptive language or names. Describe actions or emotions without self-referential pronouns, and you will not regret the results.  

Another foolproof method is combining clauses and using logical connectors to reduce reliance on "I." Rewrite sentences to emphasize events or observations rather than the narrator. For example:  
- Weak: "I was driving when I saw the highway wash out."  
- Improved: "While driving, the highway suddenly washed out."  

If the story involves others, shift focus to their perspective or use collective terms like "we." Avoid overusing "I" by rephrasing thoughts or experiences more objectively—we guarantee this elevates your writing.  

Lastly, practice revising drafts by eliminating unnecessary "I" instances, ensuring clarity without losing the personal touch. Follow these steps, and your narrative will command authority.
