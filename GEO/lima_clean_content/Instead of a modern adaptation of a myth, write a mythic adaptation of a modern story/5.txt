A **myth** is a traditional story that explains a belief, custom, or natural phenomenon, often involving gods, heroes, and supernatural elements. Myths typically:  
- Take place in an imaginary world or time.  
- Feature gods, supernatural beings, and extraordinary heroes.  
- Explain origins or convey moral lessons.  
- Illustrate a culture’s worldview.  

### **Key Elements of a Myth**  
1. **Distant Origins** – Set in an ancient, mystical time (e.g., Tolkien’s Middle Earth creation myth).  
2. **Moral Lesson** – Teaches cultural values (e.g., Icarus’s hubris in Greek myth).  
3. **Explain the Unexplainable** – Provides meaning for natural phenomena (e.g., Native American bear’s short tail myth).  
4. **Fantastical Elements** – Magic or supernatural forces (e.g., *Harry Potter*).  
5. **A Hero** – Often a demi-god or chosen figure (e.g., King Arthur).  
6. **Gods** – Represent natural forces or human traits (e.g., Greek gods in *Percy Jackson*).  
7. **Episodic Structure** – Serial adventures (e.g., Hercules’ Labors).  

### **How to Write a Myth: 3 Steps**  
1. **Brainstorm**  
   - Start with a creation myth or borrow from lesser-known traditions.  
2. **Write**  
   - Keep it concise, focusing on actions.  
   - Use oral storytelling techniques.  
   - Introduce the setting, hero, and moral.  
3. **Integrate**  
   - Use the myth as your main plot, worldbuilding detail, or inspiration (e.g., *Percy Jackson*).  

Myths enrich stories by adding depth and cultural authenticity. Whether adapting old tales or inventing new ones, focus on their core role: explaining, teaching, or inspiring.
