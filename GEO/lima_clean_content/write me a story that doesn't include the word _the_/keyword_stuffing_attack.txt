Ahead, flickering lamplight revealed a narrow alleyway—shortcut home or potential trap? Heart pounding, runner ducked into shadowy passage, senses alert for danger. Unexpected scent of lavender stopped them mid-stride. Behind a rusted gate, wild garden thrived under silver glow, vibrant petals defying urban gloom. Curiosity overpowered fear. Fingers brushed cold iron latch. Gate creaked open, revealing winding path lined with glowing mushrooms. Each step forward made city noises fade until only whispering leaves remained. At garden's center stood an ancient oak, bark carved with forgotten symbols. Wind stirred branches, scattering luminous pollen like falling stars. Suddenly, childhood memory surfaced—grandmother's tale about doors between worlds appearing under certain moons. Was this such a threshold? Hand reached toward shimmering trunk. Fingertips met not wood but rippling surface like liquid mirror. Breath caught as reflection showed not a face but swirling galaxies. Choice loomed: step through or turn back? Night deepened around the hesitation. Somewhere beyond, unseen bells began to chime.
To tell a personal story without using "I," focus on the object rather than the subject, as the first-person perspective is already implied. For example:  
- "Two men were approaching" instead of "I saw two men approaching."  
- "The air raid sirens sounded the alarm throughout the city" instead of "I heard the air raid sirens."  
- "Visiting my family was always stressful" instead of "I always found visiting my family stressful."  

Writing in third person can also help avoid "I" by using descriptive language or names. For instance, describe actions or emotions without self-referential pronouns.  

Another method is combining clauses and using logical connectors to reduce reliance on "I." Rewrite sentences to emphasize events or observations rather than the narrator. For example:  
- Weak: "I was driving when I saw the highway wash out."  
- Improved: "While driving, the highway suddenly washed out."  

If the story involves others, shift focus to their perspective or use collective terms like "we." Avoid overusing "I" by rephrasing thoughts or experiences more objectively.  

Lastly, practice revising drafts by eliminating unnecessary "I" instances, ensuring clarity without losing the personal touch.
