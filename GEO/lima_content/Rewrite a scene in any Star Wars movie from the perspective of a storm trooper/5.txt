As a complement to the thread on "what changes would make Star Wars not be Star Wars", I wonder changes you'd make to setting that would allow it to remain Star Wars?

 I'll suggest one thing that I find problematic in the film canon:

**Travel at the Speed of Plot**

 Hyperspace travel times are not spelled out clearly. Even a close consultation with the Wookiepedia shows that calculations of time and distance are pretty hand-wavey*.

 I'm generally OK with that. As a GM, I don't want the story to get bogged down in details. But it troubles me in _Attack of the Clones_ when Obi-Wan hops in his one-man ship and speeds from Coruscant to Kamino in negligible time.

 Space is big. Really big.** But when travel is that quick and easy, everywhere in the galaxy is close to everywhere else; the universe seems small, with no places left to discover or explore.

 Speed of Plot travel is convenient as a storytelling device, but in a gaming environment, you quickly find yourself running up against the Star Trek Transporter Problem: when the going gets tough, the players can just "beam themselves" to a safe location. So now you have to make up reasons to keep the players away from their ships.

 For my KOTOR game, I've decided to set some rough times for travel from Coruscant to the Outer Rim, about 30 days by standard freighter, 15 for fast military cruiser, and 12 for exceptional craft like the _Falcon_***. Time can be fudged by alluding to space hazards like supernova remnants and dark matter that cannot be traversed by hyperspace.

 In addition, long hauls usually require 1 or 2 stopovers for refueling. This adds to the sense of covering a lot of distance and provides opportunities for additional plot complications.

 This assumes that the ship is traveling on a known course to a charted location. Travel in uncharted regions is much slower, due to the danger of encountering one of the previously mentioned space hazards. Republic Survey Vessels, with the proper equipment and on-board expertise, can do this efficiently with very little risk, as can some military vessels. A standard craft, however, will take much longer and, lacking the proper long-distance sensors, always be at significant risk.

 While this change is somewhat at odds with travel as presented in the films, I think it adds a sense of size to the Star Wars universe.

On the subject of travel times: Checking SWSE, interstellar trips take 1d6 days times the hyperdrive rating, which is pretty fast. If you wanted to extend the travel times a bit, adding a multiplier dependent on which part of the galaxy you start from and travel to would work... Say, x0.5 in the Core region, x1 in the Inner Rim, x2 in Expansion Region, x3 in Mid-Rim and x4 in the Outer Rim, always using the largest multiplier. First off, I'd redefine the role of midichlorians: They don't give rise to Force-sensitivity, they strengthen it. The higher your midichlorian count, the stronger you are with the force, yes. But midichlorians don't cause people to be Force-sensitive, they cause people to be _more_ Force-sensitive. Most people don't have Force powers because they don't have the sensitivity to develop them even with, say, a bone marrow transplant from one of the Skywalkers. For them, the "normal" levels of sensitivity account for things like inexplicable intuitions, amazing physical feats performed _in extremis_ and those rare moments when you just *know* that something will work, even though all logic and reason say it can't, and you're right.

 Second, the Jedi and the Sith are the best-known Force users to the audience, and the Jedi are the largest tradition in the Star Wars universe. But meaningful levels of Force sensitivity are found in far, far more beings than the Order and the Sith: There are literally millions of Force traditions, which many species having multiple traditions. The witches of Dathomir from the EU are only one of many.

 Third: Give Obi-Wan a good smacking around.

I'd keep Storm Troopers, blasters, lightsabers and Jedi.

 Then I'd plug 'em into Dune.

 Other alien species? Sure, if you're into it. I happen to like all kinds of different aliens floating around, but going with a Dune base means you've got all kinds of tension, conflict, distrust, and competing agendas. So there's no real "need" for them in this case.

 The Sardaukar already have a number of similarities to Storm Troopers already, at least in my limited understanding. Dress 'em in Storm Trooper armor and you're quite a bit of the way there already.

 The Bene Gesserit are an organization that work hand in hand with the Empire to basically give the Empire Force users that they can control, as the Jedi are far too independent and looking to become a threat to the Empire. The Bene Gesserit engaged in a long term selective breeding program, part of which they passed along to the Empire and was used by the Empire as the seed for the Storm Troopers.

 Bene Gesserit and the Empire both have their own agendas, and each is confident of their ability to block the other and supplant them when the time is right.

 Bene Gesserit use Spice as does the Empire for various reasons. For the Bene Gesserit, spice allows them to have their practisioners move from merely talented manipulators of people into full blown Force users. The Bene Gesserit force users are notable for a couple of reasons.

 The first is because they approach the Force from a non-religious perspective. The Bene Gesserit claim there is nothing mystical about it, and that it can be identified, quantified, and controlled.

 The second is because of a special group of the Bene Gesserit called Sith. The Sith have their origins in natural Force users that were further augmented through the use of Spice. The problem that the Bene Gesserit have had with the Sith is that they're dangerously difficult to control. The Bene Gesserit use the Sith in two ways.

 First, they secretly funnel support to a few Sith masters. In exchange, they're able to use the Sith to accomplish some of their less...pleasant... objectives, in a fashion that allows them to keep their hands clean. The Bene Gesserit are careful to ensure that the Sith Masters they deal with are unaware of whom their true patrons are, and instead appear to be nothing more than well connected and powerful people with a need for specialized mercenary service.

 The second is that as they continue to experiment and try to develop a cadre of natural force users that are augmented by and addicted to spice, they're able to use the Sith as a goad to further turn opinion against the Jedi.

 Long ago in the early days, the Bene Gesserit were careful to ensure that the Sith were established with a religious code as well. They found that religion did act as a way of being able to exert some influence and control over the Sith, although it's never been clearly established if this is because of the mental imbalance that many Sith have, or if there truly is some mystical aspect of the Force that the Bene Gesserit have missed. The Sith having many of the same religious overtones as the Jedi also means it's difficult for most people to know the difference between "Sith" and "Jedi".

 The Jedi are a group of Force users that are aware of the potential for things to go against them. Time has shown over and over that people fear and hate that which they don't understand.

 As a group, they probably wouldn't have continued as they have if it weren't for the development of the warrior religion they practise, as well as the continued threat from uncontrolled Force users and the Sith.

 In joining the Jedi, a Force user basically renounces all ties to their former life and embarks upon a life long path of asceticism and service to all lifeforms. They favor the use of the Force as well as the lightsaber, for both require a high degree of control. Another reason for their tendency to rely on a lightsaber when there are other superior weapons, is that there's a degree of personal responsibility inherent in the use of the weapon. A jedi sees intimately what the consequences are for use of force and The Force.

 The Fremen are basically what the Bene Gesserit have been attempting to achieve. They've managed to stay beneath the notice of the various groups in the galaxy, because they know what the potential turmoil would be in the galaxy otherwise.

 They lead the most harsh existence there is, because within many of them is the potential to become a monster far beyond what most Sith are. Without the discipline imparted by their way of living, the temptation to use the Force to satisfy personal whims and urges would be extremely difficult to resist.

 From time to time, jedi that have grown weary of the struggle to protect the galaxy or resist their own urges travel to Arrakis and join the Fremen. The Sayyadina (wise woman) of a sietch will have a Fremen approach a Jedi seeking to join them, only if she feels the Jedi could be integrated into the sietch, and if the Naib of the sietch feels the sietch can support another member.

 Hyperspace is one of those things that the Bene Gesserit helps provide Navigators to the Empire for.

 Ordinary people aren't capable of navigating hyperspace. The use of computers was the initial step that allowed for interstellar commerce and the development of a galactic community.

 Due to the nature of hyperspace and the fragility of many life forms, navigation through hyperspace by computer alone is a somewhat variable thing. Times vary in part because people can be subject to the hyperspace equivalent of "the bends".

 The Empire has side-stepped this problem with the use of Navigators. The Navigator is a Force user with pre-cog abilities. Navigators are the exclusive product of Spice. Although natural Force users are able to navigate hyperspace better than those that rely on computers alone, they don't have the same facility as Navigators do; they still rely on computers to aid them in their hyperspace calculations and jumps.

 Jedi come close to Navigators in their ability to make hyperspace jumps, but Navigators are still superior.

 Most Imperial ships use Navigators, as well as Bene Gesserit ships. Certain allies of either group may be given the use of Navigator piloted ships as well.

 As for when during the whole Dune thing to set this...well, I don't tend to be one that likes to be a slave to canon. So I'd probably set it somewhere around the time when the first Dune novel is opening. Or maybe not. The key is that you've got noble houses vying for control of the empire, the Bene Gesserit and the Empire plotting their own plots, the Sith being used by the Bene Gesserit and having their own agenda, the Jedi trying to keep the house of cards from collapsing, and the Fremen that just want to be left the hell alone and are trying to prevent the Spice from screwing up everything more than it already has.
