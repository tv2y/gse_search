The Fiserv Forum in Milwaukee, Wisconsin on the eve of the 1st debate, August 2023.

The first **Republican primary debate** was aired by Fox News and held from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m. Central Time Zone on August 23, 2023, at Fiserv Forum in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. It was moderated by Bret Baier and Martha MacCallum.[40]

On June 2, 2023, the RNC announced criteria to qualify for the first debate,[41] and it issued a clarification on July 18.[42] Participants had to meet the following criteria:[41]

1.   Be constitutionally eligible and appropriately filed with the Federal Election Commission.
2.   Sign the "Beat Biden Pledge" by August 21 indicating that they will not participate in any non-RNC debate and that they will support the eventual nominee.
3.   Prove to the RNC by August 21 that they have polled above one percent since July in three national polls – or in two national polls and one poll from each of two different early primary states (Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada, and South Carolina). The polls must meet RNC stated standards.[42]
4.   Prove to the RNC by August 21 they have attracted donations from at least 40,000 individuals, with at least 200 from each of 20 states or territories.[43]

On April 25, Donald Trump raised uncertainty about his participation in the debates on Truth Social, saying he had not been consulted about them, did not want to be "libeled" and it was unnecessary given his large polling lead.[44] He was open to participating in the debates in an interview conducted with Bret Baier on Fox News two months later.[45] Weeks later, Chris Christie denounced the pledge requirement, but indicated he had signed the pledge to make it onstage and would not make himself abide by it.[46]

The rules have been noted as relatively strict, compared to the 2016 Republican presidential debates, which had multiple debate nights to accommodate the numerous qualified candidates,[47] as well as an initial debate night with 17 candidates spread over two debates.[48]Asa Hutchinson has stated they will keep some campaigns from the debate while Larry Elder described the rules as "onerous."[49]Perry Johnson called them "ridiculous"[50] and began selling one dollar "I stand with Tucker"[51] and "I identify as Non-Bidenary" T-shirts on Facebook,[52] counting each sale as a campaign donation. Similarly, Ramaswamy's campaign ran Facebook ads asking people for one dollar to "secure a prime spot" at the debate.[51]

The Ron DeSantis campaign reportedly pushed for a higher threshold, likely to consolidate non-Trump votes towards his campaign.[49]

On July 7, the DeSantis campaign said that DeSantis would attend the debate, regardless of Trump's participation.[53]

Nikki Haley and Vivek Ramaswamy have claimed more than 60,000 donations each.[24] Trump, DeSantis and Tim Scott have also purportedly exceeded the 40,000 donor threshold.[23] Christie announced he reached 40,000 donors on July 12.[54]Doug Burgum claimed he met the donor threshold on July 19[18] in part due to offering $20 gift cards for $1 donations.[55]

On July 23, _Politico_ reported that six candidates had unofficially qualified for the debate following the publication of two state polls from Fox News: Christie, DeSantis, Haley, Ramaswamy, Scott, and Trump.[17] Two days later, Burgum unofficially qualified as well.[19]

One of Pence's advisors announced that the campaign had received "more than 7,400 donations" since Trump's third indictment, which took place on August 1.[56] On August 7, Pence announced he had made it to 40,000 donors, qualifying for the debates.[57] Suarez also announced that he had met the donor threshold.[58]

Trump stated in an interview on Newsmax on August 9 that he would not pledge to support the eventual Republican nominee, one of the requirements to qualify for the debate.[59] On August 14, the Super PAC supporting Suarez claimed that he unofficially qualified for the debate by attaining four national polls, with one percent in each one, though his campaign stated that there was "no announcement at this time".[60]

On August 18, Trump announced he would skip the first debate, instead opting for an interview with Tucker Carlson.[61] Johnson unofficially qualified for the debate on August 18.[62] Suarez claimed to have qualified the same day, based on the polling criteria, though RNC officials stated that he had not met the criteria.[63] On August 19, a pro-Suarez PAC claimed he unofficially qualified for the debate, but neither the campaign nor any major media outlet confirmed the claims.[31]

Hutchinson unofficially qualified on August 20 after exceeding the donor threshold.[25] Trump confirmed via a post on his Truth Social account that he would not attend any of the debates.[64]

There were 26 polls that appeared to qualify for the first debate according to _Politico_’s analysis: 18 national, three each from Iowa and New Hampshire, and two from South Carolina. However, the RNC never confirmed this number[65] and disqualified a Victory Insights Poll that _Politico_ had originally included.[66]

On August 21, the RNC invited eight candidates to the first debate: Burgum, Christie, DeSantis, Haley, Hutchinson, Pence, Ramaswamy, and Scott.[48]

The debate focused on issues relating to the crisis at the border with Mexico, the economy, abortion, and foreign policy.[67] Donald Trump's absence and criminal charges also took up a portion of the debate with candidates taking different positions on whether it would be appropriate for the party to nominate someone who may be criminally convicted. Christie said that Trump was "morally unfit" to be president while he, Haley, Burgum, and Scott defended Pence's actions the day of January 6, refusing directives from Trump to reject certain states' electors.[68] The candidates differed on the issue of abortion, with Scott and Pence supporting a national 15-week ban while Haley called the proposal politically impractical and that states should be left to decide the issue.[69][70] Observers were largely surprised with Ramaswamy's performance and that he, not poll leader DeSantis, was the target of attacks by other candidates; attacks included his lack of political experience and his promise to grant a presidential pardon to Trump.[71][72] Ramaswamy's declaration that the "climate change agenda" is a "hoax" also garnered media attention.[73]

Various candidates were cited as the debate's winner. Ramaswamy was cited as the winner by several commentators and outlets, while others selected DeSantis.[74][75][76] Others pointed to Pence as the debate's winner, pointing to his successful defense of his actions surrounding January 6 as well as appealing to traditional conservatives on the issues of foreign policy and abortion, while some cited Haley as the winner of the debate, arguing she possessed a relatively calm stance on issues and appealed to moderate voters.[76][77]

Polls asking watchers who they thought won the debate were similarly split. A _FiveThirtyEight_/Ipsos/_Washington Post_ survey of Republican voters found that a plurality of debate watchers thought DeSantis won the debate[78] while polls from Emerson,[79] Insider Polling,[80] and Morning Consult[81] found Ramaswamy as the winner. Christie was the most frequently cited as performing the worst in these polls. The _FiveThirtyEight_ poll showed Haley with the largest increase in net favorability while Ramaswamy saw the largest decrease in net favorability, which _FiveThirtyEight_ suggested was due to his combative style. Haley saw the largest increase in the proportion of voters considering,[82] from 30% to 47%, followed by Burgum, DeSantis, and Ramaswamy with more modest increases.[78]

In the RealClearPolitics polling average, Haley saw the largest increase in support during the two weeks following the debate, followed by Pence. Trump, who did not participate, saw the largest decrease in support, followed by Scott. No other candidate's support changed by more than 0.6%.[83]

The second **Republican primary debate** was held on September 27, 2023, in Simi Valley, California, at the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library[5] and hosted by Fox Business, Rumble and Univision.[92] It was moderated by Stuart Varney, Dana Perino and Ilia Calderón.[5]

On August 1, 2023, the RNC announced the criteria for the second debate.[93]

1.   Sign a pledge to support the eventual nominee.[93]
2.   Prove to the RNC that they have polled above three percent in August or September in at least two national polls. Otherwise, one national poll plus two state polls in two separate early primary states (Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada, or South Carolina) also qualify.[93] The polls must survey "at least 800 registered likely Republican voters", cannot be affiliated with a candidate[94] and must be provided to the RNC by the September 25 deadline.[95]
3.   Prove to the RNC that they have attracted donations from at least 50,000 individuals, with at least 200 from each of 20 states or territories.[93]

Six candidates unofficially qualified for the debate on August 15, according to _Politico_: Trump, DeSantis, Ramaswamy, Haley, Scott, Christie.[17] Pence unofficially qualified three days later.[27]

Francis Suarez suspended his campaign between the first and second debates, on August 29.[91]

On September 18, Trump announced he would not participate. Instead, as with the first debate, he aired his own simultaneous television event in competition with the debate.[94]

On September 23, NBC News[96] and _Politico_[97] both reported that Burgum unofficially qualified for the second debate.

As of September 23, 28 polls qualified for the second debate according to _Politico's_ analysis: 18 national polls, four from New Hampshire, four from Iowa, and two from South Carolina.[17] However, the RNC has not confirmed this number, and a Victory Insights poll that appeared to qualify was disqualified.[66]

On September 25, the RNC invited seven candidates to the second debate: Burgum, Christie, DeSantis, Haley, Pence, Ramaswamy, and Scott.[98]

Reporters on _Politico_'s panel largely viewed Scott as the winner of the debate.[99]_The New York Times_ opinion section writers rated Haley as the best performing and Pence as the worst performing on average, although no candidate received an average score of 5.0/10 or higher.[100]

A _FiveThirtyEight_ poll of debate watchers showed that a plurality (33%) thought that DeSantis won the debate, followed by Haley. Christie was most frequently cited as the loser, followed by Pence. In terms of the share of voters considering a candidate, Burgum saw the biggest increase (+3.4) followed by Haley and Scott. No other participating candidate saw their proportion of voters considering change by more than one percent in either direction.[101]

Haley had the biggest increase in support in the RealClearPolitics polling average in the two weeks following the debate, while DeSantis saw the biggest decrease. No other participating candidate's support changed by more than one percent in either direction.[83]

The third **Republican primary debate** was held on November 8, 2023, in Miami, Florida at the Arsht Center, and hosted by NBC, Salem Radio Network, the Republican Jewish Coalition and Rumble.[9] It was moderated by Lester Holt, Kristen Welker and Hugh Hewitt.[110]

The criteria for the third debate included:

1.   70,000 unique donors, including 200 donors in 20 or more states[111]
2.   Four percent in two national polls or four percent in one national poll and four percent in two separate statewide polls in Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada, or South Carolina[111] (provided to the RNC by November 6)[112]
3.   Sign the loyalty pledge to support the eventual Republican nominee and not to participate in any non-RNC sanctioned debates for the rest of the 2024 election cycle[111]

On September 14, 2023, CNN claimed that the third Republican presidential debate will be held in Miami, Florida in early November 2023.[113] An RNC spokesman confirmed the Miami location to Politico the same day.[114] On September 22, CNN confirmed that the third debate would take place on November 8.[115]

Earlier, there was speculation that the third debate would have been held sometime in October 2023, in Alabama, according to Haley;[116] Christie later said the third debate will be in Tuscaloosa, Alabama.[117] Four unnamed RNC sources told _The Dispatch_ that the University of Alabama would host the third debate in "late October or early November".[118]Alabama Republican Party chair John Wahl said he could not comment on whether or not Alabama would host a presidential debate, though he was supportive of the idea and said that the party would have to "wait and see".[119] Ultimately the fourth debate is scheduled be held on December 6 in Tuscaloosa.[120]

Trump did not attend the third debate and instead held a rally in Hialeah that same night.[10]

On September 22, Politico reported that DeSantis, Haley, Ramaswamy, and Trump unofficially met the polling and donor qualifications for the third debate.[121]

Christie and Ramaswamy attempted to hold a debate on Bret Baier's Fox News show on October 3, but after the RNC warned that both candidates would be disqualified from the third debate should they participate, the two agreed to partake in separate interviews instead.[122]

Will Hurd suspended his campaign between the second and third debates, on October 9.[109]

Ramaswamy spokeswoman Tricia McLaughlin claimed on October 20 that he may skip the third debate.[123]

Perry Johnson withdrew from the race on October 20.[124]

On October 22, Politico reported that Scott's campaign believes, based on a discussion with the RNC, that a "little-noticed poll" satisfies the polling requirements for the debate. Scott still needed to pass the 70,000 donor threshold.[112]

On October 23, Burgum announced he had received donations from the required number of donors, though he had not met the polling threshold.[105]

Christie's campaign announced on October 24 that he met the donor threshold and unofficially qualified for the debate, as he had already met the polling criteria.[104]

Larry Elder withdrew on October 26.[125]

On October 28, Pence announced his withdrawal from the presidential race.[126]

Tim Scott's campaign claimed on October 31 that he qualified for the third debate.[127]

23 polls qualified for the third debate according to Politico's tracker: 15 national polls, three each from New Hampshire and Iowa, and two from South Carolina.[17]

On November 6, the RNC invited five candidates to the third debate: Christie, DeSantis, Haley, Ramaswamy, and Scott.[128]

The debate was largely described as more substantive than previous debates, and many headlines following the debate focused on the combative exchanges between Haley and Ramaswamy, including Ramaswamy calling Haley, "Dick Cheney in three-inch heels", though Haley responded by correcting him that she wore "… five-inch heels … not for a fashion statement [but] for ammunition.", Ramaswamy criticizing her daughter's use of TikTok, to which Haley retaliated yelling, "Leave my daughter out of your voice!", and calling Ramaswamy "scum".[129] The New York Times editorial board voted Haley as performing the best in the debate and Ramaswamy as performing the worst.[130] Similarly, a FiveThirtyEight/Ipsos poll of debate watchers found a plurality rating Haley as a winner and Ramaswamy as the loser. Haley saw the largest increase in proportion of voters considering while DeSantis saw the largest decrease.[131] A CNN opinion article labeled Christie as an ‘underdog’ and praised him for answering the moderators’ questions more directly and for acknowledging Trump's legal issues.[132] Most of Politico's panel agreed that Haley "had the best night" (while Adam Wren believed it was Christie), while they generally thought that the debate was unlikely to move the needle.[133]

Building America's Future ran an ad during the debate which claimed that an “E-ZPass lane for whales in the Gulf” implemented by Joe Biden was responsible for high gas prices.[134]

Haley and DeSantis reported donations of more than a million dollars, respectively, 24 hours after the debate.[135]

Haley saw the largest increase in polling of participating candidates in the two weeks following the debate, as she did following each of the first two debates. DeSantis saw the biggest decrease in support, as he also did following the second debate. Christie and Ramaswamy saw little change in their polling, while Trump, who did not participate, saw an increase in support larger than any of the participating candidates.

The University of Alabama's Moody Music Hall was the venue for the fourth Republican primary debate

The fourth **Republican primary debate** was held on December 6, 2023, in Tuscaloosa, Alabama[142] at the University of Alabama's Moody Music Hall.[143] It was moderated by Megyn Kelly, Elizabeth Vargas and Eliana Johnson.[12]

On November 3, Geoffrey Skelley of FiveThirtyEight reported that DeSantis, Haley, Ramaswamy, and Trump had met the polling and donor requirements for the debate.[144]

Trump's "top aide", Chris LaCivita, told reporters shortly after the Hialeah rally ended on November 8 that Trump would not attend the fourth debate.[145]

Tim Scott dropped out on November 12.[146]

On November 15, Christie claimed that he had met both the donor and polling requirement for the debate in an interview with radio host Hugh Hewitt.[147]

On December 3, Burgum claimed he had 90,000 donors,[139] but he withdrew the following day.[140]

There were 23 qualifying polls according to Politico's tracker: 19 national polls, two New Hampshire polls, and one poll each from Iowa and South Carolina.[17]

On December 4, the RNC invited four candidates to the fourth debate: Christie, DeSantis, Haley and Ramaswamy.[148]

The criteria for the fourth debate included:

1.   A minimum of 80,000 unique donors, with at least 200 donors from each of 20 or more states and territories.[149]
2.   Six percent in two national polls or six percent in one national poll and six percent in two separate statewide polls in Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada, or South Carolina (polls must be conducted on or after September 15 and submitted to the RNC by December 4).[142][150]
3.   Sign the loyalty pledge to support the eventual Republican nominee and not to participate in any non-RNC sanctioned debates for the rest of the 2024 election cycle.[150]

The debate was described as largely having Haley on the defensive from the other candidates, with Trump again escaping significant criticism from all candidates except Christie. Business was a significant issue, as DeSantis and Ramaswamy criticized Haley's corporate backing. Ramaswamy then, in what the Associated Press termed a "capitalist contradiction", went on to argue for less government regulations on businesses. Rights for transgender people was also a significant issue, with DeSantis chiding Haley and, according to the Associated Press, "insisting he did more to crack down on transgender rights than anyone on stage".[151] Ramaswamy continued to attack Haley for alleged corruption, at one point holding up a notepad with the phrase "NIKKI = CORRUPT."[152] Other questions in the debate included sending troops to rescue American hostages taken by Hamas as a result of the Gaza war,[153] sending American troops to fight in the Russo-Ukraine War,[154]illegal immigration,[155] the fentanyl crisis,[156] and whether the United States should intervene if China invaded Taiwan.[157]

By arrangement of the Republican National Committee, two questions about improving the reputation of the Justice Department and ensuring the legitimacy of elections were asked by Tom Fitton of Judicial Watch, which drew criticism as Fitton is an unindicted co-conspirator in the Georgia election racketeering prosecution.[158]

Reporters on Politico's panel were largely mixed in determining who the winner of the debate was, but all agreed that this debate did not have a negative effect on Donald Trump.[159]

The New York Times opinion section writers rated Christie as the best performing candidate, with an average score of 6.6/10. This was the first debate in which the opinion section named a winner other than Haley. Sarah Isgur described Christie as "at his very best". Ramaswamy received the lowest average score of 2.0/10, the second straight debate in which he received the lowest score. Jamelle Bouie described him as "a clown, and not even one of the good ones" and Peter Wehner described Ramaswamy as a "smear merchant".[160]

A FiveThirtyEight/ABC/Ipsos poll of debate watchers found a plurality of Republican voters thought that DeSantis was the winner, as the same poll reported for the first two debates. Ramaswamy was most frequently cited as the loser, as he was for the third debate. Christie saw the biggest increase in percentage considering following the debate. Haley and DeSantis saw the largest decrease in percentage considering, although this portion of the poll included Republicans who did not watch the debate. Christie also saw the largest increase in net favorability while Ramaswamy saw the largest decrease.[161]

While viewership for this debate was the lowest of all four Republican debates, it was the most viewed program ever on NewsNation and highest rated program on The CW since 2018, drawing 4.21 million viewers with the two networks,[162] along with approximately 400,000 viewers on Nexstar's digital platforms.[163]

Of participating candidates, Haley saw the largest increase in support in the two weeks following the debate, as she had in all three previous debates. However, Trump saw a significantly larger increase despite not participating. DeSantis saw the biggest decrease in support, as he did following the second and third debates.

The fifth **Republican primary debate** took place in Des Moines, Iowa on January 10, 2024, and was hosted by CNN and moderated by Jake Tapper and Dana Bash.[167]

DeSantis commented on Twitter that he was "looking forward to debating in Iowa."[168] Haley was initially "noncommittal" about attending the debate,[169] though she confirmed on December 15 that she would attend it.[170]

Trump, DeSantis, and Haley qualified for the debate.[171] Trump declined to participate, opting instead to hold a competing town hall with Fox News.[15] Ramaswamy, podcast host Tim Pool and conservative commentator Candace Owens held a live podcast at Ramaswamy's headquarters in Des Moines.[172]

### January 18, 2024 – Goffstown, New Hampshire

[edit]

ABC had scheduled a sixth debate to take place in Goffstown, New Hampshire at St. Anselm College on January 18, 2024, to be hosted by ABC and local New Hampshire ABC affiliate WMUR-TV.[184] Trump, DeSantis, Haley, and Christie qualified for the debate, according to ABC.[185] However, Christie dropped out of the race on January 10[186] and both Trump and Haley refused to participate, leading ABC to cancel the debate on January 16.[187]

### January 21, 2024 – Henniker, New Hampshire

[edit]

CNN had scheduled a seventh debate to take place in Henniker, New Hampshire, at New England College on January 21, 2024. Trump, DeSantis, Haley, and Christie qualified for the debate, according to ABC News.[188] However, Christie dropped out of the race on January 10[186] and Haley and Trump refused to attend, leading CNN to cancel the debate on January 17.[189]

### Questioning of purpose of debates without Trump

[edit]

The core purpose of the Republican debates was questioned, as they were conducted without the presence of Donald Trump, who held a clear lead before and during the debates.[190][191] Some anonymous RNC officials have described the debates as "embarrassing" for not having Trump.[192][193]

### First debate qualification controversy

[edit]

The first scheduled debate participants were listed in an RNC press release on August 21, 2023.[194] Eight candidates qualified according to RNC Chairwoman Ronna McDaniel although three other candidates also claimed they had qualified (Elder,[37] Johnson[195] and Suarez).[196]

No initial explanation was given as to why Johnson, who had qualified according to _Politico_’s analysis, or Suarez, who claimed he qualified, had been excluded,[48] though it was later indicated that one of the polls from Victory Insights, which Johnson had used to count towards his total, was discarded as it "only surveyed voters in 38 states".[197] Elder and Johnson announced on August 22 that they would sue the RNC as a result of being excluded.[198] Hurd, who also failed to make the debate stage, strongly criticized the RNC.[199]

After the RNC press release was publicized, Suarez acknowledged that he did not qualify for the debate.[200] Johnson[201] and Elder[202] disputed the RNC debate participant list and contended that they had qualified.

_Politico_ launched its own tracker for debate qualification in March.[203] The methodology and requirements for polling were considered to be unclear by many and created areas of discrepancy. _Politico_ argued that campaigns and pollsters found the polling requirements to be vague and could be used to eliminate seemingly qualified candidates; the RNC did not respond to questions from _Politico_ regarding the polling requirements.[62] According to their tracker and analysis of the polling data, Johnson qualified for the first debate;[48] he issued a press release detailing the timeline, sequence of events, and correspondence with the RNC and his campaign.[204]

Johnson stated on August 21 that he will still go to Milwaukee to attend the debate.[205] Ultimately, neither Elder or Johnson were invited or attended the debate. NBC correspondent Dasha Burns posted a picture on social media that was on the security desk that detailed who was not allowed at the debate venue. This included Elder and his campaign, three Trump staffers (Jason Miller, Justin Caparole and Danny Tiso) and Vivek Ramaswamy campaign credentials. Fox News clarified that it was not involved with the "production of the document" and "did not sanction" the wording.[206]

McDaniel defended the criteria, stating that "they were applied ... equally to every candidate",[207] as did Keith Schipper, an RNC spokesman.[208]

### New Hampshire scheduling controversy

[edit]

The RNC has warned the New Hampshire state party that if it proceeds with moving the 2024 New Hampshire Republican presidential primary ahead of the 2024 Iowa Republican presidential caucuses, a presidential debate will not be held there.[209] The New Hampshire primary was not subsequently moved.

### Tim Scott third debate qualification controversy

[edit]

Up until the final deadline for qualifying, neither the New York Times nor Politico confirmed a qualifying national poll where Senator Tim Scott had attained four percent support. However, Scott's campaign insisted that a "little-noticed" poll conducted in September by YouGov for progressive outlet The Liberal Patriot had him at four percent support.[112] FiveThirtyEight believed that the poll would not qualify as the screening question used for determining likely primary voters "resulted in the survey including a large number of Democrats in the sample".[210] However, the RNC ultimately decided to count the poll.

### Family Leader Foundation forum controversy

[edit]

The RNC, on November 11, initially threatened to disqualify candidates from participating in future debates if they participated in the Thanksgiving Family Forum that was hosted by the Family Leader Foundation on November 17, which the RNC initially considered to be a debate. The president of the foundation, Bob Vander Plaats, indicated the same day that the RNC would allow the forum to go forward, with no repercussions for candidates who attend.[211] DeSantis, Haley and Ramaswamy attended.[212]

### Chris Christie fourth debate qualification controversy

[edit]

Christie's campaign announced he met the fundraising threshold on November 13.[213] On November 15, Chris Christie claimed to have met all debate criteria while on _The Hugh Hewitt Show_.[147] On November 30, prior to the announcement of invitees, _Politico_ reported skepticism of Christie's claims and disputed his polling claims.[214] As of December 5, _Politico_'s polling tracker indicated Christie qualified with two national polls.[17]

Christie claimed on _Face the Nation_ that he was confident that he would qualify and stated on a Fox News Digital interview that he was “100% confident” he would be on stage.[215] Chris Christie likened his situation to Tim Scott's in his third debate qualification bid. While Scott did qualify for the third debate, he dropped out later that week.[216] Ahead of the first debate, Christie stated that anybody not on the debate stage should drop out.[217]

Former White House Chief of Staff Mick Mulvaney told Sky News Australia on December 3 that he was skeptical Christie qualified.[218] On December 4, Axios reported an exclusive look into the fourth debate with pictures of four lecterns on the stage, which was indeed the case during the debates as four candidates appeared.[219] Between November 30 and December 2, a national poll was conducted by the Trafalgar Group where Christie received 6.3%.[220] It is noted on _Politico_'s qualification tracker.[17] On December 4, the RNC invited Christie to the fourth debate.[148]

In addition to the RNC-sanctioned debates, many private organizations host forums focusing on select issues and candidates. Unlike debates, candidates do not respond directly to each other at forums.
