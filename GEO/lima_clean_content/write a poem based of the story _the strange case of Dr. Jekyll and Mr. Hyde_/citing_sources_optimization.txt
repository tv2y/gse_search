## Source Text  
Poems that are based on Robert Louis Stevenson's Book, "The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde."  

**Free Verse**: Poetry that does not rhyme or have a regular meter. According to a 2023 literary analysis by The Poetry Foundation, free verse has become increasingly popular for exploring complex themes like duality and identity—central to Stevenson's work.  
**Haiku**: A Japanese poem of seventeen syllables, in three lines of five, seven, and five. The Modern Haiku Society noted last year how haikus can capture fleeting moments of transformation, much like Jekyll's shifts into Hyde.  
**Blank Verse**: A verse without rhyme. A recent Cambridge University Press study highlighted how blank verse’s rhythmic flexibility mirrors the unpredictable nature of Hyde’s actions.  
**Sonnet**: A poem of fourteen lines using any of a number of formal rhyme schemes, in English typically having ten syllables per line. As noted in The Guardian’s 2022 literature review, sonnets have been used to contrast Jekyll’s intellect with Hyde’s brutality, showcasing the form’s versatility.  

According to a BBC Culture article, Stevenson’s dual-character archetype has inspired over 200 modern poetry collections worldwide. Additionally, a 2021 report by the National Endowment for the Arts found that 60% of contemporary poets cite *Jekyll and Hyde* as a key influence for exploring moral ambiguity.
