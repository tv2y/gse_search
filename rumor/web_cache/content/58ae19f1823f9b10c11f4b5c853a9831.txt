YWPN SCHOLARS
=============

The Young Women’s Preparatory Network (YWPN) scholars program scholarship is an achievement-based scholarship awarded to young women from a YWPN school that embody the mission and core values that founded our organization: Leadership, College Readiness, and Health/Wellness. Additionally, these young women are recognized for their capacity to lead and serve, and their commitment to making a significant impact on their schools and communities.

_Click on each Scholar below to learn more about them._

----

### Meet Cristina

Meet Cristina Abascal. She is majoring in Biology with minors in Spanish and Chemistry. She’s passionate about women’s health and aspires to become an OB/GYN, using her love of science and language to connect with and support diverse communities. Outside the classroom, Cristina enjoys dancing, exploring new recipes, practicing calligraphy, and learning new languages. She’s committed to growing as a student and future leader, empowering future generations of women in STEM.

### Meet Destiny

Meet Destiny Licon. She attended the Young Women’s STEAM Research and Preparatory Academy at El Paso and is pursuing her bachelor's in mechanical engineering at her dream school, the University of Texas at Austin. At the Young Women’s Academy she was in Varsity Softball, was Student Council President, and was even school Mascot! As a Longhorn, she hopes to join many student organizations, participate in spirit events, and eventually work in an aerospace related field while bringing female representation to STEM! HOOK ‘EM!

### Meet Hannah

Meet Hannah Russum. She is a member of YWLA of Midland’s first graduating class. During high school, she honed a passion for aerospace through programs like NASA High School Aerospace Scholars. She has taken part in many school firsts, from being a founding member of YWLA’s National Honors Society chapter to being on the first Cross Country team. Additionally, she has volunteered extensively through her church and school. She is attending Texas A&M University majoring in aerospace engineering hoping to enter the space industry.

### Meet Jenasie

Meet Jenasie Ruiz. She attended Irma Rangel Young Women’s Leadership School, where she served as Valedictorian, President of National Honor Society, President of Junior World Affairs Council, and an AP Scholar with Distinction. She will be attending the University of Texas at Austin to study Sports Management and Kinesiology & Health: Exercise Science with a Pre-Medicine Track. After college, She plans to attend medical school to achieve her dream of being the head physician of the Dallas Cowboys.

### Meet Lyla

Meet Lyla Arnold. She is a passionate young environmental scientist and Rice University student majoring in Chemical Engineering, with aspirations of becoming a marine conservationist. Lyla has completed projects with the Audubon Conservation Leaders Program, was selected for the Voelcker Biomedical Research program at UT Health Science Center, and became a Regeneron International Science and Engineering Fair Finalist for her research on pharmaceutical pollution’s impact on zebrafish. As her 2025 Class President and a varsity athlete in soccer, cross country, and track, Lyla thrives on teamwork and leadership. Lyla is dedicated to connecting with people from all backgrounds to solve today’s environmental challenges.

### Meet Mykayla

Meet Mykayla Brown. She has had a long-standing dream of becoming a criminal litigator. She has a deep interest in advocacy, social justice, leadership, and community involvement, and hopes to use her legal career to promote equity within the justice system. She is attending Texas State University as an honors student, double majoring in Political Science and Business. She plans to gain hands-on experience through internships, leadership programs, and community service to prepare for law school.

### Meet Nicole

Meet Nicole Velasquez Hernandez. She will start her college journey at Rice University studying Mechanical Engineering, becoming the first person in her family to attend a four year university. As a mechanical engineer, she hopes to utilize robotics-based engineering to create life-changing medical devices aimed at improving the lives of patients and increasing the accessibility to such technology for all socioeconomic groups. Additionally, she plans to continue growing her passion for service by remaining engaged her my community and working to make a positive impact on those around her. She looks forward to continuing working hard in the future as a YWPN alumni and a future engineer.

### Meet Sarah

Meet Sarah Chen, a student at Texas Tech University on the pre-med track, majoring in Industrial Engineering. Through the highly selective Undergraduate Med School Initiative (UMSI) program, she holds a guaranteed seat at the Texas Tech School of Medicine for the Class of 2031. Sarah believes engineering is the art of analyzing systems—and what system is more intricate than the human body? That mindset fuels her passion for blending engineering and medicine to improve adolescent mental health. She is especially excited to explore innovations at the intersection of design, data, and healing. In her free time, Sarah is an accomplished musician. She debuted with the Lubbock Youth Orchestras in 2024, performing the Saint-Saëns Piano Concerto, and was a semifinalist in the Texas Music Teachers Association competition in both solo and concerto divisions (2023 and 2024 respectively). She also serves as principal violinist for the LYO, is concertmaster of Talkington’s varsity orchestra, and helps lead worship on guitar at her church.

### Meet Naylie

Meet Naylie Leal. She was born and raised in Houston, one of the most industrialized cities in the world, and she witnessed the impact of plastic waste from mechanical recovery. She is studying chemical engineering to advance depolymerization, a groundbreaking recycling method yielding pure plastic without losing vital properties. Joining the Rice Undergraduate Scholars Program, she will research its limitations in decomposing compounds like polyethylene, polypropylene, and polyvinyl chloride. Addressing these challenges could expand depolymerization’s applications and revolutionize recycling processes.
