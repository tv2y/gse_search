### Animal Rescue Group Leaders Sentenced in Animal Cruelty Case

Three former leaders of the now-defunct “Luck of the Irish” animal rescue were sentenced in Loudoun County Circuit Court Thursday, August 21, 2025, following guilty pleas for animal cruelty charges for crimes committed throughout 2022 and 2023. Nicole Metz, 52, and Kimberly Hall, 53, each pleaded guilty to five counts of cruelty to animals and were each sentenced to a 40-month suspended sentence; three years supervised probation; a five-year ban on pet ownership; a three-year prohibition on any involvement with an organization that cares for, treats or places companion animals; a mental health evaluation and subsequent treatment; and 150 hours of community service over a three-year period. Alex Hall, 24, pleaded guilty to three counts of cruelty to animals, and was sentenced to a nine-month suspended sentence, two years unsupervised probation and a five-year ban on pet ownership.

An investigation by Loudoun County Animal Services (LCAS) found that Metz and the Halls had failed to provide veterinary care and basic welfare to the animals in the custody of the Round Hill-based rescue. Over the course of two months, 105 animals were received by LCAS from the rescue, with the majority of them being held in unsanitary and unacceptable conditions in the Metz and Hall residences and approximately half of them infected with contagious diseases and/or parasites.

In testimony during the sentencing hearing, LCAS Director Nina Stively indicated that a number of animals were also recovered from the homes with broken bones and other untreated medical conditions, while others died in the rescue’s care without receiving veterinary treatment and were buried in mass graves between the two residential properties.

“Thanks to the successful intervention by Loudoun County Animal Services, over 100 animals were removed from deplorable conditions and the perpetrators were held accountable for their crimes,” said Commonwealth’s Attorney Bob Anderson. “The professionalism and dedication of Loudoun County Animal Services led to almost all of the animals being successfully re-homed and put a stop to the suffering.”

In addition to failing to provide humane and adequate care to the animals in their custody, the Luck of the Irish organization was not in compliance with state and local requirements for an animal rescue. LCAS staff have emphasized that this case highlights the importance of oversight and regulation in animal rescue organizations to ensure the well-being of animals in their care. Individuals considering adoption from a pet rescue are encouraged to visit the Loudoun County website to understand the legal requirements of pet rescues in Virginia and ensure that the rescue they are working with is in compliance with the law.

### Board Amends County Ordinances Related to Tax Requirements and Exemptions

The Loudoun County Board of Supervisors recently approved several amendments to the Codified Ordinances of Loudoun County regarding certain tax requirements and exemptions related to the transient occupancy tax, surviving spouses of armed forces members killed in the line of duty and solar equipment.

During the Board’s December 10, 2025, public hearing, the Board approved the following two items:

**Amending Chapter 878 – Transient Occupancy Tax**

The Board amended Chapter 878 - Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) to exempt business owners who offer overnight stays only through online platforms from filing with the county monthly if they file an annual affidavit by January 20 with the Commissioner of the Revenue. A change in state law by the General Assembly in 2025 permits this change. The update also clarifies that hotel points, when redeemed, are not included in the calculation for TOT collection. These changes are effective beginning January 1, 2026.

**Repealing Chapter 888 – Real Estate Tax Classification of Property Owned by Surviving Spouses of Certain Persons Killed in the Line of Duty**

The repeal of Chapter 888 aligns with amendments the General Assembly has made to the Code of Virginia, effective January 1, 2025. The change by the legislature eliminates the need for the local ordinance because the exemption is now a state law, § 58.1-3219.9. Exemption from taxes on property of surviving spouses of members of the armed forces who died in the line of duty.

During the Board’s November 12, 2025, public hearing, the Board approved the following item:

**Amending Chapter 868 – Solar Equipment Tax Exemptions**

The Board approved changes to Chapter 868 of the county ordinance to align with changes to the Virginia Code by the General Assembly. The amendment will permit a tax exemption of 100% of the value of the equipment for solar projects of 25 kilowatts or less for their useful life. Detailed information about the exemption is available in the staff report prepared for the public hearing (PDF).

**More Information**

More information about Loudoun County’s programs for tax relief, exemptions and deferrals is online at loudoun.gov/TaxRelief.

### Loudoun Vehicle Personal Property Assessment Schedule Change Effective Jan. 1

Based on the results of a recent benchmarking study, Loudoun County Commissioner of the Revenue Robert S. Wertz Jr. is updating the assessment methods used for some vehicle personal property. The J.D. Power Official Used Car Guide will continue to be the primary source for vehicle valuations. However, beginning January 1, 2026, methods used to value certain types of vehicles will change, including for newer model year cars, small pickup trucks, vans and SUVs that are not listed in the J.D. Power Official Used Car Guide as well as boats, motorcycles, recreational vehicles, heavy trucks, and pull behind trailers. These assessment methodology updates impact both personal use and business use vehicles.

Virginia law gives local governments the responsibility for taxing tangible property. All property within the same classification must be taxed uniformly and assessments must be based on fair market value (VA Code §58.1-3503(B)). The Commissioner of the Revenue contracted with PFM Group Consulting to study assessment schedules used to value vehicle personal property in Loudoun County, compare them with other Virginia counties and cities, and evaluate how closely they correspond to fair market value. That benchmarking study was completed in October of 2025.

Previously, vehicles were valued at a percentage of their purchase price as reported to the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles or, if the vehicle was not listed in a recognized pricing guide due to insufficient sales information for a particular year and model, at a percentage of the Manufacturer’s Suggested Retail Price (MSRP).

To better reflect fair market value of vehicle personal property, promoting fairness and uniformity, the assessment methods used to value the affected vehicles will change as indicated at the link below. Taxpayers will see the changes reflected in their next personal property tax bill, which will be due on May 5, 2026. All vehicles are assessed on the assumption that they are in average condition for their age. Each schedule represents how the model year vehicles will be assessed for tax year 2026. Which model year is “current” changes annually; next year, it will be 2026.

Based on the results of a recent benchmarking study, Loudoun County Commissioner of the Revenue Robert S. Wertz Jr. is updating the assessment schedules for most classes of business personal property. These changes are effective beginning January 1, 2026, and impact taxes levied on computer equipment, furniture and fixtures, computer equipment in data centers, heavy equipment, and machinery and tools.

All property within the same classification must be taxed uniformly and assessments must be based on fair market value (VA Code §58.1-3503(B)). ### Fairfax Man Sentenced to 30 Years for Sterling Stabbing

William Joseph Addison, 39, appeared before the Loudoun County Circuit Court for sentencing after a jury previously convicted him of one count of Aggravated Malicious Wounding and one count of Stabbing in the Commission of a Felony. The Honorable Stephen E. Sincavage sentenced Addison to serve an active period of incarceration of 30 years in the Virginia Department of Corrections.

Addison was found guilty of the offenses on March 4, 2025, after a two-day jury trial. Senior Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Anna Hammond and Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Nono Fisher represented the Commonwealth.

Just before midnight on February 4, 2023, Addison was loitering in a busy convenience store located in the Town Center at Sterling shopping center. When the victim entered the store and spoke to the clerk, Addison believed that they were talking about him, which agitated Addison. Although the victim assured Addison that they were not talking about him, Addison followed the victim out of the store. As the victim entered a vehicle, Addison stabbed him at least 4 times in the chest area, including below the ribcage and above the waistline.

The victim’s friend and the driver of the vehicle drove to a nearby nightclub in the shopping center, as she knew that law enforcement was often present there on weekend nights. Loudoun County Sheriff’s deputies arrived and performed lifesaving measures on the victim to stop the flow of blood. They also responded to the convenience store and apprehended Addison.

The victim was transported to Reston Hospital where he underwent trauma surgery to treat internal injuries and received blood transfusions equaling almost the entire blood volume of his body.

“It is due to the life-saving actions of the Loudoun County Sheriff’s Office deputies on the scene, and the trauma surgeon who operated on this gentleman that he lived. Loudoun County is safer today because this violent criminal is off the streets. The Office of the Commonwealth’s Attorney is grateful to our agency partners for their quick action and thorough investigation,” said Commonwealth’s Attorney Bob Anderson.

During the sentencing hearing, Senior Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Anna Hammond argued that “Mr. Addison cannot function in society. He is a danger to every person he encounters. For this reason, and the aggressiveness of the assault on [the victim], the Commonwealth is requesting a life sentence.”

Before he pronounced Addison’s sentence, Judge Sincavage, noted “the shear brutality of it … the randomness of it … the lack of remorse, especially at the scene.” Judge Sincavage said that in order to keep society safe, Addison would need to be separated from it by having a lengthy period of incarceration. Addison was sentenced to 55 years in the Virginia Department of Corrections with 25 years suspended, upon the successful completion of 5 years of supervised probation.

### Ashburn Man Sentenced to 15 Years for Molesting Child

On September 18, 2025, David Clements, Jr., 45, appeared before the Loudoun County Circuit Court for sentencing for his convictions of one count of Aggravated Sexual Battery (victim less than 13 years of age), one count of Possession of Child Pornography, and three counts of Possession of Child Pornography, 2nd Offense. The Honorable Matthew P. Snow sentenced Clements to serve an active period of incarceration of 15 years in the Virginia Department of Corrections. Clements is also required to register as a sex offender for life.

In January 2025, the Loudoun County Sheriff’s Office received a report of a sexual assault from the victim’s mother. During the ensuing investigation, it was determined that Clements had sexually molested a twelve-year-old girl. Clements and the victim were known to each other. When Clements was arrested, detectives recovered child sexual abuse materials (CSAM) on his electronic devices. No nude or illicit photos of the twelve-year-old were located on Clements’s devices.

“Our thoughts and prayers are with the victim and her family, and we hope this outcome will aid in their healing. Our office prioritizes the protection of the community, particularly children, who are our most vulnerable members,” said Commonwealth’s Attorney Bob Anderson.

Before he pronounced Clements’s sentence, Judge Snow said, “I agree with the Commonwealth … to exceed the high-end of the guidelines … [I]t’s not just the act itself, but it’s the ripple effects … [I]t comes down to punishment … I believe the Commonwealth said, ‘when the most trusted individual becomes the least trusted individual.’” Clements was sentenced to 55 years, the maximum allowed by law, with 40 years suspended. The suspended sentence is conditioned upon the satisfactory completion of 10 years of intensive supervised probation.

### Leesburg Man Sentenced for Third DWI, Eluding and Hit and Run

On September 4, 2025, Miky Alexander Mendoza appeared before the Loudoun County Circuit Court for sentencing for his convictions of one count of felony Hit & Run, one count of felony Eluding, and one count of Driving while Intoxicated, 3rd Offense. The Honorable James E. Plowman sentenced Mendoza to serve an active sentence of 6 years in the Virginia Department of Corrections. Upon release, Mendoza will be placed on supervised probation for 5 years and his driver’s license will be revoked indefinitely.

Just before midnight on October 25, 2024, Loudoun County Sheriff’s deputies observed a vehicle being driven without its headlights on while running a red light in Sterling. Sheriff’s deputies attempted to conduct a traffic stop but the sedan drove away rapidly. Deputies terminated the pursuit in the interest of safety when the pursuit reached speeds exceeding 100 miles per hour. Mendoza continued eastbound on

Leesburg Pike where he caused a multi-vehicle accident by running a red light at the intersection of Sterling Boulevard. Three other vehicles were involved in the wreck and several occupants of the other vehicles needed to be transported and treated at the hospital for non-life-threatening injuries. Mendoza fled the scene on foot but was later apprehended nearby by Loudoun County Sheriff’s deputies.

“We are grateful that no one was killed or seriously injured by this criminal behavior. We hope that this sentence sends the message that Loudoun County takes driving while intoxicated offenses very seriously,” said Commonwealth’s Attorney Bob Anderson.

During the sentencing hearing, Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Jonathan Dickey asked for a sentence that greatly exceeded the recommended Virginia Sentencing Guidelines and argued that “short of killing someone, this is one of the worst-case scenario fact patterns … [T]he Commonwealth does not believe a sentence within the guidelines would be appropriate for this case and asks the Court to sentence him significantly above the guidelines … [T]his case compels the Court to take him off the street to protect the community.”

The punishment range recommended by the Virginia Sentencing Guidelines was 6 months to 2 years and 1 month.

Before he pronounced Mendoza’s sentence, Judge Plowman said that “this is an above the guidelines case … [Y]ou are a danger to the community.” Mendoza was sentenced to 16 years in the Virginia Department of Corrections with 10 years suspended, upon the successful completion of 5 years of supervised probation.

### County Hosts Meeting on Bull Run Watershed Management Plan Jan. 28

Members of the Loudoun County community are invited to attend a public information meeting January 28, 2026, to learn about the development of a watershed management plan (WMP) in the Bull Run watershed in southern Loudoun. The purpose of the WMP is to inform efforts to reduce flood risks, protect water quality and support the long-term health of the watershed. The meeting will be held Wednesday, January 28, 2026, at 6:00 p.m. at the Dulles South Senior Center, Charlotte Nurge Room, 24950 Riding Center Drive, South Riding.

The meeting will feature an open-house format, beginning with a brief presentation and opportunities for community residents to talk with county staff and subject-matter experts in smaller breakout groups. In addition to the county sharing information about the project, the meeting will also serve as an opportunity for residents, homeowners associations and other stakeholders to share local knowledge and identify potential partnership opportunities. Residents will be invited to provide comments about flooding concerns, existing stormwater infrastructure that may need maintenance, and any interest in collaborating on possible projects, such as stream restoration or constructed wetlands, that may be evaluated as part of the plan.

**Bull Run Watershed**

The Bull Run watershed covers 28.6 square miles and forms the headwaters of the Occoquan Reservoir, a regional drinking water supply. The plan will apply hydrological, hydraulic and water quality data to identify potential stormwater infrastructure projects that could improve watershed resilience. This effort will help meet the county’s Chesapeake Bay and local sediment reduction requirements and plan for continued growth in the area.

The Bull Run Watershed Management Plan project is funded in part through a $250,000 grant received from the Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation’s Community Flood Preparedness Fund.

**More Information**

More information about the development of a watershed management plan for the Bull Run watershed is online at loudoun.gov/BullRunWatershed.

Briskman will continue as chair of the Finance/Government Operations and Economic Development Committee, Little River District Supervisor Laura A. During the Board’s twice-monthly business meetings and monthly public hearing:

    *   The Board will continue to receive public input virtually and in-person; during business meetings public input will last for up to 90 minutes.
    *   Speakers will be allotted two minutes to address the Board.
    *   Members of the public who wish to provide input virtually must sign up in advance by noon on the day of the meeting; in-person sign-up begins 30 minutes prior to the start of a meeting. The Board’s procedures for providing public input are posted online at loudoun.gov/SignUpToSpeak.

The Board adopted a standard meeting schedule that generally results in its first business meeting of the month occurring on the first Tuesday of the month at 4:00 p.m. The Board’s public hearing will occur on the Wednesday of the week following the first business meeting of the month at 6:00 p.m. The Board’s second business meeting will occur on the Tuesday following the week of the public hearing at 4:00 p.m.

### Six Things to Know about the November 4, 2025, General Election in Loudoun

A general election will be held in Loudoun County Tuesday, November 4, 2025. Election officials remind voters of six key things to know ahead of the election.

**1. Know Where to Vote**

    *   Voters will vote at their normal polling place on Election Day. A list of polling places in Loudoun is available at loudoun.gov/polls. 
    *   Anyone who is not sure about where to vote may check their voter registration card or look up their polling place and related information online at vote.elections.virginia.gov.
    *   **Curbside voting**is available at all polling places for individuals who have mobility issues.
    *   Note that the Office of Elections and Voting Registration is not a polling place on Election Day.

**2. Know When to Vote**

    *   Polls in Loudoun County will be open from 6:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m., Tuesday, November 4, 2025.
    *   The last day to vote early in person is Saturday, November 1. Locations and hours for early voting are online at loudoun.gov/VoteEarly.
    *   Completed absentee ballots may be returned by mail (postmarked no later than November 4) or dropped off at the 24/7 drop box at the Office of Elections and Voter Registration, 750 Miller Drive SE, Suite 150, Leesburg, or at early voting sites.
    *   Voters may also drop off their ballots at any polling place up to 7:00 p.m. on November 4.

**3. Bring Identification**

    *   Voters will be asked to show an acceptable form of identification, such as a Virginia DMV-issued driver’s license. Voters who do not have an acceptable form of ID can sign a statement affirming their identity and they will be able to vote a regular ballot. 

**4. Know Which Offices and Questions are on the Ballot**

    *   Offices on the November 4, 2025, general election ballot in Loudoun County include the Virginia governor, lieutenant governor and attorney general; seats in the Virginia House of Delegates; mayors and town council members in Middleburg and Round Hill; and members of the Loudoun County School Board for the Algonkian, Broad Run, Dulles, Leesburg and Sterling Districts (Note: The School Board has moved to staggered terms, so only five of the nine seats are up for election in 2025.) Three county government bond questions will also be on the ballot. 

**5. Know Who Can Vote**

    *   All registered voters in Loudoun County are eligible to vote in the general election.
    *   Although the deadline to register to vote in the November 4 election or to update current voter registration information was October 24, voters may register up to—and including on—Election Day and vote a provisional ballot. Information about same-day voter registration is available from the Virginia Department of Elections.

**6. Stay Informed**

    *   Text LCVOTES to 888777 to receive text alerts from the Loudoun County Office of Elections and Voter Registration or sign up for the Election Update category through Alert Loudoun, the county’s email and text notification system.
    *   Follow the Office of Elections and Voter Registration on social media, including Facebook, X and Bluesky. Loudoun County Government also posts general information on Facebook, X and Bluesky.
    *   Get election results: **Preliminary results** of voting in Loudoun County in the November 4 election will be available following the closing of the polls on election night at loudoun.gov/ElectionResults.

More information about the general election is online at loudoun.gov/NovemberElection. General information about voting in Loudoun County is online at loudoun.gov/vote.

### County Hosts Meeting on Bull Run Watershed Management Plan Jan. 28

Members of the Loudoun County community are invited to attend a public information meeting January 28, 2026, to learn about the development of a watershed management plan (WMP) in the Bull Run watershed in southern Loudoun. The purpose of the WMP is to inform efforts to reduce flood risks, protect water quality and support the long-term health of the watershed. The meeting will be held Wednesday, January 28, 2026, at 6:00 p.m. at the Dulles South Senior Center, Charlotte Nurge Room, 24950 Riding Center Drive, South Riding.

The meeting will feature an open-house format, beginning with a brief presentation and opportunities for community residents to talk with county staff and subject-matter experts in smaller breakout groups. In addition to the county sharing information about the project, the meeting will also serve as an opportunity for residents, homeowners associations and other stakeholders to share local knowledge and identify potential partnership opportunities. Residents will be invited to provide comments about flooding concerns, existing stormwater infrastructure that may need maintenance, and any interest in collaborating on possible projects, such as stream restoration or constructed wetlands, that may be evaluated as part of the plan.

**Bull Run Watershed**

The Bull Run watershed covers 28.6 square miles and forms the headwaters of the Occoquan Reservoir, a regional drinking water supply. The plan will apply hydrological, hydraulic and water quality data to identify potential stormwater infrastructure projects that could improve watershed resilience. This effort will help meet the county’s Chesapeake Bay and local sediment reduction requirements and plan for continued growth in the area.

The Bull Run Watershed Management Plan project is funded in part through a $250,000 grant received from the Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation’s Community Flood Preparedness Fund.

**More Information**

More information about the development of a watershed management plan for the Bull Run watershed is online at loudoun.gov/BullRunWatershed.

### Four Occupants Displaced After Electrical Fire in Sterling

A house fire in the 500 block of N. Fillmore Avenue on Sunday evening left four occupants displaced and resulted in damages estimated at $270,336. The Loudoun County Fire and Rescue Fire Marshal’s Office (LCFR-FMO) has determined the fire was accidental, due to an electrical event within a first-floor bedroom’s outlets. Fortunately, the home’s smoke alarms were working as designed and alerted the occupants to the fire. No civilian or firefighter injuries were reported.

At approximately 8:30 p.m. on Sunday, December 21, 2025, the Loudoun County Emergency Communications Center received multiple 9-1-1 calls for a reported structure fire. LCFR units from Sterling, Cascades, and Kincora, as well as Fairfax County and multiple command and safety officers responded to the scene. The first-arriving engine found a single-family home with smoke coming from the front and requested a Rapid Intervention Taskforce to bring additional firefighting resources to the scene. Crews quickly located and extinguished a fire on the first floor.

Unfortunately, accidental electrical fires do occur. There are, however, several steps you can take to keep you and your family safe:

    *   Electrical work should be done by a qualified electrician and meet local permitting requirements. Immediately call a qualified electrician if you have frequent issues with blowing or tripping circuit breakers, observe sparks, discoloration, or a burning smell near outlets, or feel a tingling sensation when touching an electrical appliance.
    *   Ensure all appliances are using the proper type of wall receptacle outlet. Plug ALL major appliances directly into a wall receptacle outlet.
    *   Do not use extension cords and power strips in place of permanent wiring.
    *   Inspect cords regularly and immediately unplug and replace those with any damage or fraying.
    *   Take a moment to complete the National Fire Protection Association’s Electrical Safety Checklist to ensure that your home is protected against electrical fires.
    *   Ensure your home is equipped with working smoke alarms and test them regularly. LCFR offers free smoke alarm assessments to Loudoun County residents to ensure all homes in the community have these life-saving devices. Visit our website to learn more and schedule your free assessment.

For additional fire prevention and life safety information, visit our website.

    *   Land use considerations

    *   **Long-Term Support**: Services that focus on assisting individuals who have continuing, long-term support needs to remain healthy, safe, and independent in the community.
    *   **Prevention and Self-Sufficienc****y**: Services focused on assisting individuals and families in becoming and/or remaining independent and stable, and providing tools, skills, strategies and resources to individuals and families.

**Changes for FY 2027 Grant Application Process**

The Board of Supervisors has approved changes in the FY 2027 grant application process, including:

    *   The maximum award amount for the standard nonprofit grant application has been increased for FY 2027 from $113,000 to $135,000 following feedback from the nonprofit community on funding limitations.
    *   Nonprofits will be asked to select only one area of need in their grant application.
    *   Nonprofits will have the option of selecting optional need categories, including education access, food security, health care access, housing assistance, mental health supports and workforce development.

The mini-grant program is designed for innovative small-scale projects and new or small organizations that may lack the capacity to write a larger grant.
