I'm fortunate enough to have had a carpenter build bookcases designed for the most efficient use of space for organization by topic. Each shelf on every bookcase, top to bottom, is exactly the same height, except for art and architecture books stored horizontally on special lower, wide shelves also used for the handful of other oversized volumes.

Fiction/literature is organized alphabetically by author (and within that, by date of publication); science fiction has a separate section, also organized by author. Science is separated into astronomy, biology, physics, and "other" (everything else). Philosophy is separated into epistemology and "other"; religion is organized by specific faith.

Dictionaries, style manuals, and language books share a section. Small subjects (humor, navigation, technology, law, medical, etc.) each have their own sections. History is about half the collection, with the bulk of these being U.S. history organized by topic, arranged chronologically from early exploration to current events, not by author except for postwar memoirs.

Non-U.S. history is grouped into ancient, European, Asian, etc., each of these further organized by specific country and then chronologically. Cookbooks, hobby books, and children's books are shelved elsewhere in the house (except J.K. Rowling is upstairs in the regular literature section, where you'd find all of Roald Dahl if I only I had the room!).

UPDATE: To clarify, about half of my bookcases are carpenter built, although this answer makes it sound as if they all are. The organization is still as described.

Here’s my wall of U.S. history — note how all the shelves are of uniform height and (if you can zoom in) arranged chronologically and organized by sub topic, from pre-Columbian times through the 20th century. Post World War II American memoirs are around the corner (usually memoirs are shelved as history).

Home organization starts with reducing clutter before you find storage options that fit your daily needs. Begin your organization process by sorting all your items into their proper rooms while keeping only the essential or valuable objects. The removal of clutter from your space requires you to establish dedicated storage areas for each item which will help you keep things organized.

People who share their living space with others will find great benefits in using living room storage solutions. Furniture with multiple functions serves as an essential design choice because it offers hidden storage spaces through ottomans and coffee tables, and media units that combine open and closed storage options. Baskets and decorative boxes maintain room aesthetics while keeping small objects organized. Wall-mounted shelves help free up floor space while displaying books or décor.

The limited space in bedrooms becomes more functional through the use of drawer organizers and under-bed bins, and closet dividers. The kitchen and bathroom spaces maintain their organization through the use of stacking containers and cabinet risers, and clear jars. These make all essentials visible and within reach.

I use clear Sterilite brand 56, 16, and 6 quart containers with white lids.

I like that they are clear, because (ADHD) not being able to see something effectively erases it from existence. I also like that the plastic is compatible with the adhesive used in Brother brand label printers. I buy a bunch of these, so the variety of sizes and low cost are important features too.

That said, these are not perfect. The sides are steeply sloped so you have a lot of dead area between containers. Stacking them too high can cause the lower lids to bow or fail. The lids are removable and easily misplaced. They become brittle when cold or with prolonged exposure to sunlight. (e.g. the light from the window in the garage).

My perfect bin would be clear, ~ 50% stronger, straighter sides, and a hinged non-removable lid. These do exist, but I won’t buy them.

Why? Because I can get these. It is infuriating to start building an organization system and then find that the bin you used is no longer available. I can count on these specific bins being around in the future. Sterilite seems to have dodged the silly idea that they need to replace their product line every year. They’ve been cranking out this form factor for decades with no sign of stopping and I can get them locally from a half dozen vendors. (Dollar General, Target, Wal-Mart locally, plus Dollar Tree even has the small size). This makes me reasonably confident I can buy them in the future. That is much more important than the features of the technically superior alternatives.

I live in a rather nice studio apartment, but it only has two small closets between the bath and the entry that seem best suited as a broom closet and a coat closet. So I got a free-standing wardrobe at IKEA that has two sliding doors and four drawers underneath. I can hang up my casual T-shirts on one side, my dressier sweaters and blouses on the other with pants on hangers. I have a drawer for shorts and leggings, one for wound-up “broomstick skirts”, one for socks, and one for undergarments.

There aren’t enough kitchen cabinets or drawers, so I have a pair of IKEA open-frame plastic and mesh drawers (they’re Elfa knockoffs) with a section of countertop across them. I can store my mixer, waffle iron, baking supplies in the plastic tubs and have a toaster oven on top. The mesh drawers are good for potatoes and onions that need air circulation.
