Abstract
--------

At 20:08, on September 18, 2024, an _M_ 4.7 earthquake occurred along the Tanlu fault zone in the Feidong County of Hefei, Anhui Province. This earthquake is the largest event in the modern history of Hefei, which caused substantial social impact. To reveal the seismogenic structure of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence and assess seismic risks, we use data from both the permanent seismic network and a temporary dense nodal array deployed in the epicentral region prior to the mainshock for: (1) accurate location of the earthquake sequence and determination of the focal mechanisms; (2) obtaining the spatiotemporal distribution, _b_-value, and half-day occurrence frequency of the earthquake sequence. The Sentinel-1 satellite data are used to analyze the coseismic displacement. Additionally, velocity models from regional tomography and local high-resolution 2D active- and passive-source surveys across the Tanlu fault zone in the epicentral area are also used to reveal the detailed geometry of the seismogenic fault. The results indicate: (1) the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence is concentrated around 10.5 km in depth along a NW-dipping, subvertical fault which trends NE and is approximately 5 km in length; the focal mechanism solution also reveals that the fault hosting the mainshock is a subvertical strike-slip fault, driven by the regional compressional stress in ENE-WSW; the coseismic horizontal displacement on the surface caused by the _M_ 4.7 mainshock has a maximum value close to 1 mm; (2) the regional velocity model shows significant lateral variation in _v_ S in the source region, with the mainshock occurring in the area with higher velocity; high-resolution P-wave velocity structures obtained by full waveform inversion from active sources, and S-wave velocity structures from passive-source ambient noise tomography indicate that the mainshock occurred along the boundary between high- and low-velocity bodies, and the seismogenic fault dips NW; the deep seismic reflection profiling shows that the mainshock occurred within the Jurassic strata; (3) based on these results, we suggest the seismogenic fault for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake is either the Zhuding-Shimenshan fault, one of the major faults in the Tanlu fault zone, or a hidden fault to the east; the intersection of the NE-trending Tanlu fault zone and the WNW-trending Feizhong fault, along with significant velocity variations, likely create local stress concentrations which could have triggered the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence; (4) the strong aftershocks following the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock did not further extend the fault rupture zone; the active period of the Zhuding-Shimenshan fault was the late Early Pleistocene to Middle Pleistocene, and the imaging results indicate that this fault does not cut through the shallow Feidong depression. In conjunction with the small coseismic rupture area, it is inferred that the probability of surface-rupturing earthquakes in the future is relatively low.

1. Introduction
---------------

According to the Anhui seismic network, at 20:08 Beijing time on September 18, 2024, an _M_ 4.7 (_M_ L) earthquake struck at the Feidong County of Hefei City, Anhui Province (117.60°E, 31.98°N). The depth of the earthquake is approximately 10 km, and its epicenter is located about 37 km from the city center of Hefei (hereinafter referred to as the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake). This event is the largest earthquake in the modern history of Hefei, and caused substantial social impact. The previous _M_≥5 earthquake in this region occurred about 350 years ago (Gu GX, 1983). In the epicentral area (Liangyuan town) the shaking was strong, reaching an intensity of VI on the modified Mercalli intensity (MMI) scale. The earthquake was felt not only distinctly in Hefei, but also felt across the Anhui Province and in nearby areas such as Nanjing, Yangzhou, and Hangzhou (according to field reports). Before the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake, three earthquakes with magnitudes greater than 3.0 occurred in the region, which are the _M_ 3.5 on February 24, _M_ 3.1 on May 12, and _M_ 3.9 earthquake on September 14, 2024. Following the mainshock, several moderate aftershocks including the _M_ 3.8 on September 25 and _M_ 3.3 earthquake on October 1, 2024 occurred in sequence. Strong shakings were alerted several times, which caused train delays in the Yangtze River delta region. After the _M_ 3.9 foreshock on September 14, the research team of this study rapidly deployed 9 real-time nodal stations in the source region to monitor the seismic sequence. Seismic phases were picked automatically in real time for source location and related analyses, and the _M_ 4.7 mainshock on September 18 was also recorded closely by these temporary stations. The temporary observation array was expanded to consist of 49 stations immediately after the mainshock.

Historical documents show that though no destructive earthquakes have been recorded in the Liangyuan Town of Feidong County, three major earthquakes occurred within 100 km of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake: the 1673 _M_ 5 earthquake in Hefei (∼34 km away), the 1868 _M_ 5.5 earthquake in Dingyuan (∼49 km away), and the 1585 _M_ 5 3/4 earthquake in Chaoxian (∼87 km away) (Gu GX, 1983). In modern earthquake records, only five _M_≥3.0 earthquakes occurred within a radius of 50 km prior to the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake, with the largest being an _M_ 3.5 earthquake on April 6, 2009 (Li LL et al., 2021). The _M_ 4.7 (_M_ L 5.2) Feidong earthquake is the largest event occurring along the Tanlu fault zone (TLFZ) in recent history for the Hefei area with strong shaking and a complex sequence. According to the data from the Anhui earthquake network, a total of 293 _M_ L≥0.0 earthquakes were recorded in the region from February 24 to October 10, 2024, including 201 earthquakes with magnitudes between _M_ L 1.0−1.9, 59 earthquakes with magnitudes between _M_ L 2.0−2.9, 8 earthquakes between _M_ L 3.0−3.9, 4 earthquakes between _M_ L 4.0−4.9, and 1 earthquake with _M_ L≥5.0. The difference in magnitudes between the mainshock and the largest aftershock in the sequence is 0.8, which classifies the sequence as a mainshock-aftershock type (Jiang HK et al., 2006).

This study focuses on analyzing the characteristics of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence and investigating the seismogenic structures based on regional active- and passive-source tomography. On one hand, continuous seismic waveforms from 88 permanent seismic stations within a 300 km radius of the epicenter and 49 temporary nodal stations within 25 km were used to accurately locate the _M_ 4.7 Feidong sequence using the double-difference location method. The focal mechanisms of six _M_≥3.0 earthquakes in the sequence were inverted for using methods that combines polarities, amplitude ratios, and waveform matching. Based on these results, the spatiotemporal distribution of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong sequence and the seismogenic structure are analyzed. The Sentinel-1 satellite data before (September 16, 2024) and after (September 28, 2024) the mainshock are also used to analyze the coseismic surface displacement. On the other hand, using the S-wave velocity structure covering the crust and upper mantle in the central and southern sections of the TLFZ (Luo S et al., 2022), high-resolution seismic reflection imaging and full waveform inversion results from an ∼87 km long active-source survey across the TLFZ near the mainshock (Li HP et al., 2023), in conjunction with high-resolution S-wave velocity tomographic profiles using data collected by a 65 km long survey line with station spacing of about 300 meters deployed by the research team in 2021, the velocity structures and seismogenic environment for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake are analyzed comprehensively.

2. Regional seismicity, geological structure, and data acquisition
------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.1. Regional seismicity and geological structure

The TLFZ is one of the most distinct deep, active fault systems in eastern China with a total length exceeding 2400 km, and the fault zone plays a crucial role in controlling modern seismic activities, regional tectonics, and the formation and distribution of mineral resources in eastern China (Zhu G et al., 2016). The TLFZ can be divided into three sections: the northern section (Shenyang-Bohai segment), the central section (Shandong segment, a.k.a. the Yishu fault zone), and the southern section (Jiangsu-Anhui segment) (Zhang P et al., 2007). The central and southern segments are adjacent to the Dabie orogen, acting as the boundary between the North China Block and the Yangtze Block in eastern China (Figure 1 a). Seismic activity along the TLFZ exhibits distinct segmentation. Strong earthquakes such as the _M_ 8.5 earthquake in Tancheng, Shandong in 1668, and the _M_ 7.4 earthquake in Bohai in 1969 occurred in the central section. The Tancheng earthquake remains the largest recorded earthquake in the history of eastern China, causing over 50,000 casualties. In comparison, seismic activities to the south of Tancheng are much weaker, with few events exceeding _M_ 6.

Since 2024, seismic activities near the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake have exhibited distinct variations, which can be divided into three stages. In the first stage from February 24 to May 11, 8 _M_ L≥0.0 earthquakes occurred, with the largest magnitude reaching _M_ 3.5; in the second stage from May 12 to August 20, 46 _M_ L≥0.0 earthquakes occurred, with the largest magnitude reaching _M_ 3.1; in the third stage, which began with about 20 days of quiescence from August 21 to September 9, seismic activity burst after September 10 with 231 _M_ L≥0.0 earthquakes occurring in a short period. Also, in this stage 4 events with magnitudes greater than _M_ 3.0 as well as the _M_ 4.7 mainshock occurred. Compared to the first two stages, both the frequency and magnitude of earthquakes in the third stage increase significantly.

The Hefei segment of the TLFZ trends NE to NNE and is characterized by a shallow branched, flower-like fault structure (Li HP et al., 2023), which is mainly composed of four major parallel faults, the Wuhe-Hefei fault F2, Zhuding-Shimenshan (Sangjianzi-Guanghanqiao) fault F1, Chihe-Taihu (Chihe-Xishanyi) fault F15, and Jiashan-Liujiang (Outang-Qingshuijian) fault F16 from west to east (Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources of Anhui Province, 1987). The two faults on the western side of the basin (F2 and F1) are parallel and hidden beneath the Hefei basin. The fault F2 which dips SEE at 70°–80° is the western bound of the TLFZ, while the fault F1 dips to the west. Zheng YP et al. (2020) use shallow seismic reflection, drilling and geological profiles to reveal that the most recent activity on the fault F2 occurred during the late Middle Pleistocene. The fault F15 is the eastern bound of the Hefei basin with the most prominent characteristics observed by remote sensing, which reflects a combination of deep strike-slip faulting and shallow extensional faulting. Upon entering the Hefei basin, the fault F15 continues to extend southward, marking the boundary between the Mesozoic and Cenozoic strata of the Hefei basin and the Feidong group in the Zhangbaling uplift (Zhang JD et al., 2010). The faults F2 and F15 dip in opposite directions, creating a secondary depression in the Hefei basin known as the Feidong depression. The compressional fault F16 marks the eastern bound of the fault zone. Overall, the TLFZ has a tectonic structure characterized by westward subsidence and eastward uplift (Figure 1 b).

The _M_ 4.7 Feidong sequence occurred at the intersection of the NE- to NNE-trending TLFZ and the WNW-trending Feizhong fault F3, and is located at the southern segment of the TLFZ to the east of Hefei. This segment lies between the Sulu orogen and the Dabie orogen, with the Hefei basin-covered boundary between the Yangtze Block and the North China Block to the north. The Feizhong fault F3, which trends eastward through the central part of the basin is the suture zone between the North China Block and the Yangtze Block (Shi YH et al., 2009). To the south, the fault F3 forms the boundary between the Dabie orogen and the Yangtze Block. The Feizhong fault F3 is located about 3 km to the north of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock, which dips steeply southward and is covered by Quaternary sediments. The latest strata cut by this fault are the Cretaceous strata.

### 2.2. Seismic data from permanent network and temporary array in the study area

The seismic data used in this study are from two sources: (1) event waveforms recorded by the permanent seismic networks in the Anhui, Henan, Hubei, and neighboring provinces between 2024-02-24 and 2024-10-10, and seismic phases for 293 _M_ L≥0.0 events which are provided by the China Earthquake Networks Center; (2) data collected by a two-phased temporary dense nodal array with real-time 4G data telemetry deployed by the research team in the source region in two periods: an initial phase consisting of 9 nodal stations within a 7.5 km radius of the epicenter following the _M_ 3.9 foreshock on September 14, 2024, and a secondary phase consisting of 40 additional stations within a 25 km radius of the epicenter after the _M_ 4.7 mainshock on September 18, 2024. A dense monitoring network comprising all the 49 nodal stations was then built (Figure 1 c). We use an automatic processing workflow based on deep learning to process the real-time data from the dense network (Li JL et al., 2022; Chen and Li, 2022), and provide an accurate catalog for the sequence.

More specifically, the seismic data collected by the nodal stations deployed in the source region are transmitted in real time through the 4G cellular network to a remote data server. The asynchronous data from individual nodes are subsequently synchronized, forming common time gathers comprising waveforms from all nodes, which are then sent to a data analysis server. This server uses a retrained U-Net for event detections and picking of the P- and S-arrivals, and incorrect picks are also automatically removed (Chen and Li, 2022). The events are then located using a nonlinear location algorithm (Lomax et al., 2000), and the magnitudes are also determined using a dedicated procedure suitable for small-aperture arrays in accordance with the national earthquake magnitude determination standard (Yang W et al., 2021). The real-time seismic catalog generated in this procedure is shown in Figure 2.

### 2.3. Seismic imaging results from active and passive sources in the study area

To analyze the seismogenic structures of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake, we use a regional 3D S-wave velocity model covering the central and southern sections of the TLFZ (Luo S et al., 2022), in conjunction with high-resolution 2D velocity models from active sources (Li HP et al., 2023) and passive sources. On April 6, 2009, an _M_ 3.5 earthquake occurred 10 km to southwest of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake. To assess the risks of fault reactivations, the Anhui Earthquake Agency launched an active-source survey for active-fault characterization and seismic hazard analysis in 2015, which collected high-quality 2D deep reflection data using high-charge dynamites (Qin JJ et al., 2018). The survey line of 87 km in length crosses four fault branches of the TLFZ and several geological units such as the Hefei basin, Feidong depression, Zhangbaling uplift, and Quanjiao basin (Figure 1 b). Using the active-source data, Li HP et al. (2023) perform full waveform inversion to obtain a high-resolution P-wave velocity model for the upper 3 km of the crust.

In addition, the research team deployed a 2D passive-source seismic survey line in 2020 along the active-source line to obtain a high-resolution S-wave velocity profile for the region. Since the travel time-based tomographic results with active sources (Qin JJ et al., 2018) indicate that under the western side of the survey line the sedimentary structure is flat with no distinct faults, the starting point of the passive-source survey line of 65 km in length was set 20 km from northwest end of the active-source survey line. The passive-source survey line consisted of 221 nodal seismometers with an average station spacing of 300 m, and recorded data continuously for one month. This line crossed the Hefei basin, Feidong depression, Zhangbaling uplift, and Quanjiao basin (Figure 1 b), and the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake occurred at about 21 km from the northwest end of the line. The imaging results from this line have not been published previously.

3. Characteristics of the Feidong _M_ 4.7 earthquake sequence
-------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.1. Location of the earthquake sequence based on the permanent network

In this study, the HypoDD method (Waldhauser and Ellsworth, 2000) is used to relocate the 293 earthquakes with magnitudes ranging from _M_ L 0.6−5.2 that occurred in the epicentral area of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake from 2024-02-24 to 2024-10-10. There are 24,504 first-arrival picks in total after initial selection, or 11 picks for a seismic event on average. Figure 3 a shows the relocated epicenters. The earthquake sequence shows a quasilinear, 10 km-long distribution in the northeast direction, which is subparallel with the fault branches of the TLFZ in this region at an angle of ∼10°. The relocated earthquakes projected on profile _AB_ (Figures 3 b and 3c) indicate that the shallow section of the seismogenic fault dips northwest, and below 8 km in depth where the mainshock occurred the fault turns subvertical.

### 3.2. Locations of the earthquake sequence based on the temporary dense nodal array

The double-difference method (HypoDD) is also used to locate the 763 earthquakes recorded by the dense nodal array between 2024-09-14−2024-10-10 (Figure 4). Unlike the data from the permanent stations, waveform cross-correlation (Zhang M et al., 2022) is used to calculate precise differential times from the waveform data recorded by the nodal array, further improving the relative location accuracy. It is found that the dense nodal array can provide better constraints on the distribution of the earthquake sequence, which ruptured away on both sides of the mainshock in northeastward linear pattern. The sequence extends over a length of about 5 km, and the relocated hypocenters are more concentrated compared to the results based on data from the permanent network. After relocation, the earthquake sequence is primarily concentrated at the depth of 10.5 km. In particular, the mainshock is located at 10.6 km in depth, which is in close agreement with the catalog result (10 km) using the permanent network and the depth from the moment tensor solution (10 km) reported by the China Earthquake Networks Center.

### 3.3. Joint analysis using the permanent network and temporary dense nodal array

In the current national earthquake magnitude measurement standard, a gauge function with 5 km intervals is applied to correct for the earthquake magnitude (Liu RF et al., 2018), which is not applicable in magnitude determination with dense nodal arrays of small apertures. Therefore, it is necessary to use some common events to first determine the coefficients in the magnitude calculation formula as described in Yang W et al. (2021). The calibrated magnitude determination formula for the dense array in the Feidong region is(1)M L=log 10 A+1.26 log 10 D−0.0026 D−2.2,where _A_ is the average of the maximum displacements (nm) of the two horizontal components, and _D_ is the epicentral distance (km).

Figure 5 shows the magnitude-time (_M_-_T_) distributions of the earthquakes near the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock detected by the permanent network and the dense nodal array in different periods. It can be observed that both the frequency and magnitude of the earthquakes increased after September 10. During the period of 2024-09-14−2024-10-10, the dense nodal array detected 763 earthquakes, whereas the permanent network detected 206 events.

The _b_-value calculated with the data from the permanent network is 0.67, with a magnitude of completeness (_M_ C) of 1.4, while the _b_-value with the data from the dense nodal array is 0.72, with an _M_ C of 0.6. The results above indicate that the dense nodal array, combined with AI-based automatic processing, is rather effective in monitoring small magnitude earthquakes.

### 3.4. Focal mechanism solutions for the _M_≥3.0 earthquakes

Focal mechanism solutions for the six _M_≥3.0 earthquakes occurring in the study region in 2024 are inverted for using the method proposed by Snoke et al. (1984), which combines the polarity of direct P-waves, SV-waves, and SH-waves, along with amplitude ratios of the P/SV, P/SH, or SV/SH waves (Ni HY et al., 2021). As for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock, 85 polarities for the P-waves (including Pg and Pn), 8 polarities for the SH-waves, and 12 amplitude ratios from data of the permanent network within 300 km and 9 temporary nodal stations with good signal-to-noise ratios are inverted for the focal mechanism. The result (Figure 7) shows the strike ranges from 224° to 230°, the dip from 80° to 90°, and the rake from −178° to −175° (for the NE-oriented focal plane in Figure 7 a), indicating that the solution is well constrained. The final solution for fault plane I is (225°, 85°, −179°) for strike, dip, and rake, while that for fault plane II is 135°, 89°, −5° (red lines in Figures 7 a). The azimuth and plunge of the _P_-axis are 90° and 4°, respectively, and those of the _T_-axis are 180° and 3°, respectively (Figure 7 b).

### 3.5. Characteristics of surface deformation

The Sentinel-1 satellite owned by the European Space Agency has a revisiting cycle of 12 days, which can provide proper InSAR data for studying the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake. In this study, the image from the ascending orbit T142 covering the source area is selected, and differential interferometry is applied to the pre-event (September 16, 2024) and post-event (September 28, 2024) images. The key parameters are shown in Table 2. The processing procedure can be summarized as follows. First, the ALOS DEM (30 m × 30 m resolution) is used to remove phase errors caused by topography (Farr et al., 2007). To improve the signal-to-noise ratio, range looks of 6 and azimuth look of 1 are applied to suppress noise in the interferometric phase. Additionally, the Goldstein adaptive filter (Goldstein, 1995) is used to filter the interferometric phase, and phase unwrapping is performed using the minimum cost flow algorithm (Werner et al., 2002). Pixels with coherence lower than 0.3 are masked. Next, GACOS data and the tropospheric iterative decomposition (ITD) model (Yu C et al., 2018) are used to separate stratified and turbulent signals from the total tropospheric delay, and generate a high spatial-resolution zenith total delay map to remove the influence from atmospheric water vapors. Finally, ground control points (GCPs) are selected for orbit refinement and re-rectification. Satellite orbits and phase offsets are corrected, and terrain-related atmospheric phase delays are estimated and reduced using the DEM. The final result is the coseismic deformation field in the line-of-sight (LOS) direction caused by the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake (Figure 8). It is found that the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake only caused trivial coseismic displacement, with the maximum horizontal displacement being about 1 mm.

4. Regional velocity structure and detailed fault structures in the source region
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.1. Regional S-wave velocity structure

First, we use the S-wave velocity model that covers the crust and upper mantle in the central and southern sections of the TLFZ (Luo S et al., 2022) to analyze the crustal velocity characteristics of the source region. The model which is obtained by joint inversion of surface waves from seismic ambient noise and earthquakes has a horizontal grid size of 0.25° × 0.25° and a vertical grid size of 5 km. The horizontal resolution of the model in the crust is better than 0.5° in the study region. Figures 9 a and 9b show the slices of the S-wave velocity at the depths of 5 and 10 km, respectively. It can be seen that in the slice at the depth of 5 km, the epicenter of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake lies at the boundary between high- and low-velocities, whereas at the depth of 10 km the epicenter is located in a region with relatively high _v_ S. In the vertical profile (Figure 9 c), it can be found there is significant velocity variation in the source region, and the _M_ 4.7 mainshock is located in the region with higher _v_ S.

### 4.2. High-resolution S-wave velocity structure in the source region

The high-resolution extended range phase shift method by Ni HY et al. (2022) and Deng B et al. (2022) is used in the processing of passive-source ambient noise data, including cross-correlation, extraction of dispersion curves, and tomography. The details are not elaborated in this study. The effective tomographic depth is about 8 km, slightly shallower than the focal depth of the _M_ 4.7 mainshock (Figure 10). The lateral variation in the S-wave velocity structure corresponds well with the geological characteristics of the Hefei basin-Zhangbaling uplift-Quanjiao basin, and clearly delineates the major faults of the TLFZ including the Wuhe-Hefei fault F2, Zhuding-Shimenshan fault F1, Chihe-Taihu fault F15 and Jiashan-Lujiang fault F16 (Figure 11). The _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence occurred near the subvertical, NW dipping fault F1, which dips more steeply at greater depths. Both the spatial distribution of the earthquake sequence and the projected focal mechanism of the mainshock on the vertical profile show consistency with the fault F1 (Figure 11).

### 4.3. P-wave velocity structure and lithological interfaces in the source region

Using full waveform inversion (FWI), Li HP et al. (2023) obtain a high-resolution P-wave velocity model of the shallow crust in the source region, which reveals significant structural variations beneath the Hefei Basin (HFB), Zhangbaling Uplift (ZBLU), and Quanjiao Basin (QJB) across the TLFZ (Figure 12 a). In the Feidong Depression (FDD) where the _M_ 4.7 mainshock struck, the NW-dipping stratigraphic interface reflects the fault-controlled sedimentary process. The Zhangbaling uplift correlates with a prominent high velocity zone, corresponding to the exposed magmatic or metamorphic rocks on the surface (Liu B et al., 2015). A reflection-free zone beneath the Zhangbaling uplift (Figure 12 b) which correlates with the high velocities characterized by FWI suggests that magma intrusion may have accompanied extensions in strike-slip shearing of the TLFZ. At the depth of the _M_ 4.7 mainshock, the Jurassic strata show significant impedance contrast with strong seismic reflections. Combined with the depth distribution of the accurately located small earthquakes (Figure 4), it is inferred that the Jurassic strata may accommodate the seismogenic faults for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake.

The fault F16 cuts through the uplift, and velocity also varies across this fault. There is small variation in the P-wave velocity across the fault F1, and the P-wave velocity on the northwest side of the fault is slightly higher than that on the southeast side (Figure 12 a). The deep seismic reflection imaging reveals that the fault F1 intersects fault F2 at ∼16.5 km in depth, and the NE-dipping fault F2 intersects fault F15 in the lower crust. The fault F15 which is a main branch of the TLFZ cuts vertically into the lower crust and extends into the top of the upper mantle. The faults F2 and F15 in the profile exhibit a semi-flower-like shape, which together control the block structures along the eastern margin of the Hefei basin (Li HP et al., 2023).

5. Discussion
-------------

### 5.1. The spatiotemporal evolution of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence

Since February 2024, six _M_ 3.0 earthquakes have occurred in the source region of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake. Since September 10, there is a noticeable increase in both the frequency and magnitude of the earthquakes in this region (Figure 5), and the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence can be regarded as a typical foreshock-mainshock-aftershock sequence. Based on the occurrences of the _M_≥3.0 earthquakes and the spatial distributions of smaller events in the sequence, the spatiotemporal distributions of the seismic sequence in different stages from September 14, 2024 to October 10, 2024 are presented, which is located by the double difference method using data from the dense nodal array (Figure 13).

After the mainshock, the aftershocks migrated in both the NE-SW and S-E directions. After September 22, the aftershocks primarily extended along the NE-SW direction. On September 25, an _M_ 3.8 aftershock occurred at the NE end of the ruptured zone, which however, did not continue to further rupture northeastwards. On October 1, another _M_ 3.3 aftershock occurred again at the NE end of the ruptured zone, which still did not extend the faulted zone. It is suspected that the rupture behavior is controlled by fault asperity, i.e., the northeastward rupture encountered a weak zone with lower velocities and stopped prematurely. Alternatively, the fault strike changed abruptly, thereby restraining both the magnitude of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock and the distribution of aftershocks (Li HP et al., 2023; Xu SQ et al., 2023; Li JX et al., 2023).

### 5.2. Analysis of the seismogenic structures

Based on the location result using the double-difference method with data from the permanent network, the earthquake sequence mainly exhibits a NE-trending distribution. The earthquakes projected on the cross-sections perpendicular to the sequence strike reveal a subvertical, NW-dipping seismogenic fault (Figure 3). The dense nodal array deployed in the source region with lower _M_ C recorded many more small earthquakes, which exhibit a more pronounced NE linear distribution extending for about 5 km, at an angle of about 10° clockwise from the dominant fault strike of the TLFZ in this region (Figure 4). The focal mechanism solutions for the six _M_≥3.0 earthquakes occurring in the region in 2024 are also consistent (Figures 3, Table 1). By combining the distribution of the accurately located sequence and the focal mechanism solutions, the seismogenic structure can be characterized as a NE-striking, NW-dipping subvertical fault. Based on the results shown in Figure 3, Figure 4, Figure 11, Figure 12, it can be preliminarily determined that the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence occurred along the Zhuding-Shimenshan fault F1 of the TLFZ, or along a smaller hidden strike-slip fault to the east. It should be noted that deep strike-slip faults with small vertical throws are often difficult to identify with active-source reflection imaging (Figure 12 b).

The _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake occurred at the junction of the NE-trending TLFZ and the WNW-trending Feizhong fault. The intersected fault zones, in conjunction with the large velocity contrast (Figure 9) can result in localized stress concentration (Talwani, 1988; Rastogi et al., 2014), which can lead to formation of new faults or triggering of preexisting faults responsible for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong sequence. The high-resolution P-wave velocity structures characterized by FWI and deep reflection imaging, as well as the S-wave velocity structure by passive-source tomography are consistent, which all clearly delineate the major faults of the TLFZ in this region. The _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake occurred at the boundary between high and low velocity bodies, and the Zhuding-Shimenshan fault F1 or an adjacent hidden strike-slip fault to the east which dip NW with a growing dip angle with depth are located in the Feidong depression on the eastern side of the Hefei basin. As revealed by the deep seismic reflection imaging, the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake likely ruptured in the Jurassic strata that are cut through by the fault F1 or the adjacent hidden strike-slip fault. The strata show highly reflective characteristics in the seismic reflection imaging (Figure 12 b). The Feidong segment of the TLFZ is characterized by a shallow, multi-branched, flower-like structure with deep-rooted faults. The dating results suggest that the Zhuding-Shimenshan fault F1 was active from the late Pleistocene to the middle Pleistocene, and became inactive in the Holocene. The fault F1 also does not cut through the Feidong depression (Zheng YP et al., 2020).

Several felt earthquakes in the Feidong earthquake sequence occurred in the evening. Thus, we have made a preliminary attempt to analyze the characteristics of the foreshocks of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong mainshock and strong aftershocks, as well as the potential influence by the periodic variations in the Earth tides on these _M_≥3.0 earthquakes. It is hoped that those studies might provide some insights for the earthquake occurrence trend. However, since the duration of observations is relatively short, the analyses have uncertainties. Therefore, we only present the related discussions in the Supplementary information for interested readers.

6. Conclusions
--------------

The _M_ 4.7 earthquake that occurred at the Feidong County of Hefei, Anhui Province on September 18, 2024 ranks the largest earthquake in the modern history of Hefei and caused substantial social impact. This paper presents a rapid report by a team which has long been dedicated to the study of the TLFZ in this region. The team not only carried active- and passive-source seismic profiling near the mainshock epicenter in 2015 and 2020, respectively, but also deployed nine nodal stations prior to the _M_ 4.7 mainshock, and 40 additional nodal stations immediately after its occurrence. The field acquisitions provide invaluable data for analyzing the detailed rupture process and seismogenic structures for the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence. The preliminary understandings for the earthquake sequence are summarized as follows:
*   (1)The results from the double-difference location indicate that the earthquake sequence is distributed linearly in the NE direction, spanning about 5 km, at an angle of ∼10° clockwise from the main orientation of the TLFZ in this region. The focal depths of the earthquake sequence are concentrated around 10.5 km. Immediately after the _M_ 4.7 mainshock, the aftershocks not only migrated bilaterally along the NE-trending major fault, but also in the SE direction along branch faults. Later, the aftershocks mainly occurred along the NE-SW direction, with two strong aftershocks (_M_≥ 3.0) occurring on the NE side of the ruptured zone. Since the aftershocks are well confined in a certain extent, it is inferred that the rupture may have reached the boundary of a fault asperity and was terminated prematurely by softer strata. Alternatively, the fault segment may have a change in the orientation and thus prevents further rupture extension. 
*   (2)The focal mechanisms of the six _M_≥3.0 earthquakes in the source region are rather consistent, which are right-lateral strike-slip ruptures on a subvertical, NE-trending, NW-dipping fault. The focal mechanisms indicate that the NEE-oriented compressional stress regime is the main tectonic driver for the seismic activities in this region. The geometry of the rupture plane of the _M_ 4.7 mainshock is also consistent with the distribution of aftershocks. The SAR imaging shows that the _M_ 4.7 mainshock only caused a trivial 1 mm horizontal coseismic displacement, which should be attributed to the great depth of the rupture area. 
*   (3)The regional velocity model reveals significant lateral variations in _v_ S in the source region, and the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake occurred in an area with relatively high velocities. Also, the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake struck at the junction of the NE-trending TLFZ and the WNW-trending Feizhong fault. The intersection of these faults and the large velocity contrast in the area likely create locally concentrated stress regime, which can explain the occurrence of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake sequence. The high-resolution P-wave velocity structures, deep seismic reflection imaging, together with the S-wave velocity structures reveal that the rupture plane of the _M_ 4.7 earthquake is most likely the segment of the Jurassic strata that is cut through by the Zhuding-Shimenshan (Sangjianza-Guanghanqiao) fault or a hidden strike-slip fault to the east. 
*   (4)The Zhuding-Shimenshan fault has not shown activities since the Holocene, and the fault does not cut through the Feidong depression. In addition, the mainshock occurred at a relatively great depth, thus the probability of earthquakes that rupture to the surface in the future is low.

*   ### Determination of rupture directivity of the 2024 Feidong M4.7 earthquake using one single near-source station

2025, Earthquake Science  Show abstract Determining the rupture directivity for small earthquakes is challenging due to the small source dimension and limited resolution of far-field observations. In recent years, the burst of near-source observations provides great opportunities to study earthquake rupture. Here we present the rapid determination of the rupture directivity for the 2024 Feidong _M_ 4.7 earthquake using only one strong motion station located 4 km from the epicenter. We find that the polarization of S waves evolves during the rupture, indicating an azimuth change during the rupture propagation. Through comparing the data with the synthetic waveforms, we infer that the _M_ 4.7 event propagated dominantly to the southwest on the dextral Tanlu fault. Our inference is further validated through comparing the data with records at local stations for _M_ 3 earthquakes in the Feidong sequence. Our study highlights the superior application of near-source observations in earthquake source studies.
