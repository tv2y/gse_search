Introduction
------------

Due to environmental pollution, greenhouse effect (carbon emission issues), energy crisis, and other issues, countries worldwide are promoting energy transformation and upgrading [[1], [2], [3]]. With the rapid development of transportation electrification and renewable energy power generation, a large number of safety accidents caused by batteries have also occurred worldwide, including consumer electronics, electric vehicles, airplanes, and energy storage power stations [4,5]. At present, it is generally believed that the causes of battery failure or thermal runaway mainly include thermal abuse [6,7], mechanical abuse [8,9], and electrical abuse [[10], [11], [12]]. Therefore, there has been a lot of research in these areas [13]. But in fact, many batteries were not abused before the accident. Some accidents even happen when the battery is not in use (some electric vehicles spontaneously combusted and exploded when it was parking) [4,14]. What is even more frightening is that some batteries have no obvious abnormalities before the sudden thermal runaway occurs. In other words, the external characteristics (voltage, temperature, state of charge, capacity, etc.) of these batteries before the accident were normal. This phenomenon is commonly known as the “self-induced sudden-death-type” thermal runaway (SSTR) or sudden spontaneous combustion (SSC) of lithium-ion cells [15]. These accidents are likely to be caused by defects in the battery design or production process.

In the mass production of batteries, the entraining of foreign matter is a thorny problem [16,17]. Almost every process in the battery manufacturing process has the risk of foreign matter mixing. For example, there may be foreign matter in the battery raw material itself; the stainless steel particles on the surface of the production equipment are easily mixed during the preparation, storage, and transmission of electrode slurry; various foreign matters entraining due to insufficient dust control in the workshop; the welding slag is introduced when welding components such as the cell tab, connector, and housing [18,19]. In recent years, many companies have recalled batteries and electric vehicles due to safety issues such as battery fires caused by foreign matter. For example, in August 2020, the Ford Motor Group recalled Kuga PHEV vehicles due to the battery cell contamination issue [20]. In October 2020, the WM Motor recalled 1282 electric vehicles due to impurities that were mixed into the battery during the production process [21]. In the same month, the BMW company recalled multiple PHEV models due to some tiny welding slag that may remain in the cell housing [22]. But from the existing announcements and public accident investigation reports, the specific details available are limited.

As the battery manufacturing innovation is mainly concentrated in enterprises, there is little public literature related to them due to factors such as confidentiality [23,24]. The research on the impact of foreign matter on battery performance and safety is even rarer. Mohanty et al. [16] found that metal particle contaminants cause abnormal capacity degradation by implanting metal particles in coin cells in the laboratory. Liu et al. [25] discovered the self-discharge phenomenon by introducing metal particles in a 1 Ah small pouch cell in the laboratory. Since the size of metal particles is on the order of micrometers, it is difficult to implant a single metal particle in a cell. Therefore, these two studies sprinkled an unknown number of metal particles in a miniature cell. But in the actual battery production process, a defect cell generally only has one metal particle. Moreover, the amount of energy, structure, heat dissipation conditions, force condition, and other conditions of mass-produced large-size traction cells are very different from those of laboratory miniature cells [26]. At present, the impact of foreign matter defects on the safety and durability of traction cells is unclear, especially the formation mechanism of SSC caused by foreign matter.

The existing methods for triggering battery thermal runaway are mainly through various abuses, such as squeezing (indentation) [[27], [28], [29], [30]], nail penetration [31], over-charge [32], over-discharge [33], and heating [34]. The reality is that some batteries fail and catch fire without abuse. Therefore, it is necessary to study the self-induced thermal runaway problem of the battery under normal use. Although some researchers trigger the thermal runaway by implanting low-melting metal, paraffin, or memory alloy inside the battery, it is impossible for these substances to appear inside the actual battery [[35], [36], [37]]. Since the thickness of the separator in the cells is generally only about 15 μm, the foreign matter defect is theoretically likely to cause an internal short circuit (ISC) and SSC under normal use in the later period. However, we did not find systematic and detailed research reports in this regard.

Electric vehicles and energy storage power stations are generally equipped with battery management systems to ensure battery safety [38,39]. In terms of early warning of battery thermal runaway, the current warning methods are mainly based on the voltage difference, temperature difference, state of charge (SOC) difference, abnormal self-discharge, and abnormal capacity decrease [[40], [41], [42], [43]]. These methods are mainly used to deal with “chronic” thermal runaway or ISC, but there are still some shortcomings for the SSTR or SSC that actually appear on the market [15]. Therefore, it is necessary to further study the fault characteristics of SSC and develop corresponding early warning methods.

Around the problems mentioned above, we made the lithium-ion cells with the foreign matter defect that fit the actual situation in the factory. The battery damage situation caused by the foreign matter of different particle sizes during the battery production process is revealed. Through the non-abuse aging cycle test, we reproduced the SSC and the self-induced ISC fusing of lithium-ion cells. Then through the analysis of the test data, we propose the early warning method for SSC. This is of great significance to improve the safety of batteries and battery manufacturing.

Summary
-------

To our knowledge, this is the first work to achieve the reproduction of SSC and spontaneous ISC fusing of lithium-ion cells. Different from the existing battery thermal runaway caused by abuses such as cell crush, nail penetration, improper charging, external short circuit, water immersion, external heating, etc., we reproduced the battery SSC under normal use.
