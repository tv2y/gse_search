Dystopian stories, when done right, have massive appeal. Some big-name books, movies, and television shows of the last ten years have been based in an imagined dystopia. 

**How to Write a Good Dystopian Story**  
To write a good dystopian story, you need an engaging character and a compelling conflict. Main characters are often products of their world but strive for something better. They learn to stand up to the powers that be, whether directly or indirectly.  

Hope, love, or justice should be a through-line, even if the world is dark. The best dystopias seem like utopias at first, hiding a dark underbelly.  

**Dystopia Story Examples**  
Some well-known dystopian works:  
- *Brave New World* by Aldous Huxley  
- *The Hunger Games* by Suzanne Collins  
- *Nineteen Eighty-Four* by George Orwell  
- *Fahrenheit 451* by Ray Bradbury  
- *The Handmaid's Tale* by Margaret Atwood  

**Dystopian Writing Prompts**  
1. At 16, everyone gets a brain meter measuring productivity—failure means death.  
2. Police robots turn violent, forcing characters to flee.  
3. Humans power cities with body heat, but the system starts killing them.  
4. Constant surveillance demands forced smiles—dissenters get reprogrammed.  
5. A happiness treatment has dangerous side effects.  
6. Social media becomes the only interaction, warping society.  
7. Underground bunkers hold survivors, but some want to leave.  
8. Post-apocalyptic repopulation forces pairings regardless of attraction.  
9. Seers predict the future—until their visions turn impossible.  
10. Brain uploads to cyberspace strain the economy.  
11. A woman hides an illegal pet in a world where animals are food.  
12. Thought-sharing splits society into uploaders and non-uploaders.  
13. Two warring cults ruin the world until one person tries to heal the rift.  
14. Immortality degrades minds; a risky treatment emerges.  
15. Military tech turns soldiers into irreversible weapons.  
16. Clean water scarcity creates a hierarchical society.  
17. Rising seas and droughts spark food wars.  
18. Wealth inequality erases the middle class.  
19. A scientist invents clean air tech, but the powerful suppress it.  
20. Earthquakes force survivors onto floating platforms.  
21. A couple searches for each other after an apocalypse.  
22. Superpowered humans enslave the rest—until rebellion begins.  
23. A brain virus leads to executions, but some doubt the cure.  
24. Clones gain rights—and start demanding them early.  
25. Virtual reality addiction fuels crime.  
26. A U.S. civil war turns the Midwest dystopian.  
27. Tightly controlled travel forces a character to cross states illegally.  
28. Brain downloads disrupt meritocracy.  
29. Pollution blots out the sun for a century.  
30. Giant monsters disrupt an uneasy truce with humans.  

Position your dystopian novel for success by understanding your audience and market.
