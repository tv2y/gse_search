**MILWAUKEE —**

Nine cases of measles have been confirmed in Wisconsin, marking the state's first outbreak of the infectious virus in 2025.

The cases were identified in Oconto County, north of Green Bay, where all nine patients from the same family were exposed to measles while traveling out of state.

The Wisconsin Department of Health Services is responding to the outbreak as Milwaukee officials emphasize the importance of vaccination. Currently, 81% of Wisconsin residents have received the measles vaccine, with Milwaukee County slightly higher at 83%.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommends a 93-95% vaccination rate for effective herd immunity.

Milwaukee's health department has purchased 800 vaccine doses for those needing immunization. Mayor Johnson stressed the urgency of protecting children from the potentially fatal disease.

"In the U.S., there have been three confirmed deaths to measles, which is extremely heartbreaking," Johnson said. "As a parent myself, I cannot imagine losing my child to a vaccine-preventable disease."

Symptoms include high fever, runny nose, watery eyes, cough, and a rash that typically develops three to five days after initial symptoms appear.

Infected individuals must quarantine for 21 days.

Recent state health department data shows school vaccination rates declined slightly last year, with 86% of students meeting immunization requirements.

The decrease is attributed to a new meningitis vaccine requirement for grades 7 through 12.

The CDC reports that kindergarten vaccination exemptions are increasing nationwide, with nearly 4% of U.S. kindergartners having exemptions for required vaccinations last school year.
