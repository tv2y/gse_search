**Rare Gas Leak Causes Mass Evacuation in Beijing CBD**  

**May 15, 2025, 8:30 AM**  
**Bus Station Near China World Trade Center, Beijing CBD**  

A sudden gas leak at a bustling bus station in Beijing's Central Business District (CBD) triggered a large-scale emergency response early Thursday morning, forcing the evacuation of over 100 people and temporarily disrupting traffic in the area.  

The incident occurred at approximately 8:15 AM near the China World Trade Center, where commuters reported a strong, pungent odor resembling sulfur. Witnesses described seeing a faint yellowish haze emanating from a damaged underground pipeline adjacent to the bus stop. Authorities later confirmed the leak as hydrogen sulfide, a hazardous gas detectable even at low concentrations.  

Beijing Fire Department and emergency medical teams arrived within minutes, cordoning off a 200-meter radius and evacuating pedestrians, shop workers, and nearby office employees. Six individuals complaining of dizziness and nausea were transported to Peking Union Medical College Hospital for treatment, though none were in critical condition.  

Initial investigations by the Beijing Municipal Commission of Urban Management pointed to construction work as the likely cause. "A third-party contractor inadvertently breached a gas pipeline while excavating for fiber-optic cable installation," stated spokesperson Li Wei. The pipeline, operated by Beijing Gas Group, was swiftly shut down, containing the leak by 9:00 AM.  

CBD traffic resumed normal operations by midday, though the bus station remained closed for safety inspections. Officials assured the public that air quality monitoring showed no lingering contamination. An internal review of construction protocols has been launched to prevent future incidents.
