Creating a time-lapse effect in writing involves summarizing events over a period while maintaining a vivid, "showing" rather than "telling" approach. Here are key techniques:  

### **1. Montage Technique**  
Chain sensory details and actions in a single paragraph to imply time passing:  
*"They hammered nails, painted the door, argued about the color, smoked in silence until the sky went from bruised purple to the pale smudge of morning."*  

### **2. Repeating Motifs**  
Use recurring objects or details to signal progression:  
*"The sapling grew taller than the fence; its first red leaf browned and dropped."*  

### **3. Consequential Changes**  
Highlight outcomes rather than processes:  
*"Her shortlist of regrets now had a clean line through his name."*  

### **4. Parallelism & Lists**  
Use structured phrasing to convey evolution:  
*"Letters arrived: short, urgent, forgiving. Replies were rare, then frequent, then none."*  

### **5. Bridging Lines**  
Transition smoothly with a single line:  
*"Weeks of rain later, the river had claimed the path."*  

### **6. Ephemera Integration**  
Inject artifacts like notes or headlines:  
*"June 12 — invoice paid. June 29 — landlord’s notice."*  

### **Key Principles**  
- **Focus on action** (e.g., arriving characters collect tickets, not just "they went to the station").  
- **Avoid overuse**; balance time-lapses with full scenes.  
- **Use strong verbs** (*gnawed, ripened, mended*) to imply duration.  

Example template:  
*"They painted, argued, made compromises; by winter the house had a name."*
