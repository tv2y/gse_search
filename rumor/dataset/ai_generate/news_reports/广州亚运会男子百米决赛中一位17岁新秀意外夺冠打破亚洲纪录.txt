**17-Year-Old Phenom Stuns Asia, Shatters Record in Guangzhou Asian Games Men’s 100m Final**  

**October 3, 2023, 8:15 PM**  
**Guangdong Olympic Stadium, Guangzhou, China**  

In a breathtaking turn of events, 17-year-old Chinese sprinter Li Weiming delivered a historic performance tonight, claiming gold in the men’s 100-meter final at the Guangzhou Asian Games and breaking the Asian record with a blistering time of 9.83 seconds. The previously unheralded teenager outpaced heavy favorites, including reigning champion Tadao Suzuki of Japan and Qatar’s seasoned sprinter Hassan Al-Fardan, sending the crowd of 60,000 into a frenzy.  

Li, a native of Shandong Province and a recent graduate of Guangzhou Sports Academy, entered the race as a wildcard after surprising victories in preliminary heats. However, few expected him to challenge the Asian record of 9.90 seconds, set by Zhang Peimeng in 2013. From the starter’s gun, Li exploded off the blocks, maintaining flawless form to cross the line ahead of Suzuki (9.89s) and Al-Fardan (9.91s).  

The victory marks China’s first gold in the men’s 100m at the Asian Games since 2010 and establishes Li as the youngest-ever champion in the event’s history. "I dreamed of this moment, but never imagined it would come so soon," Li said post-race, clutching the national flag. "This is for my coaches, my family, and everyone who believed in me."  

Officials confirmed the wind speed of +1.2 m/s was within legal limits, validating the record. The performance has sparked immediate speculation about Li’s potential at the 2024 Paris Olympics. Meanwhile, Suzuki graciously acknowledged Li’s talent, stating, "The future of Asian sprinting is in great hands."  

The medal ceremony concluded with Li standing atop the podium as China’s national anthem played, capping a night that will be remembered as the dawn of a new sprinting era.
