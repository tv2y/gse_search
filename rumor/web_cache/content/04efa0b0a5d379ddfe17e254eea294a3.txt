Fire at office building in northern China kills 26
==================================================

The fire at a coal mining company in northern Shanxi province also injured at least 38 people, Chinese state media reported.

Firefighters tackling a blaze at the Yongju Coal Company in Lvliang, China, on Thursday.Xinhua / Shutterstock

*    

Nov. 17, 2023, 9:01 AM UTC / Source: The Associated Press

By The Associated Press

BEIJING — A fire erupted in a coal mining company building in a northern Chinese city on Thursday, killing 26 people and injuring at least 38, Chinese state media reported.

Rescue personnel evacuated dozens of people from the building, according to state media.

The building belongs to Yongju Coal Company and is in the city of Lvliang in northern Shanxi province, a major coal-producing region.

Videos circulating on social media, some of which were shown on state broadcaster CCTV, showed flames and heavy plumes of smoke coming out of a four-story concrete building. Some people were shown escaping the building by climbing down drain pipes.

Those dead were almost all workers, according to local media outlet Fengmian News.

The fire was first reported around 6:50 a.m. (5:50 p.m. Wednesday ET) and had been put out by mid-afternoon, according to the local emergency management department.

President Xi Jinping called for an investigation into “hidden risks” in key industries, CCTV reported.

Authorities have detained several people believed to be responsible for the fire, and an investigation is ongoing, state media said.

The fire appeared to have started in the shower area of a building with offices and dormitories, local news outlet Hongxing News reported.

Industrial accidents, in particular in coal mines, are relatively common in China, though the government has been working on improving safety. Shanxi is China’s top coal-producing province and is at the center of government efforts to reduce the economy’s reliance on coal.

In April,29 people died in a hospital fire in Beijing, setting off an online backlash against authorities, who were accused of withholding information from patients’ relatives.

In June,an explosion at a barbecue restaurant in the northwestern city of Yinchuan killed 31.
