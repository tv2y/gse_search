Want a lightning fast way to create stories?  

A writing exercise introduced by Jericca Cleland involves creating a short story in five sentences, based on an open improvisation format (TheatreSports, created by Keith Johnstone).  

**How it works:**  
1. **Sentence 1:** Introduce two characters and a setting.  
2. **Sentence 2:** Introduce what they are doing.  
3. **Sentence 3:** Introduce a problem.  
4. **Sentence 4:** Make it worse.  
5. **Sentence 5:** Create a solution and a resolution.  

This method helps:  
- Centralize the escalation of conflict.  
- Emphasize pacing.  
- Hit key moments for a simple story.  
- Serve as a starting point to expand into a bigger story.  

**Example 1:**  
1. Victor the vigilante is stuck on a raft in the ocean with Corina the criminal.  
2. They argue about what happens when rescued—Victor wants to turn her in; Corina wants a second chance.  
3. A massive storm approaches.  
4. The raft capsizes, tossing them into the ocean.  
5. Victor is rescued by Corina, proving her change of heart.  

**Example 2:**  
1. An old lady befriends a young woman at a countryside mansion party.  
2. The young woman hates the host.  
3. The host is murdered.  
4. The young woman is found with the murder weapon.  
5. The old lady discovers the host faked his death for insurance fraud.  

Try it yourself to generate new story ideas.
