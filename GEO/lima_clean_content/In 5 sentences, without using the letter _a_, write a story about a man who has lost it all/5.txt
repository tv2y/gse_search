### Facts and Case Summary: *Gideon v. Wainwright* 372 U.S. 335 (1963)  

#### **Facts:**  
Clarence Earl Gideon was charged with breaking and entering with intent to commit a misdemeanor, a felony under Florida law. At trial, Gideon, unable to afford an attorney, requested court-appointed counsel. The judge denied his request because Florida law only permitted counsel for capital offenses. Gideon represented himself but was convicted and sentenced to five years in prison. He appealed, arguing his constitutional right to counsel was violated. The Florida Supreme Court denied relief, prompting Gideon to file a handwritten petition to the U.S. Supreme Court.  

#### **Issue:**  
Whether the Sixth Amendment’s right to counsel applies to state court defendants (*Betts v. Brady* precedent had previously limited this right).  

#### **Ruling:**  
Unanimously reversed and remanded. The Court overruled *Betts v. Brady*, holding the Sixth Amendment’s right to counsel is fundamental and applies to states via the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause.  

#### **Reasoning:**  
Justice Black emphasized that a fair trial in an adversary system requires counsel for indigent defendants. The decision ensured equality before the law, stating that denying counsel to the poor undermines justice.  

#### **Significance:**  
The ruling established the principle that all criminal defendants, regardless of ability to pay, must be provided legal representation in felony cases.
