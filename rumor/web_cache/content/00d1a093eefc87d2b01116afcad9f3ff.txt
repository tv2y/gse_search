CrisisWatch is our global conflict tracker, an early warning tool designed to help prevent deadly violence. It keeps decision-makers up-to-date with developments in over 70 conflicts and crises every month, identifying trends and alerting them to risks of escalation and opportunities to advance peace. In addition, CrisisWatch monitors over 50 situations (“standby monitoring”) to offer timely information if developments indicate a drift toward violence or instability. Entries dating back to 2003 provide easily searchable conflict histories. Building on our global conflict tracker, On the Horizon sounds the alarm about conflicts and crises that may emerge or escalate over the next three to six months in support of global conflict prevention efforts.

Our monthly conflict tracker highlights **five****conflict risks**in January:

*   The U.S. escalated its campaign to oust **Venezuela**’s Nicolás Maduro, seizing oil tankers, claiming an attack on an unspecified port facility and eventually capturing the president and his wife in January, leaving the rest of the regime intact and the path to a political transition unclear.

*   In **Yemen**, the United Arab Emirates-backed Southern Transitional Council launched a lightning offensive in the east before Saudi Arabia-backed forces regained control, highlighting deep fractures within government ranks and the proxy confrontation between Riyadh and Abu Dhabi that could portend greater instability ahead.

*   Rwanda-backed M23 rebels in the Democratic Republic of Congo captured the strategic town of Uvira, which sits on the border with **Burundi**, bringing the country within close range of attack and heightening the risk that fighting could spill over the border.

*   **Uganda**’s general elections on 15 January risk sparking violent clashes between opposition supporters and security forces, as authorities adopted an increasingly hardline stance toward dissent in the run-up to the vote.

*   Following tense general elections on 28 December, the government in the **Central African Republic**may respond to mounting contestation with a violent clampdown on the opposition.

CrisisWatch identified **eleven deteriorated situations**in December. Notably:

*   The Islamic State staged the deadliest attack in years on U.S. forces in **Syria**, while government troops and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces clashed heavily and a sectarian bombing sparked unrest in the coastal region.

*   Worsening economic conditions triggered protests in **Iran**’s capital Tehran, which spread to other cities and broadened beyond economic grievances. Clashes between security forces and demonstrators left several people dead in December.

*   As **Benin** geared up for an election year, a coup attempt targeted President Patrice Talon, prompting authorities to arrest several opposition figures over alleged involvement.

*   Tensions surged in the**Taiwan Strait**as China staged its largest-ever military exercises around Taiwan shortly after the U.S. announced a major arms sale to Taipei.

*   In **Bangladesh**, the killing of a prominent youth leader triggered mob violence and a wave of anti-India anger.

Aside from the scores of conflict situations we regularly assess, we tracked significant developments in: **Bolivia**, **Bosnia and Herzegovina**, **China-U.S.**, **Guinea-Bissau**, **Honduras**, **Nile Waters**, **South Africa** and **Tanzania**.

### Benin

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Deteriorated Situation December 2025

**A coup attempt targeted President Talon as the country gears up for an election year, prompting authorities to arrest several opposition figures over alleged involvement.**

_Govt foiled coup attempt with support from regional and international allies_. Group of soldiers 7 Dec attacked residences of President Talon and several senior military officials in main city, Cotonou, and claimed on national TV to have removed Talon from power; mutineers cited multiple grievances, including “deteriorating security situation” in north and exclusion of opposition candidates from upcoming elections – legislative and municipal elections are scheduled for 11 Jan, followed by presidential vote on 12 April. Nigeria same day provided military air support to help dislodge mutineers from Togbin military camp in Cotonou, and Talon later announced on national TV that coup had failed; France also confirmed having provided logistical and intelligence support in thwarting coup. Authorities soon announced arrest of 14 coup plotters, but not coup leader – head of National Guard’s special forces Lt. Col. Pascal Tigri. West African regional bloc ECOWAS 7 Dec condemned coup attempt and announced deployment “with immediate effect” of its Standby Force, comprising troops from Nigeria, Côte d’Ivoire, Sierra Leone and Ghana, to “preserve constitutional order” and “territorial integrity” of Benin.

_Authorities arrested political figures over accusations of complicity with putschists_. Govt 8 Dec announced release of two senior military officers who had been abducted during coup attempt, said coup plotters had taken them to Tchaourou town (Borgou department), which is birthplace of leader of main opposition party Les Démocrates, former President Thomas Boni Yayi. Meanwhile, unconfirmed reports circulated online alleging that Pascal Tigri may be related to Les Démocrates’ VP Alassani Tigri. In following days, govt arrested several figures associated with opposition over allegations of involvement in coup attempt, including former defence minister and opposition leader Candide Azannaï, Boni Yayi’s son, Chabi Yayi, and former member of parliament Sounon Boke Soumaila.

_Insecurity persisted in northern departments_. Govt continued to sharply restrict flow of information on insecurity in north, but small-scale attacks appear to be continuing in Alibori and Atakora departments, as well as in Borgou department’s Kalale commune near Nigerian border; link to identified jihadist activity remains unclear.

### Burkina Faso

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Unchanged Situation December 2025

**Authorities reacted strongly to the unauthorised landing of a Nigerian military aircraft in the country; jihadist violence persisted and the government reinstated the death penalty.**

_Ouagadougou accused Abuja of airspace violation, secured formal apology._ Nigerian Air Force C-130 en route to Portugal 8 Dec made emergency landing in Bobo-Dioulasso town, Hauts-Bassins region in south west. Burkinabè authorities detained 11 Nigerian Air Force personnel who had been onboard plane and launched investigation into incident, alleging that aircraft had not received overflight clearance and thus violated Burkina Faso’s airspace. Alliance of Sahel States (AES) same day decried landing as “unfriendly act” and member states (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger) placed their air forces on alert, stating readiness to neutralise any unauthorised aircraft. Nigeria’s FM Yusuf Maitama Tuggar 17 Dec met Burkinabè President Capt. Ibrahim Traoré in capital Ouagadougou to negotiate release of detainees, delivered official apology for “unfortunate incident”; detainees were subsequently released. Incident came after Nigeria 7 Dec launched airstrikes in Benin to thwart coup attempt, and later deployed troops to country as part of West African regional bloc ECOWAS Standby Force (see Benin, Nigeria).

_Jihadist violence continued notably in northern regions._ Al-Qaeda-affiliated Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) maintained high operational tempo and vast area of operations. JNIM fighters in country’s north and east in Dec received reinforcements from Mali, and potentially Niger, while group also intensified recruitment efforts in these areas. JNIM 8 Dec attacked military position near Kongoussi city in Centre-North region, reportedly destroying mine-resistant ambush protected vehicle and seizing small arms and ammunition. JNIM 12 Dec claimed to have seized “militia” position near Pensa town, also Centre-North. Fighting also continued between rival jihadist groups. Notably, Islamic State Sahel Province (IS-Sahel) claimed killing nine JNIM fighters on or around 28 Dec near Dori town, Sahel region.

_In other important developments._ Govt 4 Dec adopted penal code reforms reinstating death penalty for acts of terrorism, espionage and high treason, which could be used to further restrict civic space. AES member state leaders 23 Dec announced creation of joint military battalion with mandate centred on counter-terrorism and border security (see Mali, Niger).

### Burundi

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Deteriorated Situation December 2025 Conflict Risk Alert December 2025

**The military deployment in neighbouring DR Congo (DRC) suffered a major setback as Rwanda-backed M23 rebels captured the border town of Uvira; the advance brings Burundi within close range of attack, heightening the risk that fighting could spill over the border in the coming weeks.**

_Fall of Uvira put further pressure on Gitega._ In DRC’s South Kivu province, Rwandan-backed M23 rebels 10 Dec captured Uvira town, just 25 kilometres from Burundi’s largest city Bujumbura, following months-long standoff with Congolese and Burundian troops. Amid fighting and fears of spillover, govt 9 Dec closed border with DRC at two points. Gitega in following days started evacuating troops stranded in South Kivu, while M23 rebels 13 Dec said they had captured “several hundred” Burundian troops during advance. Rebels 16 Dec announced withdrawal from Uvira, but further clashes were reported late Dec in area (see DR Congo). Fall of Uvira dealt heavy blow to Congolese and Burundian militaries and may embolden M23 and Rwandan troops deployed in eastern DRC, as well as Burundian rebel groups in South Kivu – most notably RED-Tabara, to stage attacks across border into Burundi. Govt continued to blame Rwanda for deteriorating security situation. FM Bizimana 8 Dec said Kigali broke ceasefire agreements, while President Ndayishimiye 19 Dec again accused Rwanda of preparing attacks against Burundi. UN 19 Dec said 84,000 had fled South Kivu into Burundi since early Dec; influx worsened humanitarian situation amid lack of shelter, food and water.

_Divisions within ruling CNDD-FDD widened ahead of 2027 presidential election_. Influential ruling party CNDD-FDD Sec-Gen Révérien Ndikuriyo late Nov embarked on political tour of country, potentially preparing to challenge Ndayishimiye in 2027 presidential election. M23 crisis could help Ndikuriyo’s attempt to form alliance with National Assembly president, Gélase Ndabirabe, and Senate leader, Gervais Ndirakobuca, gain momentum. Under pressure, Ndayishimiye’s allies tried to consolidate support for president. Notably, Defence Minister Marie Chantal Nijimbere 6 Dec met with local leaders in Buhumuza province.

_Forced disappearances continued_. Intelligence services 9 Dec allegedly abducted women in Bujumbura city, reportedly torturing her before releasing her following day; 14 Dec abducted teacher in Gitega province, whose whereabouts remained unknown.

### Cameroon

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025 Conflict Risk Alert September 2025

Deteriorated Situation October 2025 Conflict Risk Alert October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Unchanged Situation December 2025

**Political infighting within the ruling elite hindered cabinet formation, while the opposition continued to contest President Biya’s re-election from abroad; insecurity persisted in the Anglophone areas and in the Far North.**

_Long wait for new govt continued_. Following his re-election in Oct, President Biya is yet to reshuffle cabinet. Competing lists of presumptive ministers have circulated on social media since his swearing-in on 6 Nov, reflecting factional manoeuvring within ruling elite. Drawn-out process also underscores delicate balancing act of forming new govt, as some heavyweights demand rewards for manoeuvring for Biya’s re-election, while he ponders sanctions for less performing ministers.

_Opposition continued to call for civil disobedience against Biya._ From Gambia, Issa Bakary Tchiroma continued to claim victory in presidential election, while his spokesperson Alice Nkom 14 Dec urged Cameroonians to prevent Biya from exercising power. From France, group of pro-Tchiroma lawyers 15 Dec announced plans to pursue legal action against Biya and wider ruling camp over alleged electoral fraud and deadly post-election violence. Continued repression of dissent and exile of opposition figures largely suppressed post-election unrest but drew international scrutiny. Notably, opposition figure and Tchiroma supporter, Anicet Ekane, 1 Dec died in detention, drawing condemnation from civil society, while Canadian embassy and EU delegation urged independent investigation. Reports 19 Dec revealed another Tchiroma supporter died in custody few days after being detained in late Oct.

_Separatist conflict continued in Anglophone regions_. Anglophone separatist combatants in Dec killed several soldiers in North West region, including in erstwhile relatively calmer Nkambe city (Mantungia division). Locals in North West reported multiple instances of kidnappings by separatist rebels, and 5 Dec accused govt forces of killing five civilians in Bafut town. Anglophone conflict resurfaced on govt agenda, with Supreme Court 18 Dec opening appeal case for ten imprisoned Anglophone leaders – including self-proclaimed Ambazonia leader Sisiku Julius Ayuk Tabe – sentenced in 2019 to life imprisonment on terrorism and secession charges by military tribunal.

_Jihadist violence continued in Far North_. Soldiers 17 Dec repelled Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP) attack on military camp in Ngouma village, Logone-et-Chari division. Suspected jihadists 1-9 Dec abducted around a dozen civilians in Mayo-Tsanaga division.

### Central African Republic

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Unchanged Situation December 2025 Conflict Risk Alert December 2025

**The 28 December elections took place in a tense atmosphere, with opposition candidates accusing authorities of obstruction and electoral fraud; the government may respond to mounting post-election contestation with violent repression.**

_Opposition alleged campaign obstruction, irregularities on voting day_. Presidential, legislative, regional and long-delayed municipal elections 28 Dec proceeded mostly peacefully, despite some polling stations experiencing delayed openings. Electoral body 5 Jan announced incumbent President Touadéra re-elected for third term with 76.15% of vote; his main opposition challengers, Anicet-Georges Dologuélé of Union for Central African Renewal and Henri-Marie Dondra of Republican Unity, alleged widespread irregularities and fraud, including ballot box stuffing, and called for results to be annulled (await January edition of CrisisWatch for more). Earlier, Touadéra’s challengers reported restrictions to their campaign activities. Dologuélé 23 Dec accused authorities of banning rally in Bouar town (Nana-Mambéré prefecture), while Dondra said security forces prevented him from travelling during campaign. Main opposition coalition, Republican Bloc for the Defence of the Constitution, boycotted polls over lack of level playing field.

_Ethnic Azandé militia continued to drive insecurity in south east_. In Haut-Mbomou prefecture, ethnic Azandé Ani Kpi Gbé (AAKG) militia prevented elections from taking place in Bambouti and other localities; suspected militiamen 28 Dec also kidnapped electoral body official and sub-prefect of Bambouti. Earlier in month in Haut-Mbomou, suspected AAKG members 21 Dec reportedly killed three Fulani herders near Zémio town; predominantly Fulani armed group Union for Peace in the Central African Republic (UPC) 1 Dec burned dozens of houses belonging to ethnic Azandé civilians in Mboki town, injuring at least seven, in retaliation for AAKG’s late-Nov killing of UPC element near Mboki. Russian paramilitaries, along with former UPC fighters recruited as auxiliaries, 2 Dec intensified patrols in Mboki.

_Military and allies continued operations against armed groups in north east_. Army and Russian paramilitaries 2 Dec launched operation against Popular Front for the Renaissance of the Central African Republic (FPRC) rebels near Aouk river in Vakaga prefecture (north east), killing at least seven, and 25 Dec carried out further operations against various rebel groups in same area; authorities claimed dozens of rebels killed but local sources accused Russian paramilitaries of killing at least 24 civilians.

### Chad

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Deteriorated Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Unchanged Situation December 2025

**A drone strike killed Chadian soldiers near the Sudanese border, and President Déby expanded state intelligence services into the digital sphere, further restricting avenues for political mobilisation.**

_Drone strike killed two Chadian soldiers near Sudan’s border._ Drone strike 26 Dec killed two Chadian soldiers and wounded another at military camp in Tine town (Wadi Fira province) near Sudanese border. Authorities placed air force base in eastern city of Abéché on high alert, and deployed ground reinforcements to Tine to secure border, saying drone came from Sudan. Army chief of staff 27 Dec condemned “unjustified aggression” and affirmed Chad’s “right to respond” to any further violation of national territory, as Sudanese army and paramilitary Rapid Support Forces accused each other of responsibility. Similar incidents could threaten precarious calm along border going forward.

_Control of trading routes in north fuelled ethnic tensions._ Govt’s efforts to turn Amdjarass city (Ennedi-Est province), stronghold of President Mahamat Déby’s family, into commercial and trade centre in north sparked tensions in wider Borkou-Ennedi-Tibesti (BET) region. UN and NGOs 18 Dec jointly called for “concrete actions” by international community to “press the Israeli authorities to lift all impediments” to humanitarian operations or “risk the collapse of the humanitarian response”; dozens of NGOs face de-registration by Israel, which could further undermine already limited provision of aid. UK, France, Canada and seven other states 30 Dec described humanitarian conditions as “catastrophic” and urged Israel to lift “unreasonable restrictions”. Meanwhile, amid ongoing diplomatic activity to advance to second phase of ceasefire, next steps remained mired in uncertainty,including establishment and mandate of International Stabilisation Force stipulated in UN Security Council Resolution 2803. At meeting with Israeli PM Netanyahu on 30 Dec, U.S. President Trump urged Hamas to disarm within two months.

_In West Bank, Israel accelerated settlement plans._ Israeli cabinet 11 Dec approved legalisation for 19 settler outposts amid reports govt intends to revive plans to build 9,000 settlement housing units for ultra-Orthodox neighbourhood in occupied East Jerusalem; since start of 2025, Israel has approved some 30,000 housing units in settlements across West Bank, a record number. Israeli police 8 Dec raided UNRWA’s compound in East Jerusalem. Settler attacks continued: notably, settlers 3 Dec abducted and tortured 18-year-old from Khirbet Bani Harith village.

**Israel continued frequent cross-border attacks as the end-of-2025 deadline to disarm Hizbollah passed, fuelling uncertainty over the next steps.**

_Israel continued attacks amid mounting pressure to disarm Hizbollah._ Israel continued to conduct regular military operations via drone and airstrikes in Lebanon. Notably, Israel 8 Dec launched wave of airstrikes across southern Lebanon, 14 Dec killed three alleged Hizbollah operatives, 18 Dec wounded four Electricity of Lebanon workers in Nabatieh Governorate and 25 Dec killed alleged commander of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps close to Syrian border. After govt in Sept confirmed end of 2025 deadline for army to disarm Hizbollah south of Litani River, and will reportedly confirm full disarmament there imminently, army in Jan may face increased pressure to show evidence of overseeing Hizbollah’s disarmament in areas where group refuses to cooperate, namely north of Litani River; doing so could lead to dangerous stand-off with Hizbollah, while failure to make progress could invite renewed Israeli escalation.

_Govt and Israel appointed civilian representatives to ceasefire body._ Lebanon’s and Israel’s civilian appointees 3 Dec participated for first time in platform created to oversee Nov 2024 ceasefire’s implementation known as “monitoring mechanism”, which hitherto comprised only military officials from Lebanon, Israel, the U.S., France as well as UN representatives. Despite U.S. encouragement, Beirut had resisted move in line with its policy of not negotiating directly with Israel. PM Nawaf Salam stressed that appointment does not constitute step toward normalisation with Israel, while Hizbollah chief Naim Qassem denounced decision as “free concession” to Israel. U.S. Ambassador to Lebanon Michael Issa warned start of civilian talks did not mean Israel would halt its attacks.

_Syrian refugees continued to return to Syria, clashes erupted along border._ UN 4 Dec reported that more than 378,000 Syrian refugees had returned from Lebanon to Syria during 2025, majority of their own initiative and some via UN-organised return convoys that began in July. Underscoring insecurity along Lebanon-Syria border, Lebanese security forces 14 Dec sought to interdict smugglers along frontier in northern Beqaa Valley, triggering exchange of fire.

### Libya

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Unchanged Situation December 2025

**The UN mission launched a new consultation process to help foster consensus and pave the way to elections, while the army chief of staff died in a plane crash in Türkiye, and protests erupted against the Tripoli-based government.**

_UNSMIL launched new phase of its mediation efforts._ UN Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) 14-15 Dec hosted inaugural session of so-called “Structured Dialogue”in capital Tripoli, bringing together some 120 representatives of various institutions. Consultation process aims to generate common ground on key governance, security, economic and human rights issues to help create conditions for credible elections. Holding of nationwide dialogue inside country for first time in many years reflects generally improved relations between rival centres of power, but few Libyans believe it will make any dent in status quo. Meanwhile, Libyan political bodies promoted alternative political plans, stirring up confusion on path ahead. Notably, Tripoli-based PM Dabaiba 16 Dec called for swift holding of presidential election; eastern-based House of Representatives Speaker Aguila Saleh made similar call following late-Nov demonstrations in five eastern cities demanding presidential election. Protesters in western city of Misrata 20 Dec urged UN mission to speed up talks and deliver elections.

_Army chief of staff killed in plane crash in Türkiye._ Plane crash in Türkiye 23 Dec killed army chief of staff, Mohammed Haddad, alongside four other military officials and three crew members. Haddad had been visiting Türkiye on occasion of Turkish parliament’s 22 Dec vote to extend mandate of Turkish military forces in Libya by 24 months until early 2028. Accident caused shock within military and political circles in country’s west and raised concern about its potential impact on cohesion of military establishment and stability of Tripolitania region.

_Anti-Dabaiba protests erupted in western Libya._ Anti-Govt of National Unity (GNU) demonstrations from 24 Dec onward took place in several western cities, including Tripoli and Misrata; protesters accused Dabaiba’s govt of responsibility for deteriorating living conditions, widespread corruption, and foreign interference, and demanded transparent investigation into crash of Haddad’s plane. Shootout with protesters 2 8 Dec reportedly killed security officer in Ain Zara area near Tripoli.

**Riyadh opted for a military response to a lightning offensive by United Arab Emirates (UAE)-backed forces in Yemen, highlighting the proxy confrontation with Abu Dhabi.**

UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council (STC) 3 Dec launched offensive in Yemen’s east on border with Saudi Arabia, securing major territorial gains from govt forces aligned with Riyadh. Given dramatic shift in balance of power, the kingdom faced stark choice between accepting compromise that would potentially increase STC’s leverage or military confrontation to re-establish its national security red lines, which risks prolonged conflict in east that could offer Houthis, extremist groups and UAE-backed factions the opportunity to advance their respective agendas. In dramatic escalation, Saudi Arabia 30 Dec launched airstrike on UAE military shipment in Mukalla port and warned “the steps taken by the UAE are considered highly dangerous” as well as reiterated demand of Riyadh-backed governing body Presidential Leadership Council’s (PLC) for UAE forces to withdraw. UAE same day announced exit of UAE forces from country. Saudi Arabia also deployed its naval forces in Arabian Sea to “carry out inspections and anti-smuggling operations”. Saudi Arabia-UAE crisis comes amid mounting tensions over divergent positions on Sudan and issues affecting Horn of Africa and wider Middle East (see Yemen).

### Syria

December 2025

Unchanged Situation August 2025

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Improved Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

Deteriorated Situation December 2025

**The Islamic State (ISIS) staged the deadliest attack in years on U.S. forces in Syria, government troops and the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) clashed and a sectarian bombing sparked unrest in the coastal region.**

_Deadly ISIS attack exposed challenges within new security forces._ ISIS gunman 13 Dec attacked U.S.-Syrian patrol near Palmyra city, killing three U.S. personnel and wounding two more as well as two members of Syrian security forces; U.S. Central Command attributed attack to “lone ISIS gunman”, while Syrian officials confirmed assailant belonged to Syrian security services. Ambush from within Syria’s own ranks underscores one of state’s critical vulnerabilities and marks first fatal attack on U.S. forces in Syria since Assad’s fall and deadliest since Jan 2019. In retaliation, U.S. 19 Dec launched large-scale airstrikes against dozens of ISIS targets in east. ISIS-linked suicide bomber 31 Dec killed police officer in Aleppo city.

_Heavy clashes broke out between govt forces and SDF._ Ahead of end-of-year deadline for breakthrough, Damascus mid-Dec shared proposal reportedly outlining SDF bloc’s integration into three army divisions. Gaps remain, however, on implementation and whether SDF divisions will remain deployed in north east. Underscoring tensions, both sides reportedly redeployed forces and 15 Dec clashed near Tishreen Dam in eastern Aleppo province. In further escalation, after Turkish FM Hakan Fidan 22 Dec stated SDF had no intention of integrating, heavy clashes between govt forces and SDF same day erupted in Aleppo city, killing two civilians before sides same day agreed truce.

_Deadly sectarian attack sparked unrest on coast._ Islamist militant group Saraya Ansar al-Sunna 26 Dec detonated IEDs in Alawite mosque in Homs city, killing eight and wounding 22. Alawite leader in Latakia city 28 Dec called for protests demanding federalism, improved security and release of detainees, prompting protests across coastal and central Syria. In Latakia city, clashes involving govt forces, pro-govt groups and armed protesters killed three civilians and one security officer. Damascus deployed troops and imposed curfew.

_U.S. Congress repealed major Assad-era sanctions law._ In historic step toward enabling Syria’s recovery, U.S. 18 Dec repealed Caesar Act; removal is conditioned on 180-day reviews over four years tied to progress on eight points, including counter-terrorism and protection of minorities.

### Tunisia

Unchanged Situation September 2025

Unchanged Situation October 2025

Unchanged Situation November 2025

**The judicial crackdown on dissent continued as authorities enforced prison sentences against prominent opposition figures in the so-called conspiracy case, while street protests and other forms of political contestation persisted.**

_Authorities arrested and sentenced prominent opposition figures_. Police early Dec enforced prison sentences handed late Nov to prominent opposition figures in appeal trial of so-called “conspiracy case”. Notably, police 2 Dec arrested politician and human rights lawyer Ayachi Hammami and 4 Dec detained historical opposition figure Ahmed Najib Chebbi. Criminal chamber of Tunis Court of First Instance 12 Dec sentenced opposition figure Abir Moussi, who already serves prison terms, to 12 years imprisonment for staging protest outside presidential palace in Oct 2023.

_Political mobilisation and scattered street protests continued._ Most recent wave of judicial crackdown dealt another blow to what remains of opposition, with most members either imprisoned or forced into exile. However, representatives from across political spectrum early Dec gathered at headquarters of Nejib Chebbi’s party (centre left) in capital Tunis, including An-Nahda (Islamist-inspired), Abir Moussi’s party (nationalists), Ettakatol (centre right), trade unions and human rights organisations, signalling potential attempt to bring opposition forces together against President Saïed.Youths protesting law enforcement brutality 14 Dec clashed with police in Kairouan town (centre), resulting in several arrests; demonstration was triggered by young man’s death from injuries allegedly sustained at hands of security forces. Tunisian General Labour Union (UGTT) announced nationwide strike on 21 Jan to protest restrictions on freedoms and demand wage negotiations.

_Controversy grew over security cooperation agreement with Algeria._ Cooperation agreement signed in Oct with Algeria sparked controversy, with critics alleging deal compromises Tunisia’s autonomy on security matters. Television channel close to An-Nahda claimed text authorises Algeria to intervene militarily in Tunisia up to 50km from border, to deploy army in event of riots or coup attempt, and prevents Tunisia from concluding military cooperation agreements without Algiers’ approval. Saïed 18 Dec dismissed reports as “fake”.

**The Polisario Front independence movement signalled its readiness to resume negotiations with Morocco, but prospects for talks remain uncertain.**

**The Southern Transitional Council launched a lightning offensive in the east before Saudi Arabia-backed forces regained control, highlighting deep fractures within government ranks and the proxy confrontation between Riyadh and Abu Dhabi.**

_Separatists seized territory in east, triggering military action by Riyadh._ United Arab Emirates-backed (UAE) Southern Transitional Council (STC) 3 Dec launched offensive into Wadi Hadramawt region, prompting collapse of bases held by Saudi Arabia-aligned govt forces and upending years-old status quo. STC next day delivered another blow on Hadramawt’s oil plateau by striking Riyadh-aligned forces, allowing STC to seize key oil facilities, before consolidating control during Dec over neighbouring al-Mahra governorate on Omani border. Reports 4 Dec indicated Riyadh launched airstrikes on STC forces. Governing body Presidential Leadership Council (PLC) chair Rashad al-Alimi issued statement from Riyadh condemning STC’s unilateral actions; group, however, intensified secessionist rhetoric, portraying its advance as part of broader efforts to restore independent south. UAE same day announced exit of its forces from country. Head of Saudi-supported Nation’s Shield Forces 2 Jan launched military operation backed by Saudi airstrikes that rapidly reversed STC’s gains in eastern governorates; Riyadh subsequently called for dialogue conference with STC and southern actors, which STC welcomed. Episode exposed deep fractures within PLC and underscored sharp rivalry within Saudi-UAE coalition (await next month’s edition for more).

_Houthis and govt commenced prisoner exchange talks._ Houthi and govt representatives 12 Dec began ninth round of prisoner and detainee exchange negotiations in Oman’s capital Muscat in attempt to revive “all-for-all” prisoner exchange under 2018 Stockholm Agreement.
