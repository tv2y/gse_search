Two people up to the present(2022) have been known to have fallen, or leaped, from the 86th floor open-air observatory of the Empire State Building, and survived. The first was Elvita Adams, who intentionally leaped from the 86th floor, on December 2nd, 1979, but was blown back onto a 2.5 foot(.76 meter) ledge on the 85th floor by winds about 23 to 38 miles per hour. She suffered a broken hip…but was pulled to safety through a window…and survived! The second such incident occurred April 25th,2013, Nathaniel Fimone(or possibly Simone) did the same thing, leaping from the 86th floor…and he too landed on the 85th floor ledge, breaking his foot, but he too was pulled through an opened window to safety! So far, 38 people have attempted suicide at the Empire State Building…and 36 have succeeded…so only 5.3% fail…so your odds are not good to actually survive the attempt!

Only in the South Tower. The North Tower got struck at 8:46; the South Tower was struck 16 minutes later at 9:02. Because of this gap, approximately 83% of people at or above impact (the impact zone was the 78th-85th floors) in the South Tower survived. The overwhelming majority of these people evacuated in that 8:46–9:02 period; about 2,900 people evacuated these floors during those 16 minutes, leaving about 600 left. Some companies on these floors, such as CSC Corporation on the 87th floor, were able to evacuate all of their employees.

Of those still on these levels at 9:02, 18 survived; however, they were all located within the impact zone floors (on 78, 81 and 84). Although one stairwell remained passable throughout the entire tower top to bottom, no one who was above the 84th floor when the South Tower was hit survived. Therefore, by the strictest definition (above, not within, the impact zone, so 86th floor or up when the South Tower was hit) no one survived.

The reason that one stairwell remained passable, and thus some people within the impact zone survived, is that the South Tower was hit on an angle, with the impact concentrated on the eastern half of the building, and thus the westernmost stairwell was located in a relatively intact area and was narrowly spared.

One survivor who was on the 84th floor when the South Tower was hit did go up to 91 before going back down and evacuated around 9:56, just 2–3 minutes before the South Tower fell. This man is the only person who was above the impact zone at any time after the South Tower was hit who survived, although he originated from within the impact zone.

If you were in the North Tower from the 92nd floor up, you were a goner, 0% chance of survival. The plane struck the tower directly in the center from floors 93–99 and destroyed any path of escape on the 92nd floor. The highest floor of survival in the North Tower was the 91st floor.

One window was broken by the plane’s impact on the 91st floor, so by the broadest possible definition, there were 18 survivors (the 91st floor survivors) from the North Tower impact zone. However, in spite of this broken window, major fires related to impact only occurred from 92 up and structural damage to the tower from impact started on 93. Therefore, the 91st floor is virtually never considered to be in the impact zone.

Since the beginning of its construction in 1887, it is estimated that about 370 people have died there. Between suicides, stupid accidents, testings of planes and parachutes and failed bets, the strangest deaths related to the Eiffel Tower have been recorded.

For example:

1891 - A mechanic hung himself for reasons unknown and was found naked in the wee hours and hanging in the north pillar. A testament was found, all of his worldly possessions were bequeathed to Gustave Eiffel. There was only his clothes neatly folded by his hanging spot.

1912 - The Bird man’s fall to his death while testing a flying/gliding/landing/parasailing contraption. The autopsy showed that he died of a heart attack before he even touched the ground.

1926 - An airplane crash: Léon Collot, 32, a reserve lieutenant at the Orly camp, flew his Breguet 19 between the west and north pillars of the Eiffel Tower. As has already been said, a “base-jumping” rig will help (but not ensure) you live rather than a round canopy, but please bear in mind:

Base-Jumping from Public or Private buildings is in most cases illegal. When/ if you hit the ground safely you’d better have a get-away driver ready.

IF your parachute opens “on-track” (I.E. in the direction you want to go) and not right back into the building your main problem is over but others just starting:-

You’ll now need to know the wind direction, where your LZ is going to be, that your path to it is snag-free and clear of obstacles like street lighting, comms or power-lines, trees….OTHER buildings. Free of up/down draughts, crosswinds (a down-town environment with high and low-rise buildings can create all sorts of strange wind-conditions and vortexes. Track back into and hit a building with a para-foil type of canopy and you have a world of trouble, pain and injury/death approaching.

It all seems very simple as a concept, but better left to those trained for it.
