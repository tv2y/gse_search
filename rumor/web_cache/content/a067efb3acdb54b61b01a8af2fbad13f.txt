Reports of Beyoncé and Jay Z attempting to buy rights to Confederate flag dismissed
===================================================================================

The pair were allegedly willing to pay $280 million to block the controversial emblem's future use

By Luke Morgan Britton

9th July 2015

Recent reports that Beyonce and Jay-Z were set to purchase the rights to the controversial Confederate flag have been dismissed.

Earlier this week, numerous news sources claimed that the couple were willing to pay $280 million (£180 million) to block the emblem’s future use.

Advertisement

The flag was used by the Confederate States of America during the US Civil War and has long been seen as a patriotic sign for the south of the country. It has also been accused of bearing racist connotations due to the Confederate States’ history of slavery.

Website NewsWatch33 were the first to report that Jay Z and Beyoncé were considering the acquisition. The source cited the couple’s supposed lawyer Ralph Hammerstein as saying: “If my clients are successful, purchasing the rights would mean anyone who wants to produce merchandise using the Confederate flag would have to get permission from Mr and Mrs Carter”.

However, Gawker have now reported that Hammerstein does not actually exist and that the story was fictional.

Gawker note: “Jay Z and Beyoncé couldn’t legally make it their flag even if they wanted to. The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office prohibits trademarking a flag or simulation of a flag, and allows the use of flags that have been stylized, obscured, or otherwise significantly changed. Even if someone could trademark the flag itself, it wouldn’t prevent variations from being used on ugly t-shirts.”

During his ‘Yeezus’ tour in 2013, Kanye West attempted to subvert the Confederate symbol, using it on a series of t-shirts and other merchandise. See images below.

West’s reappropriation of the Confederate flag was criticised by fellow rapper Q-Tip.

Advertisement

Expressed his anger on Twitter, Q-Tip wrote: “Fuck the confederate flag. I love Kanye no shots but my people continue to suffer psychologically from hardships that happened under that banner.”

In defence of his merchandise and use of the image, Kanye spoke to radio station 97.1 AMP explaining he was trying to reclaim the symbol. He said: “React how you want. Like I said, any energy you got is good energy.

“You know, the Confederate flag represented slavery, in a way – that’s my abstract take on what I know about it, right? So I made the song ‘New Slaves.’ So I took the Confederate flag and made it my flag. It’s my flag now! Now what are you gonna do?”
