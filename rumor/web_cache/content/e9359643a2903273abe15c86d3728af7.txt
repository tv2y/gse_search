### @lsmmoore

10 years ago

This kind of technology could be potentially useful for some nonverbal autistics as well, because some who can type, like Ido Kedar, have mentioned frustration in their impairment in motor areas. Ido Kedar specifically mentioned how he could not reliably point to objects he was supposed to point to and mentally "crossed his fingers" that his finger would not go the wrong way and point to the wrong object (a flash card used by ABA therapists, in this case). Severe autistic challenges are known to include deficits in motor skills, which I suspect could be helped by such a device much the way a fully paralyzed person is. Caveat: I myself have not ever been to the Judge Rotenberg Center, but as an autistic (Aspie) myself, I suspect that at least some, if not all, JRC survivors would be reluctant to adopt this technology as they might associate the neuroprosthetic with a GED shock device. I know that PTSD can cause people to be afraid of things even slightly related to the thing that caused the PTSD in the first place.
