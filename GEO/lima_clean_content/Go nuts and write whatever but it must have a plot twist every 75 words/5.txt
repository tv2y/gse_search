Plot twists are a storytelling technique that introduces a change in the direction or expected outcome of a plot, often re-contextualizing the entire experience. They keep audiences engaged, make stories rewatchable, and are most effective when they feel earned rather than forced.  

### **How to Write a Plot Twist**  
1. **Plan Ahead** – Twists should be carefully planted and paid off throughout the story.  
2. **Write Backward** – Start with the twist and work backward to ensure logical setup.  
3. **Use Plants & Payoffs** – Foreshadowing is key (e.g., *The Sixth Sense* hinting at Bruce Willis’s character being dead).  

### **Examples of Great Plot Twists**  
- **Movies:**  
  - *The Sixth Sense* – "I see dead people."  
  - *The Empire Strikes Back* – Darth Vader is Luke’s father.  
  - *Chinatown* – The horrifying reveal about Noah Cross.  
- **TV Shows:**  
  - *Lost* – Flashbacks were actually flash-forwards.  
  - *Game of Thrones* – The Red Wedding and other shocking twists.  
  - *Mad Men* – Peggy’s unexpected pregnancy.  

### **Keys to a Successful Twist**  
- Must be surprising yet logical.  
- Should deepen the story’s themes.  
- Requires careful setup and payoff.  

Twists work best when they feel inevitable in hindsight, rewarding the audience’s attention rather than cheating them.
