TVs referred to as “LED TVs” are all LCD TVs that use LED backlighting.

TVs referred to as miniLED TVs are LCD TVs that still use LED backlighting - but the LED light bulbs are much smaller, providing more precise control of the light passing through the pixels.

A QLED TV is an LCD TV with LED backlights with a layer of Quantum Dots to improve color. The main companies that make QLED TVs are Samsung and TCL.

An OLED TV is a TV that uses Organic LEDs which are self-emitting (no blacklight required). The color is excellent, but they are not as bright as other types of TVs and are susceptible to screen burn-in if the static images are displayed for long periods of time). Also, all brands of OLED TVs use screen panels made by LG Display Company - but add their own video processing enhancements. TL;DR LED is just a better display. They reliably beat LCD in nearly every metric. They have a better HDR, better motion, and better contrast than any LCD and should be a choice of most people barring some specialised case (and money of course)

A true LED TV (LED panel that don’t actually rely on any liquid crystal substrate) is OLED and it has three advantages over a traditional LCD (whether IPS, VA or TN)

*   It can produce a perfect black, so when displaying content in the dark, your content will appear to be floating in a dark room instead of having a big bar at the top and bottom
*   It can display a perfect HDR. HDR differs from HDR in that it tries to imitate the real world lightning condition that your eyes can see, so a dark scene will get a lot darker and specular highlights (explosive, sun etc.) get a lot brighter. With OLED, you will be able to see all the details even when the scene gets very dark due to HDR better and the bright highlight get nearly as bright as a high-end LCD. A high-end LCD with FALD (Full Array Local Dimming) like Samsung QLED can display some detail in the dark too but they will suffer from a blooming effect where a lighter colour object in the dark scene will appear glowing for a few seconds before disappearing, this is especially an issue when displaying something with a subtitle.

*   And lastly, an LED display has a spontaneous response time. It can display motion so fast that they don’t even need to show the response time in milliseconds like most LCD. This means if you are using your TV as a PC monitor or gaming on it, you will have a much better time as text and objects remain crisp no matter how fast or what colours the background of your webpage is. With LCD, especially a VA like Samsung QLED, you are more likely to get a slower response time which led a ghosting issue in game, and smearing or similar ghosting issue when browsing webs in certain conditions. I have a QLED display for my PC and whenever I scrolled on a dark background like in Dark Mode, everything dark image becomes like jelly and melt all over when I scrolled and back to crisp when I stop which can be pretty annoying. IPS can solve much of this issue but is still not as good as OLED

OLED is not perfect of course, by its nature, OLED bare a risk of burn-in which may render your display unusable in a matter of months in the edge case where you make extremely heavy use of it. It can’t also get as bright as LCD so HDR specular highlight will be less impressive. It will also has an issue if you place it in a room where it faces the sun directly.

In the end, though, LED is just a better display in general. Every display has flaws and LED flaws are a lot harder to spot than LCD, which makes the difference between a cheap and an expensive display. A good display don’t wow you, it just won’t annoy you when using for some extended period of time.

*There are new types of LCD like Mini and Micro LCD that promised most of the advantages of OLED and LCD. So far some of the above-mentioned issue (like blooming) persisted but has been greatly reduced. However, some like the slower response time has not been fixed at all, it has been worse than the older generation VA like QLED in fact.

Confusingly an LCD set and an LED set both use the same LCD technology to display a picture by passing or blocking light coming from a backlight source.

The difference is that the LED TV uses LED lighting for the backlighting source for the LCD while the LCD TV uses the somewhat older electroluminescent cold cathode lighting to backlight the LCD.

The LED is probably considered a little better technology right now because it is efficient and reliable. And can be brightness modulated in specific areas dynamically to increase apparent dynamic range. But still a little more expensive than plain LCD sets.

All this is not to be confused with OLED sets which use LEDs as actual pixel light emitters. Still very high tech and expensive and not mainstream.

Why should you care? The better sets will be LED and will cost a little more so it can help you spend your money for a more bargain set or a slightly better TV.

TVs referred to as miniLED TVs are LCD TVs that still use LED backlighting - but the LED light bulbs are much smaller, providing more precise control of the light passing through the pixels.

A QLED TV is an LCD TV that uses LED backlighting combined with Quantum Dots (Q) to improve help improve color performance. QLED TVs have an LED layer in the back, and an LCD layer in the front (screen surface). The Quantum Dot layer is in between.

A Neo QLED TV is a QLED TV that uses mini-LED backlighting.

An OLED TV is a TV that uses Organic LEDs which are self-emitting (no blacklight or LCD display panel required). The color is excellent, and they can display absolute black. but they are not as bright as other types of TVs and are susceptible to screen burn-in if the static images are displayed for long periods of time).
