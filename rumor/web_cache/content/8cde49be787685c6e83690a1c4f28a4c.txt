Especially at State Street, Macy’s displays a knack for decimating everything that used to make the shopping experience unique and fun. But, in truth, the start of the decline actually goes back to 1982. That’s the year Marshall Field & Co. was purchased by BATUS, the British tobacco conglomerate. By that time, several State Street department stores were struggling or had closed, due in large part to the ill-conceived State Street transit mall.

Today's Selfridges exemplifies what the State Street store _might_ have been like had it been more capably managed. As for “The Magic of Macy’s,” the only magic trick Macy’s seems to know is how to make customers vanish.
