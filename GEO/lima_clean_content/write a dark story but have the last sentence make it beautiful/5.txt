### **The Dark Moment Story in Fiction**  
The "Story Equation" (SEQ) begins by asking the character: **"Who are you?"** This question reveals two key elements: **Identity** and the **Dark Moment Story**. From there, writers can extract a character's greatest fear, flaws, wounds, motivations, and desires.  

#### **What is a Dark Moment Story?**  
- It is a **specific, pivotal event** from the character's past that shapes their identity.  
- It provides motivation and depth, influencing their actions and decisions.  
- Example: In *Braveheart*, William Wallace’s dark moment is the deaths of his family and wife.  

#### **Three Essential Elements of a Dark Moment Story:**  
1. **Specific Event** – Not just a general trauma (e.g., "his parents divorced") but a detailed memory (e.g., "the day his father left without him").  
2. **Relatable** – Doesn’t have to be extreme; small, poignant moments (e.g., a birthday no one attended) can be powerful.  
3. **Emotionally Poignant** – The writer must feel it deeply; the character should recount it in first person with full emotional weight.  

#### **Purpose of the Dark Moment Story:**  
- Helps develop character arcs and motivations.  
- Strengthens reader-character emotional bonds.  
- Often shared in the second act to reinforce the character’s journey.  

#### **Example: Owen Christiansen’s Dark Moment** (from the author’s novel):  
- Identity: A prodigal fisherman.  
- Dark Moment: Lost his hockey career after an injury blinded him in one eye.  
- Secret Desire: To return home and rebuild his life.  

This method simplifies plot and character development by focusing on a single, formative event. Writers can stop here for organic storytelling or use the details to craft a full plot.
