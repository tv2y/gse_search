Title: The Best Shower Curtains

URL Source: https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/the-best-shower-curtain/

Published Time: 2017-04-05

Markdown Content:
Marki Williams/NYT Wirecutter

The humble shower curtain is an unexpectedly high-impact item when it comes to the look of your bathroom. It can tie together the colors of your tile and walls, lend some personality to the space or maybe even help you sell your house —as long as it’s also functional.

We spent 45 hours researching and testing 24 shower curtains, and we came away with five that we love, as well as some hooks and liners to go along with them.

### Everything we recommend

A spa classic in durable cotton-poly: Target Threshold Waffle Weave Shower Curtain. Target

The research
------------

How we picked and tested
------------------------

Marki Williams/NYT Wirecutter

We started our research by scouring user reviews, online discussions, and offerings from well-regarded companies. We mainly focused on standard-size shower curtains, which usually measure about 72 by 72 inches. Other sizes include long (72 by 84 inches), extra-long (72 by 96 inches or larger), and stall (54 by 78 inches). A standard tub-and-shower combo curtain measures around 72 by 60 inches.

We also spoke with experts at the New York City Department of Sanitation and FCC Environmental Services to learn about where discarded shower curtains end up (the landfill). And we interviewed a professional house cleaner to discuss best cleaning practices.

From there, we came up with a list of 60 shower curtains, which we then winnowed down to 24 curtains using the following considerations:

*   **Design:** We looked for a variety of patterns and colors that would work with a range of bathroom styles.
*   **Cost:** Most people aren’t budgeting big money for a shower curtain, so we wanted to provide options at a wide range of prices.
*   **Quality:** We considered shower curtains made of high-quality natural or synthetic fibers and, to a limited extent, vinyl. We steered clear of any shower curtains made of PVC because this type of vinyl off-gasses toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and phthalates. We were mindful of the negative environmental and health impacts from microplastics and toxic chemicals found in synthetic fabrics and certain types of vinyl.
*   **Care and maintenance:** We looked for low-maintenance shower curtains that were machine-washable or that could be gently scrubbed clean, and we steered clear of shower curtains that called for dry cleaning.
*   **Stock and availability:** We searched for shower curtains that seemed to be reliably available, and we eliminated several based on stock issues.

 Before hanging the curtains in the shower, we inspected the materials and construction.Dorie Chevlen/NYT Wirecutter

We then tested the curtains at our apartments. Here's what we evaluated:

*   **Materials and construction:** We spread each shower curtain out on the floor and examined it by hand, noting how the material felt, details of the texture and design, the evenness of the hook holes, and the overall construction. We measured each one, comparing it to the listed dimensions.
*   **Washability:** We then put each fabric shower curtain through the washer (cold water) and dryer (low heat, tumble-dry) twice, and we remeasured the dimensions to calculate shrinkage. We also paid attention to any wear and tear that happened in the laundry machines. For vinyl curtains, we scrubbed and wiped those according to the manufacturer instructions.
*   **Drying speed:** We poured water on each shower curtain and timed how long it took to dry. Even if you use a liner, shower curtains may still get damp, depending on how careful you are while showering or how steamy the room gets. How fast a curtain dries is important, especially if you live in humid climates or have a bathroom with poor air circulation.
*   **User-friendliness:** Lastly, we hung up each shower curtain in our small bathrooms (noting how easy or painful the hanging process was) and took a shower with each one, noting how it hung.

A classic waffle-weave shower curtain: Threshold Waffle Weave Shower Curtain
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Marki Williams/NYT Wirecutter

#### Top pick

### Why it's great

**It’s a classic.** The Threshold Waffle Weave Shower Curtain is a stylish, versatile curtain that provides a spa-like feel at an affordable price. It comes in nine colors, plenty of options for coordinating with the decor of most bathrooms.

**It’s well made.** The cotton-polyester material has a nice heft, and the curtain's even, strong stitching withstood repeated laundering in Claire's apartment building's very old (and far from gentle) washer and dryer.

**It’s easy to hang and take down.**This shower curtain has metal grommets, as opposed to unreinforced buttonholes; the grommets made the always-arduous process of hanging and taking down a curtain significantly easier.

**It’s easy to care for.** You can toss it in the washer as well as the dryer, though it will probably shrink somewhat.

Marki Williams/NYT Wirecutter

### Flaws but not dealbreakers

**It shrinks quite a bit.** After we put this shower curtain through two laundry cycles (cold-water wash, low-heat tumble dry), the width shrank by 2 inches and the length by 4 inches. These new dimensions were still more than adequate for our bathtub setup, but those lost inches may be a dealbreaker for you.

**It stays wet longer.** This curtain's fabric is thick, which means it can take longer to dry (just under six hours for us), compared with nonporous vinyl.

### Key specs:

*   **Materials:** 60% cotton, 40% polyester (white, teal, olive green, light green, dark gray, mauve); 30% cotton, 70% polyester (gray), 26% cotton, 74% polyester (khaki)
*   **Colors:** nine, including white, gray, teal, olive green, black, light green
*   **Care and maintenance:** machine-wash; tumble-dry
*   **Available sizes**: 72 by 72 inches, 72 by 84 inches (extra long)

Colorful vinyl
--------------

### Quiet Town Sun Shower

This heavyweight clear vinyl curtain comes in a wide array of colors. You can use it by itself or as a liner. It’s not machine-washable, though.

### Why it's great

**It comes in cheery colors.** This Quiet Town Sun Shower curtain offers a wide array of modern color options, from subtle peach to bright yellow (you can also get it in dual colors and grid print, but you have to pay more). If you want to first see the colors in person, you can get a swatch card for $5. This curtain can work well alone or as a liner with a fabric shower curtain.

**It’s made with heavy-duty, PVC-free materials.** The Sun Shower curtain is a sturdy, thick EVA (a safer, phthalate- and chlorine-free alternative to PVC vinyl). The material is also BPA-free and, according to Quiet Town, “non-chlorinated (zero off-gassing).” Indeed, we didn't smell anything when opening the package. The curtain has large brass grommets, which are easy to hook.

 Brass grommets and finished rounded corners allow this curtain to work well alone or as a liner.Marki Williams/NYT Wirecutter

### Flaws but not dealbreakers

**It’s kind of annoying to deep-clean.** Though you can likely get away with spot-cleaning the Quiet Town curtain most of the time, the manufacturer recommends regular deep cleaning to keep it free of mildew. However, you can’t just throw this curtain in the washing machine and call it a day. The suggested cleaning protocol involves taking down the curtain, laying it flat, spraying it with cleanser, gently scrubbing it with a soft brush, and rinsing it clean. If you’d rather machine-wash your shower curtain, this curtain may not be for you.

**It won’t hide your shower mess (or your body).** While testing the Sun Shower curtain, we quickly learned that we could no longer use our shower curtain to conceal our shower’s messy interior from guests, a surprising downside. It also won’t conceal your body while showering, relevant if you, for example, live with someone who tends to brush their teeth or wash their face at the same time you shower.

### Key specs:

*   **Materials:** 100% EVA
*   **Colors:**10 solid colors; seven grid patterns; three color-block patterns
*   **Care and maintenance:** Hand-wash only. Wipe the curtain clean with a gentle spray cleaner and gently scrub with a soft brush.
*   **Available sizes**: 72 by 72 inches, 72 by 84 inches (some colors)

**It's a classy look.** Preppy but not stuffy, and just the right amount of dainty, this curtain is a versatile piece that will fit different aesthetics and work with busier wallpapered bathrooms, too.

A work of art: Block Shop Textiles Diamond Shower Curtain
---------------------------------------------------------

Block Shop Textiles

#### Top pick

### Why it's great

**The attention to detail is truly stunning.** Los Angeles–based Block Shop Textiles works with fifth-generation master printers in Jaipur, India, who block-print the curtains, in a range of pattern options, by hand. We tested the (now sold-out) April Showers version, which was vibrant and stitched perfectly, with a thick medium-weight cotton shell and a lightweight cotton liner that felt sturdy and high-end.

**It repels water.** When we poured half a cup of water onto it, the water merely beaded up atop the tight, thick cotton, rather than immediately sinking into the fabric as with our other contenders.

**It's beautiful inside and out.** Unlike with most printed shower curtains, the inner lining of this curtain is also printed, so you can enjoy its aesthetics even while showering.

Water simply beaded on the cotton Block Shop Textiles shower curtain we tested (shown here in the now-discontinued April Showers print).Dorie Chevlen/NYT Wirecutter

Garden party: Dusen Dusen Tulip Shower Curtain

Dusen Dusen

#### Top pick

### Why it's great

**It's flirty**. Like our Sun Shower Curtain pick, the Dusen Dusen Tulip Shower Curtain is made from totally transparent EVA plastic, so you can see into and out of your shower. But the printed tulips somewhat conceal the effect. Imagine standing in a wildflower meadow, like a Brontë novel heroine, peeking at your lover across the expanse, yearning. That's the vibe!

**It's high-quality material.** Like the Sun Curtain, this one does not have any off-gassing smells and is made from PVC-free and BPA-free EVA (a type of plastic). It is the slightest bit thinner than the Sun Curtain, which makes it even more clear to see through; a liner would eliminate the problem of removing soap scum but also somewhat ruin the view.

### Flaws but not dealbreakers

**It requires more upkeep.** Like the transparent Sun Curtain, this curtain is prone to collecting soap scum and hard-water stains, so be ready to scrub it every few months. (Dusen Dusen recommends using a liner if you prefer minimal upkeep.)

Our favorite shower-curtain accessories
---------------------------------------

### A fabric-like liner: Maytex/Zenna Home Water Repellent Fabric Shower Curtain Liner

Amazon

#### Top pick

Shower curtain liners can extend the life of any shower curtain, and they’re especially useful if your curtain doesn’t dry quickly, or if you have a poorly ventilated bathroom. Of the four polyester liners we tested, the Maytex Water Repellent Fabric Shower Curtain Liner (also labeled Zenna Home) was the least expensive and most efficient; it can even work as a shower curtain on its own. It's inexpensive enough that you could replace it without breaking the bank, but it’s durable enough that you probably won’t have to. Unlike most plastic liners, it is machine-washable, making soap scum a slightly easier nuisance to handle. The liner also has weights in the bottom corners, which effectively hold the curtain flat inside your shower. We ordered the liner from various retailers and whether they're advertised as "Maytex" or "Zenna Home" they're all labeled with the 'Zenna Home' logo and look identical.

### High-quality minimalism: Quiet Town S-Hooks

Quiet Town

#### Top pick

Quiet Town, the maker of one of our curtain picks, collaborated with Seattle-based jewelry designer Cat McCadden to create these simple but elevated S-hooks, which some in either stainless steel or brass. Because each hook is a single piece of bent metal, it has no nooks or crannies for water to collect and cause rust, but the designer's lack of ball bearings means these hooks may not slide as easily on the rod. Although the open-ended design allows for easy on-and-off for your curtain, we didn't have any issues with our curtains popping off.

### Hooks that do double duty: Moen Shower Curtain Rings

Dorie Chevlen/NYT Wirecutter

#### Top pick

With Moen's Shower Curtain Rings, your curtain and liner each hang on their own hooks. Not only does this design avoid crowding, but it also allows you to easily remove the liner for cleaning without dismantling the shower curtain. The offset design causes the inner liner to hang slightly lower than the outer curtain, preventing any liner from peeking through the top. We've used our rings for four years without a rust issue (though we've spotted some complaints online), and the smooth ball bearings glide across the rod, no problem. These rings are available in four finishes (nickel, chrome, bronze, or black).

### Basic but elevated: Amazer Shower Curtain Hooks

Amazon

#### Top pick

The Amazer Shower Curtain Hooks are slightly larger than other pear-shaped, rolling shower curtain rings, so you have plenty of room to hang a curtain and liner without any crowding. Despite their low cost, these hooks were impressively durable and immune to warping. They come in more than a dozen colors and finishes.

The competition
---------------

This list doesn't include all the shower curtains we've tested and dismissed — just the ones that are still available for sale.

A previous upgrade pick, the Crate & Barrel Pebble Matelassé White Shower Curtain is an all-cotton shower curtain with a pebbled texture that can imbue any bathroom with an air of luxury — but its price reflects that. The Target Waffle Weave Shower Curtain provides a similar feel for a quarter of the price.

The Urban Outfitters Allover Fruits Patterned Shower Curtain arrived with a slight plasticky smell, and after washing and drying, it shrank 2 inches vertically and 4 inches horizontally and bled a goos amount of dye. What's more, the stitching on this curtain wasn't even, creating crooked edges. Some reviewers online also mentioned hook holes that aren't open. Still, it's not too expensive, it looks cute, and some Wirecutter staffers have used it for years without complaint.

The Geometric Watercolor Shower Curtain from artist Tina Carroll is a top seller on Etsy; although we liked its vibrant colors, we didn’t love the 100% polyester material. And returns are generally not accepted—at this price, that isn’t a small commitment.

The Jungalow Soleil Shower Curtain by Justina Blakeney is a cotton shower curtain that’s lined on the inside with polyester, but we found that water went straight through it. The cotton layer also shrank slightly more than the polyester in the laundry, so the liner was visible at the bottom.

We were intrigued by the Outlines Shower Liner System, a subscription-based system that aims to recycle used liners, rather than throwing them in the landfill. Outlines’ shower curtains have a top part made of cotton and a bottom part made of non-toxic PEVA that you can trade in as needed to be recycled. While we love that Outlines has figured out how to recycle PEVA, we think a disposable (albeit recyclable) option isn’t the answer. Maintaining the shower curtain or liner you have is the most sustainable thing you can do.

Society6 offers hundreds of design and pattern options, and the company works with independent artists. But we read too many negative reviews about uneven print quality and a confusing return process. Also, the one we tested—Soft Shapes by City Art—was not waterproof, and water from the shower sprayed straight through it, despite its being 100% polyester.

We liked the three shower curtains we tested from Urban Outfitters (the Camille Floral Shower Curtain, the Kiko Shower Curtain, and the Myla Floral Shower Curtain), but they constantly went in and out of stock.
