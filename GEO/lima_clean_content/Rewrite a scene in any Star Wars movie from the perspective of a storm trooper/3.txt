**Star Wars Episode IV: A New Hope (Scene Rewrite)**  
The following rewrite of *Star Wars: A New Hope* demonstrates how a small change in one scene can alter the feel and overall arc of a screenplay and ultimately the film itself. After 2005, George Lucas completed his saga with *Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith*, aligning the prequels with the original trilogy (Episodes IV–VI).  

This project assumes the films were released chronologically (I–VI). In Episodes I–III, we meet Anakin Skywalker, a childish and immature young man who turns to the dark side, allowing the Sith to reign. By *Episode IV*, the title reflects the new hope emerging: Luke Skywalker, Anakin’s son.  

The original scene shows Obi-Wan sacrificing himself to help the rebels and Luke escape Darth Vader. While this highlights Obi-Wan’s valor, it fails to show Luke’s immaturity, leaving room for doubt—suggesting the "new hope" might fail, mirroring Anakin’s fall. This theme is explored in later episodes but lacks setup in *A New Hope*.  

**Rewrite Purpose:**  
To strengthen the saga’s cohesion, this rewrite reimagines Obi-Wan’s death to underscore Luke’s flaws, creating tension about whether he can avoid Anakin’s fate.

**Impact:**  
The rewrite emphasizes Luke’s recklessness, adding depth to his character arc and aligning with the saga’s themes of legacy and redemption.
