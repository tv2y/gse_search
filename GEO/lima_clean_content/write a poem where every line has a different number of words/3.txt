The pantoum is a poetic form that relies on repetition of lines, with every line in the poem being reused. It consists of four-line stanzas without a set rhyme scheme or line length.  

### How a Pantoum Works  
- The first stanza introduces four original lines.  
- In subsequent stanzas:  
  - The **first line** repeats the **second line** of the previous stanza.  
  - The **third line** repeats the **fourth line** of the previous stanza.  
  - The **second and fourth lines** are new.  
- The **final stanza** recycles earlier lines:  
  - **Line 1:** Second line of the previous stanza.  
  - **Line 2:** Third line of the first stanza.  
  - **Line 3:** Fourth line of the previous stanza.  
  - **Line 4:** First line of the first stanza.  

### Example  
First stanza:  
> *Not since we moved in has it been so clean:*  
> *toys, books, chairs, beds, moved to the attic*  
> *till we see floors we barely knew were there.*  
> *The photographs make it look like a palace.*  

Second stanza:  
> *Toys, books, chairs, beds, moved to the attic—*  
> *it’s impossible to find a single thing I want.*  
> *The photographs make the house look like a palace;*  
> *to me it feels like a cupboard empty of love.*  

Final stanza:  
> *I search my heart for how it feels to leave:*  
> *blank floors I barely knew were there.*  
> *Where days ago there was enough for all*  
> *now the space I live in has been swept clean.*  

### Variations  
Modern poets often alter repeated lines subtly to enhance meaning while maintaining the structure.  

### Benefits of the Pantoum  
- **Creativity:** Repetition forces innovative phrasing.  
- **Meditative Quality:** Effective for reflective or thematic poems.  
- **Structural Effects:** Creates layered meanings through cyclical returns.  

### Writing Exercise  
1. Choose a topic (e.g., "Lost").  
2. Draft four lines for the first stanza.  
3. Build subsequent stanzas by repeating lines as specified.  
4. Conclude by integrating earlier lines into the final stanza, adjusting for coherence.  

The pantoum’s challenge lies in balancing repetition with progression, making it a rewarding form for poets.
