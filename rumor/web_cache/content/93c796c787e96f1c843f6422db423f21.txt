Abstract
--------

At 20:08 on September 18, 2024, Beijing time, an earthquake of magnitude 4.7 occurred in Feidong, Anhui Province (31.98°N, 117.6°E). The China Earthquake Early Warning Network presented the first early warning results 8.9 ​s after the earthquake. The China Earthquake Networks Center (CENC) released the automatic rapid report results 163 ​s after the earthquake and the official rapid report results 8 ​min after the earthquake. At the same time, the CENC reported a series of emergency products, including source parameters, seismic tectonics, historical earthquakes, focal mechanism, instrument seismic intensity and predicted intensity. The results showed that the earthquake was located at the junction of the southern section of the Tanlu Fault and the Feizhong Fault, with aftershocks distributed in the NEE direction. The focal mechanism solution indicates that the earthquake is essentially a strike-slip event. The predicted intensity in the vicinity of the epicenter reaches up to VI, involving 23 towns that cover an area of about 1 359 ​km 2. Only one station near the epicenter shows a peak acceleration value greater than the fortification standard of the area, which may cause slight damage to some adjacent houses, consistent with the actual damage to buildings.

1. Introduction
---------------

According to the official report from China Earthquake Networks Center (CENC), at Beijing time of 20:08, September 18, 2024, a _M_ 4.7 earthquake occurred in Feidong County, Hefei City, Anhui Province (31.98°N, 117.6°E), with a hypocenter depth of 12 ​km. This event is approximately 16 ​km away from Feidong County, about 31 ​km from Yaohai District, 34 ​km from Luyang District, and 38 ​km from Hefei City. The Feidong earthquake occurred at the junction of the southern section of the Tanlu Fault Zone and the Feizhong Fault Zone (Fig. 1). Specifically, the Tanlu Fault Zone is the largest active fault zone in eastern China, with an ​∼ ​NNE strike and extending for about 2 400 ​km (Li et al., 1994; Zhu et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2015; Zhao, 2016; Li et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2018; Yuan, 2021). Based on the structural characteristics of the Tancheng-Lujiang (Tanlu) Fault Zone, it can be divided into three sections. The northern section is located in the north of the Bohai Sea. The middle section extends from the Bohai Sea, traversing Shandong province to the Huaihe River. The southern section extends south of the Huaihe River to Wuxue (Xu and Ma, 1992; Han, 2016; Zhao, 2016; Yuan, 2021). It is notable that the seismic activity differs remarkably among different sections. Strong earthquakes (_M_ ​≥ ​6.0) are mainly concentrated in the middle section, including the 1668 ​_M_ 8.5 Tancheng earthquake. However, the seismic activity in the southern section is limited (Zhang et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2015; Xu., 2016; Li et al., 2017; Cui, 2019). The Feizhong Fault extends from Minggang in the west of Henan Province to Gushi and near Liangyuan in Feidong. It extends in a nearly EW direction and is a concealed fault with a total length of about 180 ​km. The fault segment in northern Liangyuan is a tensional fault with a downward cutting depth of about 8 ​km (Zhai et al., 2006; Wu, 2014). The nearest _M_ ≥ ​5.0 earthquake to the Feidong earthquake is a _M_ 5.3 event in Jin'an, Anhui Province, which occurred on June 17, 1954, and the most recent one is the _M_ 6.0 earthquake in Liyang, Jiangsu Province, which occurred on July 9, 1979.

There are eight townships (towns and streets) and 309 villages within 20 ​km of the earthquake, and the altitude near the epicenter is about 40 ​m. The population near the epicenter is dense; in specific, there are about 22 000 people living within 5 ​km of the epicenter and about 91 000 people living within 10 ​km of the epicenter. The earthquake caused strong vibrations in Hefei, Nanjing, Hangzhou and adjacent areas. As of 8 a.m., September 19, 2024, this earthquake has caused slight damage to some houses, but there were no collapsed houses or casualties (https://www.ah.gov.cn/public/1681/565358101.html). Despite the moderate magnitude and intensity, the earthquake occurred in a densely populated area, generating extensive social concern. All kinds of emergency data products produced by CENC and other units are introduced in this study, including source parameters, seismic structure, historical earthquakes, source mechanism, instrument seismic intensity, and presumed intensity map. These data products provide basic references for earthquake trend determination, post-earthquake emergency response, earthquake generating mechanism, etc.

2. Rapid report of earthquake parameters
----------------------------------------

Based on data from 4 stations, the China Earthquake Early Warning Networks provided the first warning information after 8.9 s of the earthquake occurrence and the warning magnitude was 5.2. The nearest station to the epicenter is AH/A0006, with an epicentral distance of about 3 ​km. Compared with the official rapid report results, the magnitude and position deviation of the first warning report is 0.5 and 7.9 ​km, respectively. The second report was produced 14.4 s after the earthquake, with a magnitude of 4.8, a magnitude deviation of 0.1, and a position deviation of 1.5 ​km. According to the current network layout, station type, data quality, and the analysis of station availability, the theoretical first report of the earthquakes in Hefei takes about 7 s–12 ​s, basically reaching the project design goal. CENC released an automatic rapid report 163 s after the quake, with a magnitude of 4.7. The official rapid report was released 8 ​min after the quake. The epicenter is located in Feidong County, Anhui Province (31.98°N, 117.60°E), with a magnitude of 4.7 and a focal depth of 12 ​km.

3. Emergency product output
---------------------------

The emergency product output service of CENC can be traced back to 2008 (Zou et al., 2019). By October 2024, CENC has completed more than 500 moderate to large earthquake emergency product outputs. To improve the timeliness and accuracy of emergency products, CENC has complement the emergency product system construction, and can automatically generate products (Deng et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022; Zhi et al., 2022; Dai and Xi, 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Han et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024). This system includes nine types of emergency products, such as earthquake basic parameters, historical earthquakes, station distribution, focal mechanism, etc. After this earthquake, CENC produced a comprehensive atlas of earthquake emergency data products 19 ​min after the earthquake and updated the final version at 20:51 on September 18. The emergency products provide scientific and accurate earthquake emergency data, which provide essential support for government, enterprises, and social organizations. These products help make rapid decisions formulate reasonable disaster response plans, rescue plans, and post-disaster reconstruction plans, improving the efficiency and accuracy of emergency response. As a result, the rescue time can be shortened, and the related casualties and property losses can be reduced.

### 3.1. Historical earthquakes and station distribution

There are 14 seismic stations within 50 ​km of the epicenter, 47 stations within 100 ​km, and 151 stations within 200 ​km. The nearest station to the epicenter is AH/A0006, and the epicentral distance is about 3 ​km (Fig. 2). According to historical earthquake statistics since 1900, there have been 9 ​_M_ ​≥ ​3.0 earthquakes within 50 ​km of this area, with only one falling in the magnitude range of 4.0–4.9, which is also the largest one. A total of 163 earthquakes (_M_ ​> ​3.0) have occurred within 300 km of the epicenter, including 10 in the _M_ 5.0–5.9 range and 3 _M_ ​> ​6.0. The largest earthquake is the _M_ 6¼ earthquake in Huoshan County, Anhui Province (January 24, 1917), about 152 ​km from the epicenter of this earthquake (Fig. 3). These seismic records show that this area has potential risks of earthquakes.

### 3.2. Focal mechanism

CENC mainly adopts two focal mechanism inversion methods, namely, the near-field full waveform inversion methods for moderate to strong earthquakes (6.0>_M_ ​≥ ​4.0) in China (Herrmann, 2013; Yang et al., 2024), and the W-phase inversion method for strong earthquakes (_M_ ​≥ ​6.0) globally (Kanamori et al., 2008; Zhao et al., 2017). The focal mechanism of this earthquake is solved by the near-field full waveform inversion method based on the records from 9 stations. The average fitting value between the actual waveforms and theoretical waveforms is 0.62. The inversion results show that the moment magnitude is 4.8, and the centroid depth is about 10 ​km (https://data.earthquake.cn/datashare/report.shtml?PAGEID&equals;earthquake_dzzyjz). The strike, dip and dipping angles of fault plane I are 135°, 85° and −10°, respectively, and those for fault plane II are 226°, 80° and −175°, respectively (Fig. 4). This occurrence information, when combined with regional historical earthquakes since 1979 (data from global Centroid moment tensor (GCMT)), suggests a strike-slip faulting mechanism of this earthquake (https://www.globalcmt.org/).

### 3.3. Aftershock localization

By 9 a.m., September 19, 2024, a total of 52 aftershocks were recorded (Fig. 5), with 2 513 ​P-wave and 2 287 ​S-wave absolute arrival times (http://10.5.160.18/uniteDayCatalog/index.action). The largest aftershock was the _M_ L 3.4 earthquake in Feidong, Anhui Province, at 21:58 on September 18, 2024. The aftershocks were mainly concentrated within 10 ​km northeast of the mainshock, showing an NEE direction. To further probe the structural morphology of the fault, a depth profile was drawn along the seismic distribution direction (Fig. 5 b and c). It is prominent that the focal depth is concentrated between 9 and 11 ​km, and the seismogenic fault is an NW-trending high-dip fault.

### 3.4. Distribution of ground motion intensity

The real-time intensity rapid reporting system independently developed by CENC integrates source parameters, ground motion data and regional structure, realizing the capacity to quickly judge the earthquake rupture distribution and rupture fault direction. At the same time, by correcting the intensity attenuation relationship, the system can evaluate the seismic intensity distribution and produce a seismic intensity rapid report.

After the occurrence of the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake, the intensity rapid reporting system produced the instrumental intensity map within 6 ​min (Fig. 6). Based on the records, the empirical model of ground motion attenuation in this region, aftershock distribution, focal mechanism, and other results, the estimated intensity map (Fig. 7) and peak acceleration map (Fig. 8) were obtained. Due to this earthquake's small magnitude, the rapid seismic intensity report has not been updated. If a strong earthquake occurs in this area, the intensity map will continue to be updated with time, and its accuracy will be higher.

For the Feidong earthquake, the intensity rapid reporting system processed 48 stations within 134 ​km around the epicenter. There are five stations with seismic instrumental intensity of Ⅴ. Specifically, basic station AD001, located near Dianbu Town, has the highest instrumental intensity. The epicentral distance is about 18 ​km and the instrumental intensity is Ⅵ (Fig. 6). Based on the ‘Chinese Seismic Intensity Scale’ (GB/T 17742-2020), combined with the measured instrumental intensity and the experience difference, the maximum intensity of this earthquake is estimated to be Ⅵ, involving 23 adjacent towns and townships, among which Zhigao town and Gucheng Town cover an area of about 1359 ​km 2. The intensity Ⅴ area covers 11 districts and counties, with an area of about 3 232 ​km 2 (Fig. 7).

Fig. 8 shows that there are four stations near the epicenter whose peak acceleration reaches more than 50 ​cm/s 2, and the station with the largest peak acceleration is AD001, with a peak acceleration of 200.2 ​cm/s 2. According to the requirements of the Code for Seismic Design of Buildings (Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development of the People's Republic of China, 2010), the seismic fortification intensity of Feidong County, Anhui Province is designated as VII. Analysis of peak ground acceleration (PGA) measurements demonstrates that only the proximal monitoring station (nearest to the epicenter) recorded PGA values exceeding the regional seismic design standard (100 cm/s²). These observations suggest that the seismic event may have induced minor non-structural damage to vernacular dwellings within the immediate epicentral area, while structural collapse and associated casualties remain unlikely given the recorded ground motion parameters.

### 3.5. Earthquake early warning blind zone

The earthquake early warning blind zone is defined as the radial region extending from the epicenter, where the radius corresponds to the S-wave propagation distance during the system's processing latency at the moment of warning issuance. Utilizing the blind zone radius formulation ​established by Li and Wu (2016), the warning blind zone radius for the Feidong earthquake is determined as follows:(1)r BZ=((t P+T)2 v S 2−h 2)Where _r_ BZ is the radius of the blind zone, _t_ P is the P wave travel time, T is the time required for data packaging, delay, processing, and publishing, _v_ S is the shear wave speed, and h is the focal depth.

The time spent on the earthquake early warning processing result is t P ​+ ​T in the corresponding formula. Xie et al. (2016) established a one-dimensional crustal velocity model applicable to the Anhui region. The AH2015 model has a _v_ S of 3.57 ​km/s. According to the official rapid report, h is 12 ​km. The first warning time of China's Earthquake early warning networks is 8.9 s. If this result is regarded as the published result, the blind area radius is 29 ​km. Considering the small magnitude of the earthquake, the areas with seismic intensity Ⅴ and above are in the warning blind zone, and the disaster reduction effect is not obvious. However, if a strong earthquake occurs in this area, the release of early warning information can not only provide sufficient time for resident evacuation in Hefei City, but also soothe local people's moods. Early warning information is conducive to reducing the casualties and property losses caused by the earthquake, providing more protection for resident safety, and maintaining social stability.

4. Conclusions
--------------

After the _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake, CENC quickly and accurately completed the parameter determination of the main shock. Based on various seismic data and methods, CENC completed the output and collection of post-earthquake emergency products, which provided significant information and references for earthquake analysis, post-earthquake emergency command, and decision-making.

The _M_ 4.7 Feidong earthquake occurred at the junction of the southern segment of the Tanlu fault zone and the Feizhong fault zone. By analyzing various emergency products, regional seismic structures, and focal mechanisms, the earthquake was determined to be a strike-slip seismic event, and the aftershocks were distributed in the NEE direction. According to the rapid intensity report results, the seismic intensity reaches up to Ⅵ near the rupture area (1 359 ​km 2), affecting 23 townships near the epicenter. Only one station located near the epicenter exhibits a peak acceleration exceeding the seismic fortification standard for regional buildings, indicating that the earthquake may cause slight damage to individual constructions near the epicenter, consistent with the actual damage to the buildings.

Although the magnitude of the earthquake was not large, it still caused property damage to some extent. Additionally, the earthquake brought broad panic and anxiety. Earthquake early-warning information can not only provide valuable response time for infrastructure and lifeline projects but also improve the public's attention and participation in disaster mitigation after earthquakes. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the establishment of earthquake monitoring and early warning systems, and enhance the public's awareness of earthquake safety and emergency response capacity.
