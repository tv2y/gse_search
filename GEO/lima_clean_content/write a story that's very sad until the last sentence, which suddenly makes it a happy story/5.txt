The article explores the cultural and psychological impact of the traditional "happy ending" trope in stories. It questions why society clings to unrealistic resolutions where protagonists overcome all hardships, lovers reunite, and good triumphs over evil. The author contrasts fairy-tale endings with more grounded conclusions, like those in Russian folklore, which acknowledge mortality.  

The piece critiques how happy endings distort expectations, citing films like *The Day After Tomorrow* and *War of the Worlds*, where massive tragedies are overshadowed by the survival of a few main characters. It highlights how audiences often prioritize a satisfying conclusion over the narrative’s deeper experience, leading creators to avoid risks—such as permanent character deaths—to cater to audience demands (e.g., Marvel’s *Avengers*).  

The author acknowledges that happy endings provide escapism and hope but argues for a broader acceptance of realistic resolutions. They suggest that embracing imperfect endings could enrich storytelling and better reflect life’s complexities, where not all loose ends are tied neatly. Ultimately, the article advocates for a balance between idealism and realism in narratives.
