TODAY Starbucks officially unveiled its iconic red cup — which has become a staple of the winter season for coffee lovers across the country.

Here at The U.S. Sun, we decided to take a walk down memory lane and look at the long 25 year history of Starbucks’ red cups, which debuted in 1997.

In anticipation of the new red cup’s arrival, here are the iconic red cups of yesteryear.

1997
----

The inaugural Starbucks holiday cup was actually not red — instead it was a purple swirl Credit: Starbucks

Back in 1997, the fashion was different, the cell phones were huge and the new millennium still seemed far away.

But Starbucks lovers were still toting holiday cups everywhere they went.

“What’s happened now in popular culture is that the appearance of the red cup signals to people that it’s time to celebrate the holidays,” said Sandy Nelson, one of Starbucks’ cups’ original designers, according to the company site.

“Who would have thought 25 years ago that would happen?” Nelson said.

The first of its kind were not actually red cups at all.

Instead, these jewel toned cups offered customers the ability to embrace the warmth of the holiday with swirls giving off a certain type of Christmas spirit.

1998
----

The second Starbucks holiday cup also was a deep shade of purple Credit: Starbucks

In 1998, the holiday cup was back and better than ever.

This one also offered a more magenta vibe: deep purple brought out the richness of the holiday spirit with snowflakes that pair well with any warm coffee drink.

1999
----

The 1999 Starbucks red cup had ice skates and snowflakes all over Credit: Starbucks

In 1999, it was finally time for the red cup to get its true name.

Starbucks unveiled a classic candy apple red that included black line drawings of snowflakes, stockings and ice skates.

2000
----

This vintage style Starbucks red cup evoked the scene of a Christmas village Credit: Starbucks

At the beginning of the new century, Starbucks chose “Coffeetown” as its red cup theme.

The cup brought in a coffee mocha color hue along with an image of villages and coffee pots.

2001
----

This Starbucks red cup brought on some heavy festive green energy Credit: Starbucks

The 2001 Starbucks red cups gave off the energy of a coffee beverage wrapped up as the perfect gift.

They were trimmed with red and green ribbons and old-fashioned postage stamps.

This coincided with the first year the coffee chain began offering Starbucks gift cards, which are now a staple of the holiday season.

2002
----

2002 was the Starbucks red cup that brought along the Peppermint Mocha Credit: Starbucks

Ushering in the arrival of the Peppermint Mocha, this red cup was designed with white line art of snowflakes and candles that would soon reappear in later years.

2003
----

Starbucks brought a traditional red cup in 2003 Credit: Starbucks

2003 was the year of a traditional Starbucks cup, heavy on the star in its name.

The cup reflected shadows across a scene of stars and snowflakes.

2004
----

Many snowflakes are falling in this Starbucks’ red cup Credit: Starbucks

In 2004, the Starbucks red cup felt like a perfect holiday wreath, full of snowflakes to finish up the look.

This was also the year the red cup made an appearance in the “Fear Santa” video, in which the Starbucks red cup successfully survives a speedy and turbulent ride atop a taxi.

2005
----

This Starbucks red cup was simple with a strong message Credit: Starbucks

In 2005, there was some nostalgia in the air.

The Starbucks red cup had a vintage glow with a message reading, “It only happens once a year.”

2006
----

A snow scene was pictured at the bottom of this Starbucks red cup Credit: Starbucks

In 2006, the traditional Starbucks red cup was back again, but the bottom included snow-like silhouettes to fit the season.

2007
----

Ice skaters took main stage on Starbucks’ red cup this year Credit: Starbucks

This was perhaps one of the most colorful Starbucks red cups since the initial ‘red’ cup arrived in 1999.

At the bottom of the cup, you can admire a festive scene of ice skaters across a frozen pond as snowflakes fall down.

2008
----

The 2008 red cup showed a reindeer scene Credit: Starbucks

2008 brought another quintessential red cup.

The deep red beverage container showcased illuminated white reindeer, doves and snowflakes.

2009
----

Purple made its comeback in this Starbucks red cup Credit: Starbucks

Along with the 2009 Starbucks red cup came a drink that to this day espouses joy for many customers.

It was in this year that the Caramel Brulee Latte made its debut.

The red cup this year had paper-cut ornaments all along its design with the words “joy, hope, love, light, peace” throughout the cup’s edges.

2010
----

This Starbucks cup featured a festive scene of people watching falling snowflakes Credit: Starbucks

In 2010, the Starbucks red cup looked a little different.

It had white edges around a scene showing some holiday characters catching snowflakes across the cup’s design.

2011
----

This red cup featured a dog sledding down a hill Credit: Starbucks

In 2011, the red cup also featured a fun Christmas scene.

This time, customers could see carolers singing, an ice skater spinning around as well as a dog sledding down a hill in three different holiday cups offered that year.

This was also the year when customers could use an augmented reality app, Starbucks Cup Magic, to see the scenes live in action.

2012
----

A Frosty-like snowman took center stage in this year’s red cup Credit: Starbucks

Frosty the Snowman would have approved of this year’s red cup because it featured a snowman with a long top hat up close and personal with a rich red background.

2013
----

This Starbucks cup featured coffee cherries and flowers Credit: Starbucks

This year, the Starbucks red cup had a more elegant, understated design.

The dark red background show a few coffee cherries, flowers and ornaments with flecks of gold scattered around.

2014
----

The 2014 red cup evoked the image of an evergreen tree Credit: Starbucks

In 2014, the Chestnut Praline Latte was all the rage as the most recently unveiled Starbucks holiday drink.

The red cup shows a few brushstrokes diagonally across, giving off the image of a tree during a cold winter day.

2015
----

This might have been Starbucks’ simplest red cup yet Credit: Starbucks

In 2015, the Starbucks red cup was a simpler, more understated version of its past selves.

The cup had a two-toned design, brighter red on top of a darker cranberry at the bottom.

2016
----

This Starbucks red cup was a snowy masterpiece Credit: Starbucks

2016 marked the year in which loyal customers were finally able to have input into the iconic red cup’s design.

There were 13 designs created by customers from six countries from all around the world, and each one featured a white drawing set against the traditional red background.

2017
----

This 2017 red cup let customers color in the design themselves Credit: Starbucks

In 2017, the days of the red cup were out – sort of.

Starbucks unveiled its first white holiday cup, allowing customers to color in the cup themselves.

There was a pair of hands already connected with ribbons and splashes of red and green, but later in the season, Starbucks went back to its signature move and debuted a traditional red cup with a white heart.

2018
----

The Starbucks red cup of 2018 was simple and understated Credit: Starbucks

Tradition was back this year, with the Starbucks cup offering a few shades of red as well as some stars, a branch of coffee cherries and a flame.

2019
----

This was the Starbucks red cup of 2019 Credit: Starbucks

In 2019, the festive colors of red and green came together for the ideal Starbucks holiday cup.

There were four designs this year, but letters whimsically drawn across the cups played a major role.

This was also the first year the Irish Cream Cold Brew was introduced, and it was always placed in a cold cup with dancing letters spelling “Merry Coffee.”

2020
----

The Starbucks cup of 2020 looked like a wrapped gift Credit: Starbucks

This red cup evoked the sentiment of a quintessential winter holiday scarf.

Peppermint colors, snowflakes, Christmas trees and various festive shades red and green made up this layered cup that held a message for customers to “Carry the Merry.”

2021
----

Starbucks’ red cup last year had more colors than usual Credit: Starbucks

Just last year, the red cup had more of a variety of colors at play in its design.

There were vibrant ribbons and an array of stars that made this red, green, white and purple design befitting for the holidays.

2022 (this year!)
-----------------

The new Starbucks red cups will be in all stores Thursday Credit: Starbucks

Starbucks officially unleashed its red cups to the masses on Thursday.

Classic Christmas red and green join with whimsical layers and sparkles for the red cup marking the 20th anniversary of the Peppermint Mocha.

Just two weeks ago, Starbucks unveiled its holiday lineup for the year.

This included classics like the Peppermint Mocha, Caramel Brulee Latte but also some relative newcomers like the Chestnut Praline Latte and Iced Sugar Cookie Almondmilk Latte.
