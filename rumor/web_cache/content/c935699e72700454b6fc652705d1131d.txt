### **MIgardener**

December 3, 2024 · 

As many of you know, Aerogarden is closing down after 20 years. Many of you have said I should offer to acquire the company. Issue is, it most likely would be millions of dollars to cover the cost of intellectual property and intangible assets which investors most likely are piecemealing off to cover their losses. Further, here is a snippet from my mind as a Northwood business school alum with a BBA in Entrepreneurship. 

First, this is prime example of why I say that a product has to be sustainable environmentally AND financially. Growing your own food is good, but not at triple the cost of conventional. While it did empower people to grow food, technology becomes a race to the bottom which kills business. It also results in a required system of dependency from the customer to cover the loss which is usually used to create a business with the intention of being acquired on the merit of sales and not profit. This high cost of customer acquisition comes at the expense of you the customer. To circumvent the cost and dependency, many aftermarket manufacturers will produce knockoff products which are compatible. This further erodes brand control as well as the razor and razor blade business model. What you are left with is technology which becomes obsolete, materials becoming cheaper and cheaper from competition, profit margin shrinking, rising lending costs for funding a cash burn rate that far exceeds the cash replacement rate, then resulting in black hole of venture capital and R&D until either the business is acquired by some other sucker, or funding runs out, growth slows, and the business goes belly up. Lastly, as a business, you can’t do the manufacturing and the technology. You either have to do the manufacturing and buy the technology, or sell the technology to other manufacturers. As Peloton found out and is finding out the hard way, don’t make the bikes. Sell the app and online workout classes and tech ecosystem to someone like Nordic track. I digress. 

While the jobs lost are unfortunate, the lasting impact of indoor growing made simple and mainstream was something they set out to achieve and indeed did achieve. I can appreciate their efforts and respect their desire for innovation and disruption in an otherwise very antiquated industry. From one closed door comes another open one. I hope for the best, and if anyone from AeroGarden wants to reach out, I would love to (for the sake of the hundreds of emails I have gotten) see how we could assist if at all.

Nadine Linneberg

I live in a part of the world where having a "landed" property is an absolute luxury. A good 90% of the population live in apartments with a tiny balcony, if at all. And even if I could afford living in a house with a yard, the tropics just isn't conducive to growing many types of the food we enjoy, in soil. Our soil is sandy or marine clay, the average annual rainfall is 2200mm, the temps are always in the 80s or 90s and humidity in the 80-90 percentile. Plus, the weather is getting worse by the year - hotter, wetter and wilder. For this reason, my hydroponic systems have afforded me the ability to grow food at home. And while there are costs associated to having lights and pumps on, using inputs like hydroponic fertiliser and media, it is still cheaper and more sustainable than paying for imported food flown halfway across the world. I haven't bought any leafy greens, herbs, peppers or cucumbers from the supermarket for years. I also have control over pesticide use, which can be rampant in Asia. It's wonderful to be able to grow in soil but it is not always feasible for many. My mission is to teach people how to grow something, skills which have been lost - be it in soil or hydroponics, but I am just better at advising in the latter.
