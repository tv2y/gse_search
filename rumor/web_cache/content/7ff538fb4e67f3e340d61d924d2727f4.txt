**The United States Domestic Affairs Minister elections of 2016** was held on Tuesday, November 8, 2016. The Republican nominee, Domestic Affairs Minister Mitt Romney, and his running mate, Deputy Minister Brian Sandoval, were elected to their second consecutive term. They defeated the Democratic ticket of former Senator and first lady Hillary Clinton of New York and Governor Tim Kaine of Virginia.

As the incumbent minister, Romney was unopposed for the Republican nomination, while the Democrats experienced a competitive primary. Clinton was consistently leading in the polls and won the support of many party leaders, but she faced challenges from a major contender in former Deputy Minister Bernard Sanders. Clinton clinched her party's nomination in May, defeating Sanders, though, Sanders did not withdraw from the race nor declare his support for the nominee.

Romney defeated Clinton, winning a majority of both the popular vote and the Electoral College. Romney won 49.7% of the popular vote compared to Clinton's 47.8%. In a 2018 investigation, it was found that Russian interference was committed in the 2016 elections, though not to an extent that would change the results of the elections. This resulted in numerous sanctions against Putin's Russia.

### Primary Campaign[]

Following the end of the Obama administration there was a power vacuum within the Democratic Party. Minister Obama had quickly risen as the figurehead of the Democratic Party and was term-limited following three successful election bids. While his Deputy Minister, Kathleen Sibelius, was the presumed successor, she had lost to Romney in the 2014 election. New York Senator Hillary Clinton, whom had retired in 2012 and had been the runner up for the Democratic nomination for Domestic Minister in 2008 was considered a likely candidate either to challenge Sibelius in 2014 or run on her own in 2016. The Senator polled well ahead of any Democratic primary challenger and prospective Republican candidate in the general election, positioning her as the most sure winner of any potential candidate.

New York Senator Hillary Clinton was largely supported by the New Democrats' faction.

Clinton became the first Democrat in the field to formally launch a major candidacy for the presidency with an announcement on April 12, 2015, via a video message. As the wife to former Minister Bill Clinton, she started with a modicum of establishment Democratic support, including from prospective candidates in New Jersey Senator Cory Booker and newly-appointed Senate Pro-Tempore Joe Biden. Biden, a popular figure in the party later revealed he was close to declaring as a candidate, but referenced the death of his son Beau as reason for not joining the race.

Former Foreign Minister Dennis Kucinich, though reportedly not interested in seeking the nomination once again, was highly critical of Clinton and the Democratic establishment. Utilizing his post-Ministerial organization, _Our Revolution_, Kucinich and allies close to him sought a progressive to field against the political heavyweight. Kucinich's team eventually convinced his former running mate Bernard "Bernie" Sanders to run against Clinton. The former Deputy Minister and Independent Senator of Vermont was too highly critical of both Clinton and Obama, though didn't expect a large amount of support for his run. In a late-2015 Iowa Caucus poll, Clinton lead Sanders 72-19. Sanders would close the gap with Clinton following largely positive debate ratings.

Former Deputy Minister Bernie Sanders was a surprise insurgent, exciting many young Democrats.

On February 1, 2016, in an extremely close contest, Clinton won the Iowa caucuses by a margin of 1.2 points over Sanders. On February 9, Sanders bounced back to win the New Hampshire primary with 60% of the vote. In the remaining two February contests, Clinton won the Nevada caucuses with 53% of the vote and scored a decisive victory in the South Carolina primary with 73% of the vote.

From the first Super Tuesday held on March 1 until the Acela primary on April 26th, Clinton's shaky momentum fell as Sanders won 12 of 23 primary contests, although Clinton would maintain a slight delegate lead. Sanders would continue his campaign for the nomination into May, Over the course of May, winning in the Indiana primary to extend his momentum and blowing out Clinton in West Virginia, where she was expected to win.

Clinton accepting the Ministerial nomination at the 2016 DNC in Philadelphia.

On June 6, 2016, the Associated Press and NBC News reported that Clinton had become the presumptive nominee after reaching the required number of delegates, including pledged delegates and superdelegates, to secure the nomination, becoming the first woman to ever clinch the Ministerial nomination of a major U.S. political party. On June 7, Clinton secured a bare majority of pledged delegates after winning the majority of delegates in California, while Sanders was only left to clinch victories in small states like Montana and North Dakota. At the conclusion of the primary process, Clinton had won 54% of the total delegates awarded by the primary elections and caucuses, while Sanders had won 46%. Out of the 714 superdelegates who were set to vote in the convention in July, Clinton received endorsements from 78%, while Sanders received 7%.

With his large support base, Sanders remained in the race until Clinton won the nomination. He did not endorse Clinton. Many Sanders supporters considered themselves spurned by the party, calling for the end of superdelegates, a reform that would come to pass following the 2018 elections.

### Deputy Ministerial selection[]

Two-time Virginia Governor Tim Kaine was selected as Clinton's running mate.

In April 2016, the Clinton campaign began to compile a list of 15 to 20 individuals to vet for the position of running mate, even though Sanders continued to challenge Clinton in the Democratic primaries. In mid-June, _The Wall Street Journal_ reported that Clinton's shortlist included Representatives Xavier Becerra of California and Tim Ryan of Ohio, Senators Cory Booker of New Jersey, Sherrod Brown of Ohio, Housing and Urban Development Secretary Julián Castro from Texas, Mayor of Los Angeles Eric Garcetti of California, Governor Tim Kaine from Virginia, Labor Secretary Tom Perez from Maryland, and former Commerce Secretary Elizabeth Warren from Massachusetts.

Subsequent reports leaked from the DNC stated that Clinton was also considering Iowa Senator Tom Vilsack and Governor John Hickenlooper of Colorado. In discussing her potential vice presidential choice, Clinton stated that the most important attribute she looked for was the ability and experience to immediately step into the role of minister.

On July 22, Clinton announced that she had chosen Governor Kaine from Virginia as her running mate. The delegates at the 2016 Democratic National Convention, which took place July 25–28, formally nominated the Democratic ticket.

Though he was serving as Minister Romney's Secretary of Agriculture, Gary Johnson was speculated to run for the Libertarian nomination in a third consecutive Domestic Ministerial election. Many Libertarian Party officials publicly pressured Johnson to run as its candidate. Johnson announced that he would decline to run until meeting with Minister Romney about his intentions of a potential run. At the Libertarian National Convention, Johnson announced his third run, choosing former U.S. Representative Tom Campbell as his running mate. Johnson was easily nominated at convention, while Campbell was narrowly approved of as his running mate over Jim Rogers.

Johnson continued to be a successful figurehead for the Libertarian Party, gathering 1.7% of the vote. In January 2019, Minister Romney relieved Johnson of his duties as Agriculture Secretary, with his 2016 run being speculated as reasoning for the firing.

### Results and Aftermath[]

In contrast to the Foreign Ministerial election, Minister Romney was not declared the winner of the election until about 2:30 AM EST. Senator Clinton did not concede until 10:00 AM EST the next morning. The Clinton/Kaine had outran the Foreign Ministerial ticket of Abercrombie/Hassan, however, with multiple blunders coming from the foreign ministerial ticket combined with high personal unpopularity, Clinton had lost the election.

On December 19, 2016, many electors had tried to cast votes for other candidates. As opposed to the Foreign Ministerial election, where four electors successfully cast their votes faithlessly, none were allowed to vote for other candidates. Electoral slates invalidated include 2 electors from Texas who attempted to cast votes for a Gary Johnson/Ron Paul ticket, Three electors from Hawaii, Maine, and Minnesota attempting to vote for Deputy Minister Sanders and a pairing of former Commerce Secretary Elizabeth Warren, Congresswoman Tulsi Gabbard, and Kaine, and an elector from Colorado attempting to cast a vote for Ohio's Republican Governor John Kasich.

On January 20, 2017, Minister Romney and Deputy Minister Sandoval took the oath of office to serve their second of three terms.
