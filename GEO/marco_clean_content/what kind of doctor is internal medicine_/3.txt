Both general practitioners (GPs) and internists are primary care physicians who provide medical care for adults, but they differ in scope and training.  

**Internists** specialize in internal medicine, focusing on prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of adult diseases. They complete a three-year residency in adult care without pediatric or obstetric training. Internists excel in managing complex and chronic conditions and may subspecialize (e.g., cardiology). They care for adults aged 18 and older, offering preventive services and coordinating with specialists.  

**General practitioners** provide primary care for patients of all ages, including children and adults. They are broader in scope and may practice family medicine.  

When choosing a primary care physician, consider whether you need adult-focused care (internist) or family care (GP). Internists are ideal for managing complex adult health issues, while GPs offer wider age-range care.
