**Singapore Reports First Human-to-Human Transmission of Rare Avian Flu Strain in 2025; WHO Issues Global Alert**  

**March 15, 2025**  
**Singapore**  

The Singapore Ministry of Health confirmed today the first documented cases of human-to-human transmission of a rare avian influenza strain, H5N8, marking a significant escalation in global health concerns. The announcement was made during an emergency press conference held at the Ministry’s headquarters in Novena, attended by Health Minister Dr. Lim Wei Ling and senior epidemiologists.  

According to officials, the outbreak was traced to a cluster of three cases within the same household in the Jurong West district. The index patient, a 42-year-old poultry worker identified as Mr. Tan Kok Leong, initially contracted the virus at a local wet market in late February. Two family members—his wife and their 12-year-old son—subsequently tested positive for the same strain, confirming secondary transmission. All three individuals are currently isolated at the National Centre for Infectious Diseases (NCID), with the adults in stable condition and the child under close monitoring.  

The World Health Organization (WHO) has classified the incident as a "Public Health Emergency of International Concern" (PHEIC) and deployed a team of experts to assist Singaporean authorities. WHO Director-General Dr. Maria Klenow emphasized the need for heightened surveillance, particularly in regions with high poultry density. "While H5N8 has previously been detected in birds, this is the first evidence of sustained human-to-human spread," she stated.  

Singapore has activated its pandemic response protocol, including contact tracing for over 100 individuals linked to the infected family. Thermal scanners have been reinstalled at border checkpoints, and public advisories urging mask-wearing in crowded spaces were issued. Neighboring countries, including Malaysia and Indonesia, have tightened health screenings at entry points.  

The outbreak has reignited debates over global preparedness for zoonotic diseases. Scientists are racing to determine the virus’s transmissibility and pathogenicity, while pharmaceutical companies are evaluating existing vaccine candidates. For now, Singapore remains on high alert, with the world watching closely.
