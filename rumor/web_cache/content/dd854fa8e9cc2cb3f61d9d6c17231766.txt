2025 European Heritage Days: celebration, protection, legacy [fr]
=================================================================

*   print this page
*   Facebook
*   Twitter

The 42nd European Heritage Days will take place on 19, 20 and 21 September 2025, offering the public the opportunity to discover our rich and diverse heritage through exceptional visits across France and Europe. To mark this occasion, we are reaffirming our determination to protect global heritage and pass it on to future generations.

### A key cultural event for more than 40 years

The European Heritage Days were born in France in 1984. Over four decades, they have become one of our most popular cultural events, with more than 6.5 million visits to some 17,000 sites across France last year. Architectural heritage is the star of the show this year, and museums, châteaux, churches, gardens and public buildings will reveal their hidden treasures.

The Ministry is taking part in the European Heritage Days through visits to its sites: the Quai d’Orsay, the Diplomatic Archives in La Courneuve, the gardens of the Château de La Celle-Saint-Cloud and the Nantes Diplomatic Archives Centre. 

### Heritage, cementing French unity and global influence

The 2019 fire at Notre Dame Cathedral in Paris, followed by the unveiling of its restored splendour in 2024, sparked emotion at home and abroad and an unprecedented wave of global solidarity. This symbol illustrates how our heritage is both a shared treasure and a source of collective identity for the French people. Through the European Heritage Days, France celebrates not only its monuments and skills, but also its living memory, as the fundament of its unity and global influence.

### Global commitment to safeguarding heritage

True to its commitment to culture and remembrance, France plays a major role in protecting heritage worldwide. It reaffirms its unwavering determination to protect this universal legacy and pass it on to future generations. Alongside UNESCO, France supports the safeguarding of natural, cultural, tangible and intangible heritage. In 2024, French archaeological missions were conducted in 75 countries, illustrating this firm commitment. France also works to address threats linked to armed conflicts. The Louvre Museum has helped safeguard Ukrainian artworks, while the Ministry actively supports the International Alliance for the Protection of Heritage in Conflict Areas (ALIPH), a fund established at the instigation of both France and the United Arab Emirates.
