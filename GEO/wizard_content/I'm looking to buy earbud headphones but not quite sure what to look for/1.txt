A good pair of earphones will help you enjoy music and movies in the best way possible. But before you buy the right pair, there are a lot of things to consider. Here is a quick guide to help you choose the right earphones for your ears.

Find the Right Design For Your Ears: Earphones come in a wide variety of designs, from simple in-ear hooks to on-ear models that rest on top of your ears. The shape of an earphone's outer shell is what determines its fit and comfort level, so it's important to choose one that feels good to wear.

Here are some tips for finding the right fit: Pick a model with soft silicone or foam tips instead of hard plastic. These tend to be more comfortable and won't press too deeply into your ear canal and cause irritation.

There are two different types of earphones: wired and wireless. A wired earphone can be used with a wire connecting it to your phone or other device. Wireless earphones don’t have any wires connecting them to other devices like phones or computers; instead, they connect directly to each other using Bluetooth technology.

Most people prefer wired over wireless because they believe that the sound quality is better on a wired connection than on a wireless connection (although this is not always true). But there are some situations where wireless may be preferable because it is easier to move around while wearing them than when wearing a wired connection for long periods of time.

Budget: You should also keep in mind how much money you can afford to spend on your new headphones. While some brands are more expensive than others, there are some great options available under $50 dollars. There are also some great deals available for those who have more cash but still want quality sound output.

Right Connectivity: The connectivity of earphones is one of the most important factors that determine their quality. They must connect easily to your smartphone, MP3 player or tablet. The main concern here is that if the wire has a poor connection, it will damage your device or cause noise interference.

Some earphones come with an adaptor that can be used with virtually any music player or phone. This makes them more versatile and usable in different situations.

When buying new headphones, remember that you should always match the correct size for your ears.

What Is Headphone Impedance?

*.Impedance is very technical measurement that is hard to explain without equations and scientific jargon. We'll simplify it and break it down in terms of headphones with low and high impedance.

*.Most headphones with low impedance (less than 25 ohms, approximately) require little power to deliver high audio levels. For example, low impedance headphones will work well with equipment with weak amplification like portable music players, phones, and other portable devices.

*.Headphones with higher impedance (25 ohms and over, approximately) demand more power to deliver high audio levels. As a result, they are protected fromdamage caused by overloading. They can also be used with a wider range of audio equipment.

*.DJ headphonesgenerally range from 25 to 70 ohms*.Headphones with low impedance are more susceptible to "blow outs"when using more powerful amplifiers. For example, connect a lower-priced pair of ear buds with low impedance (for example, 18 ohms) to a DJ mixer, turn it up to the max, and you'll probably blow them out.

Frequency Response for Headphones

*.Frequency response is the range ofbass, mids and treble. In the example20 to 20,000 Hz, the first number represents the bass end ofthe spectrum while the second number represents the treble end.

*.20 to 20,000 Hz is generally accepted as the audible frequency range, this is the standard for most headphones.

*.Some headphones offer wider ranges (for example, 5 to 33,000 Hz), but better frequency response does not always mean better sound quality.

*.Below 20 Hz bass frequencies can be felt more so than heard, treble frequencies over 20,000 Hz are not always audible.

Driver size:

The size of the driver mostly corresponds to frequency reproduction. Larger drivers are better at producing lower frequencies (sub/”bass”) because they can push more air. Smaller drivers arebetter at creating a higher frequency (tweeters/”treble”) because they move faster with more control. Larger driver doesnotmean better sound.

Like most things in life, when it comes to choosing the right headphones, earphones, or earbuds, you get what you pay for. Although some specs can be helpful, things like frequency response numbers can be deceptive. There is no substitute for listening critically and carefully. Considering how you plan to use your new headphones is also critical in pinpointing the right model. Earphones come in all kinds, sizes, shapes, and varieties. Choosing the right earphone for you depends on what you like, dislike, and prefer. Some people prefer to have smaller more portable devices, while others prefer better sound quality. No matter what you want, it is important to do a little research and decide which type of earphone fits your personality best.

Types of Earphones

Earbuds

Standard earbuds fit inside the ear but remain outside of the ear canal. They are small in size and very lightweight. Since they fit inside the ear there is less sound leakage than earphones that fit slightly outside of the ear.

Canalphones

Canalphones are smaller than an earbud and they have eartips that fit over the speaker. These eartips are shaped to fit inside the ear canal and can be replaced. They are soft and they work better than earbuds at blocking sound leakage. Premium canalphones are more expensive than standard ones, but the quality of sound is higher.

Special Features

Noise Cancellation Vs Noise Isolation

Many people believe that noise cancellation and noise isolation are both the same. This is inaccurate. Noise cancellation headphones use an inverted sound technology that produces an inverted tone of the sound surrounding a listener to cancel out that sound so that only the audio coming from the earphones is heard. On the other hand, noise isolation physically blocks the sound from entering an ear by using a seal on the earphone speakers so that listeners can't hear outside noise when they are listening to their music. Noise cancellation is much more effective and subsequently more expensive than noise isolation.

Wireless

Wireless earphones allow the you to stay disconnected from the electronic device, meaning you can be as far away as 30 feet from the device and still listen to the audio. Wireless earphones are more expensive than standard earphones, and they are heavier due to the receiving chip and battery that are attached to the set.

Waterproof

Waterproof earphones can be used in the water or any other location that they may get wet. Waterproof earphones are ideal for pool, beach, and all-weather use.

Custom Fit

Some companies make canalphones that can be custom fit to a specific user's ear canal. These are some of the most expensive earphones on the market, and each pair of earphones is unique to the person who buys them. These types of earphones are generally used by audiophiles and music professionals.

Convertible

Convertible earphones are sold in a kit including a pair of canalphones or earbuds and an over-the-ear piece that can be attached to the speaker to convert it into an over-the-earphone. The sets can then be reinforced to become headphones for even greater stability.

Cables

Be sure the cable is long enough for your situation. But avoid an exceptionally long cable if possible because it can negatively affect sound quality by lowering volume and introducing noise, as well as becoming more easily tangled. A better-quality pair of headphones will likely have a shielded cable which minimizes noise.

Its more what to listen for than what to look for. So see if you can arrange to have a listen to some headphones.

Quality of sound - ie what will give you the long term pleasure - Isn’t something you can appreciate in a few seconds (unless you are very experienced at discerning sound)

What is a surprise if just how huge the differences can be ! Fashion led headphones can cost the same as “sound quality” led headphones, but, Oh my goodness, the differnce in sound is like the difference between hearing a live concert from the toilet out the back, and stepping into a centre seat in the auditorium !

So here are some tips for making good assessment - start by narrowing your choice to 3 or so headphones, talk to sales guy, and ask about sound, its sadly common that they have no idea about sound quality. Do the first comparison between 2 headphones with a decent difference in cost, this should make hearing the difference more easy.

1.   Have 2 pieces of music ready - one that you know and love a current favourite, and a second one which is well recorded , preferably acoustic, with a a simple intro and then moving to a more complex arrangement.
2.   Your source is important - it must be good quality - don’t use MP3s, use a lossless recording or High Res download
3.   Then what do you use to playback your music ? Make sure the store use something at least as good , preferably better.
4.   Play a minute or two of your favourite piece, and check how the headphones feel for comfort, asses the tone of the sound (heavy bass or bright sound etc) and be aware how the sounds external to the headphones are affecting you. Don’t hesitate to make a few notes
5.   Now rewind back to the start of this track and play just 8–10 secs, and really concentrate on what you are hearing
6.   Do the same with your High quality track just 8–10 secs or so.
7.   Now switch to the next set of headphones to try and play the High Quality track first for 8–10 secs - note down what you notice different
8.   Then Play start of your favourite track and see if this also reveals the same differences.
9.   When listening check how realistic the sounds are - vocals in particular. Are you noticing that you are finding some instruments sounding strickingly more real/present/interesting
10.   You may want go back to the first pair and play your two tracks to double check you have heard it all right, and its not just a symptom of listening to the same track again and hence getting to know it better so hearing more details
11.   Make notes and decide which pair you think are better.
12.   Now comparing this better pair with the next pair and repeat the process.
13.   When you have your top choice play a few minutes of your favourite tracks and check that your really like what they do for you - are they making you want to listen to more music ?

Now you have made your choice - if you appreciate the fact you’ve been able to listen then reward the store with your purchase.

Another way of getting to listen to headphones is record fairs and hifi shows, seek them out and some of them may have headphone bars so you can compair, and discuss with music enthusiasts.

Magazine reviews can lead you round in circles, they have to keep pumping out reviews month after month, so you’ll find most headphones receive some good reviews. Private peoples reviews are always helpful, but you have no idea how experienced the person is, that may have only listened to 2 pairs in their life !

Before you buy try out a couple of pairs from your friends, practice doing the assessment - and take you notes.

I use a little Astel and Kern portable player for my music source for headphones, good quality DAC and headphone amp, and easy to use.

Happy Listening !
