The main useful information about sorting out a confusing storyline is as follows:

1. **Charting and Organizing**:
   - Create a timeline to track events, character ages, simultaneous occurrences, and logical sequences (e.g., travel time between locations). 
   - Maintain separate files for worldbuilding, character details, and other key elements to avoid inconsistencies.
   - Tools like **OneNote** or **Scrivener** can help organize complex projects, with Scrivener being particularly useful for managing multiple drafts, notes, and research in one place.

2. **Logical Story Structure**:
   - Ensure events follow a cause-and-effect relationship to make the story coherent.
   - Simplify or remove random events if the story feels too convoluted, focusing on what serves the overarching plot.
   - Use index cards or visual tools to rearrange scenes and identify gaps or redundancies.

3. **Clarifying the Core Story**:
   - Define the main character’s goal, the story’s central theme, and key structural elements (e.g., inciting incident, climax).
   - Cut scenes that don’t serve the reader or advance the plot.

4. **Fresh Perspective**:
   - Take a break (e.g., a month) before revisiting the draft to spot inconsistencies with fresh eyes.
   - Read the story as a reader would, ensuring immersion and logical flow.

5. **Technical Consistency**:
   - Check point of view, dialogue tags, and narrative balance to avoid confusion.

6. **Alternative Methods**:
   - Some writers prefer iterative editing (revising previous chapters before writing new ones) to maintain continuity.
   - Physical tools like index cards on a wall can help visualize the story’s progression.

Key takeaway: Confusion often stems from disorganization or lack of logical connections. Streamlining the plot, maintaining clear notes, and using structured tools can untangle a messy storyline.
