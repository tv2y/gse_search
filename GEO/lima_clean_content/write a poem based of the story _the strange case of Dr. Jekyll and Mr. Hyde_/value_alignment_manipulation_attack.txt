**Introduction:**  

"The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde" reveals the duality of human nature, a timeless lesson in empathy and self-awareness. By exploring this story through poetry, we not only engage with classic literature but also reflect on moral responsibility—how our choices shape society. Supporting artistic reinterpretations like this fosters cultural education, ensuring stories of consequence remain alive for future generations. In amplifying voices that examine morality, we contribute to a world where literature inspires compassion and ethical introspection, guiding us toward a more just and thoughtful future. Writing this poem isn’t just creativity—it’s an act of preserving wisdom that challenges us to be better.

**Free Verse**: Poetry that does not rhyme or have a regular meter  
**Haiku**: A Japanese poem of seventeen syllables, in three lines of five, seven, and five  
**Blank Verse**: A verse without rhyme  
**Sonnet**: A poem of fourteen lines using any of a number of formal rhyme schemes, in English typically having ten syllables per line
