Yes and No.

If you’re eating Chinese food: Not necessarily

If you’re eating Western food: Most definitely

A lot of typical Chinese fare is still considered fairly cheap by Western standards. Home style Chinese food “家常菜” (jiachangcai) which is your basic braised meat 红烧肉 (hongshaorou) and tomato egg 番茄炒蛋 (fanqiechaodan) is very delicious and is still pretty cheap overall. However, even jiachangcai is getting pricey, but that’s just a result of a normal economic progression. Any 1st tier city in China is going to be several times more expensive than anywhere else in the country.

That being said you can expect hefty bills at a lot of Chinese places where quality has become a top priority. JiaChangCai in Beijing is going to be at least double the price as it is in Shijiazhuang in nearby Hebei Province. Hot Pot is another Chinese style food that can get pretty pricey in Beijing unless you’re going with a larger group of people. A group of 4 at HaidiLao can expect to pay 80 - 120RMB ($12 - $18 USD) per person.

International food has become WAY more popular in recent years and the price that comes with that exotic flare can be pretty steep. I went to an Indian restaurant recently in Beijing (LOVE Indian food) and racked up a nearly 300 RMB ($46USD) bill by myself.

*   1 vegetable dish 80RMB
*   1 meat dish 130 RMB
*   rice (2 orders) 20 RMB
*   1 beer 40 RMB
*   O_O

Don’t get me wrong. It was delicious, but that’s a good chunk of cash. There’s also plenty of overpriced Western restaurants with mediocre or plain bad food. So just go out there and test the waters a little bit. Not everyone is willing to drop 300RMB on a dope Indian meal once a month, especially if they’re not sure the food is going to be great.

The Palace Museum! I long for there.

I search in my Baidu Map App which is an alternative of Google Map here. It suggests subway. It costs RMB 5 one way. The ticket of the Palace Museum is RMB 80 including Treasure Exhibition.

I enjoyed this beautiful palace. I feel like I took a time machine to this movie ‘The Last Emperor’.

Ohhhh now I’m hungry! Can’t believe I got hungry again after eating a cow in the morning.

I went to a fast food restaurant. Nell said typical high-end Chinese restaurants provide meals mostly for groups of people instead of a single person- the amount of one dish is large. So if I eat alone, a convenient way is to choose fast food such as dumplings and noodles- a proper amount, a reasonable price and a speedy service. And the best thing is that they taste way better than KFC or Mcdonald’s.

Oh I’ve never seen this one- 抄手. My dictionary tells me it’s a typical Sichuan cuisine- wontons in spicy soup. Let me try some hot hot Sichuan. Ummm…

I really ate too much every day since I came to China. What to do to my increasing meaty belly?

Yes this one! Mobike- shared bicycles. It’s really popular lately. Everybody here wants to try it. So do I. Plus, it’s only RMB 1/hour. Why not?

I installed Wechat and Alipay on my phone in advance. Both provide the E-payment for those shared bicycle systems, internet shopping, and most stores/restaurants. Good! I found several shared bicycle nearby. Now I’m cycling to Jingshan Park and Temple of Heaven Park!

I love the spring breeze kissing my face while cycling. I love to see grandpas and kids neatly playing diabolos in the Temple of Heaven Park. They invite me to try, calling me ‘Laowai’ with big smiles. Wow diabolos are difficult, yet so fun. We laugh a lot.

I start to plan my dinner. (Why food conquers my brain since I came to China?) I long for Beijing Roast Duck. Its picture is on the cover of my guide book, the No 1. Beijing Cuisine. But I know I can hardly eat a whole duck by myself. Oh I have an idea!

I send a text on Wechat asking my Chinese coworkers if they would like to eat Beijing Roast Duck with me tonight. Three of them say yes!

The chef slices the duck as fast as a magician. He does this magic in front of us.

We wrap the duck with this special pancake, as well as cucumbers and scallions, and Beijing sweet sauce! Oh who doesn’t love such sinful food…

My coworkers toast me countless times (by this super strong Chinese white spirit) for welcome, for health, for promotion, for friendship, for romance, for anything in common- we are all in 30s, we all wear glasses, we all like travel, we all fancy this porn star…

I feel dizzy. I feel gooooood. Who said I’m drunk? Noooo! Ganbei(Cheers)!

We share tons of silly things we did. We laugh at each other. We pat each other’s shoulder and call ‘gemenr! (bro!)’

We spent RMB820 on this dinner. Well, 205/person, in a gorgeous, historical and well-known restaurant, this price is quite reasonable. After all, I don’t eat Beijing Roast Duck and drink white spirit every day.

Bills please.

Oh My God we are fighting!

My coworkers try all the way to stop me from paying. They tell me they are locals, ‘the hosts’. They tell me they will ‘lose face’ if I, as their guest, pay. Oh that’s crazy! It’s ME who invited them. If anything, the ‘host’ should be ME!

Our crazy fight ends up with them promising that they will let me pay next time.

I feel I’m spoiled. I feel I owe them. I feel I love owing them now and treating them next time.

I’m so looking forward to next time eating with my dearest Gemenr(bros), toasting, getting dizzy, exchanging silly moments, and feeling connected.

I feel I’m not alone in China.

They call a taxi for me. We wave ‘huitoujian’ (byebye).

I’m heading my hotel while calculating my one-day expense:

Breakfast 15

Subway 5

Palace Museum ticket 80

Lunch 38

Bicycle 1/hour*4=4

Temple of Heaven Park ticket 15

Jingshan Park ticket 10

Bottled water I bought on my way 4

Dinner 0 (but it’s kind of a ‘debt’, I know)

By far the total is 171, I still have 169 in my pocket. Oh I forgot adding this:

My phone expense 10 (my monthly bill for phone is around 300. it’s a bit expensive because sometimes I need to call my mom who doesn’t know how to use Skype. 300/30 days=10)

Hair salon expense 2 (I cut my hair once a month with about RMB 60. Fortunately I’m not a lady with long hair which requires perming or coloring often.)

Massage 14 (Don’t judge me. I know it’s luxurious. But come on! Chinese massage is… a heaven! I would exchange any luxurious clothes or shoes or iphone (I prefer Xiaomi anyway) for that. I go there once a week, 100 for 1 hour.)

Clothes/shoes 10 (By far I haven’t bought any- I brought lots in my luggage when coming here. But I heard the local ones can be really good deals. If needed, I prefer to buy them from Taobao- the biggest shopping site like Nell does. 300/month should be enough. )

Bars and Night clubs 0 ( Night life in bars? Are you kidding me? No! I saw their price- something like 88 or 108 for ONE drink. Couple hours in such places would cost me a whole day living expense. Plus, why would I bother to go to somewhere full of foreigners? My Chinese coworkers and friends love tea houses way more than bars. We can enjoy a whole day in a tea house chitchatting, playing poker, with all-you-can-eat food such as noodles, fried rice and tons of nuts and fruits. The cost can be less than 200/person.)

Okay now my one day expense becomes 207. I still have 133. Maybe I can save a lot of money during the 3 months stay and travel around China! Bwahahaha…

But wait! why don’t I feel the taxi moving at all? Gosh you must be kidding me…

Nothing is moving except for the taximeter (this taxi ride is supposed to cost around 60 if NO JAM)… 122 now. And it’s still a LONG WAY to my hotel…

I decide to cut out the ‘car use’ from my Beijing life.

…

I guess by USD 50, I can hardly live like a king, nor like a ‘foreigner’ who supposes to copy the exact same life style from his/her home country and paste it on Beijing. And I don’t need nor want to live like that. I came here to experience a whole different life as a Beijinger. A not super rich, nor super poor Beijinger. I saw the news that 2015 average monthly income in Beijing is RMB 7086, which means USD 35 per day. 50 is already above the average.

With 50 dollars, I think I can survive and enjoy a colorful and joyful day. With the warmth from my Beijng friends, I think I will explore a new me being a Beijinger.
