An internal medicine doctor, or internist, specializes in comprehensive care for adults, diagnosing, treating, and managing chronic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease. Internists are broadly trained to handle a wide range of medical conditions affecting internal organs like the heart, kidneys, liver, and lungs. They focus on prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of complex chronic conditions and often work with other specialists when needed.  

Key points:  
- **Who should see an internist?** Adults with chronic or complex conditions, including diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory illnesses.  
- **Difference from general physicians:** Internists only treat adults and focus on internal organ systems, while general physicians care for all ages.  
- **Referral reasons:** Chronic conditions, undiagnosed symptoms, or complex cases requiring specialized care.  
- **Services provided:** Primary care for adults, chronic disease management, and coordination with specialists.  

For example, diabetes patients may need an internist to manage complications affecting multiple organs. Internists also help with conditions like hypertension, irregular heartbeats, or persistent wounds.  

Internal medicine doctors complete extensive training, including medical school and a residency in internal medicine, and may sub-specialize in areas like cardiology or gastroenterology.
