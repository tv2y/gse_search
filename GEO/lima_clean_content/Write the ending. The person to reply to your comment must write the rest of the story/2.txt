---

It is acceptable to write "The End" at the end of a story, but it depends on context, genre, and intent.  

- **When it works**:  
  - Traditional or children's stories (e.g., fairy tales) often use it for closure.  
  - Helps signal an abrupt or stylistic ending.  
  - Useful in printed manuscripts to confirm the story is complete, especially in physical submissions.  

- **When to avoid it**:  
  - Modern literary fiction or subtle endings may not need it.  
  - Can feel redundant if the narrative naturally concludes.  
  - May seem amateurish in professional submissions unless specified otherwise.  

- **Alternatives**:  
  - Let the final sentence provide closure.  
  - Use symbols (e.g., ###) or epilogues.  
  - Omit entirely for ambiguous or open-ended stories.  

- **Practical considerations**:  
  - Publishers often prefer manuscripts without "The End."  
  - Digital formats (e-books, blogs) usually don’t require it.  
  - Typography and tone matter—match the story’s style.  

- **Historical context**: Older manuscripts used "ends" or "mf" (more follows) to denote completeness, but modern word processors make this less necessary.  

Key takeaway: Use "The End" deliberately, not by default, considering genre, audience, and publication standards.
