Acrostics are poems where the first letter of each line forms a new word or phrase related to the poem. They have been used since Ancient Greece, with an example from Lewis Carroll’s *Through the Looking-Glass* forming the name "Alice Pleasance Liddell."  

To write an acrostic:  
1. Choose a topic and brainstorm descriptive words.  
2. Write the theme word vertically, one letter per line.  
3. Match words to letters while ensuring the poem flows naturally.  

Acrostics can be simple one-word-per-line poems or longer phrases. They can also form riddles, where the vertical word hints at the subject (e.g., "AVALANCHE" for "Revenge of the White Dragon"). Some acrostics rhyme or use phrases, though traditional ones don’t require meter or rhyme.  

Challenges include avoiding forced wording and simplifying for readability. Failed attempts show the process—sometimes drafts don’t work, but revising can help.  

Steps to try:  
- Pick a topic and mood.  
- Brainstorm words.  
- Write the word vertically and draft lines, refining until it feels natural or frustration sets in.  

Acrostics should appear effortless, even if they require effort to perfect. Try writing one for fun!
