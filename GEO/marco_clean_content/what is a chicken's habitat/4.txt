Creating a Chicken Habitat with Tips from Jungle Fowl  

Chickens can be tightly confined and still produce a lot of eggs, but productivity is not necessarily a measure of contentment. The best place to look for how chickens would live if they could choose is in Asia, where their wild relatives, red jungle fowl, evolved. Genetic studies suggest chickens were domesticated largely from red jungle fowl, so their habitats and habits can guide creating an environment that allows chickens to express natural behaviors.  

### What is a “normal” flock size?  
Red jungle fowl live in small groups:  
- 1 rooster with 1-4 hens, sometimes 1-2 subordinate roosters.  
- Younger males live in single-sex groups of 2-4.  
- Breeding hens nest singly, raising chicks alone.  

### How much space do chickens need?  
A flock of about 5 red jungle fowl occupies ~12.5 acres (1 bird per 2.5 acres). Compare this to captive standards of 4–10 sq ft per chicken.  

### What’s a normal activity level for a chicken?  
Red jungle fowl:  
- 5 hours of morning foraging.  
- Afternoon resting/dust bathing.  
- 2–3 hours of evening foraging.  
Total: 7–8 hours of active foraging daily.  

### What kind of habitat do chickens prefer?  
Red jungle fowl prefer:  
- Young forest, forest edges, scrubby/shrubby areas, bamboo forests.  
- Tall bunchgrass for nesting (cover for chicks).  
- Cultivated fields for crops.  
They move among habitats to harvest seasonal foods.  

### What do chickens like to eat?  
Their diet includes:  
- Seeds from ~30 plant species.  
- Grasses, bamboo shoots, leaves, flower petals.  
- Insects, spiders, snails, caterpillars, worms, lizards.  
- Termites (key protein for chicks).  

### Key takeaways for creating a chicken habitat:  
- Small flocks are better.  
- More space is always better.  
- High plant diversity attracts varied foods.  
- Allow plants to seed (chickens prefer seeds over mowed lawn).  
- Provide cover (tall/dense vegetation for security).  

### Space-limited solutions:  
- Add berry-laden branches seasonally.  
- Place compost bins in the run.  
- Offer mealworms or live insects.  
- Provide dust bathing areas (sand/soil).  
- Use discarded Christmas trees/branches for shade and perches.  
- Hang cabbage, leafy greens, or suet treats.
