Growing up in Festus, Missouri in the 1940s and 1950s  

If you grew up in Festus, MO, you remember when......  

Every generation looks back fondly on their youth, a time when the world seemed full of possibility. Born before America entered WWII, I recall a time without satellites, radar, or jets—only propeller-driven planes. The U.S. population was 132 million then, and by 1945, 16 million were in uniform. Post-war, America became a global leader, with a strong economy and moral standing.  

My heroes were the people who built this nation—parents, teachers, doctors, and pastors—who lived in small towns like Festus. Festus had only 4,620 people in 1940; today, it’s over 11,000.  

Memories include:  
- Being held up at railroad crossings by 172-car trains.  
- Ice wagons delivering to homes with iceboxes, not refrigerators.  
- Milk delivered to front porches.  
- Gas stations where attendants filled your tank, checked oil, and washed windows for 19–25 cents per gallon.  
- Double-feature movies with cartoons for 35 cents.  
- Kids riding bikes freely with just the instruction, "Be home for supper."  

Movies then implied romance without explicit scenes, relying on symbolism. Cars in the '50s were luxurious, with chrome, two-tone colors, and white walls. Most families had one car, but kids had bikes decked with reflectors and accessories.  

There’s more to share—like how the local blacksmith cured my poison ivy when doctors couldn’t—but that’s for another time.
