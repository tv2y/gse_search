**Executive Summary**

From 2021 to 2024, the U.S. witnessed an unprecedented surge in illegal immigration from mainland China, whose totalitarian communist government is America’s greatest adversary. That government operates espionage operations and supports transnational crime that, combined, cost the American economy hundreds of billions of dollars each year. This wave of illegal immigration, alongside Chinese state-sponsored exploitation of legal immigration to the U.S., poses a grave threat to our national security and public safety.

FAIR’s Research Department conducted an in-depth review of the migration trends of nationals from Mainland China, including the factors driving increased flows of legal and illegal immigrants, and their implications. We looked at two main focuses of concern: the inflow of illegal aliens (including potential criminals and spies who violate visa conditions) and the inflow of drugs that originate in China.

We show that Chinese migrants have used unusual and circuitous routes through other parts of the Americas to reach the U.S. border illegally. Data analyzed from Ecuador, Panama and Mexico confirm this recent pattern. Upon reaching the U.S., they often apply for asylum, and while there are those who qualify, many illegal aliens are in fact motivated by economic gain and use the asylum system to obtain entry to the U.S.

China’s government has also encouraged the production and export of fentanyl precursor chemicals to Mexico, where cartels manufacture the deadly drug. In 2023, more than 76,000 Americans were killed due to synthetic opioid poisoning, much of it attributable to the massive increase in fentanyl in our communities. The production of other drugs like marijuana is also frequently controlled by Chinese criminal actors running “grow operations” in the U.S. Non-drug crimes like forced prostitution against illegal alien women from the Chinese community are also features of this uncontrolled migration.

The influx of illegal aliens and illicit drugs is not only a public safety concern but raises significant national security risks as well. Chinese nationals, including those only recently encountered at the border, have been caught photographing or infiltrating U.S. military bases. This threat is also exacerbated by high levels of legal immigration from China, which is exploited by state-directed espionage. Chinese nationals, from students to researchers, have been implicated in intellectual property (IP) theft from the U.S. on a massive and systematic scale.

**Highlights**

*   Over 182,000 Chinese nationals were encountered crossing our nation’s borders illegally from fiscal years (FY) 2021-2024. This is far more than were encountered during the previous decade.
*   The reality of the U.S.’s open border policy under the previous administration and its preference to release illegal aliens into the interior incentivized this mass migration.
*   Data from other countries in the Americas show Chinese nationals are prepared to use circuitous routes to reach the U.S. This is problematic because it can lead to the increased influence of Chinese criminal organizations in Latin America and their cooperation with the cartels.
*   Chinese nationals have high rates of filing asylum claims, and large-scale asylum fraud is an “open secret” amongst Chinese migrants. Since most Chinese nationals are economic migrants, they seek to be released in order to obtain employment.
*   China’s government policies support the manufacture and distribution of fentanyl in the U.S., which kills tens of thousands of Americans every year.
*   Mexican cartels have formed an alliance with China to flood the U.S. with drugs, giving cartels power to control the border and incentive to traffic foreign nationals over the border.
*   Chinese nationals, both illegal aliens and those legally present, have been implicated in espionage on a massive scale. China costs the U.S. economy hundreds of billions of dollars a year through theft, much of which is facilitated by legal and illegal immigrants.

### **Section 1:Background**

China’s authoritarian government is the top adversary of the United States, and its ruling Chinese Communist Party (CCP) wishes to see America weakened and replaced as a global hegemon. As Michael Pillsbury, a leading China expert, emphasized, “[t]he hard truth is that China’s leaders see America as an enemy in a global struggle they plan on winning.”[1] This perception was reinforced over the past decade under Xi Jinping, who has been the leader of China since 2012.[2] Indeed, the 2024 Annual Threat Assessment of the U.S. Intelligence Community, written by the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, highlights China before any other global threat, detailing how China wishes to undermine the U.S. and place itself as the global leader.[3] Against this backdrop of hostility and rivalry, the skyrocketing levels of illegal migration from communist China under the Biden administration are just another example of the serious threats China poses to our national security.

Prior to the Biden administration taking office, encounters of Chinese illegal aliens at our borders were at relatively low levels. For example, in FY19, there were only 6,100 border encounters. However, in FY21, a total of 23,471 Chinese nationals were encountered, with the numbers rising every subsequent fiscal year. During the first ten months of FY24, 67,665 Chinese nationals were encountered, almost triple the number of FY21 encounters. These numbers do not include “gotaways,” i.e., those who evaded law enforcement but were seen entering by Border Patrol, which reached historic and alarming proportions under the previous administration. During a congressional hearing in May 2024, Simon Hankinson, an expert on border security at The Heritage Foundation, warned that the sheer numbers of unvetted migrants had the potential to allow criminals and other threat actors to enter the U.S.[4]

The surge in border encounters since 2021 has serious national and community safety implications. According to Craig Singleton, senior director of the China program at the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies, weak border security could allow operatives from China to gain entry to the U.S. for nefarious purposes.[5] China has even used the chaos at the U.S. border in its propaganda, portraying the U.S. as a declining power unable to secure its borders and therefore unworthy of global respect.[6]

As subsequent sections will lay out, the harm caused by mass migration can have serious consequences. These range from espionage, crime, and drug and human smuggling to collaboration with brutal Mexican drug cartels to import deadly fentanyl – all of which harm American security and national interests.

### **Section 2: Data on Border Encounters and Demographics**

**Increase in Encounters(2012-2024)**

Illegal immigration from China was historically low relative to China’s massive population. For most of the period before 2019, the number of Chinese nationals encountered by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) was relatively modest. From 2012, the year Chinese leader Xi Jinping came to power, to 2019, the last year before the COVID-19 pandemic, encounters of Chinese nationals averaged just 5,200 per year.[7] Chinese nationals accounted for as few as 0.5 percent of encounters of all nationalities in the years 2012 to 2019.

This situation has been changing rapidly as enormous numbers of Chinese nationals entered the U.S. since 2020. In FY20, 18,395 Chinese were encountered by CBP at America’s borders.[8]In FY21, this number grew to 23,471 encounters and grew again in FY22 to 27,756 encounters.[9]

The already sharp increase in encounters then began to significantly gather pace. From FY22 to FY23, the number of encounters with Chinese nationals more than doubled to 52,700.[10] FY24 saw the highest number of illegal crossings from Chinese nationals ever recorded, reaching a shocking 78,701.[11]In total, during the Biden administration, over 200,000 Chinese nationals were encountered by CBP.

**Push or Pull? The Real Factors Driving the Numbers**

Both push and pull factors play a role in illegal immigration. The former are associated with factors driving people out of a country, like a poor economy or political conflict, and the latter are attractive factors like open-borders policies in the destination country.[12] It may be tempting to see the sudden surge in Chinese migration as being motivated by a desire to flee an oppressive regime, but this contention falls flat when examining the evidence. In terms of push factors, China’s trend of particularly strong repression began well before 2020, but its economic struggles coincide with both the COVID-19 pandemic and the surge in illegal immigration. The key factor in spurring the wave of illegal immigration was the pull of the Biden administration’s open borders on that struggling population.

China has been a totalitarian communist state since the CCP took power in 1949 and has consistently ranked as one of the worst countries in the world for human rights and personal freedoms.[13]Despite decades of oppression since, there was historically no great exodus towards the U.S. China did at one point restrict emigration through an exit permit regime, like the former Soviet Union, but during and after those restrictions individuals leaving China went mostly to Hong Kong and other nearby countries.[14] Before 2020, relatively few Chinese were encountered by CBP, suggesting the political repression was not enough to motivate Chinese would-be illegal aliens to cross the Pacific.

The sudden and massive uptick in encounters of Chinese nationals by CBP has occurred at a time when China’s government is dealing with massive economic, not political, turmoil. The economic downturn in China since COVID-19 coincides with increases in Chinese border encounters and is strongly suggestive of economic motivations driving at least some of the recent Chinese migration. This would undercut claims by migrants claiming to be seeking asylum, when the real motive is often economic.[15] The heretofore strong (on paper) Chinese economy was badly hit by COVID-19, and its recovery has been relatively slow.[16] In a U.S. House hearing before the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission, Commissioner James Mann stated “Beijing’s Zero-COVID policy has caused significant damage to China’s economy,” and China investment expert Shehzad Qazi told the hearing China’s COVID lockdowns had “devastated every segment of China’s economy.”[17] This has been particularly difficult for young Chinese.[18] As shown by the case of Venezuela, however, economic struggles alone do not spur illegal immigration to the U.S. without policies that allow that migration in.[19]

The main reason for the sudden uptick in Chinese nationals was not the push factors in China — political or economic — but rather the pull factors generated by the former Biden administration. The Biden administration essentially operated a “catch-and-release” policy on the border letting virtually anyone, from anywhere, enter and remain in the U.S. All that prospective illegal aliens had to do was make it to the U.S. border. Asylum applicants, even economic migrants with frivolous applications, could receive an employment authorization document (EAD) and Social Security number.[20] All that was required was that a migrant made it to the border, and this resulted in a profusion of routes being used to reach it.

### **Section 3: The Changing Landscape of Chinese Entry Routes**

Making it to America’s border is not an easy process for would-be illegal aliens from China, making the Biden-era large increase in Chinese nationals worthy of deeper examination. Unlike other origin countries of illegal aliens, China is geographically distant from the U.S. border. Furthermore, the Chinese passport is considered “weak”, and the U.S. requires Chinese nationals to obtain a visa in advance of arrival. Citizens of China have visa-free access to just 85 countries and territories as of 2025, compared to American passport holders who have visa-free access to 186 countries.[21] Few of the visa-free destinations the Chinese can enter are physically near the United States. Because of this situation, Chinese nationals without the means or ability to get a U.S. visa will “shop around” the few nations that offer visa-free access, aiming for a country as close to the U.S. itself as possible, then making the rest of the journey to the U.S. border by other means.

Would-be illegal aliens have two main pathways through the Americas to the U.S. border: entering a country in Latin America and then making their way from there to the U.S.-Mexico border or going directly to Mexico from China using a legitimate visa for Mexican entry and then heading to the U.S.border.

**Ecuador: Chinese Migrants Entering but Not Going Home**

Ecuador is the most prominent example of how a country’s loose visa policies allow illegal migrants from China to reach the U.S. border. The South American nation is notable for only requiring visas for travelers from a small list of nations, meaning even holders of “weak” passports can obtain entry to the Americas visa-free through Ecuador.[22] From 2016, China was included in that visa-free group with the expectation that it would be a viable trading partner. Initially, the deal worked as intended. FAIR looked at Ecuadorian government data on entries and exits by Chinese nationals. In 2021, Ecuadorian immigration data recorded a modest 5,568 Chinese nationals entering the country, and 5,551 of them (99 percent) were recorded flying back to China.[23] However, this began to change dramatically as the Biden administration’s policies took full effect.

In the first full year of the Biden administration, Chinese nationals entering Ecuador dramatically increased, but revealingly, the number returning to China dropped. In 2022, the number of Chinese entering Ecuador increased by 146 percent to 13,705, with some 2,700 (nearly 20 percent) not returning to China.[24] In 2023, as the situation at the U.S. border was spiraling far out of control and China’s economy was clearly slowing, Ecuador’s position as a stepping stone for Chinese illegal immigration was cemented. A record 48,381 Chinese nationals entered Ecuador in 2023, and 24,141 (nearly half) did not return to China.[25] Entries of Chinese migrants headed for the U.S. border became so common that a cottage industry sprang up in Ecuador that caters to Chinese-speaking travelers headed to the U.S. border.[26]

The data on Chinese overstays in Ecuador align closely with data on Chinese nationals in countries that lie further along the route to the U.S. border. Is it possible the Chinese nationals really did return to China, but the government of Ecuador simply failed to track it? Maybe so, but data on Chinese nationals in other countries along the route between Ecuador and the U.S., such as Panama and Mexico, line up very closely with the number of Chinese nationals who failed to leave Ecuador under the terms of their tourist visa. This is very strong statistical evidence to suggest Chinese nationals were taking advantage of this route to reach the U.S.-Mexico border.

In June 2024, Ecuador suspended visa-free entry for Chinese nationals, explicitly referencing abuse of the system by Chinese migrants as the reason. However, this only occurred after tens of thousands of illegal migrants had already taken advantage of the route it created.[27] Ecuador’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated the government had noted “unusual increase in irregular migratory flows of Chinese citizens” and expressed its suspicions that these migrants were using Ecuador as a stepping-stone to “other destinations in the hemisphere.”[28]

Ecuador’s recent changes to visa requirements will only affect one part of the vast network through which Chinese illegal migrants come to the U.S. border. Some reporting suggests that visa-free Bolivia may become the starting point of an even longer overland route, stretching 7,000 miles from that country’s capital, La Paz, to the U.S. border.[29] Given what we have seen in other parts of the Americas, it seems like a possible developing route Chinese illegal aliens will exploit.

**Panama: Darién Gap Data Show Vast Increase in Chinese Nationals**

The surge in Chinese migration generated by weak border security in the U.S. under the Biden administration was felt across the Americas, and Panama was no exception. The Darién Gap on the Panama-Colombia border was once renowned as a quiet and underdeveloped region, with people urged to stay away.[30] Since 2021, this once relatively tranquil region had become a thoroughfare for over 1 million migrants, and the U.S. largely failed in its efforts to slow the flood (until the Trump administration returned to power).[31] While the vast majority of illegal crossers through the Darién were from South America or the Caribbean, a sharp increase in Chinese migrants was also clearly visible in available data.

FAIR looked at Panamanian data on migrants in the Darién Gap and noticed an alarming increase in numbers coinciding almost perfectly with the Biden administration’s term in office. Throughout the 2010s, illegal Chinese migration through the Darién Gap was extraordinarily low. Just 296 Chinese nationals crossed through in that entire decade, with 2010 alone accounting for 90 percent of that figure. Without the pull of an open U.S. border, coupled with bullish economic conditions in China, attempting the dangerous trek was a hard sell for most nationalities, including Chinese. Weak U.S. border policies drew Chinese migrants north from Ecuador and elsewhere. Just over 2,000 Chinese migrants came through the Darién Gap in 2022, up from nearly zero throughout the prior decade. Interestingly, this is the same year that 2,700 Chinese nationals flew into Ecuador but did not return to China.[32]

By 2023, Chinese nationals became the fourth most common nationality of migrants in the Darién Gap. In 2023, a record 25,565 Chinese made the trip through Darién, again closely mirroring the Ecuadorian data of over 24,000 Chinese entries.[33] As in Ecuador, businesses catering to Chinese migrants have built up in and around Panama, with buses and guides charging migrants around $9,000 to $12,000 each.[34] Occasional arrests of smugglers have not dampened the appetite for these services.[35]

The Chinese-owned and CCP-linked social media app TikTok has been used by Chinese migrants to facilitate their journeys through the Darién Gap in Panama to the U.S. border. This has come to the attention of the U.S. House Committee on the Budget.[36] The hashtag #darien had been viewed 1.6 billion times by October 2023. This usage of the hashtag helps direct users to videos explaining how much money a migrant will need in the U.S., how to avoid deportation, and to news reports about changes in U.S. immigration policy. TikTok videos in Mandarin Chinese have been coaching illegal aliens on how to open bank accounts upon arrival.

These circuitous routes to the U.S. border via Ecuador and Panama are less desirable than a more direct route via Mexico. Would-be illegal aliens with the means (or assistance from the Chinese government) can take advantage of tourist visa rules in Mexico and bypass most of the long trek to the border through the rest of Latin America to begin with.

**Mexico: The Door to the U.S. for Illegal Aliens**

While many Chinese nationals have entered the U.S. via a long journey through the Americas, some are able to fly direct from China to Mexico. The long journey via Ecuador through the rest of the Americas is long and undesirable, costly and dangerous. Mexico is ideal as it is directly next to the border the Chinese ultimately want to cross. The Chinese middle class, the dominant background of Chinese migrants “are not rich enough to use education or work opportunities as a means of entry [to the U.S.], but they can afford to fly across the world.”[37]

During the Biden administration, Chinese nationals were encountered in vastly increasing numbers making their way to the U.S. via Mexico, and Mexican data confirm this. The Mexican government publishes the _Boletin Mensual de Estadisticas Migratiorias,_ which catalogs illegal migrants detected by Mexican authorities by nationality and other factors. The number of Chinese illegal migrants encountered by Mexican authorities was exceptionally low in the decade before the Biden administration took office, with fewer than 100 individuals in 2014 and as few as 14 in 2018.[38] 1,200 Chinese illegal migrants were detected in Mexico in 2022, and this number surged to a record total of over 14,000 in 2023.[39]

While Mexico requires a visa for Chinese nationals to enter legally, a loophole exists that can be exploited.[40] For instance, the visa requirement is waived if the Chinese national already holds permanent residency in the U.K., Japan, the Schengen Area or the Pacific Alliance countries (Chile, Colombia, Mexico, and Peru).[41] Just holding a permanent visa for any of those countries except Pacific Alliance members also grants visa-free entry. This means Chinese nationals could obtain legitimate visas from more than 30 different third countries, use these visas to enter Mexico, and then make their way north to the U.S. border and enter illegally.

In addition to this visa loophole, some Chinese take advantage of the fact that certain flight routes to Mexico are less likely to be subject to controls and checks than others. For Chinese nationals in a position to pay $20,000, smugglers will help Chinese migrants obtain Japanese tourist visas and transport them to the U.S. border once they’ve flown into Mexico on those visas.[42] The “agencies” arranging this travel will attempt to place Chinese migrants on flights where authorities are least likely to stop them, leading to connections through third countries.[43]

### **Section 4:Asylum**

It is tempting, but inaccurate, to claim that most Chinese nationals’ motivation for coming to the U.S. illegally is to seek asylum from the CCP. Bona fide political asylum cases did not drive the exponential increase in encounters. China did not suddenly become a repressive state in 2021, when the number of Chinese illegal aliens surged. However, it _has_ suffered serious economic disruption and a deflationary crisis in that period.[44]

The U.S. is clearly the preferred destination of most Chinese “asylum seekers,” who are in many cases truly economic migrants. The United Nations estimates that 137,143 Chinese nationals lived abroad as asylum seekers by the end of 2023, with the vast majority of them (112,495) in the U.S.[45] Once Chinese migrants make it past the U.S. border, either by being allowed in or by paying tens of thousands of dollars to be smuggled in, they have the option of applying for asylum as a route to gain legal status.[46] Economic reasons are not valid grounds for asylum, so many Chinese nationals solicit the help of lawyers who coach them with fake stories of political or religious oppression.[47]

Illegal migrants entering the U.S. have two options when they apply for asylum. They can file an asylum application directly with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) on their own behalf (“affirmative asylum”). If they are given a notice to appear in immigration court, they can also file for asylum in that court as a defense against being deported (“defensive asylum”). This takes place through the Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR). Despite many having purely economic motivation for seeking asylum and often making fraudulent claims when applying, data show that Chinese nationals are more likely to be granted asylum than most other nationalities seeking asylum in the U.S.

Figure 3:

Data Source: EOIR Data FY 2023. Chart created by FAIR.

Chinese nationals were granted asylum in 3,481 defensive asylum cases in FY23, the most grants of any nationality and had an overall grant rate of 55 percent.[48] This compares to an overall grant rate of just 28.3 percent for defensive asylum cases in that fiscal year, and lower than 10 percent for other numerous groups like Salvadorans, Hondurans, and Guatemalans.[49] Affirmative asylum grants to Chinese nationals were down to 1,538 in FY22 from nearly 4,000 in FY19 but this drop corresponds with an overall decline in cases adjudicated by USCIS.[50] With better chances of approval than most other nationalities, Chinese nationals have strong incentives to apply for asylum as soon as they have the opportunity. As of 2024, there is a backlog of over 73,000 asylum cases involving Chinese nationals.[51]

Figure 4:

Data Source: EOIR Data, FY 2023. Chart created by FAIR.

In the event that an immigration judge denies the asylum request, the judge would then issue a final order of removal. However, in such cases, it is nearly impossible for the U.S. to deport Chinese nationals because their home country generally refuses to accept them. China has long been identified as one of the major “recalcitrant countries” which consistently block the deportation of their own nationals by refusing to issue travel documents or otherwise not accepting their return.[52] Deportations to China have to be specially arranged on charter flights at great expense to the U.S. government, and are rare enough that a single flight can make national headlines.[53] Case law generally prevents the U.S. government from holding illegal aliens that it cannot deport in custody for more than six months, so Chinese nationals can remain free indefinitely even if their asylum requests are rejected.[54]

Internal data obtained by the _New York Times_ in 2023 showed that 100,000 Chinese nationals living in the U.S. have final orders of removal, meaning that theoretically they should be deported immediately.[55] However, with fewer than 300 people deported to China from the U.S. in all of FY23, Chinese migrants are confident they will not face consequences if they enter illegally, no matter the ultimate result of their asylum case.[56] Many of them freely admit that their motives are economic (which is never grounds for asylum), with some able to achieve “monthly earnings now exceeding $10,000” as they wait for immigration court cases far in the future.[57] It remains to be seen whether the Trump administration’s renewed focus on recalcitrant countries can make a dent in this backlog.[58]

No matter how illegal aliens from China address their status, they can benefit from the same informal networks that helped most of them arrive in the first place. Immigration court case data, a proxy for illegal entries, show massive influxes of Chinese nationals into the traditional gateways of Los Angeles and New York City.[59] From there, illegal aliens from China can meet with long-established networks catering to new migrants that can place them in under-the-table jobs and informal housing across the country as they work off debt to smugglers.[60]

Chinese communities in the U.S. are ready-made networks for illegal aliens from China, giving them the ability to disappear and seek informal jobs and housing.[61] This means that Chinese illegal aliens are rarely long-term users of the shelter systems that have devastated the finances and public safety of local jurisdictions, especially “sanctuary cities.” However, other social problems caused by the fact that some Chinese migrants work for organized crime can occur.

### **Section 5: Drugs and Crime**

A weak border and weakly enforced immigration laws provide fertile conditions for foreign criminal actors to operate. Open borders allow people to be smuggled more easily by smugglers and traffickers, and overworked Border Patrol agents processing migrants (instead of being on the line) are less able to interdict drug smuggling.[62] But arguably the most dangerous type of criminality linked to Chinese criminal actors (and even the Chinese government) is the role Chinese actors play in the fentanyl trade.

One of the most immediate and pressing dangers of an unsecured border is the flooding of our communities with illicit and dangerous drugs. The deadliest drug currently being smuggled into the U.S. is fentanyl, and communist China is crucial in its manufacture, export and distribution. Fentanyl – whose street names include “China Girl” and “China Town” – is an extremely potent synthetic opioid that is 50 times stronger than heroin and 100 times stronger than morphine. A dose as small as 2mg – the equivalent of 5-7 grains of salt – is sufficient to kill one person.[63] In 2023, an estimated 74,702 Americans died from synthetic opioid overdoses, with fentanyl being the most prominent type.[64] Since 2020, around half of all U.S. drug deaths have been linked to synthetic opioids. Thus, it’s more suitable to say fentanyl is a poison, rather than suggesting these deaths are caused by an overdose.

**The Chinese Government Actively Supports Drug Smuggling into the U.S.**

China is the number one origin of chemicals that make fentanyl, and the Chinese government actively encourages and enables the deadly criminal enterprise through generous policy interventions. The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party emphasized this fact in a lengthy and detailed report it produced on the CCP and the fentanyl crisis. The report states, “[t]he PRC, under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), is the ultimate geographic source of the fentanyl crisis. Companies in China produce nearly all of illicit fentanyl precursors, the key ingredients that drive the global illicit fentanyl trade.”[65] These include directly subsidizing the manufacturing of fentanyl chemicals, providing tax rebates to companies producing them, and giving monetary grants and awards to companies that openly traffic fentanyl precursors.[66]

The CCP’s support for fentanyl has been described as part of an explicit geopolitical strategy. On March 23, 2023, a House of Representatives Subcommittee on National Security, Illicit Finance, and International Financial Institutions heard testimony that China was using drug smuggling to weaken the U.S. and its people.[67] The Chinese military also produced a treatise on effective tactics in asymmetrical warfare, which includes “drug warfare that cause[s] disasters in other countries and make[s] huge profits” as one such tactic, which is clearly being employed against American society now.[68]

**According to the Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party, the CCP plays a role in the fentanyl crisis in the following ways:**

*   The PRC Government fails to prosecute fentanyl precursors and synthetic narcotics manufacturers
*   The PRC Government has thwarted U.S. law enforcement investigations into illicit fentanyl manufacturers
*   The PRC allows the open sale of fentanyl precursors and other materials that fuel the fentanyl crisis on the extensively surveilled Chinese internet
*   The PRC government censors content about domestic drug sales—but leaves export-focused narcotics content untouched
*   The PRC aggressively prosecutes domestic drug trafficking but leaves illicit drug exports untouched

Source: Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party. The CCP’s Role in the Fentanyl Crisis.[69]

The Chinese government, running a totalitarian state with intensive mass surveillance, is in a position to stop fentanyl trafficking if it wished, but does not do so. The CCP tightly regulates the internet in China yet allows “the open sale of fentanyl precursors and other illicit materials on the extensively monitored and controlled PRC internet.”[70] As China expert Vanda Felbab-Brown pointed out, China’s “operational law enforcement cooperation tends to be highly selective, self-serving, limited, and subordinated to its geopolitical interests.” Felbab-Brown continued: “Chinese criminal groups cultivate political capital with Chinese authorities and government officials abroad by also promoting China’s political, strategic, and economic interests.”[71]

Also of interest is the relationship between the CCP and Chinese criminal gangs. Former Attorney General William Barr testified in front of the House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party in April 2024. He detailed how China works with criminal actors to produce illicit drugs and provide financing and laundering to criminal actors. He told Congress, “The CCP has been tightening its alliance with Chinese organized crime to gain influence outside of China, and U.S. law enforcement investigating Chinese money laundering have found evidence indicating that some schemes involve Chinese Government officials and Communist Party elites.”[72]

**The China-Mexican Cartel Alliance**

The nexus between the Chinese communist regime, Chinese organized crime, and Mexican cartels is a fact, and the danger posed by this unholy alliance is encouraged and amplified by open-borders policies. As part of this process, fentanyl precursor chemicals are shipped from communist China to Mexico, where the powerful drug cartels, mainly the Sinaloa Cartel and Cartel Jalisco Nueva Generación (CJNG), synthesize them in labs to produce finished fentanyl, or mix it into other drugs and pills, and attempt to traffic it into the U.S. to poison Americans.[73]

The weak policies pursued by the Mexican government have provided almost perfect operational conditions for the China-Cartel alliance to operate. The administration of former Mexican president Andres Manuel Lopez Obrador (known as AMLO), who served from 2018-2024, was very permissive towards cartel activity. AMLO disbanded anti-cartel units in the police, urged the U.S. not to launch anti-cartel activity and did not share data on drug seizures with the U.S. government.[74]These policies allowed the CCP-allied cartels to operate with much greater freedom and impunity.

In addition to fentanyl production, Chinese gangs have been running marijuana growing operations that have violated various legal requirements even in states where marijuana is now legal. In August 2023, a memo leaked from Border Patrol showed that illegal marijuana farms make Chinese criminal gangs over $4 billion annually.[75] One law enforcement source, speaking on the condition of anonymity to the press, said that gangs use Chinese nationals who have ongoing asylum claims to work for them, as it is harder to deport them.[76] Multiple U.S. states, both at the border and the interior, have been impacted.

In the summer of 2023, New Mexico law enforcement raided facilities where Chinese nationals were employed. As one media outlet described, they were part “of a new pipeline of migrants leaving China and making unauthorized border crossings into the United States via Mexico, and many are taking jobs at hundreds of cannabis farms springing up across the U.S.”[77] Other border states like California have also been impacted. In 2022 in Newberry Springs, California, a Chinese gang was charged with illegal marijuana distribution and environmental law breaking after a 14,000 square foot facility for drug production was discovered.[78]

Even America’s smallest towns, some thousands of miles from the border, have not been spared these marijuana operations run by Chinese gangs.[79] In Maine, for example, small communities with populations of barely a few thousand inhabitants have been targeted by Chinese marijuana operations. A leaked unclassified memo from DHS reportedly showed over 100 properties in Maine linked to Chinese marijuana gangs.[80] This situation is undercutting legal marijuana businesses in the state.[81] The situation prompted a U.S. Senator from Maine to write a letter to the DOJ urging a crackdown on CCP-linked marijuana operations in the state.[82]

In addition to drug smuggling, the smuggling of women and children has also flourished thanks to the cooperation between China and the cartels. Many times, the two activities increasingly go hand in hand and cannot be viewed as two distinct criminal operations, part of a model dubbed “poly-crime” by experts.[83] At the U.S.-Mexico border itself, according to cartel expert Robert Almonte, Chinese poly-crime organizations are collaborating with Mexican cartels in migrant smuggling by “paying the Mexican cartel some kind of a percentage, quota or tax.”[84]

Chinese criminal actors have been implicated in the sexual exploitation of women and girls who come to the U.S. illegally from China. Criminals recruit poor women in rural parts of China, promising them good jobs in the U.S., and then force them into prostitution in massage parlors once they arrive in the U.S.[85] In 2022, nine men were federally charged with 31 counts for running a prostitution ring that exploited female Chinese illegal aliens.[86] The gang was accused of shuttling women across multiple states for sex, and ruthlessly beating any women who resisted.[87]

Building a case against their abusers is tricky. Dominique Roe-Sepowitz, Director of the Office of Sex Trafficking Intervention Research (STIR) at Arizona State University, notes that some abuse victims “…may be too ashamed to leave and accept help, when it is normal in their culture to keep abuse a secret.”[88] A roundtable discussion at the Congressional Executive Commission on China heard testimony that some women from China feel a sense of familial obligation to accept sex work to support the family, complicating efforts to investigate it as it clashes with cultural notions of duty and filial obligation.[89]Periodic raids that break up brothels disguised as massage parlors only have a short-term impact.[90]

### **Section 6: National Security**

**China Exploits Our Open Borders and Generous Visa Policy**

Weak border security compounds the danger that China poses to U.S. national security. The FBI publicly considers the policies pursued by China’s authoritarian government to be “the greatest long-term threat to our nation’s information and intellectual property, and to our economic vitality.”[91] This threat is compounded by a 2017 Chinese law explicitly requiring Chinese citizens, in the country or abroad, to cooperate with government intelligence-gathering activities.[92] The Chinese government exploits the American immigration system in a number of ways to further its goals, clearly linking immigration to the largest national security issue America faces today.

The situation regarding China’s exploitation of America’s weak borders has alarmed lawmakers. In June 2023, 11 Members of Congress on the U.S. House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party co-signed a letter to then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin and then-FBI Director Christopher Wray expressing alarm at Chinese espionage.[93] In September 2023, five U.S. Senators sent a letter asking former Secretary of Homeland Security Alejandro Mayorkas to clarify what security measures were in place in response to increased Chinese activity at the U.S. border.[94] Most recently, in June 2024, James Comer, Chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability, wrote a 13-page letter to then-Secretary Mayorkas detailing China’s abuse of the U.S. immigration laws.[95]

**Key points of the Comer letter**

*   “Using a form of warfare meant to corrode the United States from within, the CCP has infiltrated U.S. critical infrastructure, bolstered the illicit drug trade globally, engaged in unlawful and exploitative immigration by infiltrating our borders(…).”
*   “Notably, some Chinese illegal immigrants ‘have been found to have links’ with the CCP and the PLA, and reportedly, ‘border chiefs are concerned more could be slipping through their grasp.’ DHS has been remiss in confronting the surge in Chinese nationals entering the United States via Mexico.”
*   “[S]ome Chinese nationals are brought to America and forced to work on marijuana-growing operations in which Chinese transnational criminal organizations are heavily involved.”
*   The CCP actively seeks to insert itself into Chinese diaspora communities to surveil and persecute dissidents, influence our political system, and otherwise work on behalf of Beijing’s hostile objectives.

One of the most widespread and brazen examples of Chinese exploitation of the American immigration system is the “gate-crasher” phenomenon. Up to 100 incidents have been documented of Chinese nationals accessing or attempting to access military bases and other sensitive sites.[96] These individuals, often posing as tourists, have been caught scuba-diving near secure satellite launch sites, using drones and taking pictures inside restricted areas on bases, and even sneaking off from White House tours to document security measures in place.[97] Sophisticated operations are even targeting military bases throughout the Arctic in Alaska, with individuals using drones and potentially leaving sensors behind.[98] When questioned, the trespassers often give heavily scripted responses claiming to be sightseers or claiming to be confused.[99]

Some of these “gate crasher” visitors actually hold genuine U.S. tourist visas – not only allowed in through the front door but also clearly violating the terms of their visa by engaging in acts of espionage. While consular officers are permitted to refuse visas to those whom they believe are using them as a cover to carry out espionage, the aliens can also be immediately subject to deportation.[100] However, in most cases the individuals are simply treated as trespassers and escorted out of the country rather than being prosecuted.[101] China can essentially make spying a numbers game, throwing enough “tourists” at sensitive sites that at least a few will come back with enough information to be worthwhile. Some of these individuals are recruited by the Chinese government because they are existing visa-holders, like the Chinese student sentenced to a year of prison in 2019 for taking photos of a Key West air base.[102]

Some of these gate crashers are not holders of genuine U.S. visas but instead illegal aliens who entered the U.S. illegally in order to target sensitive locations. In March 2024, a Chinese national was arrested after driving onto a Marine Corps base in Twentynine Palms, California, without presenting identification or responding to guards’ commands.[103] He was later confirmed to be an illegal alien by a Border Patrol Sector Chief.[104]

CBP statistics show 83 percent of Chinese illegal aliens who arrived at the U.S. border in FY 2024 were single adults, something that has raised concern in law enforcement circles.[105] Speaking about the surge in single adult Chinese encounters in February 2024, then-National Border Patrol Council President Brandon Judd expressed unease: “At best, they’re coming here for a better life or for a better job. At worst, they’re coming here to be part of the Chinese government, and that’s what scares me an awful lot.” Judd called on law enforcement agencies to properly vet migrants.[106]

By no means, however, is China limited to using illegal aliens in pursuit of its intelligence and espionage goals. The Chinese government has a long track record of using its own nationals with U.S. visas or legal status for espionage and intimidation purposes. This influence is especially pervasive in academia, where the Chinese government funded “Confucius Institutes” bound to follow Communist Chinese laws that attempted to influence university policies and interfere with free expression.[107] The more than 277,000 international students from China as of 2024 are corralled by Chinese government influence operations and kept under constant watch by their home country through operations like these.[108]

In addition to these overt methods, Chinese nationals studying at American universities or working in American research facilities frequently engage in state-directed espionage efforts to unlawfully export innovations or destroy American findings.[109] The FBI estimates the cost of counterfeit goods, software piracy, and trade secret theft by Chinese nationals at $225 to $600 billion annually.[110] China frequently engages in visa fraud by sending members of its military abroad as “students” to engage in this theft. In 2020, an eminent Harvard professor who was recruited by the Chinese government to secretly work for a technology development program was indicted alongside two such “student” military members, who smuggled military and biological research to China.[111] The hundreds of thousands of Chinese nationals admitted to the U.S. every year as international students give the Chinese government its pick of targets to recruit and develop, and a litany of examples have shown that’s exactly what it is doing.[112]

Government authorities are well aware of the threat that Chinese actors inside the U.S. pose to U.S. national security. The FBI’s aptly titled “The China Threat” page contains an exhaustive list of prosecutions of individuals illegally acting on behalf of the Chinese government.[113] Many of these operations involve Chinese officials traveling to the U.S. to conduct illegal harassment and stalking while acting as foreign agents, such as its “Operation Fox Hunt” campaigns against U.S.-based Chinese nationals.[114] As former FBI Director Christopher Wray stated in a 2023 indictment, “People might think of these as two separate dangers—one that threatens corporations and our workers’ livelihoods, and another that threatens individuals and their freedoms.But it’s all part of the same,single danger—the danger that a large country unconstrained by laws grinds down anyone who resists it.”[115]

**Conclusion**

The threats posed by open borders in general are magnified when exploited by a geopolitical rival with an unrepentant history of spying on and exploiting our nation. The sudden surge in Chinese nationals was almost certainly due to a perception in China and elsewhere that the U.S. border was effectively open to all, provided a migrant could have made it there in the first place. The Biden administration’s approach to U.S. border security was overly reliant on the cooperation of other countries in the Americas in this sense. While many of these migrants are economic migrants who used the formerly open border to skip the line and avoid legal migration, a certain proportion are criminal actors, and some may even be national security threats. While all illegal immigration is bad, the challenges posed by China are far more serious and the threat much more direct than with other countries of origin.

Even with a relatively secure southern border, however, the threat posed by international “students” and other legal entrants remains. Even though the Chinese government cannot necessarily use the southern border to infiltrate its agents, it has free rein to abuse the hundreds of thousands of its citizens with U.S. visas for its own gain. Increases in the number of visas given to China would only compound this risk, especially since the Chinese government has been proven to engage in visa fraud by concealing military personnel as “students” and “researchers.” The CCP is clearly willing to direct brazen schemes against the U.S. by exploiting our own immigration system’s weaknesses and blind spots.Allowing this to continue will place American innovation, prosperity, and ultimately lives at risk.
