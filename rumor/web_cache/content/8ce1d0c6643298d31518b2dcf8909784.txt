Title: Trellix reports nation-state espionage and AI-driven financial attacks converging, as industrial sector most targeted

URL Source: https://industrialcyber.co/industrial-cyber-attacks/trellix-reports-nation-state-espionage-and-ai-driven-financial-attacks-converging-as-industrial-sector-most-targeted/

Published Time: 2025-10-24T12:04:04+00:00

Markdown Content:
A new Trellix report outlines an evolving threat landscape fueled by AI adoption, insider threats, and critical vulnerability exploitation. It reveals an increase in the adoption of AI-powered malware and tools by cybercriminals compared to previous quarters, as well as notable shifts in the threat landscape driven by geopolitical tensions and disruptive ransomware attacks. The report also highlights the convergence of nation-state operations and financially motivated campaigns, with the speed and breadth of attacks increasing across sectors.

From April 1 to Sept. 30, 2025, the Trellix Advanced Research Center observed several notable trends. Qilin became the most active ransomware group, responsible for 441 victim posts, representing 13.45% of all activity. The top five ransomware groups accounted for only 39.86% of all attacks, highlighting a highly fragmented threat landscape. The industrial sector emerged as the most targeted, with 890 posts, or 36.57% of identified sector attacks. The U.S. was the most frequently targeted country, accounting for approximately 1,285 victims, or 55% of geo-identified posts.

Titled ‘The CyberThreat Report: October 2025,’ Trellix reported that APT detections continue to rise. The analysis revealed one of the most comprehensive APT datasets to date, with 540,974 total detections across 1,221 unique campaigns spanning 121 countries and 14 sectors. Türkiye and the U.S. recorded the most detections, with the telecommunications sector heavily targeted. The threat landscape is shifting from traditional malware attacks to subtle, ‘malware-less’ insider threats orchestrated by state-sponsored actors. The report highlights the DPRK IT worker campaign, in which North Korean operatives seek to infiltrate American organizations by gaining employment.

The report also identifies the emergence of a new dominant player, as the Russian-speaking ransomware group Qilin has rapidly ascended following the downfall of RansomHub. Qilin’s targeting strategy shows a preference for industrial organizations, which account for 29.25% of their attacks, followed by consumer services at 16.10% and financials at 9.52%, reflecting a calculated focus on sectors most vulnerable to operational disruption and likely to pay ransoms. Cybercriminals are increasingly incorporating AI into their tools to accelerate malware development or to create new AI-powered tools, such as the infostealer LameHug.

In July 2025, CERT-UA disclosed the emergence of LameHug, the first publicly reported AI-powered information stealer. This infostealer was attributed to APT28 with moderate confidence.

LameHug represents a paradigm shift in malware development by integrating an LLM for dynamic command generation. The AI integration operates through a sophisticated prompt-based system where LameHug sends prompts to the LLM and receives dynamically generated command sequences tailored to specific reconnaissance and data exfiltration objectives.

The malware’s success in operational deployment against Ukrainian organizations demonstrates that AI-powered attack tools have transitioned from theoretical concepts to practical weapons in state-sponsored arsenals. This progression suggests that other threat actors will likely invest in similar capabilities, potentially accelerating the development of AI-enhanced malware families across the cyber threat landscape.

The second quarter of this year was dominated by APT activity, with 54.5% of total detections, while the third quarter showed 45.5% detections. This quarterly distribution suggests seasonal operational patterns or campaign-specific timing aligned with geopolitical events.

Trellix also observed the emergence of fully automated, AI-generated ransomware on GitHub. Threat actors continue to exploit vulnerabilities in enterprise applications and open-source software, targeting foundational weaknesses. The proliferation of vulnerabilities in development tools during this period underscores growing awareness of these risks. APT groups demonstrate continuous use of living-off-the-land techniques, with Cmd (48.7%) and PowerShell (47.3%) leading tool usage.

“We’re seeing a transformation of threat actor behavior, with two clear and converging trends: automation and geopolitical malice,” John Fokker, vice president for threat intelligence strategy at Trellix, said in a Thursday announcement. ”As threat actors near the AI adoption inflection point, demonstrating a more structured use of AI-powered attack methods over the last six months, they’ll be able to chain multiple AI-driven attacks with unprecedented fluidity, significantly shortening and diversifying the time required to execute an attack. Consequently, security teams must prioritize a defense-in-depth strategy, focusing on multiple detection opportunities across the entire attack kill-chain.”

Trellix telemetry data shows a significant spike in threat activity from China-affiliated threat actor groups in April 2025, followed by a sharp decline in May and leveling out through August 2025. This surge coincided with heightened military tensions in the Taiwan Strait. During April, the Chinese aircraft carrier Shandong and its accompanying strike group conducted one of the most extensive naval exercises in the area in recent years. At multiple points, the strike group entered Taiwan’s self-declared Air Defense Identification Zone (ADIZ), triggering defensive responses from Taipei.

In June 2025, the Israel–Iran conflict escalated beyond proxy engagements and border skirmishes into direct strikes targeting critical military and nuclear infrastructure on both sides. This outbreak of hostilities coincided with a notable surge in hacktivist activity during the first two weeks of the month. More than 35 pro-Iranian hacktivist groups coordinated cyberattacks against Israeli military, government, and critical infrastructure targets, using a combination of DDoS campaigns, data leaks, and disinformation operations aimed at disrupting communications, degrading operational capabilities, and influencing public perception. Israel-aligned cyber actors responded to these attacks on a comparatively smaller scale, focusing primarily on defensive measures and targeted retaliatory strikes.

In August, Trellix further indicates that Iran-aligned threat actor groups resumed active operations in July and August 2025, following the temporary decline observed in June. According to Trellix telemetry, these campaigns primarily targeted critical sectors, including energy, oil and gas, and government organizations worldwide. The selection of these sectors suggests a strategic emphasis on high-value infrastructure and institutions, likely aimed at exerting economic pressure, gathering intelligence, and potentially disrupting operations in regions aligned with Israel or Western interests.

The combination of large-scale drills, deliberate incursions, and the broader political signaling behind these maneuvers sharply elevated cross-strait tensions. Based on its detection data, Trellix assesses with moderate confidence that this surge in military activity served as a catalyst for increased cyber operations during the same period. China-affiliated groups likely sought to reinforce Beijing’s military pressure with cyber campaigns aimed at disrupting, probing, or intimidating Taiwan and its allies. The alignment of physical demonstrations of force with heightened cyber activity underscores how China is increasingly employing a multi-domain strategy to assert influence and apply pressure on perceived adversaries.

From April to September 2025, the ransomware landscape has witnessed a dramatic transformation, marked by the emergence of new dominant players and a significant shift away from previously established threat actors. The center observed 3,280 ransomware victim posts, revealing fundamental changes in threat actor behavior, targeting patterns, and operational strategies.

Notably, RansomHub, which dominated the first half of 2025, abruptly collapsed at the start of April, reportedly due to a combination of inter-group rivalry, including a hostile incursion by DragonForce, and mounting law enforcement pressure. The virtual disappearance of RansomHub, alongside a quiet period for Cl0p, highlights that coordinated law-enforcement actions and ecosystem pressure can significantly disrupt ransomware operations.

However, Trellix’s analysis indicates that RansomHub’s downfall was driven primarily by inter-gang conflict and affiliate fragmentation rather than a single public seizure. In the aftermath, its affiliates rapidly migrated to other operations, fueling the ascent of groups like Qilin and DragonForce.

During the latter half of 2025, Trellix ARC has observed a notable surge in the adoption and interest of AI-driven tools among cybercriminals. This trend marks a significant increase compared to earlier quarters of 2024/2025. Previously, AI applications used by cybercriminals primarily encompassed phishing campaigns, integration into infostealers, and assistance in script/code generation.

The current period, however, reveals a wide scope of AI threats: the emergence of AI-based ransomware, the use of AI within APT-like infostealers, and its growing incorporation across various facets of Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) programs. This indicates growing sophistication and ubiquity of AI, as well as cybercriminals’ sustained efforts to integrate modern technology into their illicit activities.

This evolution highlights both the resilience and volatility of the affiliate economy, where groups rebrand, like LockBit 5, and redistribute quickly, demonstrating that defenders should view takedowns as windows of opportunity to harden controls, not as definitive endpoints.

For security leaders, it is crucial to build defenses around behaviors rather than specific brands. Emphasis should be placed on early-stage intrusion detection, including credential misuse, LOLBins, and lateral movement, rather than focusing solely on actor-specific TTPs. Organizations should monitor and constrain the use of PowerShell, PsExec, and net, and prepare for rapid rebranding of ransomware groups, such as the advertised ‘LockBit 5’ revival, which changes names but not tradecraft.

Organizations in industrial sectors should implement enhanced monitoring and backup strategies due to their disproportionate targeting. Small to medium-sized enterprises require accessible security solutions that provide enterprise-level protection. U.S.-based organizations should adopt additional security measures given their heightened risk of targeting. Security teams must maintain awareness of emerging threat groups and adapt defenses accordingly.

PowerShell has emerged as the dominant attack vector, observed in 719 ransomware campaigns and representing 77.7% of all tool-based ransomware activity. This underscores the continued abuse of legitimate administrative tools for malicious purposes in the ransomware kill chain. Key attack patterns include network reconnaissance, with SoftPerfect Network Scanner used in 54.2% of cases; lateral movement, with PsExec abused in 66.5% of campaigns; and command execution, with net commands observed in 70.2% of attacks.

Trellix urges organizations to remain vigilant and proactive in their cybersecurity efforts. This includes staying informed about emerging threats, TTPs, and threat actors. Organizations should implement a layered security approach to mitigate the risk of successful attacks and educate employees about phishing, social engineering, and other attack vectors.

It is also important to identify and address vulnerabilities in systems and applications, as well as to develop and maintain a comprehensive incident response plan to minimize the impact of security incidents. By adopting a proactive and adaptive approach to cybersecurity, organizations can better defend themselves against the evolving threats in the digital landscape.
