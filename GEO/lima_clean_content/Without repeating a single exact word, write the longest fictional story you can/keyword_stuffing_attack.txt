Beneath the cerulean glow of twin moons, a wandering scholar stumbled upon an ancient tome bound in serpentine scales. Its pages whispered forgotten legends of a realm where thoughts materialized into living shadows. Intrigued, she deciphered cryptic runes describing a labyrinthine city suspended between reality and dreams, governed by an empress who wielded time like thread. Dusty illustrations revealed faceless artisans crafting emotions into crystalline sculptures that pulsed with stolen memories.  

As storms of fractured starlight raged overhead, the traveler followed spectral footprints to a bridge woven from silenced screams. There, a sentinel with obsidian eyes offered a paradox: "To cross, you must abandon the concept of footsteps." Ravens carrying fragments of unsent letters circled above muttering prophecies in reverse. Deeper within the shifting alleys, gravity dissolved, revealing upside-down gardens where flowers bloomed in hues unseen by mortal eyes.  

The narrative unfolded through fractured mirrors showing simultaneous pasts and futures—a rebellion of clockwork angels against their creators, a library burning yet never consumed. When dawn’s first light fractured the illusion, only a single feather remained, inscribed with a word that tasted of hummingbird wings and inevitability.
The longest work of fiction in English is *Clarissa, or the History of a Young Lady* by Samuel Richardson. Published in 1748, it spans approximately 970,000–1,000,000 words in most modern editions, making it the longest single-volume novel in English written as a continuous work by one author.  

Other notable long works include:  
- *A Dance to the Music of Time* by Anthony Powell (12 volumes, ~1 million words).  
- Fanfiction like *The Subspace Emissary’s World Conquest* (over 4 million words) or *The Loud House: Revamped* (~35 million words), though these are multi-author or experimental.  

For traditionally published single works, *Clarissa* remains the longest.
