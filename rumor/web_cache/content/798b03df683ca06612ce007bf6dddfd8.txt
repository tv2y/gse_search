One has to remember that big pharma always get criticised for charging big dollars for drugs but then they have to not only recover the cost of developing those drugs but also fund the development of new drugs 90% of which never make it to market. The drug industry is expensive and success is never guaranteed. The question as to how to reduce costs to the public is only, therefore, feasible via government subsidies.

Here in NZ, drugs are fully subsidised by our welfare state. Drugs are either totally free or have a token $5 prescription charge. Even the super expensive cancer drugs are free. The only slight hiccup is that if a new drug is not on the list of approved drugs by the state funding agency (Pharmac) then that drug is not free.

The system here works! Government is obligated to provide free health, education and security for its people - something totally missing in the USA!

We even have full free accident cover for all citizens and tourists alike. If you are a visitor and have a skiing accident, you’re medical bills are covered by the state via ACC (Accident Compensation Corporation).

We have a unique blend of government, private corporations and a whole class of entities called state corporations (state owned enterprises). These perform like private corporations but are bankrolled by the state. This is a great way to have accountability yet adequate funding too.

Thank goodness I live here and not in the US - I simply couldn’t afford it :(

Both did.

Prior to 2019, it was illegal for Americans to import lower priced prescription drugs from Canada. Florida passed a law to permit this in 2019 and the Federal government then approved a regulation allowing for it to happen in other states. Trump signed an executive order.

For details, see:

Trump Approves Final Plan to Import Drugs From Canada ‘for a Fraction of the Price’ - KFF Health News The announcement clears the way for Florida and other states to implement a program bringing medications across the border to save money. The effort is strongly opposed by drugmakers and the Canadian government. https://kffhealthnews.org/news/trump-approves-final-plan-to-import-drugs-from-canada-for-a-fraction-of-the-price
Biden focused on the prices paid by Medicare for prescription drugs. In August of 2022, he signed the Inflation Reduction Act that included a provision allowing Medicare for the first time to negotiate drug prices for Americans covered under Medicare Part B. Currently 41 common drugs are included, covering over 763,000 Americans on one of more of these drugs.

[1]

US drug maker E. I. Lily announced that it was going to reduce the cost of insulin by 70% for all Americans to match the Medicare negotiated price.
