OLD FORT, TENN. — Polk County authorities say they discovered several explosives in a man's home after threatening to kill public officials and law enforcement.

Polk County deputies arrived at 129 Amber Way Friday morning to locate Kevin O'Neal, who had active warrants for threatening to kill public officials and members of law enforcement in the county, according to a press release.

According to an affidavit we obtained, those threats were made on O'Neal's social media pages. He's now facing five counts of harassment.

In one post he made on Facebook in July, O'Neal writes "Steve Ross, Josh Stroud you better get your Wiccan Snitch out of my life or you can die with em."

O'Neal Facebook post

In another post he wrote "I will die killing you before I take comfort in life surrendering to your wishes. I consider it an honor to kill each of you and to give my life in the process of the battle against you."

According to Polk County Sheriff Steve Ross, deputies noticed something smoldering in the bedroom where O'Neal was arrested. Officers found what they believed to be an improvised explosive device (IED).

> "They immediately took him into custody. And as they were doing, they saw something smoldering there in the floor, smoking, and they immediately, when they looked at it and saw that it looked like it was some type of IED. They immediately exited with him and the female that they had confronted there at the door and left the residence." Said Sheriff Ross.

The Tennessee Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) and the Chattanooga Police's Bomb Squad responded to the scene. According to the arrest report, deputies found 14 explosive devices in the room where they found O'Neal. Some were stuffed inside a backpack, with others scattered on the floor. They also found materials to make more bombs.

Deputies also say they found a woman hiding in the dryer while searching the house.

O'Neal later told officers he planned to ignite the explosives as officers approached the house. The affidavit says if they had gone off, shrapnel would have traveled 2500 feet; putting nine deputies and two women inside the home in serious danger.

> "The ATF said that it was a it was very potent, and it was built correctly, somehow or another, by the grace of God Almighty, that it just, it didn't, he didn't get it set off." Ross said.

O'Neal faces a total of 11 counts of Attempted First Degree murder. 9 of those counts are for the officers who were on scene for the arrest, and the other two counts are for the two people who were in the house with O'Neal as he was being arrested.

He is also facing 14 counts of Prohibited weapons charges, and one charge for Possession of Explosive Components.

While Sheriff Ross says the whole incident has left him shaken, he says he's proud of his deputies.

> "It humbles me that my guys you see just how close things could've went really, really bad. I commend my deputies they'll do whatever they gotta do to protect the public."
