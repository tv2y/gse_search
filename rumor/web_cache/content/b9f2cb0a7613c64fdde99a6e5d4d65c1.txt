Mike Pence Accepts Nomination for VP at the 2016 RNC
====================================================

Christian. Conservative. Republican. In that order.

By Joshua Eaton

July 21, 2016

DOMINICK REUTER/AFP/Getty

Vice-presidential nominee and Indiana Governor Mike Pence introduced himself to the nation during a speech on the third night of the Republican National Convention in Cleveland.

"I'm a Christian, a conservative and a Republican, in that order," Pence said, to applause from the convention floor.

That wasn't always the case. Pence spoke about growing up as a Catholic Democrat idolizing Democratic President John F. Kennedy before being swayed by the "Reagan revolution" of Republican President Ronald Reagan in the 1980s.

Pence hit all the points you'd expect from a Republican vice-presidential nominee, bashing presumptive Democratic nominee Hillary Clinton for her handling of foreign policy and criticizing President Barack Obama on the economy. He singled out blue-collar workers, African Americans, and Hispanics for special attention, saying the Obama Administration has failed them and that a Donald Trump presidency would fight for their needs.

The African American and Hispanic parts of that formula could be a hard sell given Trump's distinct lack of love for undocumented Mexican immigrants and Black Lives Matter protestors.

As usual, Twitter had some things to say about Pence's outreach efforts.

"You know, the choice couldn't be more clear," Pence said of Clinton. "Americans could choose someone who literally personifies the failed establishment in Washington, D.C. Or we can vote for a leader who will fight every day to make American great again. It's change versus status quo."

Other major speakers on Wednesday night included three of Trump's former opponents for the nomination, Wisconsin Governor Scott Walker, Florida Senator Marco Rubio, and Texas Senator Ted Cruz.

In many ways, Cruz stole the show, speaking much longer than scheduled and waxing eloquent about American freedom. "America is more than just a land mass between two oceans," Cruz told the convention audience. "America is an ideal. A simple, but powerful, idea: freedom matters." But Cruz also left the stage without endorsing Trump, instead telling his listeners to vote their conscience.

That drew boos and chants of "endorse Trump" from the crowd — leaving no doubts about whose convention it was, no matter who was on stage.
