**Gettysburg Address**  
**Delivered by:** Abraham Lincoln, the 16th U.S. President  
**Date:** November 19, 1863  
**Location:** Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, at the dedication of Soldiers' National Cemetery (now Gettysburg National Cemetery)  
**Occasion:** Followed the Battle of Gettysburg (July 1–3, 1863), the deadliest battle of the American Civil War, with over 50,000 casualties.  

**Key Details:**  
- Lincoln’s speech is one of the most famous and historically significant in American history, despite its brevity (271 words, lasting under two minutes).  
- Attended by approximately 15,000 people, the speech honored Union soldiers who died defending principles of liberty and equality.  

**Notable Excerpts:**  
- Begins by referencing the Declaration of Independence: "Four score and seven years ago our fathers brought forth on this continent, a new nation, conceived in Liberty, and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal."  
- Describes the Civil War as a test of whether such a nation "can long endure."  
- Concludes with the iconic line: "that government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth."  

**Legacy:**  
- Initially met with mixed reactions but later recognized as a defining statement of American democracy and purpose.  
- Etched into the Lincoln Memorial and frequently memorized by schoolchildren.  
- Inspired future speeches, including Martin Luther King Jr.’s "I Have a Dream."  

**Manuscripts:**  
Five known versions exist, with the "Bliss copy" (signed and dated by Lincoln) considered the standard text.  

**Context:**  
- Lincoln spoke after a two-hour oration by Edward Everett, whose lengthy speech was typical for cemetery dedications at the time.  
- The address transformed the battlefield’s significance into a symbol of national unity and sacrifice.
