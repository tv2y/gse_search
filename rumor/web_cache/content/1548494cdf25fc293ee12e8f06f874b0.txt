Introduction
------------

Combating natural disasters is a perpetual challenge essential for human survival and development. In recent years, amidst increasing global climate uncertainty, the severity of disasters such as extreme weather, earthquakes, tsunamis, and water shortages has escalated. Concurrently, accelerated human production activities have led to more frequent flood events, which inflict substantial damage on human life, property, and the ecological environment. The United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change's 2022 report, "Climate Change 2022: Mitigating Climate Change," highlights that global land precipitation has risen since the 1950s, and the likelihood of multiple extreme weather events occurring consecutively or simultaneously has increased (Li et al., 2022). These low-probability, high-impact events carry obvious potential consequences (Murphy et al., 2021). Moreover, human influence is likely to enhance the probability of multifactorial extreme weather events, and human actions will significantly shape future climate trends. According to the World Meteorological Organization, over the past 50 years, there have been more than 11,000 extreme natural disaster events related to climate and precipitation, with urban flooding becoming increasingly frequent. Notably, China has emerged as one of the countries most affected by urban flooding (Zaqout et al., 2023). For example, from July 17 to 23, 2021, Zhengzhou City in Henan Province experienced severe floods triggered by heavy rainfall, resulting in significant threats to life and property. An investigation by the State Council's "7.20″ heavy rain disaster team reported that the disaster impacted approximately 14.786 million people across 150 counties in Henan Province, with 398 people reported dead or missing and direct economic losses totaling 120.06 billion yuan (Hongchang & Hengde, 2022). The urban areas of Zhengzhou were among the hardest hit, with street flooding sweeping away pedestrians and vehicles, and extensive submersion of underground spaces. According to the "Investigation Report on the '7·20′ Heavy Rainstorm Disaster in Zhengzhou City, Henan Province," this flood disaster, caused by extreme rainfall events, posed a grave threat to human society (Chen et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2022). The "7.20″ disaster in Zhengzhou was characterized as both a "natural" and a "man-made" disaster. This calamity has served as a wake-up call globally, underlining the importance of analyzing flood disaster influencers and assessing flood risks. In response to the extreme rainstorm in Zhengzhou on July 20, 2021, some scholars have examined the characteristics and causes of this historically rare rainstorm and flood disaster from the perspectives of water vapor conditions, topography, and other factors (Liao et al., 2012; Hsu et al., 2023). Some scholars have analyzed the recurrence period, spatiotemporal distribution, and formation mechanisms of the extreme rainstorm in Zhengzhou on July 20 (Xiaodong et al., 2021). Their findings indicate that the heavy rainfall event in Zhengzhou, Henan Province, on July 20 demonstrated significant extremes in cumulative precipitation, heavy rainfall coverage, daily rainfall, and hourly rainfall intensity (Yang et al., 2021), revealing the evolution patterns of short-duration intense rainfall in Zhengzhou. However, there remains a need to enhance quantitative attribution analysis of this extreme event, as most existing research on flood disasters focuses primarily on precipitation processes, intensity, and climate factors. Few studies address the impact of human activities on extreme flood events. A comprehensive diagnosis of flood disasters, including specific event analysis and an assessment of weather patterns, hydrological conditions, and human influences, is essential for accurately understanding flood disaster characteristics.

A flood disaster is a broad term encompassing waterlogging, flooding, and related secondary disasters. It is one of the primary natural disasters impacting urban social and economic development (Liu & Shi, 2001). Research on the causes of urban flood disasters began in the 1750s, with domestic and international scholars investigating the mechanisms, formation conditions, and dynamics of flood disasters. As research has progressed, attribution analysis of extreme flood events has emerged as a new frontier in climate change and disaster studies. Diagnosing flood disasters for specific extreme events involves analyzing hydrological conditions and influencing factors (Jiahui et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2006). Current methods for urban flood disaster risk assessment fall into four main categories: the historical behavior method, comprehensive index evaluation, remote sensing imagery, and scenario simulation. The historical behavior method relies on long-term historical data to identify risk patterns and evaluate flood risk (He et al., 2023). The most widely used model constructs disaster-causing, disaster-incubating, and disaster-bearing factors based on the statistical framework of the comprehensive index evaluation method. These models apply techniques such as the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) (Han et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2019), entropy weight method, Bayesian network, principal component analysis, and game theory, or combinations of these, to calculate and integrate the weights of various indicators for flood risk (Zuo et al., 2021). The limitations of this approach include the selection of flood factors and the assignment of flood factor weights, which are determined to minimize human intervention. Additionally, combining the indicator system with a geographic information system (GIS) relies on large volumes of multi-source data (Lai et al., 2021; Guoru et al., 2015; Liu & Shi, 2001; Wu et al., 2006; Xie et al., 2006). The scenario simulation method, which visualizes and simulates regional disaster conditions by designing different scenarios, is widely used in urban flood risk research. Using mathematical models and computer simulations, urban flood risks are assessed from a spatial perspective. Researchers employ methods such as the Monte Carlo method, projection pursuit, and genetic algorithms to predict and evaluate flood disasters (Wang & Xu, 2017; Hu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2020). The historical disaster method and indicator system method offer fast calculation speeds but cannot capture the spatial variability of regional flood risks. While the remote sensing image method can rapidly identify flooded areas, it lacks key information such as water depth and flow rate, making it most suitable for rapid post-disaster assessments (Xie et al., 2006; Chen et al., 2010; Jin et al., 1998; Su et al., 2015). Despite significant progress in flood risk assessment, two main challenges remain. (1) Risk assessment results often show relatively high false alarm rates, missed alarm rates, and overall uncertainty; (2) there is limited research on integrating different methods to enhance the accuracy and reliability of flood risk assessment results. The incompleteness of risk assessment indicators has somewhat constrained the accuracy of these results. Given the varying environmental conditions across regions and differences in study area sizes, flood factors must often be manually selected. Moreover, to improve assessment accuracy, it is essential to account for the effects of uncertain factors such as climate change, land use change, and human impact (Liangliang and Xiaosai, 2022).

In this study, we first monitor precipitation during the July 20 flood disaster in Zhengzhou and extract flood points, analyzing the extreme nature of this event by statistically evaluating monthly precipitation data from Zhengzhou Station over the past 60 years. Second, we examine the event from the perspectives of natural and human factors, constructing an analysis module based on RS and GIS, and using geographic detectors to identify key influencing factors and their interactions in the "July 20″ flood disaster in Zhengzhou. We quantitatively assess the impact of disaster incubation, disaster causation, and disaster bearing factors to better understand the interactions and underlying drivers of these influences. Finally, the analysis results obtained by geographic detectors are used as the weight source for the evaluation index, and a weighted comprehensive evaluation method calculates the risk assessment values for Zhengzhou's main urban area. Streets and communities at higher risk are statistically identified, and accuracy is verified through comparison with flood waterlogging points, enabling further analysis of spatial differentiation in flood risk. This study addresses two primary objectives: First, it integrates three flood risk assessment methods—historical behavior method, comprehensive index evaluation method, and remote sensing image method—and applies geographic detectors to quantitatively analyze the impact of various factors and their interactions on flood risk, identifying the primary and interactive influences of flood disaster factors. Second, based on the geographic background of the study area, we use geographic detector results as objective weight assignments for risk assessment to minimize human intervention in weighting. We conduct a risk assessment for Zhengzhou's main urban area, verified using flood disaster data points, and propose tailored measures for disaster prevention and mitigation in Zhengzhou's main urban area.
