### Dissertation Theme:  
"A Revolutionary Idea: Gilbert Stuart Paints Sarah Morton as the First Woman of Ideas in American Art"  

### Key Points:  
1. **Focus**: Examines Gilbert Stuart's three portraits of Sarah Wentworth Apthorp Morton (1759–1846), a prominent writer and salonnière in early America. The portraits are:  
   - *Worcester Portrait*  
   - *Winterthur Portrait*  
   - *Boston Portrait*  

2. **Argument**:  
   - Stuart’s portraits depict Morton not just as a socialite or muse but as an intellectual—the first "woman of ideas" in American art.  
   - The paintings challenge 18th-century conventions of female representation by emphasizing her intellect ("lines of Reason") alongside her beauty ("lines of Grace").  

3. **Context**:  
   - Morton was a celebrated poet, essayist, and hostess in Boston’s literary circles, contributing to early American cultural identity.  
   - Her life was marked by personal scandal (her husband’s affair with her sister, Fanny, who later died by suicide).  
   - Despite societal constraints on women, Morton carved a public role through writing, salons, and strategic alliances.  

4. **Artistic Significance**:  
   - The *Worcester Portrait* (1800–1803) is the most radical, showing Morton lifting a veil—a metaphor for revealing her intellect.  
   - The portraits reflect a collaborative relationship between artist and sitter, merging portraiture with intellectual expression.  

5. **Broader Implications**:  
   - Explores tensions between public and private spheres, gender roles, and the intersection of art, literature, and reputation in post-Revolutionary America.  

### Conclusion:  
Stuart’s portraits of Morton redefined female representation in American art, positioning her not merely as an object of beauty but as a thinking, creative force—a revolutionary assertion for women in the early republic.
