Hillary Clinton defends 2016 'basket of deplorables' comment: 'Too kind a word'

by RAY LEWIS | The National Desk

Thursday, Sep 26th 2024

Former U.S. Secretary of State Hillary Clinton speaks onstage during the first day of the Democratic National Convention at the United Center on August 19, 2024 in Chicago, Illinois. (Photo by Justin Sullivan/Getty Images)

WASHINGTON (TND) —

Hillary Clinton in an op-ed published Wednesday defended her 2016 comment that half of former President Donald Trump’s supporters could be put into a “basket of deplorables.”

ADVERTISEMENT

Clinton wrote in The Washington Post that while her remark at a presidential campaign event was an “unfortunate choice of words and bad politics,” it “got at an important truth.”

Read More

> Just look at everything that has happened in the years since, from Charlottesville to Jan. 6," she wrote. “The masks have come off, and if anything, ‘deplorable’ is too kind a word for the hate and violent extremism we’ve seen from some Trump supporters.”

In 2017, protesters rallied in Charlottesville, Virginia against the removal of a statue of Robert E. Lee, a general for the Confederate States of America. The rally resulted in the deaths of three people and injuries for dozens.

Trump, who was president at the time, defended some of the protesters as "very fine people."

On Jan. 6, 2021, the former president urged his supporters to go to the Capitol to protest the certification of the results of the 2020 election, which he lost to President Joe Biden. Some protesters broke into the building, with more than 140 police officers being injured.

Clinton wrote in the op-ed that part of her would say it is “objectively true” that some of Trump’s supporters, the ones she grouped into the “basket of deplorables,” are “irredeemable,” as she called them at the campaign event.

> Just look at the lack of remorse from many of the Jan. 6 insurrectionists who’ve been convicted of sedition and other crimes,” Clinton said.

The former presidential candidate added that she wished people in 2016 listened to the rest of her remarks, when she discussed another “basket” of Trump’s supporters who felt that nobody cared about them. The U.S. needs to understand and empathize with those people too, according to Clinton.

“That’s especially true because many are living with unresolved trauma in their lives,” she wrote.
