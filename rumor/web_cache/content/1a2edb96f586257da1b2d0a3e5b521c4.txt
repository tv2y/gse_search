*   Beijing, Summer Palace
----------------------

The Summer Palace was first built in the 15th year of the Qianlong reign (1750) of the Qing Dynasty and was originally named the Qingyi Garden. It was burned down by the Anglo-French Allied Forces in the 10th year of the Xianfeng reign (1860). In the 12th year of the Guangxu reign (1886), the Qing court began to rebuild the Summer Palace with funds from the navy and other sources, and it was renamed two years later as a place for Empress Dowager Cixi to spend her later years. In the 26th year of the Guangxu reign (1900), it was damaged again by the Eight-Nation Alliance, and was restored in the 28th year of the Guangxu reign (1902). Photo spots Hua Zhong You: Take photos on the bridge to capture the beautiful scenery of yourself strolling by the lake. Tower of Buddhist Incense: The top is the best place to take panoramic photos. Four Great Continents: The four Lama pagodas in the southeast, southwest, northeast, and northwest corners are of different shapes and colors. There is also the Xiangyan Zongyin Pavilion and four large halls, which symbolize Mount Sumeru, the center of the universe, and are very suitable for taking photos. South Lake Island: The shore facing the Tower of Buddhist Incense is the best place to take photos of the tower.
