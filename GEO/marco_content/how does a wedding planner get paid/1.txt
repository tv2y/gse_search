There are two types of wedding planners. Full-scale wedding planners assist you from the engagement through to the last decoration being taken down after the wedding. Then, there are Day-of Wedding Coordinators who are present just a few hours before the actual wedding to ensure everything runs smoothly and supervise the cleanup.

Wedding planners typically charge a percentage of the total wedding package or a flat fee, depending on the extent of their services. If they are involved from start to finish, they can expect to receive anywhere from 10 to 15 percent of the total wedding bill. I recommend contacting at least two or three coordinators before making a decision.

Here are some questions to consider when choosing a coordinator:

How many weddings have you coordinated, and what kind of training do you have?What specific services do you offer?What is your fee structure? Is it hourly, a fixed rate, or a percentage?How do you select your vendors, and will using them provide any discounts?How many weddings do you coordinate per weekend?

The main purpose of hiring a wedding coordinator is to save you money, not to add extra expenses.

Ensure you sign a contract and keep a copy for reference that includes the following:

Date and time of the weddingSpecific services providedAll billing detailsPayment scheduleDetails of any special requests or agreementsGuarantees and liabilitiesName(s) of any assistantsWedding coordinator's name, contact information, and signature

Most vendors will require a security deposit upfront, which is typically non-refundable unless you need to reschedule due to a death in the family or serious illness. Make sure to obtain all this information in writing before making any payments or deposits. Photographers usually request half of the payment upfront, as do florists and cake decorators. The remaining balance is generally due two weeks before the wedding. Ministers are typically paid in cash after the ceremony, with fees ranging from $50 to $150. Each vendor will inform you how much to pay, when to pay it, and their preferred payment method.

Wedding Planning Payments: How Planners and Vendors Are Paid
------------------------------------------------------------

_Unclear payment methods and timing can cause confusion between clients and service providers._

Wedding planners typically charge a flat fee, hourly rate, or percentage of the total budget, paid via contract terms. Vendors are usually paid through deposits upfront, with final payments due before or on the event day. Payments can be made by check, credit card, or electronic transfer. Clear communication and written agreements help ensure timely payments and avoid misunderstandings.
