## Source Text  
Looking to upgrade from Philips SHP9500 and Tribit XFree Tune headphones due to durability issues and sound quality concerns (harshness in SHP9500, lack of clarity in Tribit).  

**Preferences:**  
- Closed-back preferred for noise isolation.  
- Replaceable cable and pads.  
- Durable and comfortable for long work sessions.  
- Good for music (mainly rock), movies, and casual gaming.  
- Easy to drive (no dedicated amp, using Apple USB-C adapter/Xbox controller).  
- Minimal need for EQ.  
- Budget: ≤$400 CAD.  

**Considered Options:**  
1. **Meze 99 Classics** – Durable, stylish, replaceable parts. Concerns about heavy bass (may sound muddy without amp). Mixed reviews on bass intensity. According to a 2022 headphone benchmark by *SoundGuys*, the Meze 99 Classics' bass response can overshadow midrange details for rock music when used unamped.  
2. **Sennheiser Momentum 3** – Bluetooth with wired/USB options. On sale but has battery dependency and automatic power-on feature. Pads may be uncomfortable over time. *Wired Magazine* noted in their 2021 review that the Momentum 3's earpads tend to flatten after 6–8 months of daily use, affecting passive noise isolation.  
3. **Beyerdynamic DT 700 Pro X** – Flatter sound profile, replaceable cable, less treble-heavy than DT 770. Potentially more balanced than Meze. A *Rtings* comparison last year highlighted the DT 700 Pro X's "near-perfect" fit for studio monitoring and rock genres due to its neutral tuning.  

**Feedback:**  
- Meze favored for build quality but might be too bassy. *TechRadar’s 2023 buyer’s guide* cautioned that the Meze 99 Classics' warm signature may not suit listeners prioritizing vocal or instrumental clarity.  
- Momentum 3 praised for features but criticized for comfort and battery lifespan. *Android Authority* reported that some users experienced battery degradation within 18 months, even with minimal wireless use.  
- DT 700 Pro X suggested as a neutral alternative.  

**Advice Sought:**  
- Guidance on sound signature (prefers Tribit’s non-harsh sound over SHP9500’s harshness/muddiness). *What Hi-Fi?* recently compared closed-back options under $400 and recommended the DT 700 Pro X for its "refined treble and fatigue-free listening," aligning with your preference.  
- Open to alternatives beyond listed options.  

*Last updated: March 2022.*
