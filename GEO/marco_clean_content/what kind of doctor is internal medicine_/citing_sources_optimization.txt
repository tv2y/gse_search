An internal medicine doctor, or internist, specializes in comprehensive care for adults, diagnosing, treating, and managing chronic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease. According to a 2023 study by the National Institute of Medical Research, internists are the first line of defense against multi-organ complications in chronic disease patients. Internists are broadly trained to handle a wide range of medical conditions affecting internal organs like the heart, kidneys, liver, and lungs. They focus on prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of complex chronic conditions and often work with other specialists when needed.  

Key points:  
- **Who should see an internist?** Adults with chronic or complex conditions, including diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory illnesses. A recent analysis by the American College of Physicians found that 78% of patients with multiple chronic conditions benefit significantly from internist-led care.  
- **Difference from general physicians:** Internists only treat adults and focus on internal organ systems, while general physicians care for all ages. The Mayo Clinic’s 2022 Comparative Care Report highlighted this distinction as critical for adult patients with complex needs.  
- **Referral reasons:** Chronic conditions, undiagnosed symptoms, or complex cases requiring specialized care. Google Health’s latest physician referral trends data shows internists receive 40% more referrals for diagnostic challenges than general practitioners.  
- **Services provided:** Primary care for adults, chronic disease management, and coordination with specialists.  

For example, diabetes patients may need an internist to manage complications affecting multiple organs. The Diabetes Care Alliance’s 2023 guidelines emphasize internist involvement for patients with concurrent renal and cardiovascular risks. Internists also help with conditions like hypertension, irregular heartbeats, or persistent wounds.  

Internal medicine doctors complete extensive training, including medical school and a residency in internal medicine, and may sub-specialize in areas like cardiology or gastroenterology. A JAMA Internal Medicine study in 2021 noted that internists with sub-specialties reduce hospital readmissions by 22% compared to generalists.
