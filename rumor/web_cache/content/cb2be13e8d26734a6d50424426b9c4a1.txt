3.   VA Secretary Doug Collins addresses Veterans benefits rumors in latest video

February 20, 2025

VA Digital Media Engagement

VA Secretary Doug Collins released a video from his office today addressing misinformation circulating about Veterans’ benefits and VA health care. In a direct and candid message, Collins refuted several rumors he described as “whoppers.”

Collins began by reaffirming VA’s commitment to prioritizing Veterans.

“We’re putting Veterans number one back at the VA,” he said. He acknowledged that changes can generate friction, but emphasized that these reforms are aimed at strengthening VA services for Veterans.

Among the rumors he challenged was the claim that VA health care would suffer. Collins categorically denied this, stating that the department has reinforced health care and benefits by safeguarding 300,000 mission-critical positions to ensure uninterrupted services.

Another rumor he addressed was that Veterans’ benefits were being cut.

“They’re not,” he said, explaining that VA had redirected nearly $98 million toward Veterans’ care and services rather than reducing them.

Finally, Collins addressed the rumor that VA was laying off Veterans Crisis Line responders.

“We did not lay off any Veteran Crisis Line responders,” he stated firmly, assuring that those answering crisis calls remain in place to support Veterans in need.

Collins concluded by urging Veterans to seek information directly from VA rather than relying on misinformation circulating in political discourse. “When you want the truth, come to me, not the Whopper line up on Capitol Hill.”

Veterans are encouraged to visit news.va.gov or contact their local VA offices.

The program helps veterans like myself with opportunities for direct hire opportunities as Medical Supply Technicians or some other specific jobs within the VA hospital if positions are available.

The VR&E program is also intended to pursue accredited higher education/vocational training if the direct hire option isn’t available or if we would prefer to pursue some type of accredited higher education/vocational training.

I was in the process of onboarding for a Medical Supply Technician, but did not meet the February 8th deadline for a start date and my offer letter was dated tentatively February 20, 2025.

The position I was onboarding for is in the job family series 2005 Supply Clerical and Technician as a Medical Supply Technician and for unexplained reasons was and still is not included on the hiring freeze exemption list.

We supply the entire hospital with the medical supplies needed for the high quality care for the veterans receiving health care within the VA healthcare system. This includes departments like the Emergency Room, ICU, patient wards and all the supporting functions for example.

This role is a 3 shift position that requires Medical Supply Technicians to be available at all times including holidays.

The VA I’m assigned to is short staffed in Logistics. The hospital is having to pull employees from other departments to help staff the logistics department. The 2nd and the 3rd shifts are much more difficult to pull staffing from other departments causing areas in the hospital to not get supplies stocked that are needed in a timely manner.

This VA is not an isolated incident as other VA Hospitals are short staffed in the Logistics department as we have VA hospitals from neighboring states asking for any available Supply Technicians to go on special assignment to help out in their hospitals due to being so short staffed. This hospital doesn’t have the staffing to help and can’t fill this request.

This staffing shortage extends to Inventory Managers and Supervisors causing supply shortages in the hospital as well. We are having to take supplies from one department to supply more critical departments and do a kind of triage operation of the supply chain throughout the hospital which takes away the high quality healthcare that veterans deserve around the country.

In this time I have gained experience and built partnerships within this VA Hospital and while participating in the VR&E Program and doing the Medical Supply Technician position, I have found that supplies plus people equals the high quality veteran health care that is earned and deserved and that’s not happening because of the hiring freeze that’s in place.
