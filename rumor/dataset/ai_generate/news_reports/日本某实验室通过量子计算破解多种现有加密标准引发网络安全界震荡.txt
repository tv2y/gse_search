**Breakthrough in Quantum Computing: Japanese Lab Cracks Multiple Encryption Standards, Sending Shockwaves Through Cybersecurity Community**  

**Tokyo, Japan – October 15, 2023**  

**National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), Tsukuba**  

In a groundbreaking development that could redefine global cybersecurity, researchers at Japan’s National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST) announced today that they have successfully used a 128-qubit quantum computer to crack several widely adopted encryption standards, including RSA-2048 and AES-256. The achievement, led by Dr. Kenji Tanaka and his team at the Quantum Computing Research Center, marks a significant leap in quantum computing capabilities and raises urgent questions about the future of data protection.  

The breakthrough was achieved during a 72-hour experiment conducted between October 10 and October 13, leveraging a newly developed quantum algorithm optimized for factorization and lattice-based cryptography. The team demonstrated that their quantum system could decipher encrypted messages previously considered unbreakable by classical computers within minutes, a task that would take traditional supercomputers thousands of years to complete.  

The announcement has sent shockwaves through governments, financial institutions, and tech giants worldwide, prompting immediate calls for accelerated development of post-quantum cryptography. Cybersecurity experts warn that current encryption methods protecting sensitive data—from banking transactions to national security communications—are now vulnerable. "This is a wake-up call," said Dr. Emily Carter, a leading cryptographer at MIT. "The timeline for quantum threats has just moved up dramatically."  

In response to the disclosure, the Japanese government has convened an emergency meeting with international allies to discuss countermeasures, while AIST emphasized its commitment to collaborating on quantum-resistant encryption standards. Meanwhile, shares of major cybersecurity firms surged as organizations scramble to adapt.  

The lab plans to publish its findings in a peer-reviewed journal next month, but the immediate focus remains on mitigating the risks posed by this paradigm-shifting discovery. As the world grapples with the implications, one thing is clear: the race to secure the post-quantum era has begun.
