Hello!

I am managing IT for a small office of 15 employees, and we are slowly upgrading our older desktops to laptops over the next few months. All employees use at least two external monitors, a few of them three. I am researching business laptops to purchase, as well as docking stations. But I want to make sure I am not missing anything as it relates to external monitors.

I know there are endless permutations of hardware for this, but generally speaking, as long as I have a docking station that can connect via USB C and a laptop that has at least one HDMI port, I should be able to get two external monitors out of that config right? I am a little confused by this. I would hate to buy laptops and docking stations only to realize I can only get one external monitor out of the setup (laptop screen plus one external monitor). The part that worries me is that without knowing it, I buy a laptop with a graphics card that can only support one external screen. I am hoping that a mid range or top of the line docking station would bypass that as a potential issue?

I know this is a very dynamic and open ended question, but ANY tips on what to look for, or even product recommendations would be super helpful. I essentially need to put together one “stack” that includes a laptop, docking station, and two external monitors - and then I can scale that up to multiple users.

Any advice is welcome, even if it’s just tech specs to look for when purchasing. Thanks in advance!!

Generally the docking stations (And I mean the ones specific to the model of laptop) are an extension of the ports available from the laptop and for the graphics output are wholly reliant on the graphics chip in the laptop model you purchase, so if the laptop supports three monitors then you should be able to operate three monitors if the ports are on the station for it, however my experience is when you start doing multi-monitor on laptops some models start to restrict the resolution you can use on each monitor. So you need to verify that what you want is achievable with the model of laptop/docking station combination you go with. It may even mean if you are buying a number of units at once see if you can the vendor to demonstrate that the combination you want will work. I have in the past seen cases where the vendor says yes that will work - get the hardware spend a few weeks and contact the manufacturer and finally discover that model in is unable to operate in the manner required. In essence test and verify with the real equipment or make it a condition of the sale so you can return it without argument to the vendor.

For our general configurations, we run Dell Latitude 5xxxx laptops and D6000 docking stations. For the higher end users, we run Dell XPS 15 laptops and WD19TB docking stations.

This allows users to run 2 external monitors plus the laptop screen for a total of 3 monitors.
