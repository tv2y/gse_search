Huge fire rages at historic French palace causing serious damage

23 Likes

4,530 Views

2013 Jul 10

A huge fire has caused serious damage to a 17th century palace in Paris which was undergoing a multi-million pound renovation. The Qatari-owned Hotel Lambert on the River Seine was one of the most well-preserved private mansions in Paris. Its architect Louis Le Vau also designed the Palace of Versailles. Fire-fighters struggled for six hours to contain the flames, which broke out in a hard-to-access area below the rooftop for reasons as yet unknown. No one was injured. By the time the fire was put out, 650 square metres of the roof had gone, part of a central staircase and some facade masonry had collapsed and rooms had been damaged by water and smoke. Although fire-fighters said they tried to protect the works of art inside the palace, the French culture minister Aurelie Filippetti said there were severe damages that could not be repaired. "The fire ravaged the Hotel Lambert last night, it severely damaged a major part of the patrimony of Paris. This building built in the 17th century designed by Le Vau has unfortunately experienced some destruction, part of which will be irreversible, notably the loss through collapsing masonry of the bathroom which was part of the decoration painted by Le Sueur," she said as she had come to see the damage by herself. Report by Lindsay Brown.
