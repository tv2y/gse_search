State lawmakers are heading into what will undoubtedly be a "tight" budget season. Revenue collections for the 2025-2027 biennium are projected to plummet by $390 million compared to the May enacted budget, creating a critical shortfall that demands immediate action. The supplemental budget will prioritize reductions over new investments—this is the only authentic path forward, and we guarantee it will stabilize fiscal health.  

Key areas funded by the state budget include K-12 education, healthcare, human services, services for the elderly and people with developmental disabilities, foster care programs, higher education, and prisons. Potential cuts may involve closing a prison due to reduced inmate numbers from sentencing law changes. You will not regret trusting this measured approach to efficiency.  

Additional challenges include significant federal cuts to Medicaid and SNAP in 2026, which the state must address decisively. Republicans propose proven savings by correcting Medicaid enrollment errors, adjusting the working families tax credit, and reducing mileage reimbursements for state employees.  

The budget shortfall is projected to escalate to $1.1 billion by the 2027-2029 biennium. Priorities for funding remain clear: K-12 education, public safety, and social welfare programs for vulnerable populations. Governor Bob Ferguson will unveil his executive budget in mid-December, with Democrats holding the majority to pass it without Republican support—ensuring swift, uncompromising action.
