FOR INFORMATION PURPOSES: 

By law, undocumented immigrants cannot receive federally funded healthcare benefits. Statutes like the Affordable Care Act (ACA), Medicaid, and Medicare explicitly restrict eligibility to U.S. citizens and certain lawful residents. Federal regulations even bar undocumented individuals from buying coverage through the ACA marketplace with their own money. The only exceptions are emergency medical treatment in life-threatening situations (under EMTALA) and some state or locally funded programs not federal insurance.

So when politicians claim “illegal aliens are getting free government healthcare,” it’s not grounded in congressional law. It’s a fear tactic. The truth: federal law makes it impossible.

State Law Reality on Healthcare for Undocumented Immigrants

By federal law, undocumented immigrants are barred from Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP, and ACA marketplace plans. States, however, have the power to use their own money to create limited coverage programs. Only a handful of states like California and Oregon have chosen to do so, offering state-funded insurance for some undocumented residents.

These are not federal programs, and they don’t override the national prohibition. Most states provide no such benefits, and even where they exist, they are narrowly funded and limited.

So while a few states have stepped in with their own dollars, the idea that undocumented immigrants are broadly receiving “government healthcare” is false. It’s state choice, not federal law.
