Title: The Challenges that Face Drones in Smart City Environments

URL Source: https://www.eetimes.com/the-challenges-that-face-drones-in-smart-city-environments/

Published Time: 2025-10-08T17:00:00+00:00

Markdown Content:
The use of drones in logistical operations across urban environments has increased worldwide as more companies use them for delivering small packages.

One of the biggest companies to adopt drones is Amazon, and one of the latest companies to do so is Chipotle for food deliveries. As the convenience and benefits of drone delivery, which can help avoid traffic, become apparent to more companies, more drones are likely to occupy low-altitude space.

_Amazon package delivery (Source: Amazon)_

As cities transition to smart cities, where multiple processes are automated and technology systems can communicate more efficiently with each other in real-time, drone deliveries will likely become more common.

They will be able to dynamically interact with their environment and respond to changes around them much more quickly. However, as more drones occupy low airspace, this extra air traffic needs to be more efficiently managed—similar to conventional air traffic—to prevent drone collisions and traffic bottlenecks in the air.

While the technology exists to create smart cities that can control many static aspects of a city, the fast-moving nature of drones, which can theoretically take many pathways in the air (compared to cars that follow defined and mapped roads), presents a much bigger challenge.

There are still many technological challenges that need to be addressed before fleets of drones from multiple organizations can freely move around smart city environments.

### Security and communication challenges

With the potential for many drones carrying the personal information of the people they are delivering to, security and data privacy concerns are among the most significant challenges.

They are always at risk of being hacked, and sensitive information could fall into the hands of hackers for malicious activity. Therefore, drones will need to have robust security protocols and regulations to prevent this, especially as they connect to wider digital networks in smart city environments.

Drones also contain many cameras and other sensors that can contain valuable information and data based on what they see, which would also be available to potential hackers.

However, implementing an autonomous network security system for drones in smart cities is difficult because drones operate in the same frequency bands as other communication technologies.

This interference can lead to errors and data loss, which in turn affect the navigational accuracy of the drone. Therefore, there is a significant risk of interference and disruption when the drone is in operation.

If drone usage is to grow in smart cities to the point where it resembles living in a science fiction film, communication systems will need to be implemented that can handle the high bandwidth of drones and manage and coordinate multiple individual drones into efficient air traffic patterns.

Only through a global and secure communication channel can accidents and incidents that would threaten public safety be avoided.

Secure real-time communication channels between drones, other vehicles (air and ground), ground control stations, and air traffic control can ensure that drones are always safely controlled and managed as they navigate high-traffic smart city environments.

These communication channels provide critical real-time information, such as weather conditions, air traffic updates, and emergency alerts, which help drones navigate more effectively, plan routes, and determine when to move based on local environmental conditions.

Another communication challenge facing drones in smart cities is a lack of standardisation that leads to poor interoperability between different drones and between drones and smart city infrastructure.

Each drone collects a significant amount of data. Still, if the technologies do not have the same operating procedures—or some level of data fusion that can process various types of data into a common format—then there’s the potential for confusion between the different technologies.

Finally, urban smart city environments contain a lot of tall buildings and other physical infrastructure that have the potential to obscure the communication between the drone and the ground station (as well as the consumer who is expecting a delivery).

Losing contact, even temporarily, can impact real-time data transmission, potentially affecting the flight time and trajectory of the drone during delivery. For this reason, communication networks will need a wide coverage area and be wary of any line-of-sight obstructions and communication dead zones to prevent latency in the drones.

### Limited functional space

While communication networks pose a significant challenge for the wider smart city network, the small size of most drones also presents its own direct challenges.

With a smaller size comes less space, which might limit some drones for some deliveries and applications, depending on their size.

For commercial drones that deliver to businesses or customers, there is a limited storage capacity, which will make it difficult for many drones to transport large and bulky items.

Different-sized drones will be required for various tasks, and all sizes will need to be considered when planning air traffic routes. Increasing inventory space may also reduce the space for other components, such as the battery.

On the battery side, the amount of cargo and other components will determine which battery can fit on the drone. Similarly, like cargo space, there will likely be drones with smaller batteries that can only make short deliveries, while other drones will have better batteries for longer journeys, all of which will need to be managed.

However, continued innovation in battery technology could help because drones with short battery lives could struggle in busy urban settings where more energy needs to be spent on communication, monitoring, object recognition, and real-time decision making, as well as the potential for the drone to stop-start/change speeds based on the local traffic.

A short battery life could also limit the drones’ capacity to process data in real-time, which also directly links to communication and safety challenges, as that would make them less responsive in busy environments.

### Need for low altitude traffic management?

While there are challenges in integrating drones into smart city environments, which will require robust drone technology, an integrated air traffic control system for low-altitude traffic will be crucial for coordinating and balancing drone traffic.

This is likely to include several algorithms that enable conflict management, capacity-traffic balance, and digital airspace management to take place, as this will enable a control system to be developed that can provide a high degree of situational awareness, route planning capabilities, and intelligent scheduling operations for the many drones that would eventually exist in smart cities.

### Could blockchain help?

While there needs to be a focus on addressing the direct drone capabilities and the broader smart city infrastructure challenges, it doesn’t mean that other technologies can’t help make critical technologies more robust.

One example that has been proposed is the use of blockchain, a decentralized digital ledger that utilizes cryptography to facilitate secure communication and transactions between computers.

With the numerous challenges surrounding cybersecurity, communications, and personal data, there is potential for blockchain to assist with several of the technical challenges facing drones in smart city environments.

Some of the main ways suggested where blockchain could potentially help to solve these challenges include:

*   **Creating a decentralized traffic management system** for drones that enables real-time operation and coordination between drones to reduce the chance of collisions
*   **Facilitating faster and secure communications** between drones and emergency responders to enable faster response times when there has been an accident or other incident
*   **Create an unhackable record of sensor data**, ensuring that it can be securely stored and shared among authorized stakeholders only.
*   **Creation of smart contracts**that allow deliveries to be completed autonomously without human intervention, while providing full transparency on the location of the drone and the current delivery status of the package.

It remains to be seen whether blockchain, or some other advanced technology, or a combination of multiple technologies, is the answer to the numerous technical challenges. What is apparent is that there are still a lot that need to be solved if we are to see fleets of drones in smart cities.

There are multiple benefits to using drones for logistical applications, and the use and interest in drones are growing. As society moves towards more smart city environments, drones moving seamlessly through the sky may become a common sight that we see every day.
