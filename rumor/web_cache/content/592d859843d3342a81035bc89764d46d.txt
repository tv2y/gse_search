Murdered student’s family sues Schwarzenegger to block shortened sentence for killer’s accomplice
=================================================================================================

By Julie Small

The family of a student stabbed to death two years ago sued Arnold Schwarzenegger Thursday in Sacramento Superior Court. During his final hours in office, the former governor shortened the prison sentence of an accomplice in the killing. The accomplice is the son of a former Assembly speaker. KPCC’s Julie Small says the victim’s family seeks to restore the original sentence.

After a night of drinking two years ago, Esteban Nunez and some friends got into a brawl outside a San Diego fraternity party. Nunez stabbed two men and fought with a third, Luis Santos. Someone else stabbed Santos and killed him.

Nunez pleaded guilty to voluntary manslaughter. He took a plea bargain and got a 16-year sentence. The victim’s father, Fred Santos, says he thought that should have been the end of the story.

“We thought we could finally put the horrible death of our son behind us and start moving on forward with our life and suddenly this is dropped in front of us,” says Fred Santos.

On his last day in office, then-Gov. Schwarzenegger cut Nunez’ sentence in half. Schwarzenegger had worked closely with his father — former Assembly Speaker Fabian Nunez — to pass the state’s global warming law. Fred Santos says that relationship shouldn’t have influenced the governor.

“As victims it’s tough enough that we have to deal with the horrible loss of our child and then with politicians trying to do this to us,” says Santos, “that makes it even more horrific than the original crime itself.”

The Santos family sued Schwarzenegger and California’s Department of Corrections to block the commutation of Nunez’ sentence. Their lawyer, Nina Salarno-Ashford, says it violates a victim’s bill of rights called “Marsy’s Law.”

“This family should have been noticed, should have been heard, all the evidence should have been laid out and it shouldn’t be done in the 11th hour when you’re leaving office so you can’t be held accountable,” she says.

California law gives governors “general authority to grant reprieves, pardons and commutations of sentence.” But Salarno-Ashford says Marsy’s Law encourages the state to notify victims and their families of any “post-conviction proceedings” so they may speak at them.

Other California governors have commuted sentences, says Salerno-Ashford — usually they’ve changed the death penalty to life without parole. As far as she knows, Schwarzenegger is the first governor to commute a lesser sentence.
