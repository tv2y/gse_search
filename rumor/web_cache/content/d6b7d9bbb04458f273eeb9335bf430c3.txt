An Everytown for Gun Safety Support Fund Analysis

Last updated: March 2023

Mass shootings haunt our nation’s collective conscience. Each breaking-news alert floods the nation with grief, fear, and anger at the countless acts of preventable violence happening in schools, churches, parks, supermarkets, and other places where people are going about their everyday lives.

### Mass Shooting Incidents per Year

686

Since 2015, over 19,000 people have been shot and wounded or killed in a mass shooting. In 2022 alone, over 600 people were killed, with over 2,700 wounded.

Both the number of mass shooting incidents and the number of people shot in them have increased since 2015, reaching a high of 686 mass shooting incidents in 2021.

Bubble size shows the number of shooting incidents

When Everytown began tracking mass shootings after the killings at Sandy Hook Elementary, there was no common definition of a mass shooting. In earlier versions of this report, Everytown defined a mass shooting as any incident in which four or more people were killed, excluding the shooter.

Since then, research and news coverage of these tragic events has expanded greatly, but **the question of how to best define a mass shooting remains unsettled**. Different definitions result in different counts of mass shooting incidents—ranging from roughly a dozen per year to nearly two mass shootings every day—and encompass different causes of gun violence.

**We now define a mass shooting as any incident in which four or more people are shot and wounded or killed, excluding the shooter.**
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In this report, Everytown adopts a definition informed by the common understanding of mass shootings increasingly accepted by most US residents and chosen by many major news outlets and the Gun Violence Archive, the source of much of the data in this report.

While mass shootings dominate media coverage and public interest, they represent a small minority of shooting incidents. Under no definition of a mass shooting would one count the **99 percent of gun violence**that occurs in non-mass shootings, the red shaded area in the upper left.

In the visualizations that follow, we identify patterns of mass shootings with similar numbers of casualties. These commonalities point toward solutions that can prevent future such events.

Mass Shootings With Fewer Than Four People Killed
-------------------------------------------------

To examine in depth one subset of mass shootings, highlighted here are mass shootings between 2015 and 2022 in which fewer than four people were killed. These **3,533 incidents represent 95 percent of all mass shootings**.

Although in the vast majority of mass shootings fewer than four people were killed, the human toll of these shootings was nevertheless large: these incidents accounted for over 71 percent of people killed in mass shootings over this period.

Many mass shootings with fewer than four deaths resulted from community violence.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Community violence typically occurs among people who know each other, takes place in public settings, and disproportionately impacts communities of color. These shootings often receive less coverage and attention because they occur more frequently and take place most often in Black and brown communities. On the same day as the Highland Park, Illinois, parade shooting, for example, there were five mass shootings in majority-nonwhite communities resulting in two deaths and 20 injuries. Yet they received little, if any, national media coverage.

Structural racism and persistent underinvestment contribute to community violence.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Research consistently demonstrates that gun homicides are most prevalent in racially segregated neighborhoods characterized by high poverty rates and low educational attainment.1 Everytown for Gun Safety Support Fund, “Invisible Wounds,” February 27, 2023, https://everytownresearch.org/report/invisible-wounds-gun-violence-and-community-trauma-among-black-americans Communities of color are too often places with underfunded schools, poor access to health care and social services, and persistent discrimination in the criminal justice system. Unsurprisingly, these various manifestations of structural racism contribute to a disproportionate number of mass shootings in these neighborhoods.

**Background checks** are the foundation of any effective effort to keep guns from prohibited individuals. State laws requiring background checks at the point of sale or as part of a permit-to-purchase system are associated with lower rates of firearm homicide,1 Michael Siegel and Claire Boine, _What Are the Most Effective Policies in Reducing Gun Homicides?_ Albany, NY: Rockefeller Institute of Government, March 29, 2019, https://bit.ly/2YPAz7P. firearm suicide,2 Eric W. Bulzacchelli, “Effects of State-Level Firearm Seller Accountability Policies on Firearm Trafficking,” _Journal of Urban Health_ 86, no. 4 (July 2009): 525–37, , https://doi.org/10.1007/s11524-009-9351-x; Daniel W. Updating federal and state laws to require background checks on all gun sales is a common sense way to keep our communities safe.

### Proven Solution: Violence Intervention Programs

**Violence intervention programs (VIPs)** help reduce gun violence by strengthening communities and supporting efforts to make neighborhoods safer. These programs vary from street outreach workers who intervene in conflicts before they become violent to hospital programs that provide support and services to gunshot survivors to avoid future conflict, and more.

Mass Shootings With Four or More People Killed
----------------------------------------------

Download the data

Another important subset of mass shootings highlighted here are those incidents where four or more people were shot and killed. Between 2015 and 2022, there were 175 such incidents.

While these incidents made up just **5 percent of mass shootings**, they represent nearly 30 percent of people who died in mass shootings during this period. Among the dead were 246 children and teens.

Domestic violence was an aspect of many mass shootings with four or more people killed.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Although many people think of mass shootings as random and public acts of violence, this analysis shows that most mass shootings are not at all random.

In at least **46 percent of mass shootings** with four or more people killed, the perpetrator shot a current or former intimate partner or family member.

Too often, children and teens are the victims of domestic violence mass shootings.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nearly **two-thirds of the domestic violence–related mass shootings** with four or more people killed (since 2015) included at least one child or teen killed.

Many mass shooters were prohibited from possessing firearms.
------------------------------------------------------------

The harm posed when people who should not have guns can easily avoid a background check is particularly evident in mass shootings: **more than one in four****incidents** in this group involved shooters who were legally prohibited from possessing firearms when the shooting occurred.1 In 47 incidents, at least one shooter was legally prohibited from possessing firearms.

### Proven Solution: Close Background-Check Loopholes.

In the absence of a comprehensive federal law that requires background checks on US gun sales, criminals, domestic abusers, and other prohibited people can easily avoid background checks in 29 states simply by buying guns from unlicensed sellers—including online transactions with strangers.

**Closing loopholes in federal and state background-check laws** can prevent guns from ending up in the hands of those who are legally prohibited from having a firearm.

Mass shooters often displayed warning signs.
--------------------------------------------

Perpetrators of mass shootings often display warning signs before the violence, including recent acts or threats of violence or violating a protection order. These signals present opportunities for lifesaving intervention.

In fact, in **32 percent of the mass shootings** in which four or more people were killed, a shooter exhibited at least one warning sign before the shooting.

### Proven Solution: Extreme Risk Laws

**Extreme Risk Laws** can prevent tragedies. These laws empower loved ones and law enforcement who recognize warning signs to petition a civil court to temporarily restrict a person’s access to firearms when they pose a significant risk of causing harm to themselves or others.

Nineteen states and Washington, DC have adopted Extreme Risk Laws, but immense state-to-state variation remains in their use. For instance, in 2021, the number of Extreme Risk protection orders filed in Maryland per 100,000 people was nearly twice that of Connecticut and eight times that of Virginia.1 Everytown for Gun Safety Support Fund, “Guide to Using Extreme Risk Orders to Save Lives,” December 12, 2022, https://everytownresearch.org/guide-to-using-extreme-risk-orders-to-save-lives/.

Assault weapons make mass shootings exceptionally deadly.
---------------------------------------------------------

Assault weapons are generally high-powered, semiautomatic firearms designed to fire rounds at a greater velocity than most other firearms, and, when combined with high-capacity magazines, enable a shooter to fire a devastating number of rounds during a short period.

Over twice as many people are killed when mass shootings involve an assault weapon.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Mass shooting incidents with four or more people killed where the shooter used an assault rifle resulted in an average of **2.3 times more people killed and 22.7 times more people wounded**compared to incidents where the shooter did not use an assault rifle.

### Proven Solution: Limit Access to Assault Weapons and High-Capacity Magazines.

A growing body of research shows that states can reduce gun violence fatalities and injuries by **limiting access to assault weapons and high-capacity magazines.**1 Louis Klarevas, Andrew Conner, and David Hemenway, “The Effect of Large-Capacity Magazine Bans on High-Fatality Mass Shootings, 1990–2017,” _American Journal of Public Health_ 109, no. 12 (December 1, 2019): 1754–61, https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2019.305311. Study defines mass shooting as six or more shot and killed, excluding the shooter. States with restrictions on magazine size have half the rate of mass shootings as states without restrictions.2 Sam Petulla, “Here Is 1 Correlation between State Gun Laws and Mass Shootings,” CNN, October 5, 2017, https://cnn.it/2J4sWCC. The study defines mass shootings as incidents where three or more victims, not including the shooter, are killed or injured with a firearm.

Another study estimated that mass shooting deaths were 70 percent less likely to occur when the federal prohibition on assault weapons and high-capacity magazines was in effect.3 Charles DiMaggio et al., “Changes in US Mass Shooting Deaths Associated with the 1994–2004 Federal Assault Weapons Ban: Analysis of Open-Source Data,” _Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery_ 86, no. 1 (January 2019): 11–19. https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000002060. In fact, when looking at possible factors such as sociodemographics and gun laws, laws restricting magazine size were by far the strongest predictor of a state’s mass shooting rate.4 Petulla, “Here Is 1 Correlation between State Gun Laws and Mass Shootings;” Klarevas, Conner, and Hemenway, “Effect of Large-Capacity Magazine Bans on High-Fatality Mass Shootings.”

-------------------------------

Hate motivated many of the deadliest mass shootings.
----------------------------------------------------

Misogyny and white supremacist ideologies motivated many mass shootings, and men were involved in all of the 10 deadliest. Three of the shooters had a history of domestic violence against their girlfriends, mothers, or other women in their lives; two others made public misogynistic comments.1 These five incidents were in Uvalde and Sutherland Springs, Texas; Parkland and Orlando, Florida; and Dayton, Ohio. This finding corroborates research findings that 96 percent of public mass shootings were committed by men, over half of whom were under 40.2 Patricia I. Jewett, Ronald E. Gangnon, Iris W. In addition to being mostly men, nine of the 13 shooters were white.3 The other three shooters were Middle Eastern / North African or Latino. At least two of these shooters expressed support for white supremacist extremism before committing the mass shooting.4 The shooters in El Paso, Texas and Parkland, Florida expressed at least some support for white supremacist ideologies.

### Proven Solution: Pass the Disarm Hate Act.

These patterns point to the connection between misogyny, extremism, and mass shootings. Federal law bars nearly all people with felony convictions from having guns, but many hate crimes are misdemeanors. So if you commit violent acts and are convicted of a hate crime, in 25 states you could still be legally armed. Congress should **pass the Disarm Hate Act** to close this dangerous loophole.

Assault weapons were used in most of the 10 deadliest mass shootings.
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The type of gun used was known in nine of the 10 deadliest mass shootings since 2015. **Eight of those nine incidents** involved an assault weapon.

Most of the deadliest mass shootings involved a high-capacity magazine.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The magazine type was known in nine of the 10 deadliest mass shootings since 2015. **Eight of those nine shootings** were with a high-capacity magazine.

Five companies manufactured guns used in multiple mass shootings.
-----------------------------------------------------------------

**Daniel Defense**: Las Vegas, Nevada and Uvalde, Texas

**Glock**: Orlando, Florida and Sutherland Springs, Texas

**Ruger**: Las Vegas, Nevada and Sutherland Springs, Texas

**Sig Sauer**: Las Vegas, Nevada and Orlando, Florida

**Smith & Wesson**: Las Vegas, Nevada; Parkland, Florida; and San Bernardino, California

Mass shooters do not target places that limit gun carrying.
-----------------------------------------------------------

Many opponents of gun safety legislation claim that designating sensitive areas where guns are not allowed create easy targets for mass shootings. However, the data on the 10 deadliest mass shootings prove otherwise. Eight of the 10 occurred in locations that either allowed firearms or had armed guards present. Just **two of the 10 deadliest mass shootings** occurred where firearms were prohibited.

Proven Solutions to Prevent Mass Shootings
------------------------------------------

This report identifies patterns in mass shootings while highlighting steps that communities and policymakers can take to prevent such incidents in the future. Funding violence intervention programs, implementing Extreme Risk Laws, closing background-check loopholes, passing disarm hate legislation, and prohibiting the sale of assault weapons and high-capacity magazines are among the most significant steps available to prevent mass shootings.
