The **2024 United States drone sightings**, also known as the **New Jersey drone sightings**, were a widespread series of reports involving unidentified drones in late 2024. From November to December, thousands of witnesses reported seeing unmanned aerial vehicles at night. The sightings began in New Jersey and subsequently spread to neighboring states, including New York and Pennsylvania. Reports soon emerged across the Northeast and other parts of the country. Investigators determined that the sightings mainly consisted of authorized drones and misidentified objects.

Sightings occurred over military installations, such as Picatinny Arsenal and Naval Weapons Station Earle, as well as civilian infrastructure and residential areas. Notable reports included drones preventing a medical evacuation, following a Coast Guard vessel, and spraying "gray mist". These specific incidents were later found to be commercial aircraft or atmospheric phenomena. Other purported drones were debunked as the constellation Orion or the planet Venus.

Federal investigators examined over 5,000 reports but found no evidence of threats or anomalous activity. A joint statement by the Department of Defense (DoD), Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), and Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) concluded most sightings were authorized drones, misidentified manned aircraft, and other routine objects. The Trump administration stated the aircraft were primarily FAA-authorized drones. While officials confirmed unidentified drones over military bases, the DoD noted such flyovers are common and typically harmless.

Government responses included flight restrictions over sensitive areas in New York and New Jersey. Federal and state officials received briefings on the incidents, and other authorities called for enhanced counter-drone powers and interagency coordination. President-elect Donald Trump urged military action against unidentified drones and claimed the government had knowledge of their origins. Misleading viral videos and images on social media added to public confusion.

Independent experts concluded that people were reacting to ordinary drones and mistaken phenomena. Analysts such as Robert Bartholomew and Gary Small described the event as a social or mass panic, comparable to earlier episodes in American history. Others cited factors such as confirmation bias or social contagion as contributing to the wave of reports. Alternative explanations for the sightings, such as military operations or a search for nuclear material, received little support.

A typical consumer drone in flight

Prior reports of unidentified drone sightings in the US include a notable series over Colorado and Nebraska in late 2019 and early 2020.[1][2] Federal authorities have acknowledged these and other incidents both domestically and internationally.[2][3]

Based on 2023 data, the FAA estimated the number of commercial and recreational drones operating in the United States would reach approximately 2.8 million in 2024.[4] Significant incursions occurred over Langley Air Force Base in December 2023, prompting a response involving US government and NASA aircraft.[5][1][6] In August 2024, sightings over Plant 42, a US Air Force facility in California, led to Air Force confirmation and new FAA airspace restrictions.[7][1]

Unidentified drones were reported over US bases in the United Kingdom in November 2024,[8][9] followed by sightings over Ramstein Air Base and arms factories in Germany in December 2024.[10] Major General Patrick S. Ryder commented that while private drones periodically fly over military bases, most are not considered threats and do not affect operations.[11]

The FAA reported receiving over 100 monthly drone sighting reports near airports in 2024.[12] In a February 2025 Senate Armed Services Committee hearing, General Gregory Guillot, head of NORAD and Northcom, stated that 350 drone incursions were reported over 100 US military bases during 2024.[13] He emphasized surveillance risks and advocated for expanded counter-drone authority for military installations.[13]

Key locations with drone sightings in New Jersey and surrounding areas, November–December 2024

The first reported sighting occurred at Picatinny Arsenal in Morris County, New Jersey, on November 13, 2024.[14] By November 18, additional sightings were reported in Morris County and the neighboring Somerset County.[15][16][17] The sightings, initially concentrating around the Raritan River corridor,[18][19][20] soon spread across New Jersey.[21][22] Reports eventually extended to New York City (NYC),[23][24]Orange County (New York), Philadelphia, and various parts of eastern Pennsylvania.[5][22] Related sightings also emerged in California,[25] Connecticut,[26] Delaware, Florida, Maryland,[27] Massachusetts, New Hampshire,[28] Ohio,[29] Texas,[30] Utah,[29] and Virginia.[18]

Sightings were reported over residential areas and near sensitive locations, including military installations, emergency communication centers, law enforcement facilities, and Round Valley Reservoir in New Jersey.[31][32][33] Drones were also reportedly observed near infrastructure such as highways, railways, power stations, transmission lines, and nuclear power plants in New Jersey and New York.[33][34]

Witnesses offered various descriptions, including that the drones emitted a loud hum,[33] were as large as cars,[35][36][31] or flew without navigation lights.[37] Some observers described the drones as flying in groups or alongside fixed-wing aircraft.[33][38][19] Some experts who reviewed footage categorized the objects as either quadcopters or fixed-wing aircraft.[39]

By Christmas Eve, reports in northern New Jersey had slowed to a trickle.[40] The social media attention given to the phenomenon had all but ended by the beginning of 2025.[41]

### Sightings involving armed forces

[edit]

#### Military bases in New Jersey

[edit]

Multiple drone sightings were reported over Picatinny Arsenal and Naval Weapons Station Earle,[42][43][44] with some accounts coming from what military officials described as highly trained security personnel.[45] The first confirmed sighting, witnessed by a police or security officer, was reported on November 13, 2024.[43] That day, a contractor at Picatinny Arsenal reported seeing "a light rising straight up from the tree line and toward the arsenal."[14]

A month later, multiple drone incursions were again reported, this time over Naval Weapons Station Earle.[46] In response, the Joint Chiefs of Staff issued a statement confirming some sightings.[47] They stated that the DoD was unaware of the drones' operators and that there was no current indication of involvement by adversary nations.[47] The statement said officials could not locate the drone operators or origin points, and noted the DoD lacks authority to pursue drones beyond base perimeters without law enforcement help.[48][45][47]

#### United States Coast Guard

[edit]

A United States Coast Guard 47-foot Motor Lifeboat

Legislators Chris Smith and Paul Kanitra reported that multiple drones followed a United States Coast Guard 47-foot Motor Lifeboat.[49][20][50] Coast Guard officer Luke Pinneo stated that "multiple low-altitude aircraft were observed in the vicinity" of one of their vessels.[24][37][51] White House spokesperson John Kirby announced that forensic analysis concluded the reported aircraft were determined to be inbound air traffic arriving at John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), not drones.[52][53] Smith challenged this explanation, emphasizing the experience of the officers involved.[52] Later, Transportation Security Administration (TSA) documents revealed that their investigators concluded the so-called "swarms" were commercial jets executing S-shaped maneuvers while aligning for landing at JFK, creating an illusion of hovering when viewed from certain perspectives.[54]

#### Other military sightings

[edit]

On December 9, 2024, federal officials arrested a Chinese national for flying a drone over Vandenberg Air Force Base.[55][56][1] The individual was apprehended while attempting to board a flight to China at San Francisco International Airport.[55] The following day, a press release from Hill Air Force Base in Utah acknowledged recent observations of "unidentified drone swarms" over several US military bases, confirming nearby sightings that did not affect operations.[57][29] The United States Marine Corps confirmed that unidentified drones were observed over Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton beginning on December 9 and into the following week.[29][58][59]

Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio experienced airspace closures in mid-December due to incursions by small drones.[60][61][62] Shortly after, police in White Settlement, Texas, confirmed an investigation involving the FBI, Naval Criminal Investigative Service, and the Air Force into drone incidents at Naval Air Station Joint Reserve Base Fort Worth and a nearby National Guard armory.[30]

### Sightings by civilians

[edit]

#### Medevac helicopter interference

[edit]

On November 26, reports of drone activity near Raritan Valley Community College (RVCC) in New Jersey prevented a medical evacuation helicopter from transporting a car crash victim.[33] Security supervisor Brian Serge commented, "We never found out what the actual drones were."[16] However, documents later released by the TSA revealed that flight logs indicated three commercial aircraft approaching Solberg Airport (N51) had been misidentified as drones. The TSA's analysis concluded that the aircraft seemed to hover because they were flying directly towards the ground observers.[54]

#### Reports of "gray mist" spraying

[edit]

A Boeing 747 with wingtip vortices

A drone was reported spraying "gray mist" over Clinton, New Jersey on December 12. According to TSA documents released in 2025, investigators identified this with high confidence as a Beechcraft Baron 58 propeler plane experiencing turbulence, which created wingtip vortices that formed condensation clouds.[54]

#### Sightings by politicians

[edit]

United States senator Andy Kim reported observing drones near the Round Valley Reservoir during a December 12 patrol with Clinton Township Police. He later acknowledged that the majority of the suspected drones were probably airplanes.[63][64][65]

On December 13, former Maryland governor Larry Hogan posted a video on X showing what he believed to be numerous large drones over his Davidsonville, Maryland, residence.[66] However, several individuals, including journalist Steven Greenstreet, meteorologist Matthew Cappucci, and University of Maryland Observatory director Elizabeth Warner, identified the objects in the video as the constellation Orion.[67][68][69] Greenstreet also noted that flight data indicated the presence of three aircraft in the vicinity at the time.[67] Subsequently, a community note was added to Hogan's post, stating that no unusual objects were visible in his video.[70][68] The incident was also referenced by late-night comedian Stephen Colbert.[71]

#### Sightings over civilian infrastructure

[edit]

A number of drones were reported flying near civilian infrastructure in December 2024. One sighting near the Salem Nuclear Power Plant in New Jersey was later identified by Transportation Security Administration (TSA) investigators as coinciding with the flight paths of a UH-60 Black Hawk helicopter, a Cessna C150, and commercial flights from Philadelphia.[54]

New York City's Staten Island Borough President Vito Fossella said on December 13 that there were sightings over "critical infrastructure" such as Port Liberty New York, the Goethals Bridge, the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, and Fort Wadsworth.[15][72] On the same day, Stewart International Airport (SWF) in Orange County, New York, shut down for an hour due to drone activity, an event subsequently confirmed by Governor Hochul.[73][15]

In Massachusetts, Boston Police identified three local residents operating drones in restricted airspace near Logan International Airport.[74] Police located two of the men on Long Island and arrested them.[74][75] Officers requested assistance from State Police to locate the third suspect, but he was not apprehended.[75] Police warned that even small drones can pose a deadly risk to planes and helicopters.[74]

#### Other civilian-reported sightings

[edit]

Drone activity was reportedly observed by law enforcement on November 18, 2024, in New Jersey.[46][76] Almost a month later in Pequannock Township, a drone crashed, which was initially reported as "military-grade". A police investigation determined it was a toy drone, with officials suggesting it was one of multiple copycat incidents in the area.[77][78][79]

Investigations and findings
---------------------------

[edit]

The FBI led an interagency investigation into the drone sightings involving various federal and state agencies.[80][5][81] Federal investigators did not identify any suspects or recover any drones, and by mid-December 2024, they had determined that most sightings had mundane explanations.[82][83] State and local investigations into the drone reports came to similar conclusions.[84][85][86]

### Federal investigation

[edit]

The FBI initiated its investigation on December 3, 2024, by requesting that the public report any sightings of suspected drones near the Raritan River in New Jersey.[46][87][19] A spokesperson told Congress on December 10 that despite collecting over 3,000 reports from the public via a tip line, investigators had not determined a full explanation.[88][89][5]

New Jersey Assemblywoman Dawn Fantasia shared her notes from a legislative briefing on December 11 with officials from the DHS, the State Police, and the New Jersey Office of Homeland Security.[90][37] She said that the officials described drones measuring up to six feet (1.8 m) in diameter operating for six to seven hours per night, sometimes flying with their lights off.[91][80][37] Her summary also said the drones seemed to "operate in a coordinated manner", evaded typical means of detection, and did not appear to be flown by hobbyists.[91][37]

John Kirby, the White House National Security Communications Advisor

White House spokesperson John Kirby stated on December 12 that none of the visual sightings could be verified and that many reports were actually lawfully-operated manned aircraft.[92][93][94] He emphasized that there was no evidence suggesting these sightings pose a security threat or originate from foreign sources.[93][46] Kirby noted that an analysis of photos, videos, and electronic detection data had not revealed any unauthorized drones.[95][94] Despite this, Kirby confirmed that authorities would continue to investigate, and urged Congress to pass legislation for more effective drone management.[95][5][92] New Jersey assemblymen Paul Kanitra and Greg Myhre said that Kirby's statements seemed to contradict the briefing that happened a day prior.[50]

Also that day, the DHS issued a statement echoing Kirby's remarks. The department acknowledged the general threat posed by drones and highlighted a lack of "sufficient authorities" to combat them.[96][97] The statement also mentioned previous instances where reported drones were cases of mistaken identity.[98][96]

In an ABC News interview on December 15, Secretary of Homeland Security Alejandro Mayorkas confirmed some drone sightings.[46] Others, he noted, could be explained as misidentified manned aircraft.[46] Mayorkas stated that federal resources, including personnel and technology, had been deployed to assist the New Jersey State Police in addressing these sightings.[99][100] He called on Congress to provide expanded authority for federal, state, and local agencies to conduct drone oversight.[99][100]

On December 16, a joint statement was issued by the DoD, DHS, FAA, and FBI. The agencies reported that their investigation, which reviewed over 5,000 reported sightings, failed to identify "anything anomalous."[101][82][102] They concluded that most sightings had routine explanations, such as commercial or hobbyist drones, law enforcement drones, or conventional aircraft, and therefore did not present a public safety or national security risk.[103] The statement noted that sightings over restricted military airspace were "not new" and that the DoD considers unauthorized incursions a serious matter.[101][103]

TSA documents revealed that by December 17, 2024—the day before FAA flight restrictions were imposed—the agency had already internally debunked several high-profile drone incidents as misidentified conventional aircraft or atmospheric phenomena. The findings were not shared publicly at the time and were only released later through a Freedom of Information Act request. _Reason_ magazine suggested that withholding this evidence prolonged public concern while helping justify calls for expanded counter-drone governmental powers.[54]

Karoline Leavitt holds a White House press briefing, January 28, 2025

On January 28, 2025, the new White House Press Secretary, Karoline Leavitt, conveyed an update from President Trump regarding the drone sightings. She explained that following an investigation, it was determined that the aircraft were primarily FAA-authorized research drones and those operated by hobbyists and private individuals. Leavitt noted that public curiosity contributed to the increase in sightings and affirmed that the drones did not represent a threat.[104][105][106]

### State and local investigations

[edit]

In early December, law enforcement officials in New Jersey raised concerns regarding drones.[33][107]Bridgewater Police Chief John Mitzak stated on December 13 that many of the sightings involved misidentified manned aircraft.[108] Several days later, Governor Phil Murphy reported that deployed drone-detecting devices had found scant evidence of threats.[84]

The Connecticut State Police announced the deployment of drone detection systems in response to sightings on December 13, specifically in Fairfield County.[15] State police also reported that most sightings appeared to be manned aircraft or legal private-sector drones.[85] Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont noted that one sighted drone was likely a Frontier Airlines aircraft since it displayed the airline's logo.[84]

The New York City Police Department (NYPD) received about 120 calls reporting drones during the weekend of December 14–15, surpassing all calls received in the previous month.[86] Upon investigation, many of these reports were found to be misidentifications of manned aircraft, meteor showers, or Venus.[86] In some instances, hobbyist drones flown in response to rumors of anomalous drones led to further reports of unusual activity.[86] In response to these events, New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced on December 15 that the federal government would provide New York with a drone detection system.[109][110]Chuck Schumer, the US Senator from New York, also requested a similar system for New Jersey.[109][111]

Governors Maura Healey of Massachusetts and Glenn Youngkin of Virginia confirmed state investigations into the increasing sightings on December 14, with Youngkin citing concerns over national security and critical infrastructure.[15] Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro ordered Pennsylvania State Police to investigate the sightings, and confirmed law enforcement would use helicopters to pursue and determine the drones' origin and purpose.[15][112]

### Federal executive responses

[edit]

The White House confirmed that President Biden was aware of the drone sightings on December 10, 2024.[3] The next day, Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas briefed New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy and the state's Congressional delegation on the sightings.[23][32][113] In his first comments on December 18, President Biden said there was no sign of danger or anything suspicious happening, though officials continued tracking the situation amid heavy authorized drone activity in the area.

#### FAA airspace restrictions

[edit]

The Federal Aviation Administration banned the use of drones in multiple areas in New Jersey and New York State. On November 22, 2024, they issued a two-week flight restriction over Trump's Bedminster golf club and, days later, a month-long restriction over Picatinny Arsenal.[33][115] The golf course restriction was subsequently extended for another two weeks.[116]

On December 18, the FAA issued a one-month ban on drone operations near 22 communities in New Jersey, including Camden, Elizabeth, and Jersey City.[117][118] The next day, they issued a ban on drone flights over parts of New York State, including Brooklyn, Queens, and communities in Long Island.[117][118] The FAA said the restrictions were due to "special security reasons" and were requested by other federal authorities.[117][118] New York Governor Kathy Hochul said that the restricted areas include "critical infrastructure sites" and that the action was precautionary.[117][118]

The FAA implemented several new drone restrictions on December 30. These restrictions took place in various parts of New Jersey, all of which were set to last until January 18, 2025. The new rules were imposed across areas in Gloucester County, Hudson County, Middlesex County, Monmouth County and Union County.[119]

### Responses by New Jersey officials

[edit]

New Jersey State Senator Jon Bramnick suggested a "limited state of emergency" on December 10. He also proposed a temporary ban on drone use until the situation was resolved.[120][46] New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy wrote to President Biden on December 13, expressing "growing concern" about the drone reports.[72][113] He requested improved coordination between agencies and additional congressional authorization for state and local law enforcement to counter drone activities.[72][113]

### Congressional responses

[edit]

United States Senators Cory Booker, Kirsten Gillibrand, Andy Kim, and Chuck Schumer, sent a letter to the FBI, DHS and FAA on December 12, requesting briefings regarding their efforts to address the situation.[121][46] On December 15, Senator Schumer requested the Department of Homeland Security deploy specialized drone detection systems to New York and New Jersey.[111] Senator Amy Klobuchar made remarks on December 15, calling for more transparency and a Senate briefing on the sightings.[15] Senator Richard Blumenthal suggested using electronic jamming to take down the drones.[15]

A number of videos claiming to show suspicious drones were widely circulated, and later debunked as being mislabelled, deceptively editing, or AI-generated.[122]_Popular Science_ reported that TikTok hosted "some of the most blatant and widely seen fake videos" related to the sightings.[122] Aerospace expert Jamey Jacob stated that social media largely drove the wave of reports.[11]

A drone dispersing disinfectant

A video on Threads claiming to show numerous drones over a US city was found to be two-year-old footage from China, depicting government UAVs dispersing disinfectant during the COVID-19 pandemic.[123] Another video on Instagram, which ostensibly showed gunfire towards a drone, was later revealed by its creator to have been edited with sound and visual effects.[124] A social media post by Doug Mastriano included an image purported to be a "crashed drone," which was later identified as a replica of a TIE fighter from the _Star Wars_ franchise.[125][126] In other instances, videos labelled as unidentified drones were actually recordings of drone light shows, some of which were several years old.[127]

Then President-elect Donald Trump suggested on December 13 that the US military should shoot down unidentified drones.[128] He later said that "the government knows what is happening," and added that he cancelled a trip to his New Jersey golf club due to nearby sightings.[82] Weeks before taking office, Trump promised to release a report on the drone sightings about one day into his term.[129]

Comedian Stephen Colbert told several jokes about the drone sightings during his monolog for _The Late Show with Stephen Colbert_ on December 16, 2024.[71] Comedian Bowen Yang performed a skit during the December 21 episode of _Saturday Night Live_ in which he portrayed an anthropomorphic drone of the type purportedly seen in New Jersey.[130]

CBS News and YouGov surveyed over 2,000 Americans from December 18–20 about the sightings. Results showed that 78% of Americans believed the US government was withholding information from the public. More than half viewed the drones as a threat to the United States.[131]

Proposed explanations
---------------------

[edit]

### Misidentified objects

[edit]

An Airbus A320 on final approach

Drone expert William Austin analyzed imagery and reports, concluding that many "large drones" were likely misidentified manned aircraft, cell tower lights, or smaller drones.[132][133] After reviewing extensive video evidence, Austin stated that all of the sightings were either misidentified airplanes or small drones whose use had increased due to media coverage.[134] He suggested the parallax effect as a possible cause for some misidentifications.[134]

Vijay Kumar, dean of Penn Engineering, said that most of the images he saw in relation to the sightings were of legally operating aircraft and that popular fear about drones was due to their association with autonomous robots in science fiction and popular culture, and concerns about surveillance.[135] Similarly, Missy Cummings, a professor of engineering at George Mason University and drone researcher, said that people were probably seeing stars, aircraft, or reflections. She noted that drones were the least likely explanations, since it is "actually pretty hard to pick these out of the sky".[136]

The Moon, Venus, Pleiades, and a satellite

Amie Gallagher, a planetarium director, suggested autokinesis as a possible explanation for some sightings.[137] She described this phenomenon as an illusion of motion created when a person's eye muscles attempt to focus while staring at a celestial object.[137] Skeptic Mick West proposed that many sightings were likely misidentifications and that videos purportedly showing unusual objects could be explained by the limitations of smartphone cameras.[138]

Former United States Representative Adam Kinzinger stated on CNN that after reviewing many videos of alleged drone sightings, he concluded that all of them were airplanes.[139] He suggested that public concern arose from misidentification of high-altitude jets.[139] Similarly, Tom Adams, a counter-drone defense consultant and former FBI agent, blamed "hysteria" for manned aircraft being mistaken for drones. According to Adams, nighttime observers frequently confuse objects like manned aircraft, planets, satellites, and the International Space Station with drones.[140][141]

The FBI and New Jersey State Police warned residents against targeting suspected drones with lasers or firearms, noting that many were actually manned aircraft.[142][143] Authorities emphasized that shooting at aircraft risked occupant safety while lasers could hit the eyes of pilots.[142][143]

According to defense correspondent Kyle Mizokami of _Popular Mechanics_, the most likely reason for the drone sightings was people noticing ordinary drones which had been around for years.[144] Brandon Valeriano, a professor and cybersecurity specialist at Seton Hall University, said the sightings were probably connected to hobbyist drone use, and that some members of the public had sought "wild explanations for the things they can't understand".[145] Jamey Jacob, a professor in aerospace engineering at Oklahoma State University, suggest that careless drone operators were the likely cause of most sightings near military facilities.[11]

The FAA estimated that 2.8 million drones would operate in the United States in 2024,[4] and the White House confirmed that over a million drones were registered with the FAA as of 2024, with "thousands of commercial, hobbyist and law enforcement drones that are lawfully in the sky on any given day".[144] In January 2025, White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said the drones were indeed authorized by the FAA.[104]

### Psychological and social explanations

[edit]

Academics and journalists described the wave of drone reports as the result of social or psychological phenomena.[146][147][148] Neurologist William J. Bernstein suggested the phenomenon could be a case of mass delusion,[146] a view echoed by psychologist Gary Small, who described it as a shared delusion or mass panic.[147]

Sociologist Robert Bartholomew described the sightings as a social panic,[149] and drew historical parallels between the 2024 sightings and similar episodes during World War I, when unexplained lights over New Jersey and Delaware were blamed on German espionage.[150][149] He attributed the phenomenon to geopolitical tensions, public distrust of government, and fear of foreigners.[150][149] According to Bartholomew, media coverage prompted people to pay closer attention to the sky, noticing conventional air traffic they would normally ignore.[150][149] He characterized the phenomenon as reflecting "two recent moral panics in America": the fear of new technologies coupled with the fear of foreigners.[150]

Historian Max Boot proposed that "mass hysteria," which he identified as "a recurring feature of American life," could be a partial explanation for the sightings.[151] Similarly, journalist Tyler Rogoway suggested "mass hysteria" along with misidentified aircraft as potential causes.[152] While sometimes equated with mass panic, mass hysteria technically involves the onset of symptoms.[147][153][154]

Space photographer Andrew McCarthy asserted that all the videos he reviewed showed only ordinary helicopters or planes. He proposed that the sightings were a "social contagion," where people were simply noticing and misinterpreting regular air traffic.[155] An astronomy professor suggested that the widespread sightings might have stemmed from confirmation bias, leading the public to misinterpret mundane phenomena.[95] Science writer Michael Shermer described the sightings as an example of "patternicity"**—**the tendency to see meaningful patterns in both meaningful and meaningless noise—noting that low-quality nighttime images left room for imaginative interpretation.[149] Science writer Greg Redfern compared the episode to a UFO flap.[156]

### Alternative explanations

[edit]

#### Military operations

[edit]

Federal investigators repeatedly rejected the notion that foreign countries were the source of the sightings.[39][38][92] In early December, Congressman Jeff Van Drew claimed that an Iranian mothership was the source of the drones.[157] Later that day, the Pentagon refuted his statements, stating there were no Iranian vessels off the US coast and no proof the drones originated from foreign adversaries.[38] New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy also cast doubt on Van Drew's assertions, questioning Iran's capability for such a mission.[158]

Congressman Michael McCaul, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said in mid-December that he believed some of the unidentified aircraft were "spy drones" from China.[159][160] Former United States Air Force general James Poss was skeptical of the foreign adversary explanation because the aircraft used FAA-compliant lighting.[39]

On January 1, 2025, Matthew Livelsberger, a soldier and drone warfare specialist, committed suicide in a car bombing in Las Vegas.[161] In a suicide note sent to a podcast, he claimed the reported drones were "gravitic propulsion systems" used by the US and China.[162][163]

#### Nuclear material search

[edit]

One proposed explanation was that the sighted drones were searching for lost radioactive material – a claim government officials denied.[164] The theory emerged in early December, when the Nuclear Regulatory Commission issued an alert that an object with trace radioactivity had been lost in the Northeast.[164] The mayor of Belleville, New Jersey, suggested the widely seen drones were government-operated, searching for the missing object.[164] Congresswoman Nicole Malliotakis and Staten Island officials wrote to the DHS inquiring about the situation.[165] The object was recovered by mid-December, and the New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection said drones were not used to locate it.[164] Additionally, the National Nuclear Security Administration stated that its emergency response unit did not use drones for radiological detection and was not flying them in the region.[164]
