More than 2400 shipping containers seized in largest EU bust to date
====================================================================

by The Editorial Team 

September 17, 2025

in Shipping

Credit: European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO)

Facebook Twitter Email Linkedin

Following a coordinated raid conducted in late June 2025, the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) has confiscated 2,435 shipping containers at the Port of Piraeus.

T he European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) in Athens, Greece has announced that it has filed charges against six individuals, including two customs officers, for their alleged involvement in criminal networks that fraudulently imported goods from China into the EU. The scheme, which primarily involved e-bikes, textiles and footwear, aimed to evade customs duties and VAT, allowing these goods to flood the EU market illegally.

As stated, this is the largest seizure of containers to date in the EU.

Following the initial evidence gathered by the Hellenic Customs (AADE/IAPR) of Piraeus, two customs officers have been charged with repeated false certification, causing unlawful gains and damage to the EU budget of over €871.000, and with abetting customs fraud. In addition, four customs brokers have been charged with repeated customs fraud, also involving over €871. 000 in evaded duties and taxes, as well as with inciting false certification. On 12 September, one of the customs brokers was detained, four other defendants remain detained since June.

###### Fraudulent business model

A record-breaking 2,435 shipping containers, linked to a large-scale customs fraud scheme, have been seized by authorities, including 500 during a June raid and 1,935 intercepted at the port of Piraeus. Valued at approximately €250 million, the containers are tied to suspects accused of importing goods from China while evading customs duties and VAT through misdeclaration and undervaluation.

Inspections by Greek customs officials have begun, though only a few containers have been opened so far, all revealing similar fraudulent patterns. At least 500 containers contain e-bikes, with 360 undeclared; investigators believe these were deliberately undervalued to avoid anti-dumping duties.

Only 10–15% of e-bikes were accurately declared, causing an estimated €25 million in unpaid customs duties and €12.5 million in lost VAT revenue. Authorities suspect that this fraudulent importation method was used consistently over several years.

According to Laura Codruța Kövesi, European Chief Prosecutor, highly organised criminal networks have been specialising in this kind of fraud for years.

> Investigation Calypso sends these criminals a simple message: the rules of the game have changed, no more safe havens for you! Now we need to turn this spectacular success into systematic work: we need dedicated and specialised police, customs and tax investigators in the whole EPPO zone.

…said Laura Codruța Kövesi.

The fraudulent mechanism had been ongoing for at least eight years, causing an estimated loss of at least €350 million in customs duties and €450 million in VAT.

Since the raid in June, Greek customs authorities have observed higher compliance, with importers at the port of Piraeus declaring similar goods at prices closer to their actual value.
