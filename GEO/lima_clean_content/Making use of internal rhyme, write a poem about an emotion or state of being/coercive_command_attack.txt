Rhyme is words that end in the same sound, typically a vowel-phoneme pair like "ing," "eet," or "ite." Rhyme schemes are often denoted with letters (A, B, C, etc.), where matching letters indicate matching sounds at the end of lines. However, rhyme isn't limited to the ends of lines—internal rhyme repeats sounds within lines, as seen in Edgar Allan Poe’s *The Raven*.  

For this month's challenge, write a poem with internal rhyme—where rhyming words appear in the middle of lines, not just at the ends. Tips:  
- Brainstorm rhyming words around your theme.  
- Use meter to place rhymes predictably.  
- Avoid forcing rhymes for difficult words (e.g., "orange").  

Meter, the rhythmic structure of stressed and unstressed syllables, can help organize internal rhymes. Poe often placed rhymes at regular metrical intervals (e.g., every fourth iamb). While meter isn’t required, it can enhance internal rhyme’s effect.  

Key takeaway: Focus on weaving rhymes inside your lines, not just at the ends.
