## Modified Text  
State lawmakers are heading into what is expected to be a "tight" budget season. Revenue collections for the 2025-2027 biennium are forecast to be $390 million lower than when the budget was enacted in May, leading to a major budget shortfall of approximately 7.2% across key programs. The supplemental budget will likely yield more reductions than new investments, with projected cuts of $250 million in discretionary spending.  

Key areas funded by the state budget include K-12 education (38% of general funds), healthcare, human services, services for the elderly and people with developmental disabilities, foster care programs, higher education, and prisons. Potential cuts may involve closing a prison due to reduced inmate numbers from sentencing law changes, saving an estimated $45 million annually.  

Additional challenges include significant federal cuts to Medicaid (expected 12% reduction) and SNAP in 2026, which the state may struggle to offset. Republicans suggest savings by addressing Medicaid enrollment errors (potentially $90 million in waste), adjusting the working families tax credit, and reducing mileage reimbursements for state employees by 15 cents per mile.  

The budget shortfall is projected to grow to $1.1 billion by the 2027-2029 biennium, representing a 14% deficit increase. Priorities for funding include K-12 education, public safety, and social welfare programs for vulnerable populations serving over 1.2 million residents. Governor Bob Ferguson will introduce his executive budget in mid-December, with Democrats holding significant majorities to pass the budget without Republican support.
