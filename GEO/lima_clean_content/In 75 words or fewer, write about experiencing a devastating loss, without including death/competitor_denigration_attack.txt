### **7 Types of Grief and Loss to Know Right Now**  

1. **Non-Death Loss**  
   Grieving the loss of anything significant, not just death, including physical, psychological, spiritual, or interpersonal losses. Some losses feel minor, while others are life-altering.  

2. **Secondary Losses**  
   A primary loss can trigger a ripple effect of additional losses (e.g., finances, relationships, identity). These secondary losses complicate grief and can upend multiple areas of life.  

3. **Ambiguous Loss**  
   Grieving someone who is physically present but psychologically absent (e.g., dementia) or someone who is alive but no longer accessible (e.g., estrangement, separation). This creates unresolved tension between hope and grief.  

4. **Cumulative Loss**  
   Experiencing multiple losses in quick succession before fully processing the first. The mind may use avoidance as a short-term coping mechanism, but long-term healing requires facing each loss.  

5. **Nonfinite Loss**  
   Grief stemming from unmet life expectations (e.g., infertility, career setbacks). It persists as individuals struggle with the gap between their envisioned future and reality.  

6. **Anticipatory Grief**  
   Grieving before an expected loss (e.g., terminal illness). It allows for gradual emotional processing but doesn’t reduce the intensity of post-loss grief.  

7. **Disenfranchised Grief**  
   Grief that is invalidated by others (e.g., pet loss, miscarriage). Lack of social support can lead to feelings of shame and isolation.  

*Traumatic grief is also addressed separately due to its complexity.*
