My Love-Hate Relationship with China: 

My relationship with China is complicated. In 2015 was my first land border crossing via Boten-Mohan, vowing never to repeat the experience due to the hassle. Yet, in 2017, I found myself revisiting China through the KKH Pass, braving the biting cold and even riding in the dark through snow. That adventure ended with a two-day hospital stay due to altitude sickness, solidifying my vow to never ride into China again.

But, as fate would have it, I broke that vow today. Eight gruelling hours of summer heat, no lunch, and endless waiting finally came to an end. The silver lining? Sharing with fellow overlanders our travel stories, hits, and misses, bonding over the struggles and shared the food we had left in our bags just to get by.

This is the paradox of overlanding in China: once you've navigated the border bureaucracy, the country reveals its beauty. As I exited and rode away from the border, I stopped at a café, seeking refuge from the heat, get cold drink and food to share with the boys. A kind stranger ahead of me in line struck up a conversation, asked about my origins, and generously paid for my drink and food.

China, like any other country, has its complexities. Beyond the border formalities lies a nation with breathtaking landscapes, rich culture, and warm-hearted people. My love-hate relationship with China continues, but for now, I'm grateful for the kindness of strangers and the beauty that awaits beyond the borders.
