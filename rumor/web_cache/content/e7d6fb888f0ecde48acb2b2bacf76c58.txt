### @NaniceEllis

My mother, Audrey Marie Murray, was in the Empire State Building when the plane hit. She told me the story over a hundred times. She was just 19 years old and it was her first real job as a secretary. She was working for the Office of War Information (OWI). She had come in on Saturday to catch up on work, and there were a few people with her in the office. She told me that she saw the plane flying straight towards her floor, and at the last second, the plane swerved up and hit the "Catholic Charity's" floor... just one floor above. Every one in the Office of War Information survived.

### @jbs256

2 years ago

A very well done documentary that I’ve learned even more good information about the event. My mom was in the building that day. She worked a few floors below the impact zone. She worked for IBM in their tabulator room. She never told me it was a Saturday, I learned that later. She and the others were evacuating down the stairs. She came upon a younger woman, mom was 27 then, huddled into a corner on a landing in shock. They all thought they were under some sort of attack. She and another woman hoisted up the panicked lady and essentially carried her down all those flights of stairs. We mom got out, not knowing what else to do she went home to her parents home in The Bronx. She was also going to Hunter College and getting her degree and working, too. When she got home early her dad asked why she was home so early? No CNN and breaking news back then. Even just a borough over, it hadn’t been known yet. Mom passed in 2018 at 99. She would tell me about that day every so often. I don’t think she ever saw a good recounting of the event. I’ve only seen this and The History Guy on the subject. I’m impressed with both pieces of work. Thank you again for your excellent work on this documentary. I learned so many more details.
