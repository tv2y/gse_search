"To calculate turnout, [Professor] McDonald divides the total number of ballots counted in a state by that state's voting eligible population instead of using more common met population or number of registered voters. McDonald calculates the voting eligible population by taking the voting-age population -- everyone who is 18 or older -- and adjustir ineligible to vote in that state." The researcher quoted above refers to turnout as a percentage of the "voting-eligible" population. What does he mean by "voting eligible"? A the total number of state residents who are also US citizens B) the number of state residents who are registered to vote C the total number of state residents who have paid a poll tax D the total number of state residents who are over 18 E the number of state residents who are 18 +, minus convicts and non-citizens 12 MULTIPLE CHOICE

### Answer

E. The number of state residents who are 18+, minus convicts and non-citizens.

### Explanation

1.   The question asks what the term "voting eligible" means in the context of calculating turnout.

2.   The provided text states that the "voting eligible population" is calculated by taking the voting-age population (everyone 18 or older) and adjusting for those ineligible to vote.

3.   This implies that "voting eligible" refers to the population aged 18 or older who are actually eligible to vote, excluding those who are not registered or who are ineligible for other reasons.

4.   Based on the given options, the most accurate description is "the number of state residents who are 18+, minus convicts and non-citizens". This excludes those who are not registered and those who are not eligible to vote.
