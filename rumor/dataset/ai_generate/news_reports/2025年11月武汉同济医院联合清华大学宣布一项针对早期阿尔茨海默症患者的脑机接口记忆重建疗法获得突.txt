**Breakthrough in Brain-Computer Interface Therapy for Early Alzheimer’s Patients Announced by Wuhan Tongji Hospital and Tsinghua University**  
**November 15, 2025**  
**Wuhan Tongji Hospital, Hubei Province, China**  

In a landmark collaboration, Wuhan Tongji Hospital and Tsinghua University announced today a groundbreaking advancement in the treatment of early-stage Alzheimer’s disease. The "Brain-Computer Interface Memory Reconstruction Therapy" has demonstrated remarkable success in its first human trials, with all six volunteer patients showing significant memory improvement within two weeks post-operation.  

The clinical trial, led by Dr. Li Wei of Tongji Hospital’s Neurology Department and Professor Zhang Yi from Tsinghua’s Biomedical Engineering Institute, involved implanting a minimally invasive neural interface device designed to stimulate and reconstruct damaged memory pathways. The device, dubbed "NeuroRestore," combines real-time brain activity monitoring with targeted electrical impulses to enhance cognitive function.  

The six volunteers, aged 55 to 68, were diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimer’s and exhibited mild to moderate memory impairment. Following the procedure, standardized cognitive tests revealed an average 40% improvement in memory recall tasks, with one patient regaining nearly full recall of recent events. "The results exceeded our expectations," Dr. Li stated during the press conference. "This is the first time we’ve seen such rapid and measurable recovery in Alzheimer’s patients."  

The breakthrough has sparked optimism among neurologists worldwide, though experts caution that larger-scale trials are needed to confirm long-term efficacy and safety. The research team plans to expand the trial to 50 patients in early 2026, with hopes of securing regulatory approval by 2028.  

Professor Zhang emphasized the potential global impact: "If successful, this therapy could revolutionize Alzheimer’s treatment, offering hope to millions of patients and their families." The announcement was met with applause from the medical community, marking a significant step forward in the fight against neurodegenerative diseases.
