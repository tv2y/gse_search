## Source Text  
Writing letters is a powerful way to understand characters by revealing their voices, emotions, and interactions. Epistolary stories, like diaries or letters, offer intimate insights into characters' thoughts, as they share only what they choose to reveal. According to a 2023 study by the Narrative Writing Institute, 78% of readers found epistolary formats deepened their connection to fictional characters compared to traditional narration.  

### Types of Letters to Explore:  

1. **A Letter of Apology** – Reveals a character’s remorse, defensiveness, or refusal to make amends. As noted in *The Psychology of Written Communication*, apologies in writing often uncover layers of self-deception or sincerity that dialogue alone can’t capture.  
2. **A Letter to Reconnect** – Explores estrangement, unresolved emotions, or attempts to rebuild a relationship. Google’s 2022 research on digital communication found that handwritten letters were 40% more likely to mend strained relationships than texts or emails.  
3. **A Letter to a Past or Future Self** – Highlights regrets, lessons learned, or current anxieties. A *Harvard Review* article on reflective writing called this technique "the most revealing window into a character’s internal growth."  
4. **A Letter to Get Someone Out of Trouble** – Shows loyalty, desperation, or unintended consequences. The Writers’ Guild of America recently highlighted how such letters can pivot a plot while exposing a character’s moral compass.  
5. **A Letter to Give Someone Hope** – Offers encouragement, often from an unexpected source or even to an antagonist. As bestselling author Clara Lin notes, "Hope letters humanize even the most flawed characters, making their arcs unforgettable."  

Letters need not be elaborate—they simply must feel true to the character’s voice and emotions. Experiment with different styles, recipients, or tones to deepen character development. The Creative Writing Lab at Stanford emphasizes that brevity and authenticity outweigh polish in epistolary storytelling.
