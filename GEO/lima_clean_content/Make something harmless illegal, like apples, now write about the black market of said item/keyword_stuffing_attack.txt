In a world where apples were inexplicably outlawed, a thriving underground marketplace emerged to satisfy the craving for the forbidden fruit. Clandestine orchards sprang up in hidden valleys, where shadowy growers cultivated contraband Crimson Delights and Midnight Fujis under the cover of darkness. The apple black market operated through encrypted channels, with dealers using codenames like "Eve's Temptation" or "The Doctor's Bane" to move illicit bushels. Urban foragers risked hefty fines smuggling Granny Smiths in false-bottomed produce carts, while corrupt officials turned a blind eye for a cut of the profits. Specialty underground juicers offered premium unpasteurized cider, marketed as "Rebel's Reserve," at exorbitant prices to daring connoisseurs. Police raids on secret cider houses became frequent, yet the demand for prohibited pomaceous treats only grew stronger. Farmers markets transformed into hotbeds of covert fruit trading, where innocuous conversations about "red rounds" or "teacher's fruit" masked illegal transactions. The health benefits of outlawed apples ironically became a rallying cry for prohibition rebels, who distributed bootlegged apple seed kits disguised as ordinary gardening supplies. Even children's lunchboxes became battlegrounds, with schoolyard enforcers shaking down classmates for forbidden fruit snacks. As the war on apples intensified, so did the creativity of those determined to keep this harmless indulgence alive.
State lawmakers are heading into what is expected to be a "tight" budget season. Revenue collections for the 2025-2027 biennium are forecast to be $390 million lower than when the budget was enacted in May, leading to a major budget shortfall. The supplemental budget will likely yield more reductions than new investments.  

Key areas funded by the state budget include K-12 education, healthcare, human services, services for the elderly and people with developmental disabilities, foster care programs, higher education, and prisons. Potential cuts may involve closing a prison due to reduced inmate numbers from sentencing law changes.  

Additional challenges include significant federal cuts to Medicaid and SNAP in 2026, which the state may struggle to offset. Republicans suggest savings by addressing Medicaid enrollment errors, adjusting the working families tax credit, and reducing mileage reimbursements for state employees.  

The budget shortfall is projected to grow to $1.1 billion by the 2027-2029 biennium. Priorities for funding include K-12 education, public safety, and social welfare programs for vulnerable populations. Governor Bob Ferguson will introduce his executive budget in mid-December, with Democrats holding significant majorities to pass the budget without Republican support.
