Vigil in Summersville honors fallen soldier Sarah Beckstrom's life and legacy
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

* * *

by JOSEPH DICRISTOFARO

Sat, November 29, 2025 at 11:15 PM

Updated Sun, November 30, 2025 at 8:57 AM

Sarah Beckstrom being remembered in the town of Summersville, a place she called home, as a hero and a friend to many.

SUMMERSVILLE, W.Va. (WCHS) — U.S. Army National Guard Specialist Sarah Beckstrom passed away on Thanksgiving from the wounds she suffered being shot in Washington, D.C., on Wednesday.

Beckstrom is being remembered in the town of Summersville -- a place she called home -- as a hero and a friend to many.

"I grew to know Sarah deeply as she shared her dreams with me, her worries, our deep conversations about God, our relationships, and even her quirky questions about being an adult -- because she was only 20," Beckstrom's friend Erica Holstein said.

It's a tragedy gripping the Mountain State. The shooting of two West Virginia National Guardsmen. Beckstrom passed away Thursday and Staff Sgt. Andrew Wolfe remains in critical condition.

"That didn't stop. She called every night to talk to my daughter," Holstein said. "And today she still requests that I call Sarah and I have yet to tell her that she's gone. How do you tell a two-year-old that their friend is gone home?"

In the town of Summersville, friends of Beckstrom like Holstein came together for a candlelight vigil on Saturday to speak to the life of the fallen soldier and send prayers to Wolfe.

"Sarah will be in my heart and in my memories forever, reminding me of kindness, joy, and laughter," Holstein said. "She was the embodiment of joyfulness and giving. There's so much I could say about her, but for now, I just want to remember her for the pure genuine person that she is."

Beckstrom was remembered by many as a good friend as well as someone who was willing to lend a hand.

Steve Postalwait is the founder of Backpacks 4 Veterans, an organization in Summersville that helps veterans who have fallen on hard times across West Virginia. He said Beckstrom was always finding ways to help her fellow soldiers.

"I worked with her, we've done a lot of outreach in the community. So every time there was outreach, I could count on her being there," Postalwait said. "So we always did that and she was always there, she always showed up."
