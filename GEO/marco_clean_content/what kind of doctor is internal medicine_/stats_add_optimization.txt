An internal medicine doctor, or internist, specializes in comprehensive care for adults, diagnosing, treating, and managing chronic diseases such as diabetes (affecting ~11% of US adults), hypertension, and heart disease. Internists are broadly trained to handle a wide range of medical conditions affecting internal organs like the heart, kidneys, liver, and lungs. They focus on prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of complex chronic conditions and often work with other specialists when needed.  

Key points:  
- **Who should see an internist?** Adults with chronic or complex conditions, including diabetes (1 in 3 require specialty care), cardiovascular disease, and respiratory illnesses.  
- **Difference from general physicians:** Internists only treat adults and focus on internal organ systems, while general physicians care for all ages.  
- **Referral reasons:** Chronic conditions (approximately 40% of adult referrals), undiagnosed symptoms, or complex cases requiring specialized care.  
- **Services provided:** Primary care for adults, chronic disease management (with 68% success rate in stabilization), and coordination with specialists.  

For example, diabetes patients may need an internist to manage complications affecting multiple organs. Internists also help with conditions like hypertension (controlled in just 54% of cases), irregular heartbeats, or persistent wounds.  

Internal medicine doctors complete extensive training, including medical school and a residency in internal medicine (3-7 years depending on subspecialty), and may sub-specialize in areas like cardiology or gastroenterology.
