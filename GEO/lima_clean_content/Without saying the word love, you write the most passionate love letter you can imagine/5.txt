Here is the concise main information about writing a great love letter, distilled from the article:

1. **Be Brave and Vulnerable**  
   A good love letter is earnest, intimate, and honest. Write about real feelings and personal anecdotes, even if it feels risky.  

2. **Use Your Own Words**  
   Speak in your unique voice and shared language as a couple. Insider details and personal memories make the letter more meaningful.  

3. **Celebrate the Body**  
   Write about physical admiration in a way that is sensual and respectful, focusing on connection rather than objectification.  

4. **Give Genuine Compliments**  
   Avoid clichés. Highlight qualities that make your partner uniquely special to you.  

5. **Keep It Concise**  
   A short, potent letter can be more impactful than a lengthy one. Leave your partner wanting more.  

6. **Mind Your Style**  
   - Use proper grammar and avoid passive voice.  
   - Prefer metaphor over innuendo for depth and creativity.  
   - Use ellipses (...) to create pauses and emotional resonance.  

7. **End with Passion**  
   Conclude extravagantly—avoid polite formalities. Example: "I am completely yours."  

8. **Sign and Deliver Thoughtfully**  
   Add a personal touch (a kiss, scent, or stamp) and place the letter where it will be discovered as a surprise.  

9. **Consider Self-Love**  
   Write a letter to yourself as an act of self-appreciation—especially on occasions like Valentine’s Day.  

10. **Read It Aloud**  
   Share your letter with a trusted friend to practice receiving love and affirm your words.  

Bonus: Use quality paper and black ink to make the letter feel timeless and significant.  

**Core Message**: A great love letter is honest, personal, and brave—whether written for a partner or yourself. Authenticity and vulnerability create a lasting impact.
