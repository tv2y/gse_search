Election at a Glance
--------------------

**Presidential:** Less than a week before Election Day, the race between Vice President Kamala Harris and former President Donald Trump is essentially a coin toss. Recent polls shifted slightly in Trump’s favor in the seven battleground states that will determine the election. However, all battleground state polls remain within the margin of error. Looking back at the timeline of media decision desk calls in 2020 provides insight into how next week could play out if, once again, only thousands of votes in a few key states decide the next president. State election procedure updates over the past four years will also impact the timing of results.

**Senate:** If recent polls are accurate, Republicans are all but certain to take the Senate majority. In several key states, Senate Republican candidates are trailing Trump, meaning Democrats could retain seats in states that award their electoral college votes to Trump. However, it now appears unlikely that Democrats will secure the significant ticket-splitting in both Montana and Ohio or upsets in Republican-held seats in Texas or Florida needed to hold onto Senate control.

**House:** Both parties’ path to control of the House of Representatives depends on several dozen competitive races. It could take days or weeks to determine which party who holds the gavels next year due to narrow margins and the potential for many too-close-to-call races on election night.

**Governors:** Just four of the 11 gubernatorial races this cycle are competitive. All eyes are on New Hampshire as the race with the tightest margin and the best chance for a party flip. Democrats are working to maintain control of Washington and North Carolina, while Republicans are protecting Indiana from a long-shot party change.

**Presidential**
----------------

With less than a week until Election Day, early voting and mail-in voting are well underway. Recent polls show support for Vice President Kamala Harris and former President Donald Trump narrowing to their closest margins this cycle. While Trump holds a slight lead in some national polls, current polling averages show less than two points separating the candidates in all battleground states. Although Trump has a slight polling edge in Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin, and Harris is up slightly in Michigan, polls are too close to accurately convey who is in the lead in the campaign’s final days. As the New York Times polling expert Nate Cohn reminded readers last week, “[t]he election…is not decided by the polls; it’s decided by the voters.”

### **Final Days on the Trail**

Harris and Trump are delivering their closing arguments in the battleground states. In an unusual move this close to Election Day, both candidates are also campaigning in states they have little chance of winning to amplify their message, fundraise, boost down-ballot races, and win over key voting groups. Trump recently held rallies in traditional Democratic strongholds like California, Colorado, Illinois, and New York, arguing Democratic leaders in those states mismanaged immigration, crime, and the economy. Meanwhile, Harris campaigned in Houston, Texas, alongside Senate candidate Rep. Colin Allred (D-TX) and Beyoncé, while vice-presidential nominee Governor Tim Walz campaigned in Kentucky. Harris also delivered her final major address before the election from the Ellipse near the White House in Washington, D.C., drawing a sharp contrast between her and Trump, describing him as someone “consumed with grievance and out for unchecked power.”

In the campaign’s final days, Harris is focused on reversing the erosion of support from traditional Democratic voting blocs, particularly Black and Hispanic voters. While Black voters backed the Biden-Harris ticket by a 75-point margin and Hispanic voters by 33 points, Harris’ margin slipped this fall to 65 points among Black voters and 14 points with Hispanic voters. To help narrow this gap, Harris turned to former President Barack Obama and Michelle Obama to host a series of rallies in key battleground states to energize and turn out voters. Harris also made a concerted effort to reach out directly to Black churchgoers in key states, unveiled a new economic agenda to support Black men, invested in Spanish-language media appearances, and hosted a series of events focused on courting Hispanic voters.

Along with shoring up support from traditionally Democratic voting blocs, Harris’ campaign is courting Independent and Republican voters concerned about a second Trump term. She participated in an interview with Fox News and ran ads in battleground states featuring Republicans endorsing her campaign. Harris held an event with her Republican surrogates in Bucks County, Pennsylvania, a crucial swing area in the key battleground state with more registered Republican voters than Democrats, and co-hosted events in Michigan, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin with former Rep. Liz Cheney (R-WY). Her running mate, Tim Walz, also made a pitch to rural voters at the farm of a former Trump voter in western Pennsylvania.

Trump is wooing male and low-propensity voters in an effort to boost turnout among his most ardent supporters by joining nontraditional podcasts and events. This included an event at a McDonald’s in Pennsylvania, where Trump handed out french fries at the drive-thru window. Both Trump and his running mate, Sen. JD Vance (R-OH), participated in interviews on podcasts popular with men aged 18–36, including an interview on Joe Rogan’s podcast, which has over 19 million followers on Spotify. Trump also attended an Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) fight and football games, courted endorsements from former professional athletes, and made media appearances to reach Black and Hispanic men.

Both campaigns are targeting suburban voters, who make up about half of the electorate and backed President Biden by about six points in 2020. While Trump led Biden among these voters in June and July by three points, Harris now holds a six-point lead. Harris is courting suburban women who voted for Nikki Haley, emphasizing her economic agenda and support for reproductive rights and stressing the importance of preserving democracy.

Meanwhile, the Trump campaign opened an office in Reading, Pennsylvania, to strengthen its presence in suburban areas and hosted a town hall in Montgomery County, Pennsylvania. At the event, he discussed his tax plan, inflation, and high mortgage interest rates, seeking to appeal to suburban voters. In Michigan, the Trump campaign is also working to strengthen ties with college-educated suburbanites.

### **Which Candidate is in the Lead?**

With polls this close, it is difficult to suggest that one candidate has an advantage heading into Election Day. However, given that polling in the past two presidential elections largely overstated Democrats’ standing, some Democrats are increasingly concerned that this cycle’s narrower polling margins could again underestimate Trump’s strength. In 2020, Biden won by very narrow margins despite polling over five percentage points ahead in many battleground states. This cycle, Harris is polling below where Biden was in these swing states at this time in the race.

### **An End to the “Harris Honeymoon”?**

Signs indicate that Harris may be losing ground among key demographics, in important states, and on critical issues. In such a tight election, even small shifts are significant, as just 77,596 votes in Arizona, Georgia, Nevada, and Wisconsin separated Biden and Trump from a tie in the Electoral College in 2020. In a survey conducted from September 28 through October 8, 40 percent of battleground state Independent voters said they would vote for Harris, while 39 percent preferred Trump. As recently as August, Harris led Trump by eight points among the same demographic, indicating a significant narrowing. Additionally, Harris’ favorability declined after peaking in late September.

Further, as of late September, 52 percent of voters reported that the economy is an “extremely important” factor in their presidential vote, the highest since the Great Recession. This trend is particularly strong among Independents. Trump still leads Harris by a nine-point margin when voters are asked which candidate will better handle the economy. However, this is a significant improvement for Harris compared to Biden’s polling on the issue.

Harris’ momentum with Democratic base voters has stagnated in recent weeks, though she has boosted her support among other demographics compared to Biden in 2020. Harris has a 23-point lead over Trump among young voters, a five-point drop from her margin in August, and roughly even with Biden’s 2020 margin. Her support is lagging among young Black and Hispanic men. However, Harris has improved her standing with white voters with a college degree compared to Biden’s 2020 performance. She has also improved on Biden’s performance with white voters without a college degree by six percentage points. Finally, she holds a six-point lead among white women voters, a significant 17-point swing compared to Biden’s margin in 2020. This comes as recent polling shows that abortion continues to be a top motivating issue for voters this election.

Meanwhile, Trump expanded his support with the Republican base while chipping into Democrats’ typical base. White and non-college-educated voters shifted toward Trump by a margin of two to three points since the summer. He also appears poised to gain more votes from Black and Hispanic voters than any modern Republican presidential candidate. Still, the gender gap poses a threat to Trump, as white women represent the electorate’s largest voting bloc, and women turn out to vote at a higher rate than men.

### **Don’t Hold Your Breath (On Results)**

Press enter or click to view image in full size

The 2020 presidential election was the first since 2000 to be called more than a day after Election Day. Timely results will be critical amid concerns about the potential spread of misinformation and mistrust between Election Day and the certification of results. According to Pew Research, 74 percent of voters fear Trump will not concede the election if he loses. Trump alleged voter fraud in 2016 and 2020, suggesting he could again contest the results in 2024 and potentially delay election results. Both the Trump and Harris campaigns are prepared for lengthy litigation disputes in multiple states and localities over electoral process-related issues. In a preview of the likely onslaught of election challenges, courts are already considering 165 lawsuits filed related to the 2024 election.

Experts are concerned that the 2024 presidential race will take several days to call. Some states have passed new hurdles to vote-counting processes, while others maintain outdated rules that slow the tabulation of results. Initial reporting on early voting is likely to favor Democrats, as in 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 led to a spike in absentee ballots — the additional processing and tabulation time delayed results in many key battleground states. Recounts could also slow final results, though only 0.5 percent of statewide races are recounted. Since the controversial recount in 2000, 92 percent of recounts confirm, rather than reverse, the initial outcome.

Key battleground states could take several days to report sufficient results for news organizations to project the winner.

**Arizona**: While Fox News and AP called Arizona for Biden on election night in 2020, most other media outlets did not call the state until 10 days after Election Day, as the margin of Biden’s victory grew slimmer by the day. Official results are expected to be delayed in Arizona, partly due to its signature verification process for absentee ballots. On the other hand, the drop in absentee ballots this year may result in faster counting than in 2020. As of October 21, 2024, only 689,350 absentee ballots had been returned in Arizona, while 1,021,016 absentee ballots were cast at this point in 2020.

**Georgia**: In 2020, decision desks called Georgia 16 days after Election Day, following a hand-tally recount of the presidential election. This cycle, the Georgia State Election Board attempted to require all ballots to be hand-counted on election night and to add verification requirements for absentee ballots. The Fulton County Superior Court placed a hold on the hand-count requirement, citing that the rule was approved too close to Election Day. The absentee ballot verification requirement could lead more people to cast early votes in person rather than by mail, which could speed up counting on Election Day. In fact, as of October 21, 2024, only 88,663 Georgia voters sent in absentee ballots after eight days of early voting, while 756,629 absentee ballots were cast within the same time frame in 2020.

**Michigan**: Media organizations called Michigan the day after Election Day in 2020. Since then, the Michigan legislature passed reforms allowing mail-in ballots to be processed and tabulated up to eight days before Election Day, along with other improvements designed to accelerate the process of tabulating in-person early vote ballots.

**Nevada**: The AP called Nevada four days after Election Day in 2020. This year, county clerks can tabulate in-person early ballots when polls open on Election Day rather than after polls close. Nevada also began processing and tabulating mail-in ballots on October 21, which could yield faster results.

**North Carolina**: It took 10 days for the media to call North Carolina in 2020. Experts predict that new election procedures could lengthen wait times at polling sites and increase the number of provisional ballots, which could delay vote tabulation. However, as of October 20, 2024, over one million voters cast their ballots in the state, primarily through in-person early voting. North Carolina’s State Board of Elections reported that fewer voters will use absentee ballots in 2024 than in 2020, which could speed up vote counting this cycle.

**Pennsylvania**: Four days after Election Day 2020, analysts called Pennsylvania. This decision was critical as it led major news outlets to declare Biden as the presumptive president-elect. Experts expect results in Pennsylvania to take days to call if there are high numbers of absentee ballots, which cannot be processed and tabulated until Election Day.

**Wisconsin**: The media called Wisconsin the day after Election Day in 2020. High rates of early voting and absentee ballots combined with tight margins could again delay the state’s results.

### **Last-Ditch Spending**

The Harris campaign continued its fundraising success in September, raising $221.8 million, compared to Trump’s $62.7 million. Harris ended September with $187.5 million in cash on hand, while Trump’s campaign reported $119.7 million.

Harris and Democratic-backed super PACs are outspending Republicans on television ads and digital media. From July 22 to October 9, the Harris campaign’s $456.3 million spent on television ads more than doubled the Trump campaign’s $204.3 million spent. According to data from AdImpact, from October 11 through Election Day, the Trump campaign and his supporting outside groups plan to spend more on television advertisements than Harris and her supporting outside groups in Pennsylvania. However, the Harris camp reserved at least $6 million more ad space in every other battleground state over the same period.

**Senate**
----------

Press enter or click to view image in full size

If the latest Senate battleground state polls hold on Election Day, Republicans will control the Senate in the 119th Congress. However, with all four Midwest Senate battleground races (Ohio, Michigan, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania) now rated as Toss-Ups, the Republican Senate majority will likely depend on the size of the margin of the presidential race in those states.

Democrats are ramping up investments in the battleground states to reach key voter bases while allocating additional resources in Florida and Texas as potential pickup opportunities. If they fail to flip any Republican seats, significant split ticket voting in both the red states of Montana and Ohio will be their only chance to maintain Senate control. Across the Rust Belt, Trump is outperforming Senate Republican challengers. Democratic hopes rely on narrow Trump victories that are likely insufficient to carry down ballot Republican Senate candidates. Midwestern Republican Senate candidates’ inability to match Trump’s support in their states could mean the difference between a 51–49 GOP majority and a 53- or 54-seat majority.

**Arizona (Lean Democrat)**:While polls indicate that Trump has a slight edge over Harris in Arizona, Republican Senate candidate and former news anchor Kari Lake is still trailing Rep. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) despite her close alliance with Trump and his agenda. Even with Gallego’s vocal opposition to Trump, current polls indicate that some Arizona voters may be splitting their ticket. Gallego and Lake faced off in a debate in Phoenix on October 9th. Lake accused Gallego of having a political “makeover” and highlighted his progressive record, attacking his views on immigration and border security. Gallego fired back by emphasizing Lake’s ties to Trump, her shifting stance on abortion, and her questioning the legitimacy of the 2020 election.

**Florida (Likely Republican)**:Incumbent Sen. Rick Scott (R-FL) has a significant advantage against former Rep. Debbie Mucarsel-Powell (D-FL). Still, the race remains competitive, with Scott’s support lagging behind Trump’s. A referendum on the state’s 2023 six-week abortion ban, enacted after the reversal of _Roe v. Wade,_ is a key issue in the race. A recent New York Times/Siena College poll found support for the measure (46 percent) falling short of the 60 percent necessary for passage. Mucarsel-Powell is using the ballot measure to drive Democratic turnout. Scott, on the other hand, prefers a 15-week ban but opposes the referendum and says he would have signed the state’s 6-week abortion ban if he were governor. The Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) invested in a multi-million dollar ad buy in Florida in a last-ditch effort to flip the seat and hold onto their majority. Additionally, after spending nearly $8 million of his own money, Scott launched another $10 million ad campaign targeting voters in Orlando, Tampa, and Miami.

**Maryland (Likely Democrat)**: Prince George’s County Executive Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD) leads former Maryland Governor Larry Hogan (R-MD) by double digits in the race to replace retiring Sen. Ben Cardin (D-MD). The race has largely centered on abortion rights after Hogan declared he would vote to codify _Roe v. Wade_, breaking with other Republicans and Trump on the issue. Alsobrooks will need to repeat strong turnout in her home county, which helped springboard her to a decisive victory in the primary. Her campaign spent millions on ads attacking Hogan as someone who cannot be trusted on abortion and who would be a rubber stamp for Donald Trump’s policies in the Senate. However, Hogan’s popularity from his tenure as the state’s moderate governor and his vocal opposition to Trump could make him a formidable split-ticket candidate in a traditionally Democratic state. A pro-Hogan super PAC spent $19 million on ads alleging Alsobrooks improperly claimed tax breaks. Republicans have not won a Senate seat in Maryland since 1974.

**Michigan (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) leads former Rep. Mike Rogers (R-MI) as she looks to replace retiring longtime Sen. Debbie Stabenow (D-MI). Rogers criticized former President Trump shortly after the January 6 attack on the Capitol, but he has since repaired his relationship with the former president and secured Trump’s endorsement. The race at the top of the ticket is dead even while Rogers is polling behind Slotkin. Both candidates are emphasizing their national security expertise and experience. Meanwhile, Slotkin could lose support from Arab American voters concerned over her support for Israel. The Dearborn-based Arab American Political Action Committee urged the over 300,000 Arab Americans in the state not to vote for either Republican or Democratic candidates. In a tight race, serious defections from this “uncommitted” group could sway the race in Rogers’ favor. However, Slotkin is ahead in the Five Thirty-Eight polling average, and Quinnipiac polls from October 9 to October 21 shifted from a 48–48 tie to a 52–44 advantage for Slotkin with a 2.9 percent margin of error.

**Ohio (Toss-Up)**:Bernie Moreno (R) continues to cut into incumbent Sen. Sherrod Brown’s (D-OH) narrow lead. Moreno hopes his close ties to Trump, who won Ohio by eight points in 2016 and 2020, will help him repeat JD Vance’s (R-OH) win, where Trump’s endorsement helped propel the now vice-presidential nominee to victory two years ago. Moreno attacked Brown for being a “career politician” and supporting Harris’ “radical” policies on immigration and the economy. Brown is touting his Senate record while accusing Moreno of running for personal gain and attacking his lack of experience. Moreno’s recent comments on suburban women seeking abortions could impact his momentum. In a state where Trump is projected to win by a wide margin, Brown hopes his populist appeal and connections to union and working-class voters will produce enough split-ticket votes to win another term.

**Montana (Lean Republican)**: Former Navy SEAL Tim Sheehy’s (R) lead over incumbent Sen. Jon Tester (D-MT) continues to increase in a race that will likely decide Senate control. Sheehy’s “Send in the SEAL” campaign continues to blast Tester for supporting the Biden-Harris Administration on hot-button issues, including immigration, crime, and the economy. Sheehy recently sought to tie Tester to lobbyists. However, despite Tester’s current seven-point deficit in the Real Clear Polling average, his campaign raised $32 million during the third quarter, underscoring how important the race is to the Democrats’ chances of maintaining the majority. Additionally, with support from the DSCC, Tester launched ads tying Sheehy and the millionaires who have recently moved to Montana to ongoing problems in the state. Tester is no stranger to tough races; he won narrowly in 2012 and 2018. However, this is Tester’s first race in a presidential year in a state that supported Trump by nearly 17 points in 2020 and by 20 points in 2016.

**Nebraska (Lean Republican)**: In a surprisingly competitive contest, Navy veteran and union leader Dan Osborn (I-NE) is running a tight race against two-term incumbent Sen. Deb Fischer (R-NE). Both the Fischer and Osborn campaigns tout an advantage in internal polls. Fischer’s poll shows her leading 48–42 percent with 10 percent undecided, whereas Osborn’s poll shows him leading 47–42 percent with 10 percent undecided. Supported by the Retire Career Politicians PAC, Osborn outspent the incumbent by more than $2.5 million and continues to target Nebraska’s moderate voter base. Due to the shifting dynamics of the race, the National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) recently spent $552,000 on ads supporting Fischer, including a new ad featuring Trump. In an attempt to sway voters, Trump stated that the Independent challenger was a “radical left” candidate and a “Democrat in disguise.” Osborn has said he will not caucus with either party if elected. Cook Political Report recently changed its rating from “Likely Republican” to “Lean Republican.”

**Nevada (Lean Democrat)**:Incumbent Sen. Jacky Rosen (D-NV) maintains a steady lead against her Republican opponent, Army veteran Sam Brown, despite a narrow Trump lead in the state. Rosen is currently leading Brown by around five percent in polling averages. Rosen outraised Brown in every quarter and spent nearly $64 million since the start of her campaign. The incumbent nearly broke a Nevada Senate fundraising record set by Sen. Catherine Cortez Masto (D-NV) in 2022 after raising over $12 million in the third quarter of 2024. Meanwhile, the NRSC and Brown recently spent $331,000 on a hybrid ad strategy focusing on social security, taxes, and inflation.

**Pennsylvania (Toss-Up)**: Challenger Dave McCormick (R) cut the Real Clear Politics polling average in half in his effort to oust Sen. Bob Casey (D-PA). Cook Political Report recently shifted from “Lean Democrat” to “Toss-Up.” In their second debate, McCormick blamed Casey for Pennsylvania’s economic problems and inflation. Casey labeled McCormick, a former hedge fund manager, “out-of-touch” with Pennsylvanians. Abortion remains a key issue in the state. McCormick moderated his stance on the issue after supporting the reversal of _Roe v. Wade_. He questioned Casey’s commitment to the issue by reminding voters that the senator used to identify as pro-life. Casey acknowledges that this is his toughest race yet but continues to poll ahead of Harris.

**Texas (Lean Republican)**:Hoping to become the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Texas in nearly 40 years, Rep. Colin Allred (D-TX) continues to chip away at incumbent Sen. Ted Cruz’s (R-TX) lead in the Real Clear Polling average. This is a longshot race Democrats may need to win to maintain their majority. Allred has financial momentum, outraising Cruz by nearly $10 million during the third quarter. Democrats launched ads attacking Cruz on abortion, featuring women blaming the senator for their hardships following the reversal of _Roe v. Wade_. Cruz continues to attack Allred on the U.S.-Mexico border, claiming Allred would “dismantle the southern border and would be weak on immigration.” Cruz narrowly defeated former Rep. Beto O’Rourke (D-TX) in 2018 by just two and a half points. However, compared to that midterm campaign, Cruz will benefit from Trump’s popularity at the top of the ticket.

**Wisconsin (Toss-Up)**:The Wisconsin Senate race shifted from “Lean Democrat” to “Toss-Up” in a concerning sign for Democrats. Republican challenger Eric Hovde received support from the Senate Leadership Fund through ads claiming incumbent Sen. Tammy Baldwin (D-WI) is “pushing an aggressive LGBTQ agenda on kids” by directing taxpayer dollars to a clinic that provides care for transgender children. Baldwin denies the accusations and is outspending Hovde’s campaign by more than $10 million.

### **Fundraising**

The 2024 Senate election cycle will conclude with record-shattering fundraising numbers. In the previous third quarter alone, reports show incumbent Sen. Sherrod Brown (D-OH) leading with a reported $30.6 million raised, with Texas Senate hopeful Rep. Colin Allred (R-TX) close behind with an all-time high of $30.3 million raised. Arizona candidate Rep. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) was not too far behind with record highs of nearly $22 million, and incumbent Sen. Ted Cruz followed with $21 million raised. Spending on these races is also seeing its fair share of recorded highs, thanks to the involvement of PACs such as the NRSC and DSCC. Montana had a record-shattering $250 million spent on its race — the most ever in that state. In the Pennsylvania Senate race alone, $317 million has been spent so far — out of nearly $1 billion in total across all the state’s elections. This is a vital battleground for both the Senate majority and the presidency. Topping everyone is Ohio’s Senate race, with a whopping $400 million spent and counting as of October.

The DSCC raised a little over $28 million during September and ended the month with $37.3 million in cash on hand. Meanwhile, the NRSC raised $30 million during the same month and has $21 million in cash on hand.

**House**
---------

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Roughly 70 competitive House races will determine the majority. Cook Political Report rates 25 districts Toss-Ups and 18 districts leaning either Democrat or Republican. Among the 27 other competitive seats, Democrats are likely to win 17, and Republicans will likely retain control of 10. Given the potential for many too-close-to-call races on election night, determining which party will ultimately control the House could take days or weeks. California’s slow vote-counting process could delay results, given the number of competitive races in the state this cycle. In 2022, it took 13 days to finalize the results to confirm that Republicans took back the majority. Delays in the certification of election results could also be a factor. In 2022, Rep. Matt Cartwright’s (D-PA) race was not certified until November 30, two days following Pennsylvania’s November 28 deadline. With a December 5, 2024, deadline for race certification in California, it is possible it could take weeks after Election Day to resolve tight races in the state.

The below provides the current state of play in the country’s most competitive House races as we head into the final week of the election cycle.

### **Race Spotlights**

**AK-AL (Toss-Up)**: Following Alaska’s open primary, in which Rep. Mary Peltola (D-AK) secured over 50 percent of the vote, Peltola will face Republican Nick Begich, grandson of former Rep. Nick Begich, Sr. (D-AK). While Republicans are united behind Begich, Peltola faces the unexpected complication of a second Democrat on the ballot — Eric Hafner, currently serving a federal prison sentence in New York. Under Alaska’s election law, the top four finishers from the primary election advance to the general election. Despite receiving just 0.4 percent of the vote in the primary, Hafner qualified for the general election after two higher-vote-getting Republicans departed the race. Recent internal polling from House Republicans shows Begich leading Peltola by four points with 14 percent undecided.

**AZ-01 (Toss-Up)**:Rep. David Schweikert (R-AZ) faces a challenge from Democratic state representative and doctor Amish Shah for this Phoenix and Scottsdale-based district. Schweikert, a seven-term incumbent, won by just over 3,200 votes in 2022. Whether Schweikert can hold the seat largely depends on his ability to outperform Trump. Under the district’s current boundaries, Biden won the district by 1.5 percent in 2020. With the electorate’s large population of college-educated suburbanites, it is a bellwether seat that has steadily shifted more Democratic over the past few election cycles. Shah frequently criticizes Schweikert’s stance on reproductive rights and Democratic groups recently unearthed a comment made by Schweikert in 2022 that women who get abortions “hate that baby.” Meanwhile, Schweikert accused Shah of being soft on border security and said Shah’s lack of support for extending Trump’s tax policies will mean higher taxes for voters. Two Democratic polls from August and September found support for the candidates within the margin of error.

**AZ-06 (Toss-Up)**: Former Democratic state legislator Kirsten Engel is in a rematch against first-term incumbent Rep. Juan Ciscomani (R-AZ). Ciscomani, who narrowly defeated Engel by just over 5,000 votes in 2022, has positioned himself as a moderate within the House Republican Caucus, focusing his campaign on issues like the economy and border security. However, Engel challenges Ciscomani’s position on topics such as reproductive rights to draw a sharp contrast on social issues. Polling is sparse, but a July survey funded by Democrats showed a one-point edge for Engel, while Ciscomani’s internal polling from May showed him up 11 points.

**CA-13 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. John Duarte (R-CA) will meet Democratic challenger Adam Gray in a rematch of the second-closest 2022 House race. The Central Valley seat, which Biden won by 11 points in 2020, is a key potential pickup for House Democrats. A September poll sponsored by the Long Beach Center for Urban Politics and Policy has Gray leading Duarte by two points, within the margin of error. The result of this competitive seat will heavily depend on Latino turnout, a demographic that makes up nearly two-thirds of the district’s electorate. In the September poll, Latino voters preferred Gray over Duarte by 6.5 percent, although 18 percent were undecided.

**CA-16 (Solid Democrat)**: In this all-Democratic race to fill retiring Rep. Anna Eshoo’s (D-CA) Bay Area seat, California Assemblyman Evan Low and San Jose Mayor Sam Liccardo are in a tight race. In September, polling from the Long Beach Center for Politics and Policy showed Liccardo with a modest lead, and previous head-to-head polling showed Liccardo as the frontrunner. However, 40 percent of voters were still undecided at the time of the survey. In an October debate, the two Democrats attacked each other’s records while in their respective offices. Low was critical of Liccardo’s record on public safety while mayor. At the same time, Liccardo accused Low of breaking campaign ethics laws by using state money intended for state races while running for federal office.

**CA-22 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. David Valadao (R-CA) and former Democratic state assemblyman Rudy Salas are in a rematch of the 2022 election. Valadao is distancing himself from Trump and House Republicans and touting his bipartisan approach in Congress. Recent polling is mixed, showing either a tight race where the candidates are tied at 45 percent apiece or a four-point lead for Salas, buoyed by new voters and voters who did not vote in 2022. Biden won this district by 13 points in 2020, and Democrats hold a 17-point registration advantage. A high turnout will favor Salas.

**CA-27 (Toss-Up)**: The race between incumbent Rep. Mike Garcia (R-CA) and Democrat George Whitesides in this Northern Los Angeles County district is one of the tightest in the state. The Long Beach Center for Politics and Policy poll shows a dead heat, with 44 percent supporting Whitesides and 43 percent backing Garcia. Whitesides’ lead extends to 48 percent when undecided voters are pushed to choose versus Garcia’s 46 percent. The outcome of this race will depend on undecided and split-ticket voters. Biden carried the district by 12 points in 2020.

**CA-41 (Toss-Up)**: The Chairman of the Defense Appropriations Subcommittee Ken Calvert (R-CA) faces a rematch with Democrat Will Rollins (D) in what will likely be his toughest race since his first election in 1992. After losing by five points in 2022, Rollins seeks to make up that ground and unseat a Republican in a district seeing rapid demographic changes. The Long Beach Center for Politics and Policy poll shows the race in a dead heat.

**CA-45 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Michelle Steel (R-CA) faces a challenge from consumer rights attorney Derek Tran (D-CA) in a district that covers a swath of predominantly Asian communities in Orange County. In a seat where Vietnamese Americans make up about 20 percent of the electorate, in 2022 Steel — who is Korean — attacked a different Democratic opponent, Jay Chen, by arguing he was a communist sympathizer and tied to China. While Democrats criticized Steel’s tactics, she won convincingly in a district President Biden carried in 2020. Steel is making similar attacks against Tran, though her attacks may not play as well the second time, given that Tran is the son of Vietnamese refugees. A recent poll shows Steel narrowly trailing Tran.

**CO-08 (Toss-Up)**:Freshman Rep. Yadira Caraveo (D-CO) is facing Republican state representative Gabe Evans in a district that spans much of Denver’s northeastern suburbs. According to recent polls, Caraveo, who narrowly won the district in 2022 by about 1,600 votes, is running neck and neck with Evans. An early October Emerson College poll showed Caraveo and Evans tied at 44 percent with 12 percent undecided.

**IA-01 (Toss-Up)**: On her fourth attempt in 2020, Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks (R-IA) secured her victory to Congress by just six votes. Though she won re-election more handily in 2022, her rematch against Democratic candidate Christina Bohannan is expected to be competitive, especially after Miller-Meeks’ disappointing primary showing, where she won by only 12 points against a little-known challenger. After national election forecasters shifted their race predictions from “Lean Republican” to “Toss-Up,” this district is now in the national spotlight. Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) recently joined a rally for Miller-Meeks in Indianola, Iowa, to increase enthusiasm for the two-term Republican, indicating that the party is concerned about the race.

**IA-03 (Toss-Up)**:Freshman Rep. Zach Nunn (R-IA) is facing a challenge from former deputy chief of staff to Agriculture Secretary Tom Vilsack, Lanon Baccam (D-IA). The candidates recently faced off in their lone debate, where reproductive rights and health care access took center stage. The race tightened recently, and Cook Political Report shifted the race from “Lean Republican” to “Toss-Up” in early October.

**ME-02 (Toss-Up)**: This district is the second-most rural district in the country and the second whitest (only Kentucky’s Fifth District bests it in both categories). The district also voted for Trump by seven points in 2020. However, Rep. Jared Golden (D-ME) remains a popular figure who has successfully cast himself as an Independent, often bucking his party on issues from climate change to the border. Though Golden remains popular in the district, NASCAR driver and Republican state representative Austin Theriault hopes that a decisive Trump victory in rural Maine will send him to Washington.

**MI-07 (Toss-Up)**: Two former state senators, Democrat Curtis Hertel Jr. and Republican Tom Barrett, are locked in a tight race for Michigan’s open Seventh District, which spans the Lansing area and covers some of Detroit’s western suburbs. Barrett recently came under fire from Democrats for an advertisement that listed the wrong election date in a Black-owned newspaper. While the Barrett campaign said this was nothing more than a proofing error, Democrats have sought to paint it as an attempt to suppress the heavily Democratic Black vote. Though Barrett lost by over five points in his last run for this seat against current Senate candidate Rep. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI), Republicans see this district as one of their best pickup opportunities.

**MI-08 (Toss-Up)**: Republican Paul Junge, a former external affairs director at U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), is running for a third time in this mid-Michigan seat based in Flint and the Tri-Cities. After losing to the retiring Rep. Dan Kildee (D-MI) in 2020 and 2022, Junge now faces Democratic state senator Kristen McDonald Rivet in an open race. This region of Michigan has been shifting in favor of Republicans over the past several years, and Junge hopes to finally flip this seat.

**MT-01 (Lean Republican)**: Rep. Ryan Zinke (R-MT) and Olympian rower Democrat Monica Tranel are facing off to represent Montana’s First District. The two ran against each other in 2022, when Zinke defeated Tranel by three percentage points, a drastically tighter margin than the 16.4 points that Trump bested Biden by in the state in the 2020 presidential election. Skyrocketing housing costs due to the population increase in Montana are a key issue in the campaign. Tranel criticized Zinke for operating a rental property, accusing him of profiting from the housing crisis. Cook Political Report recently shifted the race from “Likely” to “Lean Republican.”

**NC-01 (Toss-Up)**: After redistricting reconfigured the district to favor the GOP, Republican candidate Laurie Buckhout hopes to make history by flipping North Carolina’s First District for the first time since 1883. Despite the challenging map, incumbent Rep. Don Davis’ (D-NC) moderate reputation and deep ties to the district have bolstered his chances of protecting the seat. As the only toss-up House race in the state, the contest is drawing millions of dollars in outside funding, with both parties reserving another $7 million each for the campaign’s final weeks.

**NE-02 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE) is no stranger to close races, serving four terms in Nebraska’s Democratic-leaning Second District. This year, he faces a rematch against Democratic state senator Tony Vargas, who he defeated by two points in 2022. Given Nebraska’s manner of allocating electoral votes based on the winner of congressional districts, the Omaha-area district is drawing national attention, with Harris favored to win one of the state’s three electoral votes.

**NJ-07 (Lean Republican)**: In the state’s only competitive race, incumbent Rep. Tom Kean Jr. (R-NJ) hopes to fend off Democratic candidate Sue Altman. Kean is painting Altman as a far-left extremist based on her record as executive director of the New Jersey Working Families Alliance. While Altman is a strong fundraiser — raising $2.1 million in the third quarter alone — she remains relatively unknown. She faces an onslaught of negative ads funded by the Congressional Leadership Fund (CLF). Cook Political Report recently shifted the race from “Toss-Up” to “Lean Republican.”

**NM-02 (Toss-Up)**: Incumbent Rep. Gabe Vasquez (D-NM) is in a rematch against former Rep. Yvette Herrell (R-NM), who he previously defeated by a narrow 1,300-vote margin. While the 2024 rematch will likely be another close race, recent polling shows Vasquez gaining momentum. Two third-party polls in late August and September show Vasquez leading Herrell by 10 and 9 points, respectively.

**NY-04 (Toss-Up)**: In a district that Democrats view as a key pickup opportunity, freshman incumbent Rep. Anthony D’Esposito (R-NY) is defending his seat in a rematch against Democratic candidate Laura Gillen. D’Esposito won the seat by four percent in 2022, despite Biden carrying the district by nearly 10 percent in 2020. Gillen is focused on reproductive rights and anti-corruption in the wake of recent alleg ations that D’Esposito had a romantic partner and his fiancée’s daughter on his congressional payroll. The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) and House Majority PAC are investing heavily to flip the seat this cycle.

**NY-17 (Toss-Up)**: After a month of significant ad spending, former Rep. Mondaire Jones (D-NY) closed the gap to a one-point deficit against freshman incumbent Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY). Democrats are suing to disqualify Anthony Frascone from appearing on the Working Families Party ballot line, claiming Republicans propped up the unknown candidate to siphon votes from Jones. While Democrats significantly outnumber Republicans in this Westchester and Hudson Valley-based district, Lawler effectively established himself as a moderate in Washington and made inroads with labor groups in the district.

**NY-19 (Toss-Up)**:The rematch between freshman Rep. Marc Molinaro (R-NY) and Democratic candidate Josh Riley is among the most bitter and expensive House races this cycle. The campaign has already cost the candidates over $35.5 million in ads, and the race is viewed as a national bellwether on reproductive rights and the border. Democrats hope Harris’ presence at the top of the ticket will help boost turnout for Riley, who lost to Molinaro in 2022 by less than two points.

**NY-22 (Lean Democrat)**: Freshman Rep. Brandon Williams (R-NY) is considered this cycle’s most vulnerable House incumbent after the district shifted to a Biden plus 11 in New York’s recent redistricting. That said, Republicans have not given up. The National Republican Campaign Committee (NRCC) and CLF are running ads tying Democratic candidate state senator John Mannion to contentious bail reform policies passed by the New York legislature and to Governor Kathy Hochul (D-NY), whose favorability rating slipped below Trump’s in New York. Despite this, Mannion has been leading in polls over recent months.

**OH-09 (Lean Democrat)**:Of the five Democrats representing districts won by then President Trump in 2020, Rep. Marcy Kaptur (D-OH) is considered one of the best positioned to win re-election. First elected to Congress in 1982, Kaptur witnessed the political winds shift in Northwest Ohio. Republicans hoped 2022 would be their chance to oust Kaptur but failed to flip the seat after a poor performance from controversial candidate J.R. Majewski (R). While the GOP has more faith in state representative Derrick Merrin, leader of the Ohio House Republican Caucus, polling shows Kaptur consistently ahead.

**OH-13 (Lean Democrat)**: Freshman Rep. Emilia Sykes (D-OH) is battling former Republican state legislator Kevin Coughlin in Ohio’s 13th District, which spans the cities of Akron and Canton. Sykes, one of a few Black lawmakers representing a predominantly white district, is highlighting her constituent services work and federal funds flowing into the district. At the same time, Coughlin is focused on national issues and is attempting to tie Sykes to both Biden and Harris. An August poll conducted by the National Journal shows Skyes with a 4-point lead over Coughlin.

**OR-05 (Toss-Up)**: After narrowly defeating progressive candidate Jamie McLeod-Skinner in 2022, Rep. Lori Chavez-DeRemer (R-OR) faces what will likely be a tougher challenge against Democratic state senator Janelle Bynum this November. Moderate Democrat Bynum previously defeated Chavez-DeRemer in two elections for the state house. A poll conducted in August found this race essentially tied.

**PA-07 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Susan Wild (D-PA) is working to hold on to her purple district in a deadlocked race against Republican state representative Ryan Mackenzie. The district is seen as a presidential bellwether and voted for the Electoral College winner in the past four presidential elections. The district is evenly divided between Democrats and Republicans, and President Biden narrowly edged out former President Trump in 2020, 49.7 percent to 49.1 percent. Party leaders are flooding the district, with recent visits from Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY).

**PA-08 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Matt Cartwright (D-PA) is the only House Democrat to successfully win re-election four times in a district Trump carried in both 2016 and 2020. He hopes to tap into the same playbook this November, this time against Republican candidate Rob Bresnahan, a wealthy construction company CEO and first-time candidate for public office. Cartwright will lean on his personal brand and appeal to Independent and Republican voters as he defends this Republican-leaning district.

**PA-10 (Toss-Up)**: Former House Freedom Caucus Chairman and six-term incumbent Rep. Scott Perry (R-PA) faces longtime television anchor Janelle Stelson, his most formidable challenger in recent years. Stelson is outraising Perry, bringing in $2.7 million in the third quarter. While recent internal polling from the Stelson campaign has her within the margin of error, some national Republicans were initially not convinced the race would be competitive. They largely sat out of the race, focusing their dollars on other competitive races in the Commonwealth. With the district’s recent shift from “Lean Republican” to “Toss-Up,” outside Republican investment is pouring into the district. House Freedom Fund, the PAC associated with the House Freedom Caucus, is running an ad tying Stelson to Democratic megadonor George Soros.

**VA-07 (Toss-Up)**: Republican Army Special Forces combat veteran Derrick Anderson and Democratic retired Army lieutenant colonel Eugene Vindman are locked in a neck-and-neck race to fill the seat vacated by gubernatorial candidate Rep. Abigail Spanberger (D-VA). Vindman, a first-time candidate known for his role in the Trump impeachment hearings, lacks some of the crucial inroads that Spanberger built with Independent and Republican voters in this purple district. Cook Political Report recently shifted the rating from “Lean Democrat” to “Toss-Up.” The presidential race will likely impact the outcome of this congressional contest, and Democrats hope a strong showing by Harris will help secure a Vindman victory.

**WA-03 (Toss-Up)**: Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-WA) is in a tight rematch against Trump-aligned Republican Joe Kent. While Gluesenkamp Perez led Kent 46 to 39 percent in their open, top-two primary, she must secure a significant portion of Republican voters as she did in 2022. Polling from early October shows Kent and Gluesenkamp Perez in a dead heat with 46 percent each and shows Trump leading Harris by a 50 to 45 percent margin. In 2020, Trump won the district by approximately four points.

**WA-04 (Solid Republican)**: Rep. Dan Newhouse (R-WA), a five-term incumbent, faces a challenge from fellow Republican and former NASCAR driver Jerrod Sessler. Newhouse drew criticism from Trump-aligned Republicans like Sessler for his vote to impeach Trump in the wake of January 6. However, Newhouse’s moderate appeal might benefit him in the general election, especially with Democrats and Independents who have only a more conservative option in Sessler.

### **Party Committee Updates/Fundraising**

The DCCC raised $30.3 million in September, for a total of $70.1 million in the third quarter and $280.9 million this cycle. The NRCC raised $18 million in September and took out a $15 million line of credit. The two committees head into the final days of the campaign neck and neck in cash-on-hand; the DCCC has $49.9 million cash on hand and no debt compared to the NRCC’s $48.8 million.

The House Majority PAC, the Democratic super PAC, raised $99 million in the third quarter compared to the $81.4 million by the Republican super PAC, the Congressional Leadership Fund. As of October 1, each PAC raised roughly $210 million this cycle.

Analysis of third-quarter Federal Election Commission filings shows Democratic candidates in competitive races continue to outraise their Republican opponents. In the 43 races Cook Political Report rates as Toss-Up, Lean Democrat, or Lean Republican, Democrats raised an average of nearly $2 million in the third quarter, compared to just under $800,000 for the Republican candidates. Incumbent Rep. Ryan Zinke (R-MT) and former Rep. Mayra Flores (R-TX) were the only two Republicans in competitive districts to outraise their Democratic opponents in the third quarter. In the 43 districts, 13 Republicans currently have more cash on hand, while 30 Democrats have the cash advantage.

### **Race Spotlights**

**Indiana (Likely Republican)**: Earlier this month, Cook Political Report shifted the race between frontrunner Sen. Mike Braun (R-IN) and former Indiana Superintendent of Public Instruction Jennifer McCormick from “Solid Republican” to “Likely Republican.” The rating changes cited McCormick’s recent party affiliation change from Republican to Democrat as appealing to moderate voters. Libertarian nominee Donald Rainwater is polling at nearly 6 percent, which could pull support from Braun’s base. Additionally, Braun drew criticism for running an attack ad featuring an AI-generated image of McCormick without proper disclosure. The DGA and RGA are pouring money into this race, contributing $600,000 and $1 million respectively. Despite all the buzz, this race is a longshot for Democrats. Republicans have held the governorship for 20 years, and Braun has Trump’s endorsement in a state that Trump won by 15 points in 2020.

**New Hampshire****(Toss-Up)**: Recent polls show former U.S. Senator Kelly Ayotte (R) with a narrow one-point lead over former Manchester mayor Joyce Craig (D) in the race to replace retiring Governor Chris Sununu (R). Republicans are heavily outspending Democrats on TV ads in this race, with Ayotte’s campaign and the New Hampshire GOP spending a combined $10.1 million to date compared to Craig and the state Democrats’ combined $4.4 million. In TV ads and the second debate, the candidates continued to attack each other’s records, with Ayotte spotlighting crime and proposed tax increases during Craig’s mayoral tenure. Meanwhile, Craig focusing on Ayotte’s record on abortion and, more recently, her position as a board member of private equity real estate investment firm Blackstone amid rising housing costs in the state.

**Washington (Likely Democrat)**: Polling since the August primary shows state Attorney General Bob Ferguson (D) with a comfortable 16-point lead over former Rep. Dave Reichert (R) in the race to replace retiring Governor Jay Inslee (D). While the Republican Governors Association (RGA) has yet to contribute to this race, Ferguson and aligned Democratic groups — including the Democratic Governors Association (DGA) — spent over $5 million in the past few months on TV ads, nearly as much as Reichert’s campaign raised throughout the cycle. Democratic ads attacked Reichert’s record on abortion, a top three issue among Washington voters. Reichert’s campaign is focused on crime, highlighting his background as a county sheriff and accusations of the state’s increased crime rates compared to pre-pandemic levels under Ferguson’s tenure. Republicans hope ballot measures to rollback a handful of climate, tax, and health policies enacted by the Democratic-controlled Washington legislature will help boost turnout among conservative voters in a state where Trump lost to Biden by nearly 20 points.

**North Carolina (Likely Democrat)**: Attorney General Josh Stein (D) is leading Lieutenant Governor Mark Robinson (R) by double digits after a bombshell CNN report alleged that Robinson made explicit racial and sexual posts on a pornography message board. Robinson denied the claims and sued CNN for slander. However, the scandal is impacting other contests on the ticket, with a Democratic lieutenant governor candidate running an ad joking about cleaning up Robinson’s office and Trump declining to comment on whether he would still urge voters to support Robinson after formally endorsing him in March. Robinson and Stein are also working to get out the vote in the aftermath of Hurricane Helene.
