Title: Texas Gov. Greg Abbott directs lawmakers to undergo mid-decade redraw of congressional maps

URL Source: https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/capital-region/news/2025/07/10/abbott-special-session-texas-congressional-redistricting

Published Time: 2025-07-14T08:15:00.000-05:00

Markdown Content:
WASHINGTON — Buried in the list of more than a dozen agenda items for Texas’s special legislative session is a highly unusual and controversial directive to redraw the state’s congressional maps in the middle of the decade. Democrats say it’s a political power grab, an attempt to create more districts likely to elect Republicans to the House of Representatives. Republicans maintain redistricting is necessary to address constitutional concerns about the current maps.

* * *

#### **What You Need To Know**

##### *   Gov. Greg Abbott is asking the Texas legislature to redraw the congressional maps, because President Donald Trump’s Justice Department raised constitutional concerns

*   Spectrum News obtained the DOJ’s letter to the state’s leaders urging them to “rectify the race-based considerations" allegedly used in four heavily Democratic congressional districts, three are anchored in Houston and one is in Fort Worth

*   The White House is reportedly behind the push to redraw Texas’ congressional lines to create more Republican-held seats and blunt Democrat gains across the country in next year’s election

*   While Rep. Marc Veasey, D-Fort Worth, and Rep. Sylvia Garica, D-Houston, said it was a power grab, some redistricting experts question the timing of the DOJ’s letter

* * *

Rep. Marc Veasey, D-Fort Worth, is not holding back. He notes that as Texas reels from last week’s deadly flash floods, Gov. Greg Abbott, R-Texas, and other Texas Republicans are moving to redraw the state’s congressional district lines.

“To mix all of that in while we're trying to help our neighbors in Central Texas, I think this the lowest of the low,” Veasey told Spectrum News.

The governor says it is because President Donald Trump’s Justice Department raised constitutional concerns about the current district lines, which were drawn after the 2020 census.

Spectrum News obtained the DOJ’s letter to the state’s leaders urging them to “rectify the race-based considerations" that allegedly were used in creating four of the current congressional districts. All of them are heavily Democratic. Congressional districts 9, 18, and 29 are anchored in Houston. Texas’s 33rd congressional district is based in Fort Worth, and currently held by Veasey, a former state lawmaker.

“Obviously it's done in order to be able to maximize gains at the expense of everyone else's rights, particularly in this case, the rights of Black and Latino voters, and so I'm not surprised at all that they did it this way,” Veasey said.

Rep. Sylvia Garcia, D-Houston, represents the 29th congressional district. In a statement to Spectrum News, Garcia said, in part, "Let's call this what it is: a brazen, racist power grab. It's pathetic and rotten to the core—a desperate attempt to ensure that they can keep the majority in the House of Representatives by force, despite Trump's increasingly unpopular agenda for our country."

“This fight is about protecting our shared values of fairness, dignity, and the belief that our democracy belongs to all of us, not just the powerful few. The people of my district have fought for generations to claim their rightful place in our democracy," Garcia continued.

Just three years ago, the Republican-led Texas state legislature redrew the congressional maps to be more favorable for the GOP, with 25 districts that are largely safe for Texas Republicans and 13 for Democrats, one of which is currently vacant.

Spectrum News asked one redistricting expert if the DOJ’s concerns about racial gerrymandering appear legitimate.

“It seems like it's very well-timed,” said Shawn Donahue, clinical assistant professor at the University of Buffalo.

“So what all of a sudden, on July the 7th, 2025 made it where these districts are of particular concern? Or is it that this is just trying to give the governor and the legislature some type of a pretext?” Donahue continued.

The New York Times reported the White House is behind the push to redraw Texas’s congressional lines to create more Republican-held seats.

The goal is to blunt Democrat gains across the country in next year’s election, and maintain GOP control of the House. But reportedly not all Texas Republicans are eager to redraw the lines.

The White House did not respond Spectrum News' request for further comment.

“I think the question is, you know, were Republicans actually drawing districts in 2021 and 2022 to benefit racial minorities, or were they really trying to pack Democrats into as few districts as possible in order to make the surrounding districts more Republican?” Donahue said.

Veasey vows that he will fight these changes.

“What disappoints me more than anything else is that, you know, when will state leaders finally stand up to Trump?” Veasey said.

“If you're the governor of a state and let Trump come in and just dictate this thing from top to bottom, I think, is a bad look for the state of Texas and a bad look for Texas independence and not what I would call real Texas leadership,” he continued.

Veasey said he finds it hard to believe that state Republicans would only focus on the four congressional districts the Justice Department singled out and, and says they may rethink all of the state’s 38 House seats.
