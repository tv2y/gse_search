Protest-ridden Columbia University is advising teachers to go "fully remote" after 100 people have been arrested on or near campus in the past week.

In an email Monday night, provost Angela V. Olinto told professors to offer hybrid classes if even one student wants to finish the year out online.

The option would last for the rest of the semester, which ends next Monday.

The main Columbia campus in Manhattan's Upper West Side has turned into a tent city as pro-Palestine protesters demand that the school divest from companies "complicit in genocide."

In response, pro-Israel protesters and other outside agitators have shown up to make their voices heard, turning the usually quiet campus into a tumultuous scene in the last several days.

Now, teachers are being advised to consider virtual learning as administrators seek to avoid any more trouble.

"A degree from @Columbia today is not worth more than a roll of toilet paper. Parents: definitely demand a refund & compensation!" said Councilwoman Vernikov in a post on X Monday.

The Columbia protests appear to be largely peaceful, though some demonstrators near the school have taken to chanting inflammatory phrases like, "al-Qassam you make us proud, kill another soldier now."

The protests there have sparked similar demonstrations at New York University and Yale University in New Haven, Connecticut.

At NYU, about 120 people were arrested on Monday and charged with either trespassing or resisting arrest.
