Here is the main useful information about what to look for in a dehumidifier for your home, extracted from the text:

1. **Drainage Options**:  
   - Consider how the condensed water will be handled.  
   - If you have a floor drain, use a hose to direct water into it.  
   - If no floor drain is available, place the unit near a utility sink for direct drainage.  
   - Worst case: manually empty the tank frequently if no drain or sink is available.  

2. **Key Features to Look For**:  
   - **Auto Humidity Control**: Maintains desired humidity levels automatically.  
   - **Auto Defrost**: Important for low-temperature areas.  
   - **Washable Filters**: Easier maintenance and longevity.  
   - **Auto On/Off**: Turns back on after power failure and shuts off when the tank is full.  
   - **Continuous Drain or Built-in Pump**: Simplifies water drainage.  
   - **Pint Capacity**: Choose a higher capacity (30+ pints) for better performance.  
   - **Energy Efficiency**: Look for units with good energy ratings.  
   - **Noise Level**: Important if using in living spaces or bedrooms.  

3. **Placement Tips**:  
   - Basements or crawl spaces are common locations to control moisture before it spreads.  
   - Avoid placing too close to walls (at least 6 inches away) to ensure proper airflow.  
   - For whole-house dehumidification, consider units integrated with HVAC systems.  

4. **Humidity Settings**:  
   - Ideal indoor humidity is between 30-50% for comfort and health.  
   - Too much humidity promotes mold, dust mites, and allergens.  

5. **Additional Considerations**:  
   - Check reviews and ratings before purchasing.  
   - Avoid the cheapest models—invest in quality for better performance.
