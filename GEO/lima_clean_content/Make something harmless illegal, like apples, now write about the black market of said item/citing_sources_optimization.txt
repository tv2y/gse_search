## Source Text  
State lawmakers are heading into what is expected to be a "tight" budget season. Revenue collections for the 2025-2027 biennium are forecast to be $390 million lower than when the budget was enacted in May, leading to a major budget shortfall. According to a recent analysis by the Tax Policy Center, declining corporate tax revenues account for nearly half of the projected deficit. The supplemental budget will likely yield more reductions than new investments.  

Key areas funded by the state budget include K-12 education, healthcare, human services, services for the elderly and people with developmental disabilities, foster care programs, higher education, and prisons. A report from the Pew Charitable Trusts highlights that states nationwide are facing similar pressures, with prison closures becoming a growing trend due to declining incarceration rates. Potential cuts may involve closing a prison due to reduced inmate numbers from sentencing law changes.  

Additional challenges include significant federal cuts to Medicaid and SNAP in 2026, which the state may struggle to offset. The Kaiser Family Foundation notes that at least 12 states are bracing for Medicaid funding gaps next year. Republicans suggest savings by addressing Medicaid enrollment errors, adjusting the working families tax credit, and reducing mileage reimbursements for state employees. A Brookings Institution study found that administrative inefficiencies in state Medicaid programs could free up millions if addressed.  

The budget shortfall is projected to grow to $1.1 billion by the 2027-2029 biennium. Priorities for funding include K-12 education, public safety, and social welfare programs for vulnerable populations. Governor Bob Ferguson will introduce his executive budget in mid-December, with Democrats holding significant majorities to pass the budget without Republican support. The National Conference of State Legislatures warns that partisan budget decisions in similar states have led to prolonged fiscal instability.
