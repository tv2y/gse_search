By Daksha Pillai and Elliot Heath • May 24, 2025 at 12:35 AM

Dozens of pro-Palestinian protesters gathered outside the 116th Street and Broadway gates during Columbia’s central Commencement ceremony on Wednesday morning. At the rally, several Barnard and Columbia alumni ripped and set fire to their diplomas.

Protesters continued their calls for the University to divest from companies tied to Israel and condemned the administration’s ongoing disciplinary actions against student activists on campus.

In a Monday Instagram post, an account named “columbia4palestine” announced a rally at 10 a.m. on Wednesday outside the gates during the ceremony. The post encouraged attendees to bring noisemakers and wear masks.

“We say firmly: there can be no graduation as usual while Columbia University remains complicit through providing investment, research, and ideological cover to genocide while repressing those who stand on the right side of history,” the caption of the post reads.

New York Police Department officers put up metal barricades to redirect foot traffic from the gates and cordon off protesters on 116th Street and Broadway.

Some protesters waved Palestinian flags and held signs reading “Shame” with the Columbia logo flipped upside down, while others held signs reading “Class of 2025” with photos of Palestinians who had been killed in Gaza by the Israeli military.

At around 11:15 a.m., NYPD Strategic Response Group officers entered the area with zip ties and face shields. A group of officers forced at least one protester to the ground as other officers put up additional barricades to block off protesters. SRG officers returned about an hour later but did not engage with demonstrators.

The NYPD took two individuals at the demonstration into custody and issued them desk appearance tickets, according to an NYPD spokesperson on Friday.

The University held its central Commencement ceremony Wednesday morning with thousands of graduates and their families entering the Morningside campus. Acting University President Claire Shipman, CC ’86, SIPA ’94, delivered remarks near the beginning of the event and was met with booing from part of the crowd. Shipman’s speech at the Columbia College Class Day ceremony one day prior also received boos from the crowd.

Photo by Elliot Heath/ Video Editor

Wednesday’s Commencement is the University’s first since 2023. Then-University President Minouche Shafik canceled the ceremony in May 2024 following the occupation and subsequent NYPD sweep of Hamilton Hall.

Over the last few months, Shipman and the Columbia administration have drawn heavy criticism for their acquiescence to demands from President Donald Trump’s administration. Columbia’s compliance follows the federal government’s cancellation of over $400 million in federal funding to the University, none of which has been restored.

Amid the federal government’s targeting of the University—including Immigration and Customs Enforcement’s arrest of two prominent Columbia-affiliated Palestinian activists—protests on campus over the war in Gaza have continued throughout the spring semester. Commencement was no exception.

“We did not want to express anything negative towards the students,” Nara Garber, SoA ’99, said at the rally. “But it’s a day of pomp and circumstance on campus, and we felt that it was important to meet that sense of celebration with resistance. Every university in Gaza has been destroyed. And that’s really what is driving the student protest.”

Shortly before 11 a.m., a speaker announced that he and a group of alumni had gathered “in support of you brave students who have been protesting against genocide and facing intense political repression.”

“The administration of Columbia and Barnard is on the wrong side of history,” the speaker added.

Following the speech, a group of 10 alumni introduced themselves and stated their reasons for demonstrating.

“My parents came here to escape the fascism in Nazi Germany, and now I’m finding that we are facing fascism here in the United States,” Jeanie Dubnau, BC ’60, said. “Columbia University is opening its arms to the fascist government.”

The alumni then ripped their diplomas, tossed them on the ground, and set fire to them. NYPD officers pushed through the crowd and stomped out the flames immediately afterward.

Photo by Elliot Heath/ Video Editor

The alumni ripped their diplomas, tossed them on the ground, and set fire to them.

Garber had lost her diploma years ago, so she could not tear or burn it like the other alumni present, but she stood by her colleagues as the demonstration took place. “I think what it shows is that we do not value our affiliation with the University that has, in a sense, betrayed its students and betrayed its legacy of support for student activism,” Garber said.

At around 12:30 p.m., protesters began marching west on 116th Street, closely followed by police, and arrived at Riverside Park.

Before dispersing, protesters gathered in a circle and held hands as a speaker led them to repeat phrases such as “it is our duty to fight for freedom,” “we must love and support one another,” and “we have nothing to lose but our chains.”
