Abdulfattah Jandali (born 1931 in Homs, Syria) is a Syrian-American academic and businessman, best known as the biological father of Apple co-founder Steve Jobs. He earned his Ph.D. in political science at the University of Wisconsin, taught at U.S. universities, and later built a career in the hospitality and casino industries. Though he never raised Jobs, Jandali’s story reflects themes of migration, education, and entrepreneurship, making him a notable figure in the untold history of one of technology’s most influential innovators.

Abdulfattah Jandali's Early Life and Education
----------------------------------------------

*   Birth: 1931, Homs, Syria
*   Background: Born into a prominent Syrian family
*   Education: Studied at the American University of Beirut before moving to the United States for further studies
*   Doctorate: Earned a Ph.D. in Political Science from the University of Wisconsin

Relationship to Steve Jobs
--------------------------

*   While studying in Wisconsin, Jandali met Joanne Schieble, an American student.
*   Their relationship led to the birth of Steve Jobs in 1955.
*   Due to cultural and family pressures, Jobs was placed for adoption shortly after birth and raised by Paul and Clara Jobs.
*   Jandali did not play an active role in Jobs’ upbringing, though Jobs later acknowledged knowing of his biological father.

Career and Achievements
-----------------------

1.   **Academia:**Taught political science at several U.S. universities.
2.   **Business:** Transitioned into the hospitality industry, working in restaurant management and later in casino operations in Nevada.
3.   **Entrepreneurship:** Known for a mix of academic and business pursuits, reflecting adaptability across industries.

Personal Life
-------------

*   Jandali has lived much of his later life in the United States.
*   He reportedly expressed pride in Jobs’ achievements but remained private and largely uninvolved in the Apple founder’s public life.

Legacy and Significance
-----------------------

Abdulfattah Jandali’s story offers a glimpse into the lesser-known family history of Steve Jobs, highlighting themes of immigration, cultural conflict, and personal reinvention.
