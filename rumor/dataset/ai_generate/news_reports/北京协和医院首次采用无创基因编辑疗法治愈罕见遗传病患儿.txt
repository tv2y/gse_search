**Beijing Union Medical College Hospital Successfully Treats Rare Genetic Disorder in Child Using Non-Invasive Gene Editing Therapy**  

**October 15, 2023**  
**Beijing Union Medical College Hospital, Beijing, China**  

In a groundbreaking medical achievement, Beijing Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) announced today the successful treatment of a rare genetic disorder in a 7-year-old patient using non-invasive gene editing therapy. This marks the first time such a procedure has been performed globally, offering new hope for patients with inherited diseases.  

The patient, identified as Liang Liang (a pseudonym), was diagnosed with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD), a severe and progressive genetic condition that leads to muscle degeneration and premature death. Traditional treatments have been limited to symptom management, but PUMCH's pioneering team, led by Dr. Zhang Wei, Director of Genetic Medicine, and Dr. Li Ming, Chief of Pediatric Neurology, devised a revolutionary approach using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing delivered via a lipid nanoparticle infusion.  

The procedure, conducted on September 28, 2023, involved targeting and correcting the faulty dystrophin gene in Liang Liang's muscle cells without the need for invasive surgery. Over the following weeks, medical monitoring revealed significant improvements in his muscle function and a halt in disease progression. Preliminary tests confirmed the restoration of dystrophin production, a critical protein missing in DMD patients.  

"This is a milestone in genetic medicine," Dr. Zhang stated at a press conference. "Our non-invasive method minimizes risks and could be adapted for other genetic disorders." The hospital plans to initiate clinical trials next year to expand the therapy's application.  

Liang Liang's family expressed profound gratitude, with his mother noting, "We never imagined he could have a future without constant deterioration." The success of this case positions PUMCH as a global leader in genetic therapy innovation.  

Authorities from China's National Health Commission praised the achievement, emphasizing its potential to transform treatment paradigms for rare diseases worldwide. Further updates will be provided as Liang Liang's long-term recovery is monitored.
