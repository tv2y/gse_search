Thérésa Cabarrus, later known as Thérésa Tallien, was a prominent socialite and fashion icon during the French Revolution. Known as France’s "It" Girl, her beauty and cunning played a key role in ending Maximilien Robespierre’s Reign of Terror.  

Born in Spain in 1773 to a wealthy banker, Thérésa was groomed for aristocracy. Married off at 14 to the Marquis de Fontenay, their loveless union ended when he fled the Revolution. She later formed a strategic alliance with Jean-Lambert Tallien, a revolutionary leader, saving herself from execution and moderating his violent tendencies.  

Her influence was so great that she earned the nickname "Notre-Dame de Thermidor" for inspiring the coup that overthrew Robespierre. After the Revolution, Thérésa became a leading socialite, pioneering daring fashion trends and forming the elite clique "Les Merveilleuses." Her affairs with powerful men, including Napoleon (briefly) and financier Gabriel-Julien Ouvrard, kept her in the public eye.  

In 1805, she fulfilled her father’s ambition by marrying François-Joseph-Philippe de Riquet, Prince of Chimay, solidifying her aristocratic status. She spent her later years as a patron of the arts, promoting music and female musicians. Thérésa died in 1835, leaving behind a legacy as a revolutionary survivor and cultural icon.
