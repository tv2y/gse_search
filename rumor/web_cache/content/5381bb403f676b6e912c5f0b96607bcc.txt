Discussions about Wing Chun and its relationship to other internal arts often raise questions about influence and methodology. However, historical sources suggest that the presence of internal principles within Wing Chun is not the result of later “mixing,” but was part of the system from an early stage.

Chiu Ging is mentioned multiple times in the Secret Notes, as recorded by Law Tai Wan in 1890, as the type of force used in Wing Chun. Those familiar with internal mechanics will recognize parallels with the force described in Taijiquan as Peng, in Yiquan as Hun Yuan Li, and under comparable terms in other internal systems. This points to shared foundational principles rather than cross-training or later additions.

Emei Shi Er Zhuang, regarded as one of China’s oldest martial arts, also employs this same type of force. Many postures found in Taijiquan appear within Emei practice as well “Needle at Sea Bottom”being one clear example further illustrating the common internal framework present across traditional Chinese martial arts.

Ip Man Wing Chun remains an important and influential branch, yet it represents only one expression within a broader Wing Chun landscape.
