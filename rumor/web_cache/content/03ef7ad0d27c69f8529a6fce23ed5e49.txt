In southern China, Super Typhoon Ragasa, the most powerful typhoon to hit the region in recent years, made itself felt in the Pearl River Delta before midday on Wednesday as the typhoon moved closer to making landfall, according to the National Meteorological Centre.

In Taiwan, 14 people were confirmed dead, 34 injured and 152 missing by Super Typhoon Ragasa, according to the island’s Ministry of Health and Welfare.

Wind speeds of 212km/h were recorded in the city of Zhuhai.

Around 11am, the National Meteorological Centre forecast that Ragasa would weaken in the coming hours because of the influence of “dry air intrusion” and “terrain effects”, according to the Guangzhou Broadcasting Network.

Guangdong province had upgraded its typhoon alert to Level 1, the highest in a four-tier warning system, as officials declared a “combat-ready” status on Tuesday.

The National Meteorological Centre said that in addition to high winds, rain between 25cm to 45cm (9 to 17 inches) and waves as high as 7 metres (23 feet) were expected.

The typhoon was forecast to make landfall between Taishan and Xuwen county in western Guangdong on Wednesday afternoon or evening, and heavy rainfall and wind would continue through Thursday, according to Guangdong’s weather forecaster.

More than 371,000 people have been evacuated across the province while helicopters and drones, 23 ships and 38,000 firefighters remain on standby.

In Guangdong, more than 10 cities announced schools, businesses, markets and transport closures, affecting tens of millions of people.

By midday on Tuesday, the southern tech hub of Shenzhen had cancelled 210 departing flights and 319 incoming flights. All buses, taxis, subways and highways were closed by Tuesday evening.

Residents have been stockpiling food, water and even portable phone batteries for the past two days. Businesses have taped their windows, tied down rubbish bins and outdoor chairs, while local governments trimmed trees and checked underground passageways, construction sites and mountainous regions for safety hazards.

Residents told to move inland

Zhuhai is being battered by 212km/hour winds. On Tuesday, with the typhoon expected to intensify, authorities in the city’s Jinwan district advised residents living on the coast to relocate inland. They said arrangements had been made with several hotels to offer discounted rooms – ranging from 150 yuan to 300 yuan (US$21 to US$42) a night – for coastal residents to take shelter.

Ragasa compared with Mangkhut

Shenzhen’s weather bureau says Ragasa’s “central intensity” – with a wind force of 55 metres per second – has surpassed that of Super Typhoon Mangkhut, the world’s biggest storm in 2018, state-run NFNews reports. But as Ragasa passes over waters near Shenzhen on Wednesday, its winds affecting the city are expected to be weaker than Mangkhut’s. At least four people were killed in Guangdong as a result of Mangkhut, with direct economic losses from the typhoon exceeding 4.2 billion yuan.

11:50AM

Zhuhai hit by 212km/h winds

The Pearl River Delta in Guangdong is starting to see the full effects of Ragasa as the typhoon moves closer to making landfall, according to the National Meteorological Centre. Wind speeds of 212km per hour have been recorded in the city of Zhuhai. Heavy rainfall is also expected in eastern China, including Jiangsu and Anhui provinces. Ragasa had been at the intensity of a super typhoon for 74 hours as of 10am, the national weather bureau says.

11:15AM

Vow to protect nuclear power plants

The state-owned China General Nuclear Power Group says it will make every effort to protect its nuclear power plants in Guangdong and the Guangxi Zhuang region, news outlet Yicai reports. The company has evacuated more than 14,000 people at its Lufeng plant, which is still under construction in eastern Guangdong. Meanwhile, China Southern Power Grid – one of two main grids covering southern China – has mobilised more than 40,000 personnel for emergency response preparations in Guangdong, Guangxi, Hainan, Yunnan and Guizhou.

11:11AM

Ragasa to weaken in coming hours

The National Meteorological Centre has forecast that Ragasa will weaken in the coming hours due to the influence of “dry air intrusion” and “terrain effects”, according to the Guangzhou Broadcasting Network.

11:07AM

Troops standing by

The People’s Liberation Army Southern Theatre Command says its troops are standing by and are “combat-ready” across Guangdong. It says they are equipped with assault boats, life jackets and emergency lighting devices for evacuation missions. They have also conducted checks on emergency shelters to ensure they are prepared.

10:40AM

Hainan to stop train services

Authorities in Zhanjiang, in the southwest of Guangdong province, announced that all economic activities will be suspended from 3pm – the latest city to issue such a notice. To the south, the tropical island of Hainan will suspend all train services in the afternoon because of the typhoon.

10:39AM

Condolences to Taiwan families

Chen Binhua, a spokesman for the Taiwan Affairs Office, has expressed condolences to the families of those in Taiwan who lost their lives in the typhoon. “We extend our sympathy to the families of the deceased and injured and hope the lives of those affected will soon return to normal,” Chen told reporters in Beijing.

10:26AM

Ragasa to the south of Shenzhen

The typhoon is passing about 156km south of Shenzhen and will continue moving west in the next three hours at a speed of 20km to 25km per hour, according to the city’s weather bureau. Strong winds are expected to persist until midday. More than 100 trees are reported to have fallen across Shenzhen and an emergency task force is working to clean up debris from the typhoon. No injuries have been reported.

10:24AM

Businesses count the cost

Ragasa has brought transport and business to a standstill across most cities in the Greater Bay Area and parts of western Guangdong since late Tuesday. The area is home to more than 87 million people and its GDP exceeds 14.5 trillion yuan (US$2.04 trillion), according to official data from 2024. As residents post to social media about the force of nature they are witnessing, businesses are counting the cost. Shenzhen Delp Instrument, a hi-tech manufacturer of products used for heavy metals, alloy and halogen analysis, said on WeChat on Tuesday: “In 2024, Shenzhen’s GDP reached 3.68 trillion yuan, averaging about 10.08 billion yuan per day. But a full shutdown doesn’t mean losses are half of that proportionally – the impact varies greatly across industries.”

10:13AM

Shenzhen insurers open hotlines

Insurance companies in Shenzhen have launched 24/7 hotlines to provide claims services for customers affected by Typhoon Ragasa. Insurers have been working with the government to monitor the city during the super typhoon using drones, while China Pacific Insurance says it has made 181 rescue cars and tow trucks available for emergency use.

10:10AM

New typhoon to east of Philippines

The typhoon is expected to make landfall between the city of Taishan and Xuwen county in western Guangdong on Wednesday afternoon or evening, with heavy rainfall and strong winds to continue through Thursday, according to Guangdong’s weather bureau. Meanwhile, a new typhoon – named Bualoi – formed at 5am on Wednesday to the east of the Philippines. Bualoi is expected to enter the South China Sea by Saturday.

10:04AM

Transport and flights disrupted

Transport across Guangdong has been disrupted by the typhoon. Trains, buses and taxis, including ride-hailing services, were suspended in Guangzhou and Shenzhen on Tuesday evening and remain shut on Wednesday morning. All highways in Shenzhen and most in Guangzhou have been closed since 8pm on Tuesday. Meanwhile, airports in Shenzhen and Zhuhai halted flights on Tuesday evening. Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport urged passengers to “keep a close eye on changes to flight information”. Train services across Guangdong province have been suspended on Wednesday. The Humen Bridge – which links Dongguan and Guangzhou – has been closed since 8pm on Tuesday. The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge and Shenzhen–Zhongshan Link has also restricted traffic.

9:55AM

Flying into the storm

Hong Kong’s Government Flying Service has released a clip of its officers flying into the eye of Super Typhoon Ragasa. The mission on a Challenger 605 plane took place on Tuesday, capturing wind speeds exceeding 135 km/h (81mph), 120km (75 miles) from the eye of the storm. Crews deploy meteorological data detectors to help forecasters determine the strength and direction of major storms.

Ragasa lashes Hong Kong

Hong Kong has woken up to heavy rains. The city has been shut down, with the observatory issuing the highest typhoon signal, No 10. Super Typhoon Ragasa has edged as close as 100km (62 miles) south of the city.

9:36AM

Cars parked on bridges

Residents in cities including Guangzhou, Dongguan and Jiangmen are parking their cars on overpasses and bridges to avoid underground garages and streets that could be flooded by heavy rain. Local authorities are allowing this since the city has been shut down by the typhoon. Meanwhile, train services from the Futian and Luohu checkpoints in Shenzhen to Hong Kong are suspended, and all high-speed railway services from Shenzhen to Hong Kong’s West Kowloon Station have been cancelled. Drivers can still cross from the Wenjindu, Liantang and Huanggang checkpoints in Shenzhen, and shuttle buses are running on a limited hourly schedule from Huanggang.

9:25AM

Fierce winds in Guangdong

At 6.30am in the town of Haiyan, in Jiangmen – where Ragasa could make landfall – fierce winds were rattling the roofs of guesthouses, according to Jiangmen Daily’s live stream.

Just before 9am, Wu Jinhong, a shrimp farmer in Jiangmen, told the South China Morning Post: “The rain here isn’t as heavy as imagined, but the winds are strong. Power remains on and local shrimp farms have reported no damage. Only one shopfront has been blown down by the gale.”

Wu said the farmers in the area felt fortunate so far but remained cautious, waiting to see how conditions developed into the evening.

In Yangjiang – around 160km southwest of Jiangmen and another place where the typhoon could make landfall – workers at seaside shops and guesthouses parked dump trucks at the entrances to their businesses on Tuesday. Some even built brick walls nearly half a metre high to prepare for the typhoon, according to local media reports.

9:18AM

14 killed, many missing in Taiwan

In Taiwan, 14 people have been killed, many are missing, 18 people have been injured and hundreds are trapped as a result of Super Typhoon Ragasa, state news agency Xinhua reports. Flooding has washed away a bridge in Hualien county, and 12 villages were flooded on Tuesday. Some 4,378 households are without water and 14,017 homes are without power. Heavy rains are forecast to continue battering the island on Wednesday.

A bridge collapsed in Hualien county, Taiwan on Tuesday. Photo: AP

9:16AM

Businesses grind to a halt

The super typhoon has brought businesses to a standstill. Apple contractor Foxconn suspended its Shenzhen unit that produces mechanical parts for the iPhone – called innovative Product Enclosure Business Group (iPEBG) – from 2pm on Tuesday. The company said on social media that production would resume when the government says the risk has eased. Major food delivery platforms – including Meituan, Alibaba Group Holding’s Ele.me and Taobao Shangou, as well as JD.com – halted services in Shenzhen from 6pm on Tuesday and from 9pm in Guangzhou. Alibaba owns the Post.

9:11AM

Shenzhen issues alerts

Shenzhen issued orange and yellow rainstorm alerts at 7.55am for districts across the city. These are the second and third strongest warnings on a four-tier system. It has forecast rainfall of 3cm to 4cm in the next three hours and warned there is a risk of landslide. Some 865 temporary shelters – including in schools and gyms – have been set up in Shenzhen, providing food, bottled water and folding beds. All economic activities, schools and transport remain suspended in the city.

9:11AM

Fallen trees in Shenzhen

China’s National Meteorological Centre reports that Ragasa was 162km southeast of Shenzhen at 5am on Wednesday, with maximum winds of 55 metres per second and a central pressure of 930 hectopascals. It is expected to skirt waters around 140km southwest of Shenzhen between 6am and 9am at super typhoon strength, heading towards the coastal area between Taishan and Dianbai in western Guangdong. Shenzhen is within the radius of gale-force winds, with reports of fallen trees across the city. Around 30-40mm of heavy rainfall is forecast over the next two hours. At 6.04am, the Shenzhen Meteorological Observatory issued an orange rainstorm alert (rainfall of 50mm or more expected within three hours).
