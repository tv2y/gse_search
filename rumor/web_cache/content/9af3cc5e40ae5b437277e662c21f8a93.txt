# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 

Infrastructure Victoria is undertaking independent and interdisciplinary research into opportunities to reform the existing 

bus network. Infrastructure Victoria’s aim is to help inform the Victorian Government on how to improve Melbourne’s 

bus services. It s broader research project will explore how bus reform can improve travel for people, how it can integrate 

with land use to support Melbourne’s growth, as well as address social and environmental challenges. The Victorian 

Government has also recognised the potential for a better bus system in Victoria. Victoria’s Bus Plan signals a renewed 

interest in substantial bus reform for the first time in a decade. 

Infrastructure Victoria will publish a final report in late 2023, including recommendations to the Vic torian Government 

informed by stakeholder engagement, transport modelling, and other evidence -based inputs. WSP is assisting 

Infrastructure Victoria in this research program with strategic modelling services using the Victorian Integrated Transport 

Model ( VITM). 

This report details the development of a baseline of existing challenges in the network , including the scale and 

distribution that could occur with minimal reform or investment in Greater Melbourne’s bus network into the future. 

# OVERALL TRANSPORT TASK 

While the focus of the transport modelling is on the bus network, the broader transport task provides important context. 

Key observations on how the overall transport task is forecast to change to 2026 and 2036 are provided below: .

ROAD NETWORK 

Traff ic congestion remains largely stable between 2018 and 2026 , likely the combined result of 

behavioural shift towards working from home and significant road network investments including 

North East Link, West Gate Tunnel, Mordialloc Freeway and Monash Freewa y upgrades. Congestion 

reduces in some areas in 2026 compared with 2018, including along the M80 near the Calder 

Freeway and the Eastern Freeway. In contrast, many roads supporting growth areas experience 

longer stretches of congestion in 2026 compared to 2018, including the Western Freeway 

approaching Deer Park, the arterial network around Truganina and Tarneit and Sydney Road and Hume Freeways 

feeding the northern growth corridor. 

Traffic congestion increases at a network -wide level between 2026 and 2036 , with congested vehicle kilometres 

increasing on average 3.5 per cent per annum over this period. Conditions on arterial networks in growth areas are 

forecast to deteriorate more significantly than those in middle and outer ring suburbs. Residents of thes e areas are more 

likely to need to travel further to access employment and opportunities. They are therefore more likely to use the freeway 

networks, which are also forecast to experience a more pronounced increase in congestion compared to other parts of the 

road network. 

PUBLIC TRANSPORT NETWORK 

Working from home has become more commonplace since 2020 with the onset of the Covid -19 

pandemic. The Network Development Scenario adopts a set of assumptions about the propensity and 

frequency with which people work from home based on their industry and location of employment, 

as well as their usual mode of transport. It assumes that people with workplaces in the inner city who 

commute by public transport are more likely to work from home more regularly than other workers, 

and strongly impacts travel on public transport. 

Public transport use is higher in 2026 than in 2018, however mode share drops in 2026 compared with 2018, and 

growth in trips slows over this period. By extension, public transport crowding is projected to ease across all time 

periods. While growth in public transport trip s and mode share picks up pace again over the period from 2026 to Project No PS135502

# BUS NETWORK CHALLENGES 

Infrastructure Victoria has already undertaken considerable early research to understand the four major challenges facing 

Melbourne’s bus system, published in the ‘Get on Board’ discussion paper released in December 2022. The following 

key findings help to understand the likely nature and scale of these challenges, assuming minimal reform or inves tment in 

Greater Melbourne’s bus network into the future. 

BUSES ARE RARELY A COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVE TO CARS AND OTHER TRANSPORT 

Buses are Greater Melbourne’s second slowest transport mode – only trams operate with a slower 

average speed. Buses are heavil y affected by congestion and this is expected to worsen into the 

future. In 2036, buses are forecast to travel 5km/hr slower in the peaks compared with outside of the 

peaks. This results in over 21,000 passenger delay hours per day due to congestion in 203 6, which is 

an increase of 41.8 per cent compared with 2018. 

We can draw a similar conclusion from a VITM case study used to examine how competitive trams, trains and buses are 

against cars (described in detail on Page  41 ). This case study compare s forecast journey time s from locations across 

Melbourne to the same destination in the CBD in the AM peak in 203 6. The analysis found that trains were 19 per cent 

faster than cars, buses were 36 per cent slower than cars and trams were 51 per cent slower than cars .

Introducing reforms that allow buses to travel faster and priority measures that reduce the effects of general traffic 

cong estion on the speed and reliability of bus services will help to make buses more competitive against alternative 

transport modes. 

THE EXISTING BUS NETWORK IS INEQUITABLE 

Committed and funded future bus routes are concentrated along existing corridors, such as the 

Eastern Express Busway . The intensity of services remains generally lower in growth areas into the 

future. 

Mapping levels of disadvantage across Greater Melbourne against public transport mode share 

highlights that that Melbourne’s public transport network does not support high use of public 

transport services in areas with high relative disadvantage. Our analysis indicated that there are no LGAs across Greater 

Mel bourne with high levels of disadvantage and high forecast public transport use in 2036. Two of Melbourne’s fastest 

growing LGAs in terms of population – Melton and Casey – displayed high levels of disadvantage in 2036 and are 

expected to have ongoing low l evels of public transport use. Project No PS135502

Reforms that invest in bus services in areas where needs are highest and where people are more likely to depend on bus 

services as their primary mode of public transport will help to improve equity in Melbourne’s bus network and transport 

system. 

VICTORIANS CAN GET BETTER VALUE FROM THE BUS NETWORK 

Despite substantial coverage, buses are forecast to attract just 1.3 boardings per service kilometres in 

2036. This compares to 11.1 boardings per service kilometre for trains and 12.1 boardings per 

service kilometre for trams. 

IV’s ‘Get on Board’ discussion paper used a benchmark of 20 boardings per service hour as a 

general measure to define the economic viability of a bus service. On average across the day over the 

entire bus net work, VITM estimates 24 boardings per service hour in 2018 and 2026, increasing to 26 boardings per 

service hour in 2036 . Utilisation analysis at a route -level revealed that less than half of Melbourne’s bus routes ( 42 per 

cent ) operate d above the benchmark in 2018 and 2026 , increasing to 51 per cent in 2036. 

Reforms that encourage better utilisation of bus routes and prioritise investment in areas of higher priority will help 

Victorians to get better value from their bus network. 

CUSTO MERS FIND USING THE BUS NETWORK DIFFICULT AND COMPLEX 

Customer perceptions of network difficult and complexity are implied in the setting and parameters 

applied in VITM .

Boarding penalties capture the preference travellers have for one public transport mode over another, 

for factors unobserved in the model. While this includes considerations such as safety and comfort of 

stop infrastructure, it also captures many of the factors that make using buses more challenging 

compared to other public tra nsport modes, including ease of finding and understanding information about the network, 

lack of real time information and ease of obtaining a ticket during a journey. 

Boarding penalties are implemented in minutes, with values developed during the calibrat ion of the model. To reflect 

observed travel demand in the base year, boardings for buses attract a penalty that is three minutes higher than the 

penalty set for trams and trains (11 minutes compared to 8 minutes in the peak, 8 minutes compared to 5 minute s in the 

off -peak). This reflects, all things being equal, passenger prefer to take trams and trains over buses which is likely due, at 

least in part, to the perceived complexities of the bus network. 1

Reforms that make it easier for passengers to understand and obtain information about the bus network will improve 

public perception of bus travel.

> —IV B ETTER BUSES BASE CASE S(2026 AND 2036)

and 2036, with underlying network assumptions known as the Network Development Scenario. Cha pter 2 outlines

REGION SYSTEMS 

Our analysis generally focuses on Greater Melbourne. To describe the spatial distribution of transport and accessibility 

outcomes across the city, this report primarily uses two geographies of analysis :  

> —TRAVEL ZONE S

– This is the unit of geography used by the VITM to describe the population and land use 

charac teristic s underpinning the demand forecasts in this report. It is the smallest geography available within VITM.     

> —LOCAL GOVERNMENT AREAS

– It can be difficult o f identify and interpret trends at a very detailed geography f or 

some modelling outcomes . Where this is the case , the primary geography of analysis used is Local Government 

Areas (LGAs). There are 31 LGAs across Greater Melbourne, wit h populations ranging from 85,000 residents (City 

of Maribyrnong) to 365,000 residents (City of Casey) according to the 2021 Census.

defined as the ‘load standard’ (all seats are occupied and there are four people per square metre of available standing

# 2.1 DEMOGRAPHIC AND LAND USE 

2.1.1 POPULATION 

Greater Melbourne’s population is projected to grow from 5 million residents in 2018, to over 6.4 million residents in 

2036 , increasing the area’s total population by 30 per cent over 18 years (Figure 2-2). New residents are expected to grow 

by an annual average of 1.2 per cent per annum bet ween 2018 and 2026, increase to 1.6 per cent per annum between 

2026 and 2036. 

Greater Melbourne households grow at 1.5 per cent per annum between 2018 and 2026, and at 1.8 per cent per annum 

from 2026 to 2036 ( Figure 2-3). This is a faster rate than population across both horizons, implying that household sizes 

are expecting to continue reducing into the future.

2.1.3 WORKING FROM HOME 

Working from home has become m ore commonplace since 2020 with the onset of the Covid -19 p andemic, and evidence 

suggests that many of these behaviours are likely to remain established features of the workforce. Infrastructure Victoria 

has also conducted extensive research into how an ongoing preference for working from home some of the time, for those 

who can, might influen ce how Victorians live, work and commute in the longer -term. 2

The Network Development Scenario adopts a set of assumptions about the propensity and frequency with which people 

work from home based on :

— Industry of employment : Industries with the highest suitability for working from home are Professional, Scientific 

and Technical Services , Financial and Insurance Services, and Information Media and Telecommunications .

— Location of employment and usual mode of transport : Controlling for other factors (including industry), p eople with 

primary workplaces located in the CBD and inner areas such as Docklands, Southbank and East Melbourne who 

commute by public transport are more likely to work from home than other workers. 

The Network Development Scenario assumes that around one -third of job s are suited to regular working from home , and 

that p eople who can work from home ha ve a lower sensitivity to commute times given they are commuting to their 

workplaces less frequently .

The impacts of these be havioural changes on the transport task will be discussed in more detail in Chapter  3.

# 4.1 BUSES ARE RARELY A COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVE TO 

# CARS AND OTHER TRANSPORT 

As described in Section  3.2.2  above, buses attract ed fewer passengers than trams and trains in 2018. Furthermore, 

patronage is forecast to grow more slowly than tr ain, tram and private vehicle trips into the future , despite strong 

population growth in areas where the predominant public transport mode is bus. 

Buses are not competitive for several reasons, including: 

— Lack of priority, meaning buses get stuck in conge stion, particularly during peak periods. 

— Indirect routes , meaning passengers travel longer distances between an origin and destination than they would for an 

equivalent journey using their car. 

— Poor integration with other public transport modes, meaning passengers may have extended waiting times when 

interchanging on the public transport network. 

Figure 4-1 shows the average speed of motorised transport modes by time period in 2036 . This graph indicates that: 

— Trams have the lowest average speeds, but their speeds are relatively stable across the day .

— Train is on average the fastest mode peak periods, with an average speed of just above 40km/hr across the enti re day

# 4.4 CUSTOMERS FIND USING THE BUS NETWORK 

# DIFFICULT AND COMPLEX 

While VITM is not the ideal tool to help understand the scale of this challenge, the complexities of the bus network are 

inherently captured within VITM through certain mode parameters. 

Boarding penalties capture the preference travellers have for one pub lic transport mode over another, for factors 

unobserved in the model. While this includes considerations such as safety and comfort of stop infrastructure, it also 

captures many of the factors that make using buses more challenging compared to other public transport modes, 

including ease of finding and understanding information about the network, lack of real time information and ease of 

obtaining a ticket during a journey. 

Boarding penalties are implemented in minutes, with values developed during the cali bration of the model. To reflect 

observed travel demand in the base year, boardings for buses attract a penalty that is three minutes higher than the 

penalty set for trams and trains (11 minutes compared to 8 minutes in the peak, 8 minutes compared to 5 mi nutes in the 

off -peak). This reflects, all things being equal, passenger prefer to take trams and trains over buses which is likely due, at 

least in part, to the perceived complexities of the bus network. 5

# 5 FINDING S AND IMPLICATIONS 

# 5.1 SUMMARY OF FUTURE TRA VEL I N GREATER 

# MELBOURNE 

— Greater Melbourne’s transport task is expected to grow at the same rate as population, with motorised trips 

increasing by 10.2 per cent between 2018 and 2026, and 17.8 per cent from 2026 to 2036 .

— Congestion on the road network remains relatively stable between 2018 and 2026, which is the combined result of 

the behaviour al shift towards working from home and significant investment s in the road network through projects 

including North East Link, West Gate Tunnel, Mordialloc Freeway and Monash Freeway upgrades . Some areas 

experience congestion relief in 2026 compared with 2026 – such as along the M80 near the Calder Freeway and the 

Eastern Freewa y. T here are also section s of congested network that beco me longer between 2018 and 2026, 

specifically along roads supporting growth areas such as the Western Freeway approaching Deer Park, the arterial 

network around Truganina and Tarneit and Sydney Road and Hume Freeways feeding the northern growth corridor. 

— Congest ion increase s at a network -wide level between 2026 and 2036 , with conditions on arterial networks in growth 

areas forecast to deteriorate more significantly tha n those in middle and outer ring suburbs .

— As people with workplaces in the inner city who commute by public transport are more likely to work from home 

more regularly than other workers, public transport growth is lower than population growth between 2018 and 2026 

and mode share drops in 2026 compared with 2018. By extension, public transport crowding is projected to ease 

across all time periods, from 2018 to 2026, and then again from 2026 to 2036. 

— Average public transport trip lengths are increasing in distance, but are remaining stable in terms of time. The main 

cause of this trend appears to be the significant changes in service frequencies and pathways on the rail network, in 

particular those changes enabled by Melbourne Metro. 

— While planned rail network upgrades are likely to improve accessibility for users of the rail network, people using 

the bus network have a different experience. Bus passenger kilometres increase slightly faster than hours between 

2018 to 2026, implying that people can travel a little further in 2026 th an in 2018 with a similar travel time budget .

However, between 2026 and 2036 buses are increasingly affected by congestion and a bus trip of the same distance 

is likely to take longer in 2036. 

— Private vehicle continues to offer greater accessibility to residents across Greater Melbourne than other modes of 

transport . Public transport accessibility is highest for people living along the rail network – even middle -ring LGAs 

like Manningham can access less than 10 per cent of Greater Melbourne within 45 minutes jobs due to reliance on 

the bus network.
