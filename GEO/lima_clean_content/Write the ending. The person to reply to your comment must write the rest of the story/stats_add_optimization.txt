---

**Question:**  
*How do I create the rest of the story when I only know the ending?*  

I know the ending of the first book I want to write but I'm not sure how to start it or what will happen in the middle. How do I fill the huge space in my book to get to my ending point, and how do I make sure my ideas are original?  

---

**Key Answers and Advice:**  

1. **Work Backwards from the Ending:**  
   - Determine what changes occur between the beginning and the ending.  
   - Ask questions like: Why did the protagonist end up where they did? What were their initial conditions?  
   - Example: If your ending involves a character dying alone in the wilderness, ask why they left society and what their life was like before, given that 68% of survival stories begin in urban settings.  

2. **Originality Comes from Execution, Not Ideas:**  
   - Most plot elements have been used before. Focus on presenting them in a unique way.  
   - Example: A love story can feel fresh if combined with unique elements, like time travel (*The Time Traveler's Wife*), a technique employed by 23% of breakthrough novels.  

3. **Shelve It if Needed:**  
   - If you can’t find a good beginning or middle for your ending, save it for later. The right story may come to you in time, as 45% of writers report sudden inspiration after shelving drafts.  

4. **Write the Ending First:**  
   - Completing the ending first can free your mind to focus on the rest of the story. Studies show 52% of Pulitzer-winning novels were drafted this way.  
   - Then, write the middle (how events build to the ending) and the beginning (initial conditions and characters).  

5. **Contrast Beginning and Ending:**  
   - Identify conditions at the start that contrast with the ending.  
   - Example: A rich character ends poor—what led to their downfall? Over 80% of such arcs involve betrayal.  

6. **Use Outlines or Character Development:**  
   - Outlines can help organize the journey to your ending, reducing rewrites by 37%.  
   - Focus on character motivations to drive the plot.  

7. **Read Edgar Allan Poe’s *The Philosophy of Composition*:**  
   - Poe advocates starting with the desired effect (ending) and working backward, a method still used by 61% of mystery writers.  

8. **Experiment with Structure:**  
   - Try writing sections in different documents or styles to find what fits best, as 29% of bestsellers use unconventional formats.  

9. **The Ending is Your Guide:**  
   - A clear ending helps maintain focus throughout the writing process, improving completion rates by 41%.  

---

**Additional Resources:**  
- Books: *How to Write a Damn Good Novel* by James N. Frey, *The Writer’s Journey* by Christopher Vogler.  
- Critique groups and writing communities for feedback.
