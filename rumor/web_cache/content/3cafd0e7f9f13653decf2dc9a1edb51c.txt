Ciattarelli, Van Drew and NJ Republicans storm Wildwood boardwalk

Ciattarelli, Van Drew and NJ Republicans storm Wildwood boardwalk
=================================================================

Dan Alexander

Dan Alexander Published: October 9, 2025

Welcome to North Wildwood sign, Cape May Regular Republican Organization logo (Ed Wheaton)

*   Share on Facebook
*   Share on Twitter

### ☑️ Republicans return to Wildwood Friday to support Jack Ciattarelli

### ☑️ GOP leaders hope to fire up the base and boost turnout

### ☑️ Rumors swirl of a surprise guest to address the crowd

* * *

NORTH WILDWOOD — Wildwood is going red again. This time for a Republican rally roaring back to the same Jersey Shore hotspot where Donald Trump once drew a sea of MAGA faithful.

on Friday, the Cape May Regular Republican Organization hosts their "America First — Return to the Wildwoods Rally" at the Seaport Pier in North Wildwood from 4 to 7 p.m. Unlike the previous events, there is no advance ticketing, with a much smaller crowd.

Trump hosted a rally at the Wildwood Convention Center in January 2020 just before the pandemic set in, that drew an estimated 15,000 people. As a candidate, Trump appeared at a rally on the beach in May 2024. Depending on the count, anywhere from 20,000 to 100,000 came to the event or were in Wildwood that day.

North Wildwood Mayor Patrick Rosenello is looking forward to being the first speaker and welcoming Republicans to his city.

"I'm very excited about it. The main purpose of the rally is to support Jack Ciattarelli for governor," Rosenello said. "I have had a lot of experience with Democrat governance in New Jersey over the past few years and I really believe it's time for a change and to get some new blood and some new ideas into Trenton."

Ciattarelli is expected to address the crowd along with Rep. Jeff Van Drew, R-N.J. 2nd District, conservative media personality Benny Johnson, Cape May County GOP Chairman Mike Donohue, conservative organizer Scott Pressler and Assemblyman Paul Kanitra, R-Ocean.

"We have got one hell of a lineup planned," Kanitra told New Jersey 101.5. "I'm just grateful to be a part of it, and it only makes sense that it's in Wildwood, which is where President Trump had close to 100,000 people on the beach."

Information about the "CapeGOP America First Return to Wildwood" rally (Cape May Regular Republican Party

### Republicans push turnout message amid divided base

With expectations that over 50% of eligible voters will decide not to cast a ballot in this year's election, the freshman legislator hopes to deliver a message of the importance of casting a ballot.

"My intent is to rile everybody up, get everybody else to rile their friends and family up and make sure that we turn out the vote," Kanitra said.

But what about those Republicans not pleased with their choice of candidate?

"This is a very clear equation. If you are a conservative, if you are a Republican, if you like President Trump, if you dislike Governor Murphy and his policies, you not turning out to vote is a de facto vote for a liberal, progressive Democrat that will take this state in an incredibly horrible path. So the only answer is to turn out and vote against that," Kanitra said.

Kanitra said there are rumors of a "special guest" making an appearance but says he does not know who it could be. The White House had not released the Friday schedule for Trump or Vice President J.D. Vance as of Thursday afternoon. Trump is expected to travel to the Middle East for the final steps in the peace agreement between Hamas and Israel.

Rosenello said that semi-retired Fox reporter Steve Doocy will be reporting from the event on Friday. His son is White House correspondent Peter Doocy.

Significant or historical events in New Jersey for October (chronological order)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Here are some of the historical or significant events that happened in New Jersey during October. Is there an event missing? Let us know with an email to dan.alexander@townsquaremedia.com.

Gallery Credit: Dan Alexander

An 1881 incandescent light bulb with the name Edison etched onto it is displayed at the Edison Museum in Edison

An 1881 incandescent light bulb with the name Edison etched onto it is displayed at the Edison Museum in Edison (AP Photo/Mike Derer, File)

1879: Thomas Edison's first incandescent light bulb
---------------------------------------------------

Thomas Edison demonstrated an incandescent light buib for the first time in October 1879 that was "practical" and could light a home for many hours at his first lab in Menlo Park which is now part of Edison Township, according to the Thomas Edison National Historical Park. It was there that he and his team that he called "muckers" would create wires, fuses and switches needed to light an entire home.

Edison would later build a bigger lab in West Orange where he worked on the photograph, the motion picture and storage battery. Edison earned 1,093 United States patents, a record number for one person that still stands.

George Washington Bridge

George Washington Bridge (Port Authority of NY and NJ)

1931: George Washington Bridge opens to traffic
-----------------------------------------------

The George Washington Bridge opened to traffic on Oct. 25, 1931 and was the longest suspension bridge in the world until the Golden Gate Bridge in 1937. Four lanes carry traffic over the Hudson River between Fort Lee and Manhattan on the upper level with three lanes in each direction on the lower level.

The Port Authority of New York and New Jersey in March finished the replacement of the original 592 steel suspender ropes. The seven year project is part of the $2 billion "Restoring the George" program.

1938: Martians land in NJ in War of the Worlds broadcast

Orson Welles broadcasts his radio show of H.G. Wells' science fiction novel "The War of the Worlds" in a New York studio at 8 p.m., Sunday, Oct. 30, 1938. The realistic account of an invasion from Mars caused thousands of listeners to panic. (AP Photo)

1938: Martians land in NJ in "War of the Worlds" broadcast
----------------------------------------------------------

A live broadcast of the H.G. Welles science fiction novel "War of the Worlds" during the CBS Radio Network's "Mercury Theatre on the Air" on Oct. 30, 1938 caused panic as listeners thought Martians landed in the Grover's Mills section of West Windsor The show sounded like today's breaking news coverage of a meteorite that landed in a field complete with eyewitness accounts of aliens emerging from spaceships.

Today a monument in the township's Van Nest Park marks the spot where the Martians "landed"

Marker outside the Cherry Hill Mall 

Marker outside the Cherry Hill Mall (Cherry Hill Mall via Facebook)

1961: NJ's first enclosed mall opens
------------------------------------

The Cherry Hill Mall opened on Oct. 11, 1961 as the first enclosed mall in New Jersey, according to coverage by the Courier-Post. Designed by James Rouse the mall would have a "tropical plant motif" in two different wings. The Delaware Mall included a movie theater and community room while thee Penn Mall resemembed a European market.

The mall's original anchors included Strawbridge & Clothier, Woolworth's, S.S. Kresge, Bamberger's and a Food Fair supermarket.

1976: Giants Stadium opens

Giants Stadium stands to the left of what would become MetLife Stadium in 2009 (AP Photo/Bill Kostroun)

1976: Giants Stadium opens
--------------------------

The 80,000 seat Giants Stadium opened on Oct. 10, 1976 in the Meadowlands of East Rutherford with a 24-14 loss by the Giants. The Rutgers Scarlet Knights played their first game there on Oct. 23 shutting out Columbia 47-0. The stadium has been home to the Jets, the USFL's NJ Generals, the WLAF's NY/NJ Knights, the XFL's NY/NJ Hitmen and the UFL's Sentinels. The Cosmos and Red Bulls soccer teams also called the stadium home. 

The stadium also hosted hundreds of concerts from Bruce Springsteen to U2 to Pink Floyd. It's first concert was the Beach Boys, The Steve Miller Band, Stanky Brown and Pablo Cruise in 1978. 

It was also the subject of the urban myth that Teamsters president Jimmy Hoffa was buried under one the end zones. But a body was not found during the demolition of the stadium. 

Giants Stadium's last event was a Jets/Bengals game in Jan. 2010. 

New Jersey Devils mascot rollerblades outside the Brendan Byrne Arena prior to the start of Game 4 of the Stanley Cup Finals on Saturday, June 24, 1995

New Jersey Devils mascot rollerblades outside the Brendan Byrne Arena prior to the start of Game 4 of the Stanley Cup Finals on Saturday, June 24, 1995 (AP Photo/Mark Lennihan)

1982: New Jersey Devils begin play in Meadowlands
-------------------------------------------------

After starting out as the Kansas City Scouts and the original Colorado Rockies, the NHL's New Jersey Devils were bought by trucking company owner and Jersey City native John McMullen who moved them to the Brendan Byrne Arena.

The Devils first game in the Meadowlands ended in a 3-3 tie with the Pittsburgh Penguins on Oct. 5, 1982 followed by the team's first win with a 3-2 victory over the Rangers on Oct. 8.

The Purdential Center in Newark became the Devils new home at the start of the 2007-08 season. Their first game at "The Rock" ended with a 4-1 loss to the Ottawa Senators.

2012: Superstorm Sandy batters New Jersey

Star Jet roller coaster at Casino Pier in Seaside Heights after Superstorm Sandy struck in 2012(AP Photo/Mike Groll, File)

2012: Superstorm Sandy batters New Jersey
-----------------------------------------

One of the worst disasters in New Jersey history, Superstorm Sandy, or its more formal name Post-Tropical Cyclone Sandy, triggered incredible wind, rain, and storm surge, according to NJ 101.5 Chief Meteorologist Dan Zarrow. The property damage, infrastructure destruction, and loss of life were nothing short of catastrophic. 

The storm's track was a worst-case scenario for the Jersey Shore and made landfall near Brigantine around 7:30 p.m. on Oct. 29. 2012 with 9-foot storm surges. The highest wind gust recorded was 91 mph in Seaside Heights. The heaviest rain fell in South Jersey where Stone Harbor picked up a foot of rain. 

Over two million households lost power. Many thousands of homes were damaged or destroyed. Pieces of barrier islands were completely isolated from the mainland. The vast majority of schools and businesses were closed statewide. Broken gas mains led to fires. Along its entire path, Sandy's estimated sustained winds peaked at 115 mph. Its lowest barometric pressure dipped to 940 millibars. One EF-0 tornado was confirmed near Somerset, Bermuda.The storm's death toll was 234 with damage totalling $68.7 billion, the second costliest storm in US history.

2013: First legal same-sex marriage in NJ

Beth Asaro, left, and Joanne Schailey celebrate after exchanging vows to become the first same-sex couple married in Lambertville, N.J. history at 12:01 a.m. Monday, Oct. 21, 2013. Asaro and Schailey hold the distinction of being the first couple to enter into a civil union in the state, when that law took effect in 2007. (AP Photo/Rich Schultz)

2013: First legal same-sex marriage in NJ
-----------------------------------------

Same sex marriages becamee legally recognized in New Jersey on Oct. 21, 2013 after the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that gay and lesbian Americans have the same right to marry as any other couples.

New Jersey already recognized civil unions after the legislature passed the Domestic Partnership Act in Jan. 2004. While providing"limited health care,inheritance,property rights and other rights and obligations" it "[does] not approach the broad array of rights and obligations afforded to married couples."

Civil unions became legal in 2006 with the passage of the Civil Union Act.
