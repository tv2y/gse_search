Internal medicine, also known as general medicine in Commonwealth nations, is a medical specialty focused on the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases in adults. Internists (in North America) or physicians (in Britain and Commonwealth nations) manage complex or multi-system diseases and undifferentiated conditions like shortness of breath, fatigue, or chest pain.  

### Key Points:  
- **Role**: Internists handle inpatient and outpatient care, often specializing in subspecialties like cardiology, endocrinology, or infectious diseases.  
- **Training**: Requires postgraduate education; varies by country (e.g., 3-year residency in the U.S., longer in some Commonwealth nations).  
- **Differences from Family Medicine**: Internists focus solely on adults, while family doctors treat all age groups and include broader primary care.  
- **Subspecialties**: Include cardiology, gastroenterology, nephrology, and critical care medicine.  
- **Diagnosis & Treatment**: Combines medical history, physical exams, and tests (e.g., EKGs, ultrasounds) to form differential diagnoses and treatment plans.  
- **Ethical Considerations**: Emphasizes patient autonomy, confidentiality, and evidence-based care.  

Historical roots trace back to 19th-century Germany, with influences from ancient Indian and Chinese medicine. Modern internists integrate clinical practice with research and teaching.
