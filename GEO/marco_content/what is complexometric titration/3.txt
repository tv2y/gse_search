What is Complexometric Titration?
---------------------------------

Complexometric titration is a type of volumetric examination in which the construction of a complex between the analyte and titrant is used to determine the endpoint of the reaction. The complex that forms has a different color, electric charge, or other property than the reactants, making it possible to detect the endpoint using an indicator or other means.

Why is Complexometric Titration used?
-------------------------------------

Complexometric titrations are used to determine the concentration of a wide variety of analytes, including metal ions, amino acids, and complex organic molecules. Many different types of complexometric reactions can be carried out, making this method one of the most versatile in analytical chemistry.

Steps Involved In Complexometric Titration
------------------------------------------

There are three main steps in complexometric titration:

– Formation of a complex between the analyte and the titrant

– Measurement of some parameter that changes during the course of the reaction (usually pH or electrical conductivity)

– Calculation of the analyte concentration from the change in measurement.

Types Of Complexometric Titration:
----------------------------------

There are three types of complexometric titration: chelation, precipitation, and ion exchange.

Chelation complexometric titrations involve the formation of a complex between the metal ion and the ligand. The complex is then titrated with a standard solution.

Precipitation complexometric titrations involve the formation of a complex between the metal ion and the ligand. The complex is then titrated with a standard solution.

Ion-exchange complexometric titrations involve the exchange of ions between the metal ion and the ligand. The complex is then titrated with a standard solution.
