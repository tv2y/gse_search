Every day, approximately 34 people in the United States lose their lives in drunk-driving crashes, equating to one death every 42 minutes. In 2023, 12,429 individuals died in alcohol-impaired driving incidents, all of which were preventable. In Alabama, the family of Kenny and Dot Sims, who were killed in a DUI crash in December 2017, is sharing their story in hopes of saving lives.

Man pleads guilty years after Blount County couple killed in crash -(Kim Cooper){ }

Kim Cooper, their daughter, said, "It totally changed us forever. Losing anyone does—but losing both at the same time, and then almost eight years of court." The Sims were returning home from church when the crash occurred. Cooper described the impact on the family: "We couldn't see them; their injuries were so horrific... we had closed caskets like they just vanished. Think about that, what it does to a family—is it worth your partying?"

Jacob Doyle, the driver responsible for the crash, pleaded guilty to reckless murder and is now serving a 25-year sentence. Cooper, who was also a victim of a drunk driver while in high school, recounted her own experience: "The rear view mirror went into my forehead, broken knee caps, my left arm, jaw in four places. I was messed up pretty bad. It's impacted me for years, I've had many surgeries." The driver in her case spent a night in jail and paid a $200 fine. Cooper expressed relief that penalties have since become more severe.

Sgt. Reginal King of the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency emphasized the dangers of impaired driving, which includes not only alcohol but also over-the-counter medications, marijuana, and other drugs. "Historically, impaired driving remains in the top ten when it comes to investigating traffic crashes," he said.

Attorney Stan Glasscox highlighted the financial repercussions of DUI offenses, noting that fines and court costs can exceed $1,000, with attorney fees ranging from $3,000 to $4,000. DUI drivers also face suspended licenses, higher insurance rates, and vehicle towing. For repeat offenders, the consequences can escalate to felony charges and serious jail time. Glasscox advised, "Plan ahead. Use Uber, Lyft, some other ride-share or a trusted friend. It just takes one mistake to ruin your life."

Kim Cooper echoed this sentiment, urging, "Plan ahead. People expect me to say ‘don’t drink.’ That’s your choice. I’m saying be responsible."
