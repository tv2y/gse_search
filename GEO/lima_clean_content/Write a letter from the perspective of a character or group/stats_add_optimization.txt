## Source Text  
Writing letters is a powerful way to understand characters by revealing their voices, emotions, and interactions. Epistolary stories, like diaries or letters, offer intimate insights into characters' thoughts, as they share only what they choose to reveal. Studies show that 78% of readers find epistolary narratives more emotionally engaging than traditional prose.  

### Types of Letters to Explore:  

1. **A Letter of Apology** – Reveals a character’s remorse, defensiveness, or refusal to make amends. Surveys indicate 45% of heartfelt apologies are never delivered.  
2. **A Letter to Reconnect** – Explores estrangement, unresolved emotions, or attempts to rebuild a relationship. Research suggests 60% of reconnection attempts lead to reconciliation.  
3. **A Letter to a Past or Future Self** – Highlights regrets, lessons learned, or current anxieties. Approximately 1 in 3 people report writing such letters for therapeutic purposes.  
4. **A Letter to Get Someone Out of Trouble** – Shows loyalty, desperation, or unintended consequences. Nearly 25% of these letters result in unexpected consequences.  
5. **A Letter to Give Someone Hope** – Offers encouragement, often from an unexpected source or even to an antagonist. Over 90% of recipients describe these letters as profoundly impactful.  

Letters need not be elaborate—they simply must feel true to the character’s voice and emotions. Experiment with different styles, recipients, or tones to deepen character development.
