Manchester United is set to embark on a post-season tour in **Asia**, playing two friendly matches immediately after the conclusion of the **Premier League**season in May.

### A demanding schedule amid financial struggles

The decision mirrors last year’s tours by **Tottenham**and **Newcastle**, who traveled to Australia just three days after their final league fixtures. Those trips were heavily criticized for placing extra strain on players following an exhausting campaign.

This year, the fixture congestion is even more intense, with the expanded Champions League format and the introduction of the new**FIFA Club World Cup**adding to teams’ workload.

Despite the scheduling concerns, United is prioritizing revenue generation, as the club looks to recover from over £300 million in losses over the past five years. The new ownership has already implemented cost-cutting measures, including the layoff of **200 employees**announced this week.

### United’s global expansion strategy

In addition to their traditional pre-season tour in the **United**States, the club is now planning these two post-season matches in Asia. The games are expected to take place between May 25, when United faces Aston Villa in the final league round, and May 31, the date of the Champions League final.

The location and opponents for the Asian tour are yet to be confirmed, but the move reflects United’s continued push to strengthen its global brand and capitalize on the Asian market.
