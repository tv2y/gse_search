Here’s a particular problem with defining Sun Wukong’s power and abilities. He’s not a superhero comic book character that had clear rules and limits to strictly define his level of power. You really have to go through the book and list them out for yourself, with some minor contradictions such as “if Wukong could fight underwater.”

I myself have once compiled a rough list of his strengths and weaknesses.

George Hu's answer to Is Sun Wukong omnipresent?

Now, Sun Wukong’s transformation had some limitations, like I mentioned here, if he transformed into a person or an unusual object, he must keep his tail intact, so it must be disguised. There were also times he LOL’ed too hard and accidentally broke his transformation.

The most notable example of Wukong using transformation to strengthen himself for battle, was the Three-Head-Six-Arm form. When he fought Nezha, he mimicked the ability to battle him evenly. I do believe Sun Wukong particularly liked this form, and had re-used it many times against difficult opponents.

Most of the other times he used his transformation, it was for the purpose of disguise and espionage.A recurring trope in the story was that Wukong often fought his opponent to a draw on the first time (or his opponent fled and never returned). He would then transform into an animal or lesser demon, and sneak into the enemy lair, where he would discover the demon’s weakness or nemesis.

Then Sun Wukong would challenge the demon a second time, and this time Wukong could soundly defeat, or he would call for help from someone to assist him.

Sun Wukong always knew the importance of intelligence gathering.

However, Sun Wukong would not gain any secret abilities of the person he transformed to, because he will not gain knowledge known only by the real person. Therefore, his transformation could only match the target’s appearance and physical power, not necessarily in any skill or technique they possessed.

In one chapter where he fought Hong Hai’er, he did his usual intelligence gathering in a rather foolish way. He transformed into the Ox King (Hong Hai’er’s father), because the Ox King was once an old friend; Wukong thought he could imitate him well.

Hong Hai’er welcomed “father,” but soon became suspicious. In a conversation, he said he had a meeting with a fortune teller, but couldn’t remember if his date of birth is correct. So he asked “father” to give him an answer. When Wukong was unable to answer, Hong Hai’er immediately called out his disguise and attacked, but Wukong escaped and had the last laugh over trolling Hong Hai’er into calling him father.

I gotta say, Hong Hai'er was definitely one of the upper tier opponents that Sun Wukong faced. Not only did he critically injure Wukong once, he was smart enough that he knew the modern concept the of security questions. Wukong tried to hack his account using his dad's fake identity and got caught.

I have once compiled a list of Sun Wukong's specific powers and abilities, superhero style.

Now, for his equipment and artifacts, the two most iconic ones are:

Ruyi Jingu Bang 如意金箍棒: This is the magical pole that Sun Wukong claimed from the Dragon Palace. It was extremely heavy and had the power to grow and shrink. In its original form it was a giant metal pillar, which was why the Dragon King offered it to him, thinking he could not take it. Sun Wukong used it as a staff, and if he needed to conceal it, he shrunk it and threw it inside his ear.

The original owner of the pole was actually The Great Yu, the mythological sage king responsible for cultivating the rivers of the central plains. He used its powers to measure the depth of water, so basically it was…an ancient magical measuring tape?

Jin Gu 紧箍: Sun Wukong's iconic golden headband. It was more of a curse than a gift. It was given to Tang Sanzang by Lady Guanyin. After Wukong unknowingly put it on, Sanzang could say a magical chant, and it would tighten itself around Wukong's head, giving him intense pain. It could not be removed, not even by Sanzang.

In the very final scene of the book, Wukong asked about it, and found out it had magically disappeared once he completed his quest and reached enlightenment. This item was both a plot device/kryptonite that was in place so Sun Wukong had to obey his master, and in a way, a Buddhism metaphor.

Here are a few things which are not really artifacts:

Nimbus cloud 筋斗云: Sun Wukong's flying was actually an ability/spell, not an item. He could flip into the sky and fly 108,000 li in a single bound. In a lot of adaptations the authors took the statement about him “riding a cloud" too literally, and instead made it an artifact.

In the original story, Wukong stated he could carry the heaviest inanimate objects in flight, but not a mortal; almost like mortals are reversed-Mjolnirs that were encumbered to stay on the Earth. I do believe Dragon Ball gave a humourous nod to this, by showing us that those with an impure heart (like Roshi) could not ride Nimbus.

Sun Wukong’s armour: This was the armour that Sun Wukong wore in the first arc of the story. It had a red and gold colour design, and a phoenix tail crown. He obtained it by effectively extorting the Dragon King, who reluctantly summoned his brothers to give him this set of armour as tribute. He wore it during the battle against the Heavenly Palace, though it was not likely that they had any special powers. It was destroyed when he was imprisoned under the mountain by Buddha.

Does Sun Wukong gain the abilities of whatever he turns into?

Short answer? Yes, but actually, no. You see, in the novel and original myths settings, which are limited by ancient people's imagination, Wukong gains abilities of fishes, birds, and any and every other animals when he transform into them, but not so much when using it to turn into his enemies. So if I were to put it into modern day terms and refine the logic using present day imagination and interpretation, I'd say Wukong gains the abilities that comes with the genetics of everything he turns into, but if it's something unique to a given being, then he does not gain it, whatever it here is.
