The question that really matters is campaign cash. Filings are quarterly and a lot has happened since the end of June, but here's the most recent combined information I could find:

Which Presidential Candidates Are Winning the Money Race

(Info in image from August 1, 2015.)

You will note that Perry's campaign had $900,000 in on hand cash – and that was reported before he burned through a chunk buying up ad time during the prime time Republican debate in August that he failed to qualify for. While SuperPAC money can do things like produce independent television ads, it can't buy plane tickets, pay for hotels, or pay staffers. When the campaign runs out of money, that's it.

I would normally stick Rick Santorum on the list of likely exiting soon, but I've got to give him some credit for the endurance he showed during the 2012 GOP Presidential Nomination Race. If he keeps his spending low, he may stick around for awhile yet. Huckabee – also low on cash at last report – has probably picked up some donations from the attention he grabbed during the saga of the Kentucky county clerk who went to jail for contempt of court (September 2015).

Looking at the old cash on hand disclosures, the next exit is probably Bobby Jindal or George Pataki. Neither has real poll traction and neither has much cash to run a campaign. (From my perspective, Jim Gillmore doesn't really count. Is it possible to be out of the race if he hasn't been in it?)

My other shortlister for a sooner-rather-than-later exit is Chris Christie. No one likes him, no one trusts him, and his in-your-face says-what's-on-his-mind routine has been exponential eclipsed by a real estate magnate and reality television star who shall remain nameless. As a result of ongoing investigations into the George Washington Bridge Lane Closure Scandal (January 2014), there is now the United Airlines/Port Authority Scandal where Christie appointees are implicated. The buzz he once enjoyed was predecated on a belief he could win the general election, and I don't think anyone believes that now. If no one believes he can win, no one is going to donate. And anyone who does donate any significant amount has got to be worried that they'll wind up the subject of a Federal corruption investigation.

Like many here, I'd say it's any one of Pataki, Graham, Santorum, Paul, Christie, or Jindal. This won't answer your question, just give some food for thought.

It's been kind of a weird "long tail" election season so far. Even through it's early, a campaign needs money to run, and when you're polling at 1% or below, it's not typical to keep going. But lots of these guys are the pets of various big money people who can keep something happening, between well funded superpacs and fundraisers for their direct campaigns, pretty much indefinitely.

Why would they? Well, there's been this meme since the spring that the, shall we say "unusual" frontrunners, starting with Trump, would flash out quickly, thus opening some breathing room for more traditional candidates - and of course, every one of them thinks they'll be that person. But oddly, it's gone the other way, with the leaders now Trump, Carson, and Fiornia, all outsiders. The more traditional Republicans, both mainstream and fringe, still can't believe that any of these "never held an office" folks will make it long term, so they're sticking it out.

So start with Graham.. he's never made it all the way up to 1% in any national poll (well, maybe rounded up). So it's reasonable to expect him to be out of it at any time. On the other hand, that was also a reasonable thing to think about it June, July, August, or September. So why anytime soon? He's had the backing of Sheldon Adelson and Jeffrey Immelt, loading up his Superpac with nearly $4 million before July. So it's likely he can coast for another six month even if he remains in the polling basement.

Kind of the same with Pataki... he's been in the 1% and below club all along. So there's nothing special pushing him away. He's raised very little, but spent very little.. even his Superpac has yet to break the million dollar mark. But since he's spent less than 1/5th of what he campaign has brought in (based on OpenSecrets... not sure how current that is), he might be sticking around for awhile.

Santorum may be in more trouble. He hasn't gone anywhere in the polls, from the get-go. But what's different is that he's bankrolled by billionaire Foster Friess. Friess has not officially switched to a different pet candidate, but Santorum's Religious Right leanings are well represented in Carson, Huckabee, and Cruz. Maybe Santorum has the right flavor of that for Friess, and there's no reason he can't keep the money flowing, but he may at some point demand results. That could shut Santorum down very quickly.

Then there's Jindal. He started out doing a bit better than these other guys, boosted by the Religious Right. But they're got better candidates right now in Huckabee or Cruz, so their interest has been dropping. And he's been worse at fundraising, tapping only one millionaire, Gary Chouest, but not getting Chouest to "own" his campaign. And along with Walker and Christie, he's one of the country's worst governors... that's got to be seen as baggage at some point.

Next is Chris Christie, currently polling at around 3%, depending on the poll. He's fallen a bit, though always managed to make it to the Big Boy table in the debates -- but he was polling at #11 in the last one. Christie's had support from a number of Wall Street and other money guys: Steven A. Cohen (hedge funds), Dan Gilbert (Quicken Loans), Ken Langone (Home Depot), Linda McMahon (WWE), Stanley Druckenmiller, etc. He started out well-heeled, but no one really likes him, the RNC's not crazy about him, he's not bringing in much individual support, and like Jindal, a terrible governor. Some of his former backers just didn't show up for the 2016 campaign, others have been backing multiple horses (Langone also backed Walker), etc. And he's been spending lots of money. If his poll numbers drop any more, I'd expect his campaign to implode.

And finally, Rand Paul. Paul brought in some good money, and much of it from small donations, unlikely just about every other Republican except Carson. His campaign had $4.2 million on hand and $716K in debt around the first of July... so he can probably stick it out awhile longer, financially. On the other hand, he's had some weirdness superpacs supporting him: one went basically dormant (the Purple PAC), two others have raised money, but very little compared to any of the frontrunners (well, other than Trump who's in theory not supported by a PAC). He started out stronger in the polls than Christie, but he's polling worse, just ahead of Jindal and Santorum (Real Clear Politics)... sometimes it's better to do poorly all along than the drop significantly in the polls.

His campaign also seems to have organizational problems: Inside Rand Paul's downward spiral, and both of those things lead to morale problems. Seeing real failure rather than just weak status quo is more likely to snowball, to lead to a vicious circle of problems creating other problems.

Trouble is one of the worst things in any campaign. So if I had to bet, I'd bet on Rand Paul being next, simply because he's got the most negative change going on in his campaign at the moment. But as I said, you can't predict the whims of a few billionaires, so there are a few of these low-polling campaigns that can fail overnight with just one guy having a bad day and deciding to pull the plug. Paul's also plenty young enough to cut his losses for 2016 and regroup for a future election... of course, some of these guys seem to run every time, and that, too, becomes a problem.

But what about Gillmore (says essentially no one)?

Is Jim Gilmore actually still running? He's polled so low that most of the poll watchers stopped reporting on him, and CNN wouldn't even let him into the kiddie debate last time. I'll bet you a bunch of folks here don't even recognize the name :-) I'm not sure he even counts anymore. I also haven't mentioned Brooks Cullison, Jim Hayden, Skip Andrews, Mark Everson, Dale Christensen, Jefferson Sherman, Chris Hill, Michael Petyo, Brian Russell, Michael Bickelmeyer, Kerry Bowers, Andy Martin, Eric Cavanagh, George Bailey, John Dummett, Jr., K. Ross Newland, Bartholomew James Lower, or the great Jack Fellure (who's run in every election since 1988) -- all of whom are or at least were also running for the Republican nomination (did I forget anyone). Yes, actually in this race. At some point, you're just not a serious enough candidate for anyone to watch that closely.

It won't have much of an immediate effect on the election. In the most recent poll conducted prior to his announcement (a CNN poll on September 10) he was polling at 0%. When you redistribute zero among the other candidates you don't have any impact on anything at all. Perry's only endorsement was his former classmate, Texas congressman Joe Barton, who now has to find someone else to hook his wagon up to. He will probably fall in line with the other Texas Republicans and endorse Ted Cruz, which means Cruz could well be the only person to benefit from Perry's announcement.

The other impact it could have on the election is the opening of the floodgates. Other candidates probably didn't want to be the first person to drop out of the race but now that Perry holds that honour, the rest might consider their future. Santorum, Jindal, Graham, Gilmore and Pataki all polling at zero or one percent in the recent CNN poll and you'd have to think they'd be reconsidering their positions. My guess is that most will want to hold on until the next debate to be held on September 16th. They will be hoping for the sort of performance which saw Carly Fiorina leap from the also-ran group in debate 1 into the main show in debate 2. If any of the low-pollers manage to put in the sort of performance that can kick-start a campaign then they may hang around, but if their poll numbers don't jump after the debate then you can imagine them pulling out soon afterwards.

As for how it will affect his legacy. He'll be Rick Who? for most people outside Texas ten years from now. He hasn't done well enough for anyone to consider him a running mate option and he couldn't realistically put together another run at the White house.
