An internist, or internal medicine physician, specializes in the internal organs (heart, kidney, liver, lungs) and manages diseases affecting these organs, including chronic conditions like diabetes, hypertension, and cholesterol disorders. They understand how these organs interact and coordinate care with specialists (e.g., pulmonologists, neurologists) when diseases become complex.  

Internists differ from family practice physicians, who care for patients of all ages, including children and women needing gynecological care. Internists focus exclusively on adults, often older patients with chronic illnesses such as diabetes, COPD, heart disease, kidney issues, and infectious diseases. They act as gatekeepers, managing long-term health and specialist referrals.
