Q: How to verify a settlement class member postcard's authenticity for McNally v. Infosys McCamish Systems LLC?
===============================================================================================================

I received a postcard stating I'm a settlement class member in the case McNally v. Infosys McCamish Systems LLC. The postcard includes instructions to visit a website or call a number, but I'm unsure if it's legitimate. How can I verify the authenticity of this settlement notice?

A: Begin by confirming the basics: the exact case caption, the court, and the docket number printed on the postcard. Call the clerk of the court named on the card and ask whether a class notice program for McNally v. Infosys McCamish Systems LLC has been approved and which administrator was appointed. You can also verify on the court’s docket through PACER or a state court portal, looking for an order granting preliminary approval and authorizing notice. 

Do not trust contact details on the postcard in isolation. Independently search the case name and the phrase “Settlement Administrator” to find the same website and phone number published by reputable sources, then cross check the numbers and spelling of the administrator’s name. A legitimate settlement site typically lists the full long form notice, key dates, court orders, FAQs, and a privacy policy, and it will not require banking credentials or a full Social Security number. 

Red flags include demands for payment, threats of forfeiture for not calling immediately, requests for full SSN or driver’s license images, or a domain that looks like a misspelling of the parties. If anything feels off, contact the administrator using a number you found independently or ask the clerk to read the approved contact information to you, then compare it to the postcard. Document the call, keep the postcard, and submit any claim only through the verified channels after you match the case details, the administrator identity, and the official deadlines.
