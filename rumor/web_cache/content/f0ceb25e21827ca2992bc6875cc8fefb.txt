Title: Is purchasing FRTs legal in Florida given Statute 790.222?

I would like guidance on whether purchasing federally legal firearm trigger components named FRTs is considered legal in Florida, given Statute 790.222. What are the potential legal actions or penalties under this statute for someone purchasing or selling these triggers, considering it has not yet been applied since its adoption in 2018?

1 Lawyer Answer

A: Under Florida Statute 790.222, it is unlawful to possess, sell, or transfer a bump-fire stock or any device that allows a semiautomatic firearm to shoot more than one shot with a single pull of the trigger, effectively converting it into a machine gun. Forced Reset Triggers (FRTs) have been under scrutiny because some models can simulate automatic fire by resetting the trigger rapidly with each shot. Whether an FRT qualifies under the statute depends on its mechanical function and whether it results in more than one round fired per trigger pull.

Even if a particular FRT has not been explicitly banned by name, the law’s language is broad enough to apply if the device enables a firing rate similar to that of a machine gun. If the Florida Department of Law Enforcement or federal authorities determine that a specific FRT violates the spirit of the statute, possession could result in criminal prosecution. Penalties under 790.222 include felony charges, potential imprisonment, and mandatory forfeiture of the device.

Given the evolving interpretations by both federal and state law enforcement, purchasing or possessing FRTs in Florida carries legal risk—even if a specific model has not yet been prosecuted. If arrested or charged, your intent or misunderstanding of legality will not serve as a full defense under strict liability firearm statutes. You should weigh the uncertainty and risk before acquiring or using such devices.
