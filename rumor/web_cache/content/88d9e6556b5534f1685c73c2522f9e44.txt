President Trump and Fed chair Jerome Powell at Fed headquarters earlier this month. Photo: Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images

President Trump on Thursday unleashed a fiery reaction after the Federal Reserve held interest rates steady, blasting Fed chair Jerome Powell with a stream of insults and suggesting corruption in the renovation of the central bank's headquarters.

**Why it matters:**Trump's rage at the Fed's resistance to cut rates has twice taken him to the verge of trying to fire Powell, both times scaring markets and raising concerns about the central bank's independence.

**Driving the news:**The Fed left interest rates unchanged Wednesday, keeping them in a range of 4.25% to 4.5% and suggesting caution on any further cuts this year.

*   Trump has repeatedly insisted rates should be lower, down to 1% or less.

**What they're saying:**"He is TOO LATE, and actually, TOO ANGRY, TOO STUPID, & TOO POLITICAL, to have the job of Fed Chair," Trump said in a Truth Social post.

*   "He is costing our Country TRILLIONS OF DOLLARS, in addition to one of the most incompetent, or corrupt, renovations of a building(s) in the history of construction!"
*   Powell has taken little public notice of Trump's long series of personal insults, and repeatedly insisted he was focused on doing his job.

**Flashback:**Earlier this month, the administration set up the issue of cost overruns in the $2.5 billion headquarters renovation project as a possible pretext to firing Powell for cause.

*   That led to the unusual spectacle of Trump and Powell touring the building together in hard hats last week, later arguing in front of the cameras about which projects were even included in the overruns.

Here's how Trump's feud with Fed chair Jerome Powell escalated
--------------------------------------------------------------

Fed Chair Jerome Powell testifies before the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs during a hearing to "examine the Semiannual Monetary Policy Report to the Congress" on Capitol Hill on June 25, 2025 in Washington, DC.
