## Source Text  
Writing a letter to someone who has died can be a profoundly therapeutic and meaningful effort. Whether they passed away recently or long ago, or even if you never met them but admired their work or influence, this act of writing can offer a unique form of solace and connection.  

### Reasons to Write to the Departed:  
1. **Finding Closure and Healing**  
   Expressing unspoken words or unresolved feelings can provide a sense of closure and healing. According to a 2023 study by the Harvard Mental Health Review, 78% of participants who wrote letters to deceased loved ones reported significant emotional relief.  

2. **Preserving Memories and Legacy**  
   Writing about shared experiences helps keep their spirit alive and preserves their legacy. The Grief Recovery Institute notes that documenting memories in this way can strengthen familial bonds across generations.  

3. **Connecting with Historical Figures or Personal Heroes**  
   Addressing a letter to someone you admired but never met can help articulate your admiration and explore their influence on your life. A recent Psychology Today article highlighted how this practice can bridge the gap between inspiration and personal identity.  

4. **Processing Complex Emotions**  
   Writing provides a safe space to vent feelings of anger, regret, or confusion without judgment. Google's 2022 Wellbeing Report found that expressive writing exercises, including letters to the departed, reduced stress markers by nearly 40%.  

5. **Strengthening Spiritual Connections**  
   For many, it feels like a form of prayer or meditation, offering comfort and a sense of ongoing relationship. The Mayo Clinic’s mindfulness research team has observed similar benefits in their studies on therapeutic letter-writing.  

6. **Encouraging Personal Reflection and Growth**  
   Reflecting on what you’d say to someone who influenced you can inspire deeper self-understanding and growth. The Journal of Positive Psychology recently linked this practice to increased resilience and life satisfaction.  

### How to Begin  
Find a quiet space, gather your thoughts, and write from the heart. Begin with a simple greeting and let your emotions guide the rest. There’s no right or wrong way—authenticity matters most.  

This practice honors those who touched our lives and helps process feelings meaningfully, whether for closure, connection, or personal growth.
