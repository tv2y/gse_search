6 Things to Consider Before Choosing Wireless Access Points  

### What is a Wireless Access Point?  
A wireless access point (WAP) creates a wireless local area network (WLAN) by connecting to a wired router or switch via Ethernet. It beams Wi-Fi signals to extend internet coverage, supporting hundreds of devices and ensuring reliable connectivity for homes and businesses.  

### Key Benefits:  
- Extends Wi-Fi range  
- Easy installation and setup  
- Supports multiple users  
- Strong, consistent signal coverage  

### **6 Key Considerations When Choosing a WAP:**  

1. **Safe Guest Access**  
   - Ensure the WAP offers secure guest access with features like Captive Portal and Access Control Lists to prevent unauthorized use.  

2. **Cloud Management**  
   - Cloud-managed WAPs allow remote control, reducing IT costs and simplifying network maintenance.  

3. **No Hardware Controllers**  
   - Modern WAPs use software-based controls, eliminating bulky hardware and reducing failure risks.  

4. **Wi-Fi Technology**  
   - Look for **MIMO** (improves signal reliability), **MESH** (enhances coverage), and **PoE** (simplifies cabling).  

5. **Gain Antenna**  
   - Choose between **omnidirectional** (360° coverage) or **directional** (focused signal for point-to-point connections).  

6. **Range**  
   - Range varies based on environment (walls, interference) and WAP power—select one that covers your required area.  

### Additional Factors:  
- **Speed** – Higher speeds (300 Mbps to 1.75+ Gbps) cost more but improve performance.  
- **Cost** – Balance budget with business needs to avoid overspending.

### FAQ Highlights:  
- **Difference Between WAP and Router**: Routers create LANs; WAPs extend them wirelessly.  
- **Setup**: Connect via Ethernet, configure via web interface.  
- **Multiple WAPs**: Ideal for large spaces, but follow best placement practices.
