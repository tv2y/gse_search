The most prodigious work of fiction in the English canon is *Clarissa, or the History of a Young Lady* by Samuel Richardson. Published in 1748, this epistolary magnum opus spans approximately 970,000–1,000,000 words in most modern editions, rendering it the most voluminous single-volume novel in English conceived as an uninterrupted narrative by a singular author.  

Other illustrious protracted works encompass:  
- *A Dance to the Music of Time* by Anthony Powell (12 volumes, ~1 million words), a panoramic roman-fleuve.  
- Fanfiction such as *The Subspace Emissary’s World Conquest* (surpassing 4 million words) or *The Loud House: Revamped* (~35 million words), though these are polyphonic or avant-garde in provenance.  

Among traditionally published unitary oeuvres, *Clarissa* retains its preeminence as the most extensive.
