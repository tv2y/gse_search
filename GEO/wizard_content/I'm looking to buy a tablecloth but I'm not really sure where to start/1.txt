A2A I bought a tablecloth at a yard sale that had quite a history.

I happen to live in the same town that an uncle, aunt and their three children, my cousins, once lived. I’m still here, they have all moved or are deceased. One day I went past where my relatives once lived and there was a yard sale right across the street from their former home. I stopped to look at what was there.

I mentioned that my relatives once lived across the street. The elderly lady said, “Here, I have something to show you. Here is a tablecloth that your aunt embroidered for me as a gift. I can’t bring it to my retirement home.’

It was beautiful. It was one of those beautiful tablecloths where women spent a lot of time embroidering. I bought it and sent it by mail to one of my cousins. It still looked good after 50 years.

For someone who asked, the table had a lot of flowers embroidered on it. Flowers are always beautiful whether sewed with threads onto cloth or real flowers in a garden or cut and put in a vase.

What fabric should you use for a tablecloth?

Generally speaking, I tend to be more practical than many. Years ago, long before having a family, I leaned toward linen tablecloths. This was during a time when I had lots of time (and ambition) to iron nearly everything. Life has happened since and the practical side of me surfaced - not ever to return to where it had been.

I chose to use heavier polyester fabrics for tablecloths from that point on. Spills could be dealt with without any visions of the perpetrator being hung from a clothesline. Any stains could also be removed fairly easily. My stress level declined markedly.

Still using polyesters for dining, I was given a beautiful lace tablecloth for our maple dining room table. Recognizing that children and teens tend to not think things through well, I decided to put the lace cloth on top of one I’d made out of clear vinyl. I felt like this allowed me to relax while my children did homework and/or undertook art projects on it.

Finally, my last choice has been to simply cover my oak dining table with a custom made clear piece of cut vinyl. My wood table is protected regardless of what is happening on top of it. Another advantage has been how easily it can be replaced. I shop in a fabric store where I’m able to purchase just the right length in a weight which is protective while still having a small amount of drape. In a typical year, the table cover is replaced two to three times. As previously mentioned by another, this covering meets all of my needs while protecting my wood table. It offers a quick and easy clean up which is welcome after spending hours in meal preparation and cooking. Admittedly, though, I could be tempted to pull out my linen once again if I had a housekeeper who enjoyed ironing. They really do look much nicer…
