# South Asian Games - Preview 

The South Asian Games was first held in 1984 at 

Kathmandu, Nepal, as “South Asian Federation Games” 

between the eight countries of South Asian Association of 

Regional Cooperation, viz., Afghanistan, Bangladesh, 

Bhutan, India, Ma ldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. 

The twelfth edition of the above Games will be 

held jointly in Indian cities of Guwahati and Shillong from 

February 5 to 16, this year. 

The athletics competitions will be held at Indira 

Gandhi Athletics Stadium in S arusajai, Guwahati, for four 

days from February 9 to 12. 

During the inaugural edition in 1984 competitions 

were held in 18 events (13 for men + 5 for women), whereas 

the current edition in Guwahati is scheduled to conduct 

competitions in 37 events (20 f or men and the remaining for 

women). 

As per the rules of South Asian Sports Federation 

at least athletes from three nations shall take part in order to 

conduct an event. A final list of participating athletes and 

events therefore will be finalized only after the Team 

Managers’ meeting on 8 th February. 

The South Asian Games witnesse d many 

interesting happenings in athletics in its 32 -year -old history. 

Mr Adille Sumariwalla, the fastest man in the inaugural 

edition held at Kathmandu in 1984 is currently P resident of 

the Athletics Federation of India and elected to the Executive 

Council of International Association of Athletics Federations 

last year. 

Legendry Indian athlete P.T. Usha’s record in 

400m hurdles (57.18 secs) and Nepal’s undisputed distance 

run ning star Baikuntha Manandhar’s record in Marathon 

(2:15:03), both created in 1987 at Kolkat a, remain intact for 

the past 28 years! Manandhar achieved a rare distinction of 

“hat -trick” of victories in Marathon between 1984 and 1987 

while Usha holding the record of winning a record 5 gold 

medals in a single edition in Kolkata ( 3 individual + 2 relay 

events). However the credit of winning more number of 

medals in South Asian Games ’ history belongs to another 

Indian. Shiny Wilson ( -Abraham) had an amazing c ollection 

of 21 medals, 18 of them Gold (+2 silver and 1 bronze) 

during her campaign which lasted for 11 glorious years 

between 1984 and 1995. 

Island nation of Sri Lanka fielded a large number 

of its star -studded athletes in the past. Leading among the m

were champion sprinter Susanthika Jayasinghe, the 200m 

silver medalist in both Olympic Games and World 

Championships. Another of their fine athletes Sriyani 

Kulawansa had won 100m hurdles on five consecutive times 

between 1991 and 2004. 

Medal prospect s in Guwahati -2016: 

Based on the provisional list of entries available from various 

sources there will be a keen contest for the fastest man title 

in Guwahati. Sri Lanka’s Himasha Eashan, the fastest man 

in south asian region last year (10.36s at home in Dec) will 

log horn with teammat e Mohamed Ashrafu and Hassan 

Saaid of Maldives , both of them credited with an identical 

10.42 secs. 

Maldives had won only a bronze med al in the Games so far 

through a third place finish of their men’s 4x100m relay 

quartet at the last edition in Dhaka -2010. Saaid finished only 

sixth in 200m at Dhaka. But will he make amends and 

create history in Guwahati? 

Another top -notch competition is exp ected on field. 

Sumedha Ranasinghe thrilled everyone as he set the spear 

out to a remarkable distance of 83.04m – the best by a south 

asian javelin thrower to date – during the Sri Lankan trials for 

South Asian Games. Four weeks later Neeraj Chopra 

unleashed the 800 grams implement to a fantastic 81.04m 

during the qualifying rounds of Indian Universities 

championships at Patiala, the best by a junior (U20) athlete 

in the World in 2015. 

In the women’s side, Sri Lanka’s 2013 world championship 

finalist and Asian silver medalist Nadeeka Lakmali will 

expect a tough fight from India’s 2 014 Asian Games bronze 

medalist Annu Rani. 

The Indians fielded strong contenders in distance running 

and throwing events. It would be a cake -walk for Asian 

champion Inderjeet Singh in men’s shot put. The same will 

be the case in the women’s category wit h national record -

holder as another Rio Olympics qualifier Manpreet Singh will 

fight it out with her namesake Manpreet junior in Guwahati. 

Sri Lankan women are formidable in both long and triple 

jumps through NCD Priyadarshani and Vidusha Lakshani. 

They will be pitted against India’s former Asian long jump 

champion Mayookha Johny. 

In marathon , Sri Lanka’s 2004 SAG champion Anuradha 

Indrajith Cooray (14 th in last year’s London Marathon) will 

trying to gain another victory this time. The hosts are 

expe cted to challenge the Lankan runner in the form of 

Nitender Singh Rawat (8 th in World Military Games last year). 

It should be an easy go for the reigning champion Gayathry 

Govindaraj in women’s 100m hurdles this time. 

In the absence of Asian champion Ti ntu Luka, Wuhan 

bornze medalist Nimali (Sri Lanka) and fourth placer 

Gomathi (India) will try their might in Guwahati. 

There may be one or two surprises from other participating 

teams such as Pakistan which comes with a strong field that 

includes Mohsin Ali, the defending champion in 110m 

hurdles among others. Asian Athletics Digest February 5, 2016
