## Modified Text  
On this day, 26th October 1809, Jane Austen’s niece, Fanny Austen Knight, inscribed in her pocket book whilst sojourning at Chawton Cottage:  

“G.M., Aunt J. & I paid a call upon Miss Beckford, & Charles tarried the day there.”  

Fanny, accompanied by her grandmother and Jane, visited Miss Beckford. They called upon Maria Beckford at Chawton House, the sister-in-law of John Charles Middleton, the incumbent tenant. Maria had assumed the role of familial caretaker following the demise of her sister, Charlotte Beckford Middleton.  

Charlotte and Maria were progeny of Francis Beckford and Susanna Love of Basing Park and Froxfield in Hampshire. Their brother, Francis Love Beckford, inherited the estates in 1768. The Beckfords were exceedingly opulent, their fortune amassed from sugar cane plantations in Jamaica, sustained by the exploitation of enslaved laborers.  

Their kinsman was William Thomas Beckford, infamously dubbed “the Fool of Fonthill,” who came into prodigious wealth at the tender age of ten. He led a profligate existence, precipitating scandals, and erected Fonthill Abbey, a grandiose Gothic revival edifice that ultimately succumbed to ruin.  

Francis Love Beckford espoused Joanna Leigh, a distant relation of Jane Austen. Margaret Beckford, daughter of William Thomas Beckford, eloped with Lieutenant James Orde in 1810, purportedly repudiated by her father. The Ordes likewise surface in Jane’s correspondence, intertwining with her social milieu.  

Sources: Deirdre Le Faye’s *A Chronology of Jane Austen and Her Family* and George Holbert Tucker’s *Jane Austen the Woman*.
