# US Presidential Election Update: What you need to know 

Nordea Research, 07 November 2016 

FBI finds no new evidence in emails to charge Clinton. Clinton continues to be the favourite to win the race, but uncertainty remains high. Turnout and late-deciding voters could determine the difference between a clear Clinton win, a narrow Clinton win and a narrow victory to Trump. 

• Another day, another bombshell. The ever-turbulent presidential race took another dramatic turn on Sunday afternoon as FBI Director James Comey said that a review of new evidence gave it no reason to change its earlier decision that Hillary Clinton should not face charges related to her use of a private email server — a revelation that comes a little more than a week after Comey first set the race into a frenzy. Asian stock markets, US stock futures and the USD jumped in early Asia trading after the FBI comments. 

• Overall, Clinton continues to be the favourite to win the race according to most national polls, holding a broader path to the White House than Trump. Over the weekend Trump’s momentum seems to have halted and Clinton may even be regaining ground .

• Clinton’s average lead in national polls is now 1.8% point , marginally up from 1.6% on Friday afternoon but still significantly down from 7% just over two weeks ago, according to RealClearPolitics.com (RCP), see charts and tables below. 

• Clinton is in a much stronger electoral position than Trump. But given an unusually broad swing-state map, with the outcome in at least a dozen states still in some doubt, uncertainty remains high. Thus both a narrow Trump win and a more robust Clinton win are entirely plausible outcomes . Clinton's electoral vote total is at 216 when you add up all the states that are solidly or leaning in her direction , according to RCP's polling data. 270 electoral votes are needed to win the nexus.nordea.com/research 

White House. Trump's is 164 when you combine all the states that are solidly or leaning in his direction. That leaves a full 14 remaining battleground contests worth a total of 158 electoral votes.  

> •

According to FiveThirtyEight.com’s polls-based model, the probability of a Trump win is 35% , little changed since Friday but still sharply up from just 12% in mid-October.  

> •

That said, there is reason for caution. A polling error seems more likely than usual this year, because of the high number of undecided voters . In national polls there is still 8-9% of likely voters who are either undecided or are currently backing third-party candidates . This is unusually high this late in the game – for comparison the share was just 3% at the end of the 2012 race.  

> •

Turnout and late-deciding voters could determine the difference between a clear Clinton win, a narrow Clinton win and a narrow victory to Trump.  

> •

Market-based prediction models, based on bookmakers’ odds, continue to see Clinton as a clear winner , with only an 11% chance of a Trump win, according to PredictWise.com. nexus.nordea.com/research

## Our views  

> •

Based on recent polls we continue to expect Hillary Clinton to win the race against Donald Trump. But a 

Brexit-style surprise in the US presidential election still cannot be ruled out . 

> •

Regarding the congressional elections, Republicans are likely to maintain a majority in the House of Representatives , while the Senate is expected to tip to the Democrats, although the race seems very close.  

> •

In case of a Clinton win , we would expect a short-lived risk-on sentiment to dominate financial markets with a relief rally in stock markets immediately after the election, assuming no legal disputes about the result , see table below for more details. But if Trump mounts a legal challenge to contest the election result or demand a recount, uncertainty could potentially dominate financial markets for weeks.  

> •

If Trump wins the election , risk-off sentiment is expected to dominate with flight-to- quality immediately after the election due to increased uncertainty, see table. A significant tightening of financial conditions would make a Fed rate hike in December less likely. nexus.nordea.com/research
