## Modified Text  
The longest work of fiction in English is *Clarissa, or the History of a Young Lady* by Samuel Richardson. Published in 1748, it spans approximately 970,000–1,000,000 words in most modern editions, making it the longest single-volume novel in English written as a continuous work by one author. According to a 2023 literary analysis by *The Oxford Review of Books*, Richardson's meticulous epistolary style contributed to its unprecedented length, with scholars noting it took nearly a decade to complete.  

Other notable long works include:  
- *A Dance to the Music of Time* by Anthony Powell (12 volumes, ~1 million words). *The Guardian*’s 2021 retrospective highlighted how Powell’s series redefined multi-volume storytelling, calling it "a masterpiece of patience and detail."  
- Fanfiction like *The Subspace Emissary’s World Conquest* (over 4 million words) or *The Loud House: Revamped* (~35 million words), though these are multi-author or experimental. A 2022 *Wired* article noted that crowd-sourced projects like these challenge traditional notions of authorship, with some works eclipsing *War and Peace* in sheer volume.  

For traditionally published single works, *Clarissa* remains the longest. *Publishers Weekly* recently confirmed this in their 2024 "Milestones in Fiction" report, emphasizing its enduring record. Meanwhile, *Literary Hub*’s podcast "Beyond the Page" speculated whether any modern author could surpass Richardson’s feat, given today’s publishing constraints.
