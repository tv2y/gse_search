Abstract
--------

Foreseeing changes in how smart cities manage their resources and provide services to the residents; research, development and production in various relevant technology fields is accelerating. Taking advantage of recent advances and innovations in Information and Communication Technologies (ICT), robotics and software; smart cities can optimize resources utilization and enhance operations in health, transportation, energy, and water services, as well as elevating the level of comfort of residents. Effectively and efficiently utilizing ICT and robotics in smart cities will result in reducing costs and resources consumption in addition to engaging more effectively and actively with the citizens. One of these technologies is the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), which can provide many applications for smart cities and create a positive impact on the society. For example, UAVs can be used for environmental monitoring, traffic management, pollution monitoring, civil security control, and merchandise delivery. UAV applications among several others can provide cost-effective services to help achieve the objectives of smart cities. However, the integration of UAVs in smart cities is very challenging due to several issues and concerns such as safety, privacy and ethical/legal use. This paper reviews the potential applications integrating UAVs in smart cities, their implications, and the technical and non-technical issues facing such integration. It also discusses regulations and enabling technologies currently available and being developed that can be utilized to support such integration.

Introduction
------------

The world population is rapidly increasing and expected to double by 2050. There is also the continuous where many people are moving from rural areas to the cities. This trend is anticipated to grow more rapidly in the coming years especially in developing countries. This rapid increase in population in cities will create many challenges for cities' administrators striving to maintain or enhance the cities' services and the residents' quality of life. Consequently, there is an increased interest in utilizing advanced Information and Communication Technologies (ICT), robotics and smart solutions in smart cities developments (Shreih, 2013). These technologies will help create smart automated services to improve the performance of infrastructures and comfort level of residents. The main goal of a smart city design is to provide efficient infrastructures and services at reduced costs. The European Platform for Intelligent Cities and the European Network of Living Labs defined smart cities as “The use of discrete new technology applications such as RFID and Internet of Things through more holistic conception of intelligent, integrated working that is closely linked to the concept of living and user generated services” (European Platform for Intelligent Cities, 2017). There are well-defined pillars of any smart city, such as smart mobility, smart governance, smart environment, and smart people. These pillars help create and enhance smart cities applications such as public health, energy and water services, city management and economic development, transportation, security and emergency services, and waste management (Khan et al., 2013).

The design and operations of smart cities require full integration of ICT-based solutions with the smart cities services. Many technologies can significantly contribute in this regard and UAVs (unmanned aerial vehicles) are one of those that can become an essential component in smart cities (Mohammed et al., 2014a). In this paper we aim to highlight several aspects relating to the integration of UAVs with smart cities components (e.g. infrastructures, buildings, facilities, administrations, and residents). Our study of current and future applications of UAVs in smart cities and their implications, potential advantages and risks is one of the contributions of this paper. In addition, we offer a concise discussion of the technical and non-technical issues that must be addressed to facilitate this integration and lead to an effective, safe and secure set of applications. Moreover, we cover the different enabling technologies (current and future) that will support UAVs and smart cities integration along with the relevant regulations imposed in different countries regarding the use of UAVs.

UAVs are flexible and fast mobile platforms that can be used for many applications in smart cities. These include traffic and crowd monitoring, environmental monitoring, civil security, merchandise delivery, to name a few (see Fig. 1). The UAV applications designed to meet smart cities requirements and functionalities will tie UAVs to smart cities in productive manners. For example, when a UAV is equipped with monitoring sensors and cameras and provided with the software to analyze sensed data from a city's infrastructure, we get applications for smart cities infrastructure inspection and control. UAVs were initially only utilized for military applications. However, they have recently been widely used in civil applications in many domains such as agriculture, logistics, environmental protection, public safety and traffic control. In addition, one of the emerging areas of UAVs is their involvement in smart cities applications resulting in various benefits. Unlike manned planes, UAVs can be more cost-effective. They are more flexible and can operate in various locations and situations, including those that are challenging or posing high risks for humans. They can also fly very close to target objects, which allows for higher measurement accuracy and better targeted actions. These features provide advantages if UAVs are used for smart cities applications. This paper aims to provide insight into the potential applications and issues of integrating UAVs in smart cities.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides an overview of smart cities, UAVs, and other related technologies. Section 3 discusses some potential UAV-based smart cities applications. Section 4 addresses the general technical and non-technical issues and concerns of utilizing UAVs for smart cities. Enabling technologies for integrating UAVs in smart cities are discussed in Section 5, while Section 6 discusses some enabling regulations. Section 7 discusses the potential impacts and risks of employing UAVs in smart cities. Section 8 concludes the paper.
