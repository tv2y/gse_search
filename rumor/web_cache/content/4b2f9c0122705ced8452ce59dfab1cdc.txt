DHS confirms 5 new cases of measles in Oconto County
====================================================

BY  Rachel Ryan Wisconsin

PUBLISHED 1:54 PM ET Aug. 22, 2025 PUBLISHED 1:54 PM EDT Aug. 22, 2025

SHARE

WISCONSIN — There are five new cases of measles in Oconto County, bringing the total number of cases in the state up to 14, according to the Wisconsin Department of Health Services.

* * *

#### **What You Need To Know**

##### *   There are five new cases of measles in Oconto County

*   DHS said the new cases are linked to the nine prior cases confirmed in the same county this month

*   This brings the total number of cases in the state up to 14

*   In light of the new cases, DHS is urging people to check if they are vaccinated or not. You can do that,here

* * *

DHS said the new cases are linked to the nine prior cases confirmed in the same county earlier this month.

The organization said this “indicates that measles is spreading locally.”

It said it’s working with Oconto County Public Health and local health departments to identify and notify others who may have been exposed. However, DHS said it has not yet identified any public locations where people may have been exposed.

Measles is highly contagious, spreading from person to person through the air. It can stay in the air for up to two hours after a sick person coughs or sneezes.

Up to 90% of people around someone that’s infected with measles can get it if they aren’t vaccinated.

Symptoms of measles include:

*   Runny nose
*   High fever (may be greater than 104°F)
*   Tiredness
*   Cough
*   Red, watery eyes, or conjunctivitis (“pink eye”)
*   A red rash with raised bumps that starts at the hairline and moves to the arms and legs three to five days after symptoms begin

These symptoms can start 10 to 21 days after exposure. If you develop symptoms, DHS recommended staying home and said to call your doctor’s office or clinic before going to ensure you don’t expose others.

Measles is typically prevented through the measles, mumps, rubella vaccine (MMR), with two doses being 97% effective in prevention. In light of the new cases, DHS urged people to check if they are vaccinated or not. You can do that, here.

DHS also issued a warning on Tuesday that an out-of-state traveler may have exposed people to measles at truck stops in Roberts and Beloit. That exposure is not related to the Oconto County cases.
