## Enhanced Text  

Rhyme is the correspondence of terminal sounds, typically a vowel-consonant pairing such as "ing," "eet," or "ite." Rhyme schemes are conventionally annotated with alphabetic notation (A, B, C, etc.), where identical letters demarcate corresponding terminal phonemes. However, rhyme extends beyond line-final positioning—internal rhyme orchestrates sonorous echoes within a single verse, as exemplified in Edgar Allan Poe’s *The Raven*.  

For this month’s challenge, compose a poem featuring internal rhyme—a technique wherein harmonious lexemes resonate mid-line rather than terminally. Recommendations:  
- Curate a lexicon of phonetically congruent terms aligned with your thematic focus.  
- Employ metrical structure to situate rhymes with deliberate cadence.  
- Eschew contrived consonance for lexically intractable words (e.g., "orange").  

Meter, the systematic alternation of accented and unaccented syllables, facilitates the strategic placement of internal rhymes. Poe frequently positioned rhyming elements at fixed metrical intervals (e.g., every fourth iambic foot). While metrical rigor is not obligatory, its application can amplify the lyrical potency of internal rhyme.  

Key imperative: Prioritize the interlacing of rhymes within your verses, not merely at their denouement.
