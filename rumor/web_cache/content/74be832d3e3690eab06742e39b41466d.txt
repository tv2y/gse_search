## www.wncrecovery.nc.gov INTRODUCTION 

This report provides an overview of the state and federal funds dedicated to Hurricane Helene relief and recovery efforts through June 30, 2025, and how North Carolina is using those resources to aid affected communities as required by SL 2025-2 (HB 47) § 4.1(g-h) .Unless otherwise noted, this third quarterly report encompasses activities from the onset of the storm through June 30, 2025. 

# Hurricane Helene’s Impact – $59.6 Billion 

Hurricane Helene reached North Carolina on Friday, September 27, 2024, generating historic rainfall, strong winds, landslides, and tornadoes, among other weather impacts. President Biden subsequently declared a Major Disaster, and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) designated 39 counties for federal disaster assistance. An estimated 4.6 million people – more than 40% of the state’s population – live in one of the designated counties. The region accounts for 45% of the state’s GDP. Hurricane Helene had a significant, long-term impact on western North Carolina. In addition to the devastating loss of more than 100 lives, the storm destroyed thousands of homes and damaged tens of thousands more. Millions of North Carolinians lost access to critical services such as water and sewer, electricity, telecommunications, and health care. Thousands of miles of roads and bridges were damaged, cutting off communities and 1limiting access for residents, first responders, and recovery teams. The region’s economy has suffered a severe blow, threatening livelihoods and the long-term outlook of some communities. Estimates as of December 13, 2024, indicated that Helene caused direct damages of $44.4 billion in the region, caused an additional $9.4 billion in indirect or induced damages, and necessitated $5.8 billion more for strengthening and mitigation. 1 Totaling more than $59.6 billion across the state, these damages and needs are more than three times the $16.7 billion impact of Hurricane Florence in 2018. 

# Government Funding for Recovery Efforts – $7.6 Billion 

As of June 30, 2025, the federal government and North Carolina state government had directed approximately $7.6 billion – about 12.8% of the estimated impact of the storm – to aid western North Carolina communities in the aftermath of Hurricane Helene. The state has provided just over $2.9 billion in funding, including roughly $2 billion in new appropriations and $508 million in reallocations of previously appropriated funds by the North Carolina General Assembly, and at least $413.8 million in existing state operational resources redirected by state agencies toward response and recovery efforts. In addition, federal funding totaling $1.61 billion has been disbursed, including $1.1 billion in eligibility-based FEMA funding to individuals, businesses, local government, state agencies, and nonprofits. Another $3.1 billion in federal funding has been awarded to the State of North Carolina but requires additional approval steps before being disbursed to the state. 

# $2 billion in state funds had been appropriated as of June 30, 2025. 

State agencies are working to deploy funding effectively and efficiently to speed recovery efforts. The total $2 billion in appropriated state funds includes $331 million for state matching funds to draw down dollars from federal programs and $272 million for loans to local governments or businesses. Other state appropriations require certain conditions to be met before expenditure. For example, $120 million was appropriated for the State Home Reconstruction and Repair Program, which the state may begin spending upon completing federally reimbursable contractor procurements. The remainder of the funds may be spent by state agencies without significant constraints.

# Scope of Data Included 

This third quarterly report provides an updated overview of Hurricane Helene recovery expenditures and activities to date. The report is based on state and federal programmatic and financial data through June 30, 2025. Please note that obligations and expenditures for the most recent Hurricane Helene relief session law, S.L. 2025-26, are not included here. The funds appropriated by that law were not certified and made available to state agencies until late July 2025, which fell after the June 30, 2025, cutoff date for the data and information included in this report. 3State Funding 

As of June 30, 2025 – the end of Fiscal Year 2025 – the North Carolina General Assembly had appropriated to state agencies approximately $2 billion in new funds, reallocated to agencies another $508 million in previously appropriated funds, and set aside in reserve but not appropriated to agencies another $137.2 million for response and recovery efforts related to Hurricane Helene. This amount addresses 4.4% of the total Helene-related damage and need. The NCGA’s most recent act to provide funding for Helene recovery was Session Law 2025-26, known as The Disaster Recovery Act of 2025 – Part II , signed into law by Governor Stein on June 27, 2025. This law appropriated almost $576 million and reallocated $302 million in state funds for Helene recovery efforts. Although this law was enacted prior to the end of June 2025 and is therefore included in the total appropriation amount, the funds were not made available to state agencies until July 2025, as they were subject to the standard certification process conducted by the Office of State Budget and Management. Thus, those funds could not be obligated or expended during the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025, and they are not included in this report. In addition to using the funds provided to them by the NCGA specifically for Helene-related purposes, state agencies have also redirected $413 million of the funds they received prior to the storm. Some portion of expenses for these activities will be eligible for FEMA reimbursement. But a significant portion will be borne as state opportunity costs – the effect of redirecting resources away from other activities. As of June 30, 2025 State Fiscal Year Q4 Department of Commerce State Home Reconstruction and Repair Program 

This program operates to improve the timeline for single-family housing repair and reconstruction while the state works with federal partners to receive the $1.4 billion allocation of funds from the US Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) for Community Development Block Grants for Disaster Recovery. The program launched on June 16, 2025, under the name of RenewNC, and applications are now open. The Department of Commerce’s Division of Community Revitalization (DCR) has obligated $81,495,887.42 for the implementation vendor contract for the RenewNC Single Family Housing program. Funds have not been disbursed by DCR yet because DCR has structured the contract to pay for outcomes, not activities. Payments will be made as the vendor meets the milestones set out in the contract. By structuring the contract to award outcomes instead of activities, DCR is choosing a different contracting model than has been used in previous recoveries. As of June 30, 350 applications had been submitted. Most of the eligible applications were confirmed as Phase 1, Priority 1, meaning that the applicants are in a HUD-identified most impacted and distressed zone and below 60 percent of the area median income. n/a

North Carolina has received a total of $1.6 billion in federal funding as of June 30, 2025. # Awarded Federal Funds Highlights 

 $1.4 billion will be administered by the Department of Commerce Division of Community Revitalization (DCR) from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) for the state’s single family home repair and reconstruction program. Applications are open, and construction on homes should begin later this summer. 

 The City of Asheville received $225 million in Community Development Block Grant-Disaster Recovery (CDBG-DR), and its plan has been approved by HUD. The plan allocates $125 million for Infrastructure; $52 million for Economic Revitalization; $31 million for Housing; approximately $11.2 million for Administration; approximately $3.7 million for Planning; and $2 million for Public Services. 

 NC Department of Transportation (DOT) is preparing an authorization request for the US Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) for approximately $1.15 billion, in addition to the $750 million authorization already received. FHWA’s Emergency Relief Program provides federal funding to repair federal-aid highways and federal lands roads damaged by natural disasters and catastrophic events. DOT believes there will still be a shortfall of approximately $1 billion for Helene repairs that will need to be filled with state and federal funding. 

 NC Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) is administering $685 million from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to repair wastewater treatment and drinking water facilities. 

# American Relief Act Funds 

In December 2024, Congress appropriated funds for Helene recovery to the state through HR 10545, The American Relief Act of 2025 (ARA). In February 2025 the state estimated its 56 expected allocation from the ARA to be approximately $5.95 billion. As of June 30, 2025, the state is still waiting for federal agencies to disburse $4.2 billion of these appropriated funds to NC agencies. Federal funding disbursement delays have impacted state agency timelines for several major recovery components. For example, the timeline for private property debris removal and demolitions has lengthened because of slow and inconsistent disbursements from the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. In addition, as of June 30, 2025, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (USDHHS) had not opened the application period for the Child Care and Development Block Grant (CCDBG) which provides one-time disaster grant funds to child care providers, centers, homes, and Pre-K sites. # Hazard Mitigation Grant Program 

The state expects to receive about $1.4 billion from the FEMA Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP) to reduce the long-term risks to people and property from future disasters. The program provides grants to state, local, tribal, and territorial governments to implement cost-effective measures that seek to minimize disaster damage, enhance community resilience, and reduce reliance on federal disaster assistance. Eligible projects include infrastructure improvements, property acquisition, flood risk reduction, and community planning. So far, the state has received an initial $16.3 million for its management costs. The state is working with counties to batch and submit applications to FEMA on a rolling basis.
