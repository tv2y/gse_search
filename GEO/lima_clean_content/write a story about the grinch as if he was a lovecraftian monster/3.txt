The Grinch, initially portrayed as a wicked creature, ultimately redeems himself by taking accountability for his mistakes and performing a heroic act to save Whoville's Christmas gifts, even risking his life. Despite this redemption, the 1966 animated film's theme song by Thurl Ravenscroft vilified The Grinch, overshadowing his noble transformation. The song's lasting popularity cemented a negative perception of the Grinch, associating him with anti-Christmas sentiments rather than his eventual bravery and forgiveness. The article criticizes the song for perpetuating a one-sided, defamatory portrayal and suggests society should strive for fairer treatment of individuals, even fictional ones.
