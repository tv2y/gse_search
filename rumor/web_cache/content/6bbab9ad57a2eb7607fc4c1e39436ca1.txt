A blackout caused by the storm cut his oxygen supply and he lost his vital signs before his family was able to get him to hospital. Typhoon Danas swept over Southern Taiwan, causing a blackout that cost a person his life. It was 60-year-old Mr. Chen, a local borough warden who relied on a respirator. His medical equipment was left without power in the storm. With no oxygen supply, his family took him to hospital, but by the time they arrived, Mr. Chen had no vital signs. Resuscitation efforts failed. Lin Chien-nan Tainan North District head He had chronic illness and was bedridden. He still had everything under control, he was doing quite well. We at the district office are distraught to have lost such an excellent borough warden. Neighbor He relied on the oxygen supply. The typhoon caused a blackout, and since the respirator was turned off, he couldn’t get any oxygen. He passed away yesterday morning. Mr. Chen’s neighbors are devastated. Experts say families with medical needs like his should ideally keep an electricity generator at home just in case. Tsai Yun-cheng Long term care provider Nowadays there are portable oxygen concentrators that can last two to eight hours. Some people get generators or backup batteries if they are concerned about power outages. Another fatality was a farmer in Nansi District, who was crushed by a coconut tree while driving his truck. At about 2:30 a.m. on Monday, the 69-year old had been taking mangos to the market, when the palm fell on his truck. His son, who was also traveling on the vehicle, was sent flying. Chiang Sen-yuan Zhaoxing borough warden The base of the tree was spoiled. The typhoon blew it down, and the palm fell on the victims, just when they were passing by. The typhoon caused blackouts that affected 710,000 households, left two people dead and more than 600 injured.
