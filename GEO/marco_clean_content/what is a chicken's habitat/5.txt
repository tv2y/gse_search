The chicken (Gallus gallus domesticus) is a domesticated junglefowl species originating from Southeastern Asia. Males are called roosters or cocks, castrated males are capons, and adult females are hens. Chickens were initially raised for cockfighting or ceremonies before being kept for food during the Hellenistic period (4th–2nd centuries BC). Today, they are primarily sources of meat and eggs and are also kept as pets.  

Chickens are the most common domestic animals, with a population of 23.7 billion (2018). They have multiple maternal origins in Asia but spread globally from the Indian subcontinent.  

**Appearance & Behavior:**  
- Chickens may live 5–10 years (up to 16 years recorded).  
- Roosters have striking plumage, combs, and wattles, while some breeds show less sexual dimorphism.  
- They are gregarious, forming flocks with a pecking order.  
- Chickens can fly short distances, primarily to escape danger.  
- Hens are protective of chicks, leading them to food and shelter.  

**Mating & Reproduction:**  
- Roosters perform courtship dances; mating involves cloacal contact ("cloacal kiss").  
- Hens prefer communal nesting, often laying eggs in shared locations.  
- Eggs hatch after ~21 days, with chicks breaking shells using an egg tooth.  

**Domestication & Pets:**  
- Chickens became popular pets in the 2000s, valued for companionship and personalities.  
- Breeds like Silkies are docile and suitable for children.  
- They are social, intelligent, and can recognize handlers.  

**Cultural Significance:**  
- The term "rooster" emerged as a euphemism for "cock" in the 18th century.  
- In Greek mythology, Alectryon was turned into a rooster for failing to guard Ares and Aphrodite.  
- Roosters are associated with the sun due to their dawn crowing.  

**Adaptations:**  
- High-altitude chickens (e.g., Tibetan) produce more hemoglobin for low-oxygen environments.  

Chickens exhibit defensive behaviors, such as mobbing predators, and communicate through calls. Their reproduction is hormonally regulated, with declining fertility linked to neuroendocrine changes.
