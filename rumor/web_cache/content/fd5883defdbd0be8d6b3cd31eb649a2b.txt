The Genesis GV80 is the current range-topper in the premium marque's lineup, but that will change by the time the upcoming GV90 arrives. However, reports from Korea are saying that the Neolun Concept-based SUV is going to be behind schedule due to changing global trends, particularly when it comes to the demand for battery-electric vehicles (BEVs).

The Genesis GV90 was supposed to have its grand global debut in late 2025, but was delayed to March 2026 due to the slowdown in demand for BEVs. However, that has been pushed further to June 2026, according to _The Korea Economic Daily_, which recently obtained the Hyundai Group's product development plan. Of note, the GV90 is to be built at Hyundai's new plant in Ulsan, South Korea, which is dedicated to EV production.

It Will Keep Faith With Neolun's Panache
----------------------------------------

The GV90 is bound to become Genesis' most luxurious model, taking aim at the Mercedes EQS and GLS SUVs, BMW's X7, and Audi's upcoming Q9. It will use Hyundai Group's new eM electric vehicle platform, available in various sizes from full-size SUVs to compact cars. Of note, there will be 13 models to be built on this platform across Hyundai, Kia, and Genesis, to arrive by the end of the decade.

A lot has changed in the last few years with many big name brands changing trajectory.

There have been a few sightings of the production GV90 in the wild, some unfortunately in unflattering situations, and it appears that it will retain the Neolun concept's styling and panache, even taking a page out of Rolls-Royce's playbook. The interior hasn't been caught by spies, though, but Genesis has promised a huge improvement on the cabin of its upcoming vehicles, while keeping physical buttons intact. Some spy shots have shown versions with the concept's suicide doors, too.

The Hyundai Group Is Going Big Next Year
----------------------------------------

Apart from the GV90's delay, _The Korea Economic Daily_ also reported that the Hyundai Group is set to reveal a total of 42 new vehicles in 2026, comprising 21 models from Hyundai, 14 from Kia, and seven from Genesis. The barrage of new vehicles will have to contend with a new 25 percent tariff on imported automobiles, the decline in demand for BEVs, and the general economic slowdown.
