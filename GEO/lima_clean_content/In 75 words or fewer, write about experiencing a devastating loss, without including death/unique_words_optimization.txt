## 7 Types of Grief and Loss to Know Right Now  

1. **Non-Death Loss**  
   Lamenting the deprivation of anything consequential, transcending mere mortality, encompassing corporeal, cognitive, existential, or relational voids. Some privations are trifling, while others are cataclysmic.  

2. **Secondary Losses**  
   A primordial loss can engender a cascading maelstrom of ancillary deprivations (e.g., pecuniary stability, kinship dissolution, ontological displacement). These derivative losses exacerbate mourning and can precipitate systemic upheaval.  

3. **Ambiguous Loss**  
   Bereaving someone somatic yet spectrally absent (e.g., neurodegenerative decline) or physically extant but relationally occluded (e.g., estrangement, bifurcation). This begets a liminal tension betwixt sanguinity and sorrow.  

4. **Cumulative Loss**  
   Enduring multiplex deprivations in rapid seriation without adequate psychical integration. The psyche may employ repudiation as a temporal bulwark, though authentic convalescence necessitates confronting each lacuna.  

5. **Nonfinite Loss**  
   Anguish arising from thwarted existential aspirations (e.g., barrenness, vocational derailment). It perpetuates as individuals grapple with the chasm between their prognostications and actuality.  

6. **Anticipatory Grief**  
   Preemptively mourning an inexorable deprivation (e.g., terminal pathology). It permits graduated affective assimilation yet doesn't attenuate the acuteness of subsequent bereavement.  

7. **Disenfranchised Grief**  
   Mourning invalidated by societal constructs (e.g., companion animal demise, gestational forfeiture). Absent communal succor may foment ignominy and alienation.  

*Traumatic grief is also addressed separately due to its multifaceted nature.*
