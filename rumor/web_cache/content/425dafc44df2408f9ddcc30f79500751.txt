Plumber suing dealership after ex-company vehicle ends up with Syrian fighters
==============================================================================

Wednesday, December 16, 2015

*     

The photo of terrorists riding on a truck that used to belong to him went viral on social media last year. Now he's suing for damages after threats began pouring in.

TEXAS CITY, Texas -- A Texas City-based plumbing company filed a lawsuit against AutoNation Ford Gulf Freeway for failing to remove a company decal on a truck before reselling it.

The Ford F-250 pickup truck had the company name, Mark-1 Plumbing, and phone number printed on the side of the truck. The company information was never removed from the truck, and the truck ended up in the hands of fighters in Syria. A picture of the truck carrying anti-aircraft guns appeared in a terrorist propaganda video.

A Texas plumber is suing a used car dealer after his work truck ended up in the hands of ISIS overseas.

He explains, "Constant calls, threats - nothing about plumbing."" /]

Since then, Mark-1 Plumbing employees have received death threats, and their business has suffered, according to attorney Craig Eiland.

"His secretary was recently called an ISIS *****," Eiland said, "Some (threats) were scary, some were people saying, 'I'm gonna kick your ***. You don't want to be an American, get outta here and I'll help you go."

Eiland said AutoNation resold the truck at an auction in Dallas, and a buyer from Turkey had the truck shipped out of the country from the Port of Houston. He said AutoNation was supposed to remove the company decal.

"The son started to take the decals off and was told, 'Don't take it off. You might scratch the paint. We will do it,'" Eiland said.

AutoNation Chief Marketing Officer Marc Cannon said the company is investigating how this happened.

"We immediately sent the vehicle to auction, and the understanding was that the markings would come off the vehicle," Cannon said. "I'm not sure where the breakdown was between AutoNation and the auction. It's an unfortunate situation."

The owner of Mark-1 Plumbing, Mark Oberholtzer, is currently on a business trip in McAllen, Texas. His administrative assistant told Eyewitness News she is still afraid to come to work because she has received so many threatening phone calls.

Oberholtzer says that his family is getting death threats.

He explains, "Constant calls, threats - nothing about plumbing."
