Here are the key points to consider when buying a PC case:

### **1. Form Factor and Motherboard Compatibility**  
- Match the case size (ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX, E-ATX) to your motherboard.  
- Ensure the case supports your motherboard size explicitly.  

### **2. Cooling and Airflow**  
- Check fan mounts and included fans. More mounting points offer better cooling flexibility.  
- Mesh front panels improve airflow but may lack aesthetics; tempered glass looks better but restricts airflow.  

### **3. GPU and CPU Cooler Clearance**  
- Measure maximum GPU length and CPU cooler height to ensure fit.  
- Remove drive cages if needed for longer GPUs.  

### **4. Storage and Expandability**  
- Number of 3.5" (HDD) and 2.5" (SSD) drive bays.  
- Tool-less drive trays or modular brackets simplify installation.  

### **5. Build Quality and Cable Management**  
- Adequate space behind the motherboard tray for cable routing.  
- Grommets and Velcro straps help organize cables.  

### **6. Front I/O and Connectivity**  
- USB ports (preferably USB-C and multiple USB-A).  
- Audio jacks and easily accessible power/reset buttons.  

### **7. Dust Management**  
- Removable dust filters for easy cleaning.  

### **8. Noise Control**  
- Sound-dampening materials reduce noise but may restrict airflow.  

### **9. Aesthetics and Materials**  
- Tempered glass for visibility vs. steel for durability and noise reduction.  

### **10. Power Supply Compatibility**  
- Check PSU size (ATX, SFX) and clearance, especially with modular cables.  

### **Quick Checklist**  
- **Must-Haves:** Correct motherboard size, GPU/CPU cooler clearance, fan mounts.  
- **Nice-to-Haves:** USB-C ports, modular drive bays, dust filters, tempered glass.  

### **Tradeoffs to Consider**  
- **Compact cases** save space but may limit cooling and compatibility.  
- **Soundproofing** reduces noise but can increase temperatures.  

**Final Tip:** Cross-check specs with manufacturer details and recent build reviews for real-world compatibility.
