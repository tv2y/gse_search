About the Crisis in Syria
-------------------------

As the crisis in Syria extends into its fourteenth year, the humanitarian situation inside and out of the country is more difficult than ever. An estimated 16.7 million people still need humanitarian assistance and protection and more than half of the pre-war population remain displaced from their homes. More than 7.4 million people are displaced inside Syria and at least 6 million are refugees, mainly in neighboring countries including Türkiye, Lebanon and Jordan. Women and children make up more than two thirds of those displaced.

Since the fall of the Assad Government in December 2024, UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency, has reported spontaneous returns to Syria—which are expected to continue throughout 2025. More than 1 million Syrians have returned home, including some 301,967 Syrians from neighboring countries like Türkiye, Lebanon and Jordan, and 885,294 internally displaced persons (IDPs) since the end of November 2024. Although these recent returns mark a turning point in the crisis, humanitarian needs persist and concerns about safety and security inside Syria remain a key obstacle to return. Over 90 percent of Syrians live under the poverty line and it's estimated that 12.9 million people are food insecure.

UNHCR stands ready to support Syrian refugees who choose voluntarily to return and urges continued protection and support for Syrians inside and outside the country.

What is UNHCR doing to help Syrian refugees?
--------------------------------------------

The UN Refugee Agency has been on the ground since the start of the crisis providing shelter, lifesaving supplies, clean water, hot meals and medical care to families who have been forced to flee their homes. During winter, UNHCR also supports vulnerable families with vital winter relief items - such as warm clothes, sleeping bags, thermal blankets, stoves, fuel for heating and insulation for tents - as well as cash assistance to help Syrian refugees pay rent and purchase food and medicine.

UNHCR and its partners are scaling up humanitarian aid to meet the huge needs of returnees. This includes supporting people voluntarily returning from neighboring countries with transportation and legal support, as well as help to repair their damaged houses, providing cash assistance and relief items such as mattresses, blankets and winter clothing.
