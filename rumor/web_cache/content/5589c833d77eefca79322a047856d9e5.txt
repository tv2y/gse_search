#Register for September Hotel Tester 2025 Huangdi City, Hebei Walk through the ruins of Huangdi City, observe the remaining rammed earth city walls, explore relics such as the Huangdi Spring within the city, and learn about the layout and function of the ancient city. Huangdi City is one of the earliest surviving capital city ruins in China. Its rammed earth walls bear witness to the capital construction techniques of tribal alliances in the late primitive society, reflecting the social organization and defensive needs of the time. Huangdi Spring, the city's core water source, exemplifies the primitive wisdom of "living near water" in site selection. It also carries the legend of "Huangdi drawing water to nourish his people," a commemoration of our ancestors' spirit of benefiting the people. Three Ancestors Hall of China In the Three Ancestors Hall, you can admire statues of Huangdi, Yandi, and Chiyou. View the mural themed "Three Ancestors Culture" and listen to instructors explain the historical background and significance of the "Battle of Banquan" and "Battle of Zhuolu." The Three Ancestors Hall breaks with the traditional narrative of "exclusively honoring the Yellow Emperor" by listing Yan Emperor (a representative of agricultural civilization), Chi You (a leader of the Dongyi tribe and a pioneer in metal smelting and weaponry), and the Yellow Emperor as the "Three Ancestors of China." This core message acknowledges the diverse origins of Chinese civilization: the agricultural techniques brought by Yan Emperor and the handicrafts techniques introduced by Chi You, combined with the tribal governance wisdom of the Yellow Emperor, together laid the foundation of Chinese civilization. This embodies the national spirit of "harmony, coexistence, and inclusiveness" and echoes the contemporary concept of "the Chinese nation as one family." Hefu Altar In the core area of ​​the Hefu Altar, the central Nine Soaring Dragons sculpture is the focal point. Zhuolu, Zhangjiakou Starting from Deshengmen in Beijing and heading 130 kilometers north, you can reach Huangdi City in Zhuolu, Hebei. Huangdi City, also known as Xuanyuan City, is an ancient city site located on the west side of Fanshan Town in Zhuolu County. It is said that this is the ancient battlefield of the Battle of Banquan between the Yellow Emperor and the Yan Emperor, as well as the Battle of Banquan between the Yellow Emperor and Chiyou. Here, the Yellow Emperor's tribe united with the Yan Emperor's tribe to defeat Chiyou's tribe, leading to the Yellow Emperor unifying various tribes of China and establishing the first capital city in Chinese history. To this day, Zhuolu still preserves multiple sites and relics related to the culture of the three ancestors, such as Huangdi Spring, Chiyou's Three Villages, and the Hall of the Three Ancestors. The Battle of Banquan was a key battle in the establishment of the Huaxia ethnic group. There are various historical accounts regarding the exact location of the Battle of Banquan. Some believe it was in the southeast of today's Zhuolu County in Hebei Province, while others think it was within the borders of Changzhi in Shanxi, or Yanqing County in Beijing, or other places. However, regardless of the exact location, the Battle of Banquan is the most famous battle between the two ancient ancestors of the Chinese nation, the Yellow Emperor and the Yan Emperor. It holds significant importance in the ultimate formation of the Chinese nation and is therefore regarded as the battle that marked the beginning of Chinese civilization. Of course, this period of history is still largely in the realm of legend, with not much archaeological evidence to support it. Even the history of the Xia Dynasty is not yet clear, let alone the history before the Xia Dynasty. However, people today still tend to believe that this is the ancient battlefield of the Battle of Banquan, providing a focal point for their remembrance. Today's Zhuolu County is a small, unremarkable, and not very affluent county not far north of Beijing, with no visible traces of the tribal conflicts that took place here five thousand years ago.
