### **Overview of Grief, Bereavement, and Coping with Loss**  
- Grief is an emotional response to loss (primarily death), while **mourning** refers to the public display of grief, influenced by cultural and religious practices.  
- **Bereavement** describes the objective state of having lost someone important.  

### **Types of Grief Reactions**  
1. **Anticipatory Grief**:  
   - Occurs before death, often in caregivers or terminal patients.  
   - Symptoms resemble post-death grief but may not lessen subsequent grief intensity.  
   - Risk factors: dependent relationships, low social support, pessimistic thinking.  

2. **Normal/Common Grief**:  
   - Experienced by 50–85% of bereaved individuals.  
   - Symptoms include shock, yearning, sadness, guilt, and disrupted daily functioning.  
   - Typically resolves within 6 months to 2 years.  

3. **Complicated Grief (Prolonged Grief Disorder)**:  
   - Diagnosed when intense grief persists beyond 6–12 months (per DSM-5/ICD-11).  
   - Symptoms: persistent sorrow, preoccupation with the deceased, inability to accept the death, social withdrawal.  
   - Risk factors: traumatic death, lack of social support, preexisting mental health conditions.  

4. **Models of Normal Grief**:  
   - Kübler-Ross’s stages (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) have limited empirical support.  
   - Four-stage model (numbness, yearning, disorganization, reorganization) aligns better with evidence.  

### **Risk Factors for Complicated Grief**  
- **Situational**: Sudden/unexpected death, unresolved conflict.  
- **Personal**: Insecure attachment styles, rumination, younger age.  
- **Interpersonal**: Lack of social support, financial instability.  

### **Treatment Approaches**  
1. **Psychosocial Treatments**:  
   - **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**: Effective for complicated grief, focusing on exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring.  
   - **Complicated Grief Treatment (CGT)**: Combines CBT with interpersonal therapy.  
   - **Family Therapy**: Addresses communication and cohesion after loss.  

2. **Pharmacological Treatments**:  
   - Antidepressants (e.g., citalopram, nortriptyline) help with comorbid depression but have limited impact on grief intensity alone.  
   - Combined therapy (medication + psychotherapy) shows the best outcomes.  

### **Children and Grief**  
- Developmental stages influence understanding:  
  - **<6 years**: View death as temporary/reversible.  
  - **6–9 years**: Personify death (e.g., as a "boogeyman").  
  - **≥9 years**: Understand death’s finality.  
- Key concerns: Self-blame, fear of abandonment, need for caregiving stability.  
- Interventions: Honest explanations, participation in rituals, play therapy.  

### **Cross-Cultural Responses**  
- Grief expressions vary widely; rituals (e.g., wakes, memorials) are culturally prescribed.  
- Clinicians should ask about cultural practices, beliefs about death, and family roles to provide sensitive care.  

### **Key Takeaways**  
- **Normal grief** resolves naturally; **complicated grief** requires intervention (therapy ± medication).  
- Children grieve intermittently and need age-appropriate support.  
- Cultural practices deeply influence mourning and should be respected in caregiving.
